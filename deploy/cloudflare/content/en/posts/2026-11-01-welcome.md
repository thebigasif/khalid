Your new blog is live. Cloudflare built it from the files in your GitHub repository and serves it from its network around the world, for free. Every change you push to GitHub builds the blog again, so publishing is as simple as saving a file.

This post shows the three things to do first: write a post, set your address, and let Khalid check your work. Read it once, then replace it with the first real post of your new blog.

## Write a post on your new blog

A post is a text file in `content/en/posts/`. The easiest start is to copy this one, give the copy a new file name, and change the lines between the two `---` marks at the top:

- `title` is the headline readers and search engines see.
- `slug` is the end of the post's address, such as `/welcome-new-blog/` for this post. Keep it short.
- `date` is the day the post goes live.
- `author` names the file in `content/en/authors/` that says who wrote it.
- `focus_keyword` is the phrase you want the post to be found for.
- `description` is the short summary search engines show under the title.

Write the post below those lines in plain text. A line that starts with `##` is a heading, a line that starts with `-` is a list item, and a word in square brackets followed by an address in round brackets is a link. Read more about the blog on the [about page](/about/).

## Set your address

Open `site.toml` and put your blog's address after `address`, such as the `workers.dev` address Cloudflare gave you, or your own domain once it points at Cloudflare. Every page tells search engines its one true address, so this matters for being found. Commit the change, and Cloudflare builds the blog again within a minute or two.

## Let Khalid check your work

Khalid checks every post before it builds, the way a careful editor would. A post that breaks a basic search rule, such as a missing description, is left out of the blog, and the build log in Cloudflare says what is wrong, why it matters and how to fix it. Warnings, such as a post that is too short, still publish, but they show you what would make the post stronger.

Search engines reward posts that answer a question fully and point to good sources. Google's own [guide to helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) is a good read before your first real post.
