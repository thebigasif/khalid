Many newsletter writers send an issue and then lose it in inboxes. With Khalid, each issue is also a blog post that search engines and AI tools can find, and the list of readers stays on your own site.

## Who this is for

Newsletter writers who publish on a regular day, alone or with a small team, and want their archive to bring in new readers from search.

## The problem it solves

When the newsletter and the website live in different tools, the archive is hard to find, the sign-up form sits on someone else's page, and moving away means exporting and hoping nothing is lost.

## What Khalid gives newsletter writers

- **One place for both.** Each post can go to subscribers by email from the [blog newsletter](/khalid/features/blog-newsletter/).
- **Clean lists.** Readers confirm their address by email, and you can run more than one list.
- **A welcome for each reader.** A welcome email goes out when someone joins.
- **A digest when you are busy.** Send a round-up of recent posts in one email.
- **An archive that ranks.** Every issue is a post with the [SEO and AI search setup](/khalid/features/seo-ai-search/) built in.
- **Your list stays yours.** Your subscribers sit in your own database, and a [backup](/khalid/features/blog-backups/) holds them with everything else.

Khalid does not count email opens and does not take payments. If you need either, keep that part in a tool that does.

## How to set it up

1. Add your mail provider and your postal address to `site.toml`.
2. Write a welcome email in plain words: who you are, what you send and how often.
3. Add a sign-up form to the end of your posts and to your about page.
4. Publish your next issue as a post, then send it with `khalid newsletter send` and its slug.

If you are moving from Substack, Ghost or Medium, the [blog migration tools](/khalid/features/blog-migration/) bring your old issues over and keep their addresses working. The [newsletter guide](https://github.com/thebigasif/khalid/blob/main/docs/newsletters.md) walks through each step.
