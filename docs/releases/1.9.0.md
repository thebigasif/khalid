# Khalid 1.9.0

Khalid 1.9.0 gives every site its own HTTPS certificate with no extra server in front, adds admin screens for the parts of your blog you could only change in files, installs with manual pages and Tab completion, and checks more of what you publish before readers see it.

## Added

- **HTTPS for every site.** `khalid serve --https` serves every site of a sites folder over HTTPS, each with its own free certificate from Let's Encrypt that Khalid gets and renews. A site you add while the server runs gets its certificate at once. You no longer need a proxy such as Caddy in front. The steps are in `docs/deploy.md`.
- **New admin screens.** You can now manage these in the admin instead of editing files:
  - **Pages**, such as About or Contact: add, edit and delete them in every language.
  - **Navigation**: the links in your header and footer menus.
  - **Tags and categories**: rename or remove a tag on every post at once, and add, rename or delete categories.
  - **Snippets**: edit or delete a saved snippet, and every post that uses it changes too.
  - **Forms**: read what visitors sent through your forms, and delete one answer for good when its sender asks.
  - **Subscribers**: search your newsletter readers, label them, forget one who asks, and send a published post to a newsletter.
  - **Design**: your blog's colours, fonts, corners and logo, with a warning when a colour is too faint to read.
  - **SEO**: four new tabs for breadcrumbs, site search, files for AI tools and your local business.
  - **Look**: the words and link of your call-to-action button.
  - **Privacy**: an imprint page, and your own analytics or ads code.
- **Faster work on many posts.** The posts table can add or remove a tag or set the category on many posts at once, and "Quick edit" changes a post's title, description, tags, category or author without opening the editor.
- **Change a live post's address.** The old address now sends readers and search engines to the new one, so no link breaks.
- **Notes for your team.** Leave comments on a draft in the editor, answer them and mark them done. Readers never see them.
- **A date readers can trust.** The editor's "Real update" switch moves a post's "Updated" date only when you say something real changed. A post can also list what changed under `updates:`, shown after the post.
- **More blocks in the editor.** Book, chart, course, dataset, event, location, privacy facts, folding table and key takeaways now each have a short form in the editor's block menu.
- **Media tidy-up.** Delete a picture, unless a post still uses it, file it in a folder the library already has, and find and replace words in the alt text of every picture. Alt text is the description a screen reader reads aloud.
- **Spam in comments.** Mark a comment as spam to hide it, and move it back if you were wrong.
- **Suggested publishing days.** The admin calendar shows the day the planner suggests for each ready draft, and one button schedules them all.
- **Google search numbers in the admin.** The Posts, Search Console and Analytics screens now show clicks, times shown and position from Google Search Console, the posts losing clicks, and pages that get search traffic but no longer work. `khalid serve` also sends the weekly Search Console email on its own.
- **What AI bots read.** The Analytics screen shows how often the bots of ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and others read your pages, and how many visits each assistant sent you.
- **Block AI training, keep AI search.** `ai_training = "block"` under `[seo.crawlers]` turns away the bots that copy posts to train AI models, while search engines and AI answer tools keep reading and linking your blog.
- **Facts you change once.** Write `[[fact:pro-plan-price]]` in a post and keep the value in `facts.toml`. Change it there, and every post shows the new value.
- **Your own words in any language.** A file such as `theme/strings/fr.toml` gives the text around your posts its words in French or any other language.
- **An imprint page.** The Impressum German law asks for, written from your contact details and linked from every footer.
- **Newsletter welcome series.** A newsletter's welcome can be a few emails over several days, such as `welcome-day-3.md`. Newsletters can also count clicks on their links, never tied to a person.
- **Sign in by email.** Staff can ask for a sign-in link by email on the admin's sign-in page.
- **More ways to install.** `khalid completions` sets up Tab completion for your shell, every command has a manual page, such as `man khalid-init`, and the install script sets both up for you. Khalid can also be installed with Homebrew: `brew install thebigasif/khalid/khalid`.
- **Search engines hear about every change.** IndexNow, the way Bing and others learn a page changed, now also hears about posts that went live or changed while Khalid was stopped.
- **Redirects for nginx and Apache.** A static build now writes the redirects of moved posts for those two web servers too.
- **Cards for other sites.** Another site that is given a post's address can show it as a card with its title, author and picture. This uses oEmbed, the common way sites do that.
- **Checks that catch more.** A site can require a named reviewer on posts about health, money or any other category. Case studies and changelog entries are checked too. The site audit now warns about parts of a page wider than a phone, pages that need JavaScript to work, pictures over 100 KB, and a blog with no AI info page. `khalid serve` runs the audit once a day and the dashboard shows your site's health score.
- **A trash that empties itself.** A deleted post stays in the trash for 30 days, then goes for good.

## Changed

- **People, not codes.** The admin shows people's names where it showed a 16-digit code: who is editing, who saved each version, who changed what.
- **Better in more languages.** The focus keyword checks, the page-shape checks and the Search Console follow-ups now understand French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch. The AI info page has a German copy.
- **Every message a webhook sent.** A webhook's history now reaches every message it ever sent, 50 at a time.
- **A smaller program.** Two changes take about 1.8 MB off the `khalid` program, which makes room for everything new in this release.
- **A server log.** `khalid serve` now writes what it does to standard error, including why an email could not be sent. It never writes a password or key.

## Fixed

- **The right author, everywhere.** The editor's SEO panel names a post's author as the published page does, and the admin shows who last changed each post.
- **Posts with no slug line.** A post with no `slug:` line in its front matter now shows in the admin, and can be opened and edited.
- **Structured data you can trust.** The publish check now reads all of a post's structured data, the hidden notes that tell search engines what a page describes. It stops a post whose event or recipe lacks what Google needs, and catches a misspelt name such as `startdate` for `startDate`. A post's category now reaches its structured data and share tags.
- **Gated content.** Unsubscribing locks gated content again in that browser, its headings show in the table of contents, and its pictures no longer make the page jump while they load.
- **Small fixes.** Each language gets its own "page not found" page on `khalid serve`. Forms work on a static build under a folder such as `/blog/`. A long formula scrolls in its own box on a phone. A push to your git host always shows on a running server. `khalid theme sync` works with the file the Design screen writes. The slow-site alert works when the watch looks only every 30 or 60 minutes. The Content API returns your menus, snippets and wikilinks.

## Security

- **Site code needs the highest key.** Changing the code that runs on every page, `[code]` in `site.toml`, through the Admin API now needs a key with `admin:all`. Owners and admins are not affected.

## Upgrading from 1.8.0

Install 1.9.0 over it the same way you installed 1.8.0, and restart `khalid serve`. Your site folder needs no change. Take a `khalid backup` first if you want a copy to go back to.

- **Script:** `curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thebigasif/khalid/main/scripts/install.sh | sh -s -- --version v1.9.0`
- **Docker:** use `ghcr.io/thebigasif/khalid:1.9.0`.
- **From source:** `git clone --branch v1.9.0 https://github.com/thebigasif/khalid.git`, then the steps in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

`khalid --version` then prints `khalid 1.9.0`. Four things to know:

- **The trash now empties itself.** `khalid serve` deletes a post for good 30 days after it went to the trash. To keep deleted posts until you remove them yourself, add `trash_days = 0` to `site.toml` before you restart.
- **Run `khalid lint` once.** The publish check now reads all structured data and refuses misspelt schema names, so `khalid publish` and the publish button may refuse a post they let through before. `khalid lint` names each one and what to fix.
- **A key that changes site code.** A program that changes `[code]` with a `settings:write` key now gets a 403. Give it a key with `admin:all`.
- **The weekly Search Console email.** If a timer sends it today, you can remove the timer while `khalid serve` runs. The email still never goes out twice in one week.

## Still checked after launch

Three of the four checks promised after 1.0.0 are still open. [The after-launch page](after-launch.md) says where each stands, what our tests prove meanwhile and what to do if it matters to you.

- **Ahrefs and Semrush on the demo site.** Still open. On every change, Khalid's own audit must give the demo site and the showcase site 100.
- **HTTPS with a live Let's Encrypt certificate.** Still open. On every change to the HTTPS code, our tests get and renew a certificate from Let's Encrypt's test server.
- **The Astro and Next.js starter kits, deployed.** Still open. Our tests build both kits against the showcase blog whenever the kits, the API or the page renderer change.
- **SerpDad's own blog on Khalid.** Done, 2026-11-10. serpdad.com/blog runs the published Khalid 1.8.0 Docker image.

## Checking a download

Each release has a `SHA256SUMS` file. The install script checks against it for you. By hand, run `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing` in the folder that holds the download.
