# Khalid 1.1.0

Khalid 1.1.0 is about how your blog looks. It brings ten new templates, a screen in the admin to pick one, new blocks for posts, a fix for blogs that live under a folder of another site, Khalid's own website with live demos, and a Deploy to Cloudflare button. Every change is listed in `CHANGELOG.md` under 1.1.0.

Nothing changes for an existing blog when you upgrade. A site without a template setting keeps the look it has today, now called classic, until you pick another.

## See it live

Everything below runs on GitHub Pages, built by the same Khalid you download.

- **Khalid's website:** https://thebigasif.github.io/khalid/
- **The demo blog:** https://thebigasif.github.io/khalid/demo/, a made-up content studio's blog with sixteen posts in English and German and nearly every block Khalid has.
- **The same demo in each template:** https://thebigasif.github.io/khalid/templates/<name>/, where `<name>` is one of the templates below, or `classic`.
- **Six example sites:** https://thebigasif.github.io/khalid/examples/<slug>/, listed under "Example sites to copy" below.

## Ten new templates

A template is the look of your whole blog: the front page, the lists and each post. Pick one in the admin's new Look screen, or by hand in `site.toml`:

```toml
[template]
name = "ledger"
```

Each template has its own guide in `docs/templates/`, and its own demo.

Templates can now also show:

- **Each post's category**, on the post, on its card and as a row of topic links on the front page.
- **Breadcrumbs**, the trail of links from the home page to the post, and the post's category as a step in them for search engines too.
- **A call to action.** Add `[template.cta]` to `site.toml` with a label and a link, and templates show it as a band after posts, as a button in the header, or wherever a post places `::cta`.
- **Share links** under a post's title or after it. They are plain links to each network's own share page, so nothing is loaded from the networks and readers are not tracked.
- **A newsletter sign-up form** after posts or on the front page, on a blog that takes sign-ups.
- **Footer columns.** `[[nav.footer_groups]]` in `site.toml` gives the footer columns of links, each under its own heading.
- **The main site's icon** in the browser tab, when theme sync found one.

As before, every page works with scripts off and from small phones up, in one light theme that follows your main site's colours.

## The Look screen

The admin has a new Look screen. It shows every template, says what each one turns on and off, and lets you set that template's options, such as Ledger's tagline. It saves to `site.toml`, and the blog shows the new look after Khalid restarts. Programs can do the same through the Admin API: `GET /api/admin/v1/templates/` lists the templates and their options.

## New blocks for posts

A block is a part of a post with its own shape, such as a tip box or a FAQ. Each one is written in markdown and edited in the admin.

- **Columns**: two to four cards side by side, stacked on a phone. Good for comparing two options.
- **Pull quote**: a short line set large, with who said it.
- **Pros and cons**: a plus list beside a minus list.
- **Folding tables**: a long table shows its first rows and a "Show more rows" line that opens the rest, with scripts off too.
- **Call to action**: `::cta` places your call-to-action band anywhere in a post.
- **Three new callout kinds**: key takeaways, further reading, and one number worth remembering.

Every block also has its own look now, in every template.

## Blogs under a folder of another site

When a blog lives under a folder of a bigger site, such as `https://example.com/blog/`, the admin and both APIs now live under that folder too: `/blog/admin/` and `/blog/api/`. Before, they answered at `/admin/` and `/api/`, which the web server rule from `khalid init` did not send to Khalid. Sign-in links, preview links, webhooks and the newsletter form use the folder as well, and an old `/admin/` address moves to the new one. A blog at the root of its domain is unchanged.

## Deploy to Cloudflare

The README and `docs/deploy/cloudflare.md` have a Deploy to Cloudflare button. One click copies a small, ready Khalid blog into your GitHub account and publishes it on Cloudflare for free. Each push to that copy rebuilds the blog. The guide also covers Cloudflare Pages, the blog's address and your own domain.

The starter installs Khalid 1.1.0 and stays on it until you change one line in its `build.sh`, so a new release never changes your blog by surprise.

## For developers

- The library crates `khalid-core` and `khalid-lint` move to 0.2.0. `khalid-core` gained the template choice, the reader settings and the footer groups. The two crates always share one version.
- Khalid reads settings files with toml 1.1 and XML with roxmltree 0.21. The newer XML reader no longer crashes on very deeply nested files.
- The TypeScript SDK and the OpenAPI document are at 1.1.0, with the templates route and the `line` option kind.

## Upgrading from 1.0.0

Install 1.1.0 over it the same way you installed 1.0.0, and restart `khalid serve`. Your site folder needs no change. Take a `khalid backup` first if you want a copy to go back to.

- **Script:** `curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thebigasif/khalid/main/scripts/install.sh | sh -s -- --version v1.1.0`
- **Docker:** use `ghcr.io/thebigasif/khalid:1.1.0`.
- **From source:** `git clone --branch v1.1.0 https://github.com/thebigasif/khalid.git`, then the steps in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

`khalid --version` then prints `khalid 1.1.0`.

If your blog lives under a folder such as `/blog/`, the admin is now at `/blog/admin/`. Bookmarks to the old `/admin/` address move there on their own.

## Checking a download

Each release has a `SHA256SUMS` file. The install script checks against it for you. By hand, run `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing` in the folder that holds the download.
