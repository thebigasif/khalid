Battery charging works the same on every Brindle. You can plug in with the battery on the bike, or take it off and plug in indoors. Either way, use the charger that came in the box. Another charger may look the same but give the wrong voltage, and that can damage the cells.

## Charging with the battery on the bike

1. Turn the bike off.
2. Open the rubber cover on the charging port, on the left side of the battery.
3. Plug the charger into the wall first, then into the port.

This is the easy choice when you keep the bike in a hallway or a warm garage near a socket.

## Taking the battery indoors

Turn the key in the lock under the saddle and lift the battery out by its handle. Stand it on a hard, dry floor, away from anything that burns. Then plug in the charger as above. This is the better choice when the bike lives in a cold shed.

### Where battery charging works best

Charge indoors, at room temperature. If the battery is cold, let it warm up for an hour first. Cells that take a charge below 5 °C age faster.

## What the lights mean

| Charger light | What it means |
|---|---|
| Red | Charging |
| Green | Full, or nothing plugged in |
| Red and green flashing | A fault. Unplug both ends and try again |

A charge from empty takes four to five hours. Most of it goes in the first two hours. The last part is slower on purpose, to protect the cells.

## How often to plug in

Plug in whenever it suits you. Lithium cells do not need to run empty first, and short top ups do no harm. Good battery charging habits are simple: avoid heat, avoid frost, and do not leave the bike empty for weeks.
