Every Brindle has hydraulic disc brakes. They stop well in the wet and need little care. The brake pads wear down slowly, and how fast depends on where you ride: hills and rain wear them most. Check them once a month.

## Feel the levers first

Squeeze each brake lever hard. It should stop firmly, at least a finger's width before it touches the handlebar. If a lever feels soft or spongy, or goes all the way to the bar, do not ride. Book a workshop visit.

## Look at the brake pads

1. Shine a torch into the brake caliper, the part that holds the pads over the disc.
2. Look from above. You will see the two pads, one each side of the disc.
3. Check how thick the pad material is on its metal back.

New brake pads are about 3 mm thick. Change them when less than 1 mm is left.

### Listen as you ride

A grinding or squealing sound when you brake often means the pads are worn to the metal. Stop riding and have them changed before you ride again.

## Keep the discs clean

Never oil anything near the discs. Oil on a disc or a pad stops the brake working. If oil does get on a disc, clean it with alcohol on a clean cloth. Pads that have soaked up oil must be replaced.

## How long brake pads last

On flat city roads a set of pads often lasts a year or more. On steep hills, or with a child seat and shopping on the back, they may last a few months. An electric bike is heavier and faster than a normal bike, so its pads work harder. That is why the monthly look matters, even when the brakes still feel fine.

## That completes the monthly checks

You have checked the tyres, the chain and the brakes. Start again next month with [tyre pressure](/blog/tyre-pressure/). If you got oil near the brakes during [chain cleaning](/blog/chain-cleaning/), check the discs again. The [Wikipedia page on disc brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_brake) explains how they work.
