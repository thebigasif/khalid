Every Brindle comes with warranty cover against faults in materials and making. It starts on the day your bike is delivered. You do not need to register it: your order is your proof of purchase.

## What the warranty cover includes

| Part | How long |
|---|---|
| Frame and fork | 10 years |
| Motor, battery and display | 2 years |
| Lights, wiring and other parts | 2 years |

The battery is included if it loses much of its distance within two years, as long as it was charged and stored as this help centre says. See [winter battery storage](/blog/winter-battery-storage/) for the storing part.

## What it does not include

- Parts that wear with use, such as tyres, the chain and grips, and the [brake pads](/blog/brake-pads/).
- Damage from a crash, a fall or misuse.
- Damage from water jets, such as a pressure washer.
- Changes made to the bike, such as a tuned motor.

## How to make a claim

Contact us with your order number, a short description of the fault and a photo or a video if you can. We reply within two working days. Most faults are fixed at a partner workshop near you, at no cost. If a part cannot be fixed, we replace it.

### Keep the bike safe to ride

If a fault makes the bike unsafe, such as a cracked frame or a soft brake, stop riding it and contact us before you do anything else.

## Look after the bike to keep the warranty

The warranty asks for normal care, not a perfect record. Keep the tyres pumped, the chain clean and the brakes checked, as the monthly checks describe, and have the bike serviced once a year at any good workshop. Keep the receipt from the service. It helps if a claim ever needs it.

## Faults we see most

Most claims are for a light that stops working, which [the bike lights guide](/blog/bike-lights/) can often fix in a minute, and for a display that will not turn on after the bike was washed.

To send back a bike you no longer want, see [returns within 30 days](/blog/bike-returns/). For your rights beyond our warranty cover, the [Wikipedia page on warranties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warranty) is a good overview.
