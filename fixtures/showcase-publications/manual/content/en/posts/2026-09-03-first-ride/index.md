Your bike is built and fits you. Before your first ride, fill the battery all the way. A full first charge lets the battery measure itself, so the distance it shows on the display is right from day one.

## Fill the battery once

Plug the charger into the wall, then into the port on the battery. The light on the charger turns red while it works and green when the battery is full. The first fill takes about four hours. You can do it on the bike or off it, and [the charging guide](/blog/battery-charging/) has the details.

## Turn the bike on

Press the power button on the display for two seconds. The display shows the charge left, your speed and the level of help from the motor. It starts at level 1, the lowest.

### Learn the buttons

- **Plus and minus** change the level of help.
- **Hold minus** for three seconds to switch the lights. [Bike lights](/blog/bike-lights/) explains the automatic mode.
- **Press power** once to switch between trip distance and total distance.

## Your first ride, step by step

Pick a quiet street or a park path for your first ride. Start in level 1 and pedal gently. The motor helps as soon as the pedals turn, so the bike moves off faster than you may expect. Keep a finger on each brake lever for the first few minutes.

Try each level in turn on a flat stretch, then on a gentle slope. You will feel the difference at once. [Choosing between the assist levels](/blog/assist-levels/) explains when each one helps most.

## Ride safely from the start

Wear a helmet, and ride a little slower than feels possible until you know the brakes. An electric bike reaches 25 km/h with little effort, faster than people walking or driving expect a bike to be.

## After the ride

Turn the bike off with the power button. If you used more than half the battery, top it up the same day. That is the last step of setting up. To keep your bike in good shape, start the monthly checks with [tyre pressure](/blog/tyre-pressure/). If the riding position felt wrong on your first ride, go back to [saddle height](/blog/saddle-height/). The [Wikipedia page on electric bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle) explains how pedal assist works.
