Both bike lights on your Brindle run from the main battery, so they never need a charge of their own. They use very little power: an hour of light costs less than a kilometre of riding.

## Switch them on and off

Hold the minus button on the display for three seconds. A small lamp sign shows on the display while the lights are on. Hold the button again to switch them off. The lights also go off when you turn the bike off.

## Let them come on by themselves

The display has a light sensor. To use it, turn the bike on, hold plus and minus together for three seconds, and choose Auto. The bike lights then come on at dusk and in tunnels, and go off again in daylight.

### Riding in daylight

Many riders keep the lights on all day. Drivers see a bike with lights much sooner, even in sunshine. With Auto set, the front light glows dimly in daylight and brightens in the dark.

## The brake light

The rear light glows brighter when you brake. It works whenever the lights are on, and you do not need to set anything. Riders behind you see you slow down, which matters most in traffic.

## Keep your bike lights clean

Road dirt on a lens can halve the light that reaches the road. Wipe both lenses with a damp cloth when you clean the bike, and check that the front light points a little down, at the road about ten metres ahead. A light that points too high dazzles drivers and people walking towards you, and lights up less of the road for you.

## If a light does not work

1. Turn the bike off and on again.
2. Check that the light's cable is pushed fully into its plug.
3. Check that the light is clipped fully onto its bracket.
