Tyres lose a little air every week, even without a puncture. Check the tyre pressure once a month. Soft tyres wear faster, puncture more easily and take a tenth or more off the distance you get from each charge.

## Find the right tyre pressure

The right tyre pressure is printed on the side of each tyre, as a range in bar and PSI. On every Brindle it is 3.5 to 4.5 bar. Use the lower end for comfort on rough roads, and the higher end for a longer range and a heavy load.

## Pump the tyres

1. Take the cap off the valve.
2. Push the pump's head firmly onto the valve and close its lever.
3. Pump until the gauge shows the number you want.
4. Open the lever, pull the head off straight, and put the cap back.

### Which valve your bike has

Every Brindle has car type valves, called Schrader valves. Any pump at a petrol station fits them too. The [Wikipedia page on Schrader valves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrader_valve) shows what they look like.

## Look at the tread while you are there

Spin each wheel slowly and look at the tyre. Pick out any glass or flint stuck in the tread. If you can see the canvas under the rubber, or a cut longer than a few millimetres, the tyre needs changing. A worn tyre is not covered by the warranty, but a new one is cheap and quick to fit.

## Why it matters on an electric bike

An electric bike is heavier than a normal bike, so soft tyres squash more and drag more. Keeping the tyre pressure right is the easiest way to get more from each charge: see [battery range by model](/blog/battery-range/).

## How often to check

Once a month is enough for most riders. Check more often in winter, since cold air makes the tyres softer, and before a long ride or a ride with a heavy load.
