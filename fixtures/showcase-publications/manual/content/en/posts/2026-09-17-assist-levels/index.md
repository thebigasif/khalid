The assist levels set how much the motor helps you pedal. Level 1 adds a little help and level 3 adds the most. You change between the assist levels with the plus and minus buttons on the display, at any time, even while you ride.

## The three assist levels

| Level | Help from the motor | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | About half your effort again | Flat roads, the longest distance |
| 2 | About the same as your effort | Everyday riding |
| 3 | About twice your effort | Hills, headwinds, a heavy load |

The motor stops helping at 25 km/h, the legal limit in most of Europe. Above that speed you pedal on your own, as on any bike.

## When to change level

Change up before a hill, not halfway up it. The motor responds best when you are still pedalling at a steady pace. On the way down, drop to level 1 or turn the help off to save the battery. In heavy traffic, level 2 gives a quick, smooth start at lights without a jump.

### Riding with the motor off

You can ride with the help switched off and the bike still on. The lights and the display keep working. The bike is heavier than a normal bike, so pick a low gear: see [how to use the hub gears](/blog/hub-gears/).

## Assist levels and your battery

Each step up uses more power. Riding in level 3 all the time can halve the distance you get from a charge. [Range figures for each model](/blog/battery-range/) shows what to expect.

## Walk assist

When you push the bike up a steep ramp or out of a cellar, hold the minus button and walk beside it. The motor then moves the bike at walking pace, up to 6 km/h, so you only need to steer.

## A simple rule

Use the lowest of the assist levels that lets you pedal at an easy, steady pace. Most riders spend most of their time in level 2.
