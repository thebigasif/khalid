The chain carries all the power from your legs and the motor to the rear wheel. A dirty chain wears out fast and wears the gear wheels with it. Do your chain cleaning once a month, and after any ride in heavy rain.

## Wipe the chain

1. Lean the bike against a wall, with the chain side facing you.
2. Hold a dry rag round the lower run of the chain.
3. Pedal backwards slowly by hand, so the chain runs through the rag, for about twenty turns.
4. If the chain is still black, scrub it with an old toothbrush and a little warm, soapy water, then dry it with the rag.

### Chain cleaning without a mess

Put an old newspaper under the bike before you start. Wear thin gloves if you like: chain dirt is hard to wash off.

## Oil the chain

Use oil made for bike chains. Kitchen oil and spray grease attract dirt. Pedal backwards and put one drop of oil on each link as it passes. Keep the wheel turning for another minute so the oil soaks into the links.

### Wipe off the extra oil

Oil works inside the chain, not on its outside. Wipe the outside with a clean rag. A chain that looks wet picks up grit on the next ride, and grit wears it.

## Check the chain for wear

A chain stretches as it wears. A chain checker tool tells you when it needs changing, usually after 3,000 to 5,000 km. Our workshop checks it at every service. On an electric bike chains wear faster than on a normal bike, because the motor adds force.

## When to clean more often

After a ride on a wet or salty road in winter, give the chain a quick wipe and a few drops of oil the same day. Salt and grit left overnight start rust, and a rusty chain is noisy and wears the gear wheels.
