Battery range is the distance you can ride on one charge. On a Brindle it is 40 to 90 km for most riders. How you ride matters more than the size of the battery, so this article explains what uses the most power and how to make each charge go further.

## What changes your distance

- **The level of help.** Level 3 uses about twice the power of level 1.
- **Hills.** A long climb can use a fifth of the battery.
- **Weight.** A child seat or full panniers take a tenth or more off.
- **Cold.** Below 5 °C the cells give less, and the distance comes back when they warm up.
- **Soft tyres.** They drag. [Pump your tyres](/blog/tyre-pressure/) once a month.

## Battery range by model

| Model | Battery | Typical distance |
|---|---|---|
| City | 540 Wh | 60 to 90 km |
| Folding | 360 Wh | 40 to 60 km |
| Cargo | 625 Wh | 45 to 70 km |

These figures are for a rider of 75 kg on mixed roads in level 2.

## Make each charge go further

Start in a low gear and change up as you gain speed. The motor works best when you pedal at a steady, brisk pace, and [using the hub gears well](/blog/hub-gears/) helps a lot. Ride in the lowest level of help that feels comfortable, and save level 3 for hills and headwinds. [Picking the right assist level](/blog/assist-levels/) has more on this.

### Plan longer rides

For a long day out, check the route for hills and carry the charger. A café stop of an hour adds about 20 km.

## What the display shows

The display gives a distance left as well as the bars. It works this out from your last few kilometres, so it drops fast on a climb and rises again on the flat. Trust the bars on a hilly ride.

## When the distance drops

Every battery holds a little less each year. After about 800 full charges it still holds most of what it held when new. If your battery range drops suddenly, that is not normal wear.
