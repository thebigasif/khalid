The City and Cargo models have eight hub gears, sealed inside the rear hub, away from dirt and rain. The motor helps you, but the gears still matter. In the right gear you pedal at an easy pace, the motor works well, and the battery lasts longer.

## How to change gear

Twist the grip on the right side of the handlebar. Twist towards you for an easier gear and away from you for a harder one. The number on the grip shows the gear you are in.

### Ease off as you twist

Hub gears change best when the pedals are under light pressure. Ease off for half a turn of the pedals as you twist, then pedal on. You can also change gear while you stand still, which is useful at traffic lights: drop to gear 2 or 3 before you stop.

## Which gear to use

- **Starting off:** gear 2 or 3.
- **On the flat:** gear 5 to 7.
- **Up a hill:** change down early, to gear 1 to 3.
- **Fast and downhill:** gear 8.

Aim for a pace of about one turn of the pedals a second. If your legs spin fast, change up. If you push hard and slowly, change down.

## Gears and the battery

Pushing a hard gear slowly makes the motor work harder, and it uses more power. A lighter gear and a steady pace give you more distance from each charge. For the full picture, read [which assist level to choose](/blog/assist-levels/) and [what affects battery range](/blog/battery-range/).

## Gears on the folding model

The Folding model has a single gear and no shifter, to keep it light and simple to fold. On that model the levels of help from the motor do the work that gears do on the others.

## Looking after hub gears

Hub gears need very little care. They do not need oil from you, only a check at the yearly service. The chain still does, so keep it clean with [ten minutes of chain cleaning](/blog/chain-cleaning/) each month. If a gear slips or jumps, the cable may have stretched: ask us or a local workshop to adjust it. The [Wikipedia page on hub gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hub_gear) explains how they work inside.
