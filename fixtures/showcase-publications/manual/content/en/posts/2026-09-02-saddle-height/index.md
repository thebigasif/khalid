The right saddle height makes riding easier on your knees and your back. Most new riders set the saddle too low, because a low saddle feels safe when you stop. This guide shows a quick way to find your height, then how to set the handlebar to match.

## Find your saddle height

Sit on the saddle in your usual shoes, with one hand on a wall. Put your heel on the pedal at the bottom of its turn. Your leg should be straight. When you then pedal with the ball of your foot, your knee bends a little at the bottom of each turn, which is what you want.

If your leg is bent with your heel on the pedal, the saddle is too low. If your hips rock from side to side as you ride, it is too high.

## Move the seat post

1. Open the quick release lever on the seat clamp.
2. Move the seat post up or down. Keep the line marked MIN hidden inside the frame.
3. Point the saddle straight ahead, in line with the frame.
4. Close the lever firmly. It should leave a mark on your palm.

### Level the saddle

Most riders like the saddle flat. Use the 5 mm hex key on the bolt under the saddle to tip the nose up or down, then tighten it again. A nose tipped down makes you slide forward onto your hands.

## Raise the handlebar

The stem on every Brindle can be raised. Undo the top bolt and the two side bolts, lift the stem by up to 3 cm, and tighten the bolts again in the same order. A higher bar gives a more upright position, which most city riders prefer. A lower bar suits longer, faster rides.

## Try it on a short ride

Ride slowly round the block, then make small changes of half a centimetre at a time. When the saddle height feels right, mark the seat post with a strip of tape, so you can find the height again after a service or after lending the bike.
