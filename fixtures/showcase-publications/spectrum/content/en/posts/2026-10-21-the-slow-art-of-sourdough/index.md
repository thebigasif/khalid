Sourdough is the oldest way to make bread rise. It needs only flour, water, salt and time. Most of the work is waiting, which is why so many people took it up and so few give it up.

## What a starter is

A starter is flour and water left to sour. Wild yeasts and bacteria from the flour and the air begin to feed on it. The yeasts make gas, which raises the bread. The bacteria make lactic and acetic acids, which give the sour taste and help the bread keep.

To make one, mix 50 g of wholemeal flour with 50 g of water in a jar. Each day, throw half away and feed it the same again. After about a week it will double in size within a few hours of a feed. Then it is ready.

## Why sourdough rises slowly

Wild yeasts work slowly compared with the yeast you buy. A loaf may take a whole day from mixing to baking. That slow rise is the point. It gives the bacteria time to make flavour, and it lets enzymes break down some of the starch and gluten. Many people find slow-risen bread easier to digest.

## A first loaf

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Strong white flour | 450 g |
| Wholemeal flour | 50 g |
| Water | 350 g |
| Active starter | 100 g |
| Salt | 10 g |

1. Mix the flours and water. Leave for an hour.
2. Add the starter and salt, and squeeze them in.
3. Over the next three hours, stretch and fold the dough four times.
4. Leave it to rise until it is about half as big again, then shape it into a ball.
5. Put it in a floured bowl in the fridge overnight.
6. Bake in a lidded pot at 240 °C for 20 minutes, then without the lid for 20 more.

## When it goes wrong

A flat loaf usually means the starter was not lively enough, or the dough rose too long. A pale, gummy loaf needs a hotter oven. Write down what you did each time. After a few loaves, the bread will tell you what it needs.
