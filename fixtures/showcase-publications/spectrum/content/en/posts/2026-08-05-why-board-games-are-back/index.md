Board games were meant to lose to screens. Instead they have had their best twenty years. New games come out by the thousand each year, cafés lend them by the hour, and many libraries now keep a shelf of them.

## A new kind of game

The old family games were often slow and cruel: you rolled dice, moved, and someone went bankrupt. From the 1990s, designers began to make games that were shorter, fairer and full of choices. Players build, trade and plan, and nobody is knocked out early. Many games now ask players to work together against the game itself.

## Why board games are back

Part of the answer is the screen. After a day of looking at phones, sitting round a table with friends feels different. A board game gives people a reason to meet, something to do with their hands, and a way to talk without having to fill every silence.

Crowdfunding helped too. Small designers can now raise the money to print a game before they make it, so far more games reach the shops.

## Old games still matter

[Chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) is older than any of them, and more people play it now than ever before, many of them online first and then at a real board. Its rules fit on one page, and a lifetime is not long enough to master it.

## Where to start

1. **Pick a short game.** Something that takes thirty minutes is easier to learn and easier to play again.
2. **Play at a café or library first.** Staff will teach you, and you can try before you buy.
3. **Choose for the group.** A game for four adults is not a game for two children.
4. **Read the rules aloud together.** It is quicker than one person learning them alone.

Like the [return of the neighbourhood library](/blog/the-neighbourhood-library/), the board game boom is about people wanting a reason to be in the same room.
