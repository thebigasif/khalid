Winter citrus is the bright spot of the cold months. Oranges, lemons and mandarins ripen from late autumn to early spring, just when the rest of the fruit bowl looks tired.

## Why citrus peaks in winter

Citrus trees grow in warm places, but the fruit needs cool nights to colour and sweeten. As nights cool in autumn, the green pigment in the peel breaks down and the orange shows through. The fruit also turns some of its acid into sugar. So the best fruit reaches shops from December to March.

## Winter citrus worth buying

| Fruit | Best months | Taste | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clementine | November to January | Sweet, few pips | Eating, lunch boxes |
| Blood orange | January to March | Sweet with a berry note | Salads, juice |
| Seville orange | January to February | Very bitter | Marmalade |
| Meyer lemon | December to March | Mild, floral | Baking, dressings |
| Bergamot | November to January | Fragrant, sour | Zest, curd |
| Pomelo | November to February | Mild, sweet | Salads |

## Use every part

The peel holds most of the scent, in tiny oil glands near the surface. Zest it before you juice the fruit: it is much harder after. Freeze spare zest in a small box and use it straight from the freezer.

The pith is bitter, so keep it out of salads. But in marmalade, the pith and pips hold pectin, which makes the jam set. Tie them in a square of muslin and boil them with the fruit.

## Three easy ideas

- **Blood orange salad.** Slice the fruit into rounds, add thin red onion, olive oil and a pinch of salt.
- **Salted lemons.** Pack quartered lemons in salt in a jar for a month. Chop the soft peel into stews and grain salads.
- **Clementine syrup.** Simmer the juice with half its weight in sugar for five minutes, and pour it over a warm cake.

[Citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) stores well somewhere cool, but not in a sealed bag. Keep it loose, and eat the softest first.
