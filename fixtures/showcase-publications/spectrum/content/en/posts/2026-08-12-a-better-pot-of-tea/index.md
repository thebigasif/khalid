A good pot of tea costs almost nothing and takes five minutes. Most bad tea comes from three mistakes: stale water, the wrong heat, and leaves left in too long or too short.

## Start with fresh water

Fill the kettle with fresh cold water every time. Water that has boiled twice has lost some of its air, and tea made with it tastes flat. If your tap water is hard, a jug filter helps, since minerals dull the flavour and leave a film on the cup.

## Warm the pot

Swirl a little hot water round the teapot and pour it away. A cold pot cools the water before the leaves have done their work. China and glass are fine. A metal pot can make the tea taste of metal.

## The right heat for each pot of tea

| Tea | Water | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Black tea | Just off the boil | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Green tea | About 80 °C | 2 to 3 minutes |
| White tea | About 80 °C | 3 to 4 minutes |
| Oolong | About 90 °C | 3 to 5 minutes |
| Herbal | Boiling | 5 to 7 minutes |

Green and white teas turn bitter in boiling water. Let the kettle stand for two minutes after it boils, or add a splash of cold water to the pot first.

## How much tea to use

Use one teaspoon of loose leaves, or one bag, for each cup, and one more if you like it strong. Loose leaves need room to open, so use a pot with a large basket or none at all, and pour through a strainer.

## Pour it all

Pour every cup once the time is up. Tea left on the leaves keeps brewing and turns bitter. If you want a second pot, make a fresh one. Milk goes in after for black tea, and never in green.
