A hand-thrown bowl looks simple. On the wheel it takes a skilled potter five minutes. Yet that bowl will not reach a table for two or three weeks, and every step can ruin it.

## Wedging and centring

The potter starts by kneading the clay, which is called wedging. It pushes out air bubbles that would burst in the kiln. Then the clay goes on the wheel and the potter centres it, pressing it with wet hands until it spins without a wobble. Beginners spend weeks learning just this.

## Throwing the bowl

With the clay centred, the potter opens it with the thumbs, then pulls the walls up between the fingers inside and out. A bowl is shaped by pushing the walls outward from inside as they rise. The potter cuts it from the wheel with a wire and sets it aside.

## Why a hand-thrown bowl takes weeks

Fresh clay is soft. The bowl dries slowly until it is leather hard, firm enough to hold but still damp. Then the potter turns it upside down and trims a foot ring into its base, which makes it lighter and lifts it off the table.

Next it must dry completely, which can take a week. A bowl that dries unevenly will crack. Then comes the first firing, called the bisque, at about 1,000 °C. It turns clay into a hard, chalky pot.

## Glaze and the second firing

The potter dips the bowl in glaze, which is mostly ground glass. In the second firing, at up to 1,300 °C for stoneware, the glaze melts into a smooth, waterproof skin. The kiln then cools for a day before it can be opened.

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Wedging and throwing | 15 minutes |
| Drying to leather hard | 1 to 2 days |
| Trimming the foot | 10 minutes |
| Drying fully | About a week |
| Bisque firing and cooling | 2 days |
| Glazing and glaze firing | 2 to 3 days |

## How to spot good work

Pick the bowl up. It should feel lighter than it looks, with walls of even thickness. Run a finger round the foot: it should be smooth, so it does not scratch the table. Look inside: good [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) has no pinholes or bare patches in the glaze.
