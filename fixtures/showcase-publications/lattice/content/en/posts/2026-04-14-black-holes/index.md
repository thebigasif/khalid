What is a black hole? It is a place where so much matter is packed into so small a space that its [[glossary:gravity|gravity]] lets nothing escape, not even light. Despite the name, it is not a hole. It is the densest kind of object we know.

## Escape speed

To leave Earth, a rocket must reach about 11 kilometres a second. A heavier or smaller planet would need more. Squeeze enough mass into a small enough ball and the speed needed to escape grows past the speed of light. Nothing can go that fast, so nothing gets out.

The edge where this happens is the [[glossary:event-horizon|event horizon]]. Outside it, light and matter can still get away. Inside it, every path leads inward.[^1]

## How black holes form

Most form when a very large star runs out of fuel. Its core can no longer hold itself up, and it collapses in a fraction of a second. The outer layers blow off as a supernova, and the core becomes a black hole a few times heavier than the Sun.

The biggest sit at the centres of galaxies and weigh millions or billions of Suns. The one at the centre of our Milky Way, called Sagittarius A star, weighs about four million Suns.[^2]

Gas falling inward heats up and glows before it crosses the event horizon. Telescopes see this glow, not the dark centre itself.
:::

## How we see something black

A black hole gives off no light, but the matter around it does. Gas that spirals in rubs against itself and heats to millions of degrees, so it glows in X-rays. Stars close by race around it, and their orbits reveal its mass.

In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope, a network of radio dishes across the world, showed the first picture of such a shadow, in the galaxy M87.[^3]

## The short answer

A black hole is mass packed so tightly that its escape speed is faster than light. Small ones come from dead stars and giant ones sit at the centres of galaxies.
