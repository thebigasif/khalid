Every leaf is a small factory that runs on light. This is how photosynthesis works: a plant takes in water through its roots and carbon dioxide from the air, catches the energy of sunlight, and builds sugar. The oxygen it lets go is the oxygen we breathe.

## Chlorophyll catches the light

Leaves are green because of [[glossary:chlorophyll|chlorophyll]], a pigment packed into tiny parts of each cell called chloroplasts. Chlorophyll takes in red and blue light strongly and reflects most of the green. That reflected green is the colour you see.

:::figure{src=chloroplasts.jpg alt="Moss cells under a microscope, packed with the green chloroplasts where photosynthesis happens"}
Moss cells under a microscope. Each round green body is a chloroplast, where the chlorophyll sits and photosynthesis happens.
:::

## Photosynthesis in two stages: catch the energy, then build sugar

Photosynthesis runs in two stages.[^1]

1. **The light stage.** Light energy splits water molecules. This frees oxygen, which leaves the leaf, and stores the energy in two small carrier molecules.
2. **The building stage.** The cell uses that stored energy to fix carbon dioxide from the air into sugar. This cycle does not need light directly, but it stops soon after dark because it runs on what the light stage made.

In one line: six molecules of carbon dioxide plus six of water, with light, make one molecule of glucose and six of oxygen.

## Where the sugar goes

The plant burns some sugar for energy, as we do. It turns the rest into starch to store, and into cellulose to build its stems and leaves. Almost all the food on Earth, and the oxygen in the air, comes from this one process.[^2]

## Plants are not very efficient

A crop field stores only about 1 to 2 percent of the sunlight ([photosynthetic efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthetic_efficiency)) that falls on it as sugar. Much of the light is the wrong colour, some reflects away, and the plant uses energy to run itself. Scientists are testing ways to raise this, such as plants that recover faster after bright sun.

## The short answer

Chlorophyll catches red and blue light, the energy splits water and frees oxygen, and the cell uses that energy to turn carbon dioxide into sugar.
