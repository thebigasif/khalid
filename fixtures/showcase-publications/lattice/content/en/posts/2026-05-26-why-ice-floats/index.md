Drop a lump of solid wax into melted wax and it sinks. Drop an ice cube into water and it floats. Water is one of very few things that is lighter as a solid than as a liquid. This explains why ice floats, and why fish survive the winter under frozen lakes.

## Ice floats because of density

An object floats when it is less dense than the liquid around it. [[glossary:density|Density]] is how much mass fits in a given space. Ice has a density of about 0.92 grams per cubic centimetre. Liquid water is about 1.00. So ice floats, with about nine tenths of it under the surface.

## Hydrogen bonds build an open lattice

A water molecule is one oxygen atom with two hydrogen atoms. The oxygen end is slightly negative and the hydrogen ends slightly positive, so neighbouring molecules attract each other. Each of these links is a [[glossary:hydrogen-bond|hydrogen bond]].

In liquid water, the bonds break and form all the time, and molecules crowd close together. As water freezes, every molecule locks into four bonds at fixed angles. The result is an open, six-sided lattice with more empty space than the liquid had.[^1] That is why snowflakes have six arms.

:::figure{src=icebergs.jpg alt="A long, low iceberg on a dark sea, showing how ice floats with most of it under water"}
An iceberg in the Southern Ocean. What shows above the water is only about a tenth of it.
:::

## Water is densest at 4 °C

As water cools, it shrinks like other liquids until about 4 °C. Below that, hydrogen bonds start to build the open shape, and the water grows again. So in winter the densest water, at 4 °C, sinks to the bottom of a lake, and colder water floats above it. The lake freezes from the top down, and the ice above keeps the water below from freezing solid.[^2]

## The short answer

Ice floats because hydrogen bonds hold frozen water in an open lattice that is about a tenth less dense than the liquid.
