Good shortbread fingers need only four ingredients, so each one matters. Use real butter, and keep the dough cool. We add a little rice flour, which gives a fine, sandy snap.

They keep for a week in a tin and make a good gift. For a richer biscuit, try our [walnut brownies](/blog/walnut-brownies/).

## Ingredients

- 200 g salted butter, soft but cool
- 100 g caster sugar, plus extra to sprinkle
- 250 g plain flour
- 50 g rice flour

## Method

1. **Cream.** Beat the butter and sugar together until smooth, but not fluffy. You do not want to beat in air.
2. **Add the flours.** Add both flours and mix with your hands until the dough just comes together.
3. **Press.** Press the dough into a 20 cm square tin lined with paper, and level it with the back of a spoon.
4. **Mark and prick.** Mark it into 16 fingers with a knife and prick each one three times with a fork.
5. **Chill.** Chill the tin for 20 minutes, so the shortbread holds its shape.
6. **Bake.** Bake at 160°C for 35 to 40 minutes, until pale golden at the edges and firm in the middle.
7. **Finish.** Sprinkle with sugar, cut along the marks while hot, and leave in the tin until cold.

## What makes shortbread fingers crisp

A low oven dries the shortbread right through without browning it. If you bake it hotter, the outside colours before the middle is crisp. Cool it in the tin, since warm shortbread breaks easily.

The Wikipedia page on [shortbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortbread) explains the Scottish tradition behind it.

## Storing shortbread fingers

Keep the fingers in an airtight tin at room temperature for up to a week. Do not store them with soft cakes, since they take up moisture and lose their snap. The raw dough freezes well for a month: press it into the tin, wrap it, and bake it from frozen with five extra minutes.

## Flavours to try

Add the zest of a lemon or an orange with the sugar, or swap 25 g of the plain flour for cornflour for an even finer crumb. A spoon of chopped rosemary goes well with a pot of tea.
