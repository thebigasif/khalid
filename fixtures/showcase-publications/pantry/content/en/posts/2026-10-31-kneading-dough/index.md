Recipes give a time for kneading, but every flour and every pair of hands is different. Kneading dough by feel, and learning to tell when it is ready, is the most useful bread skill there is.

Kneading builds gluten, the stretchy network that traps gas so the loaf can rise. Too little, and the loaf is dense. Too much by hand is very hard to do.

## How kneading dough changes it

At the start, the dough looks shaggy and rough, and it tears when you pull it. As you knead, it turns smooth and a little shiny. When it is ready, it holds a round shape on the worktop instead of slumping flat.

## The press test

Press it with a floured finger. Dough that is kneaded enough springs back slowly. If the dent stays, keep going. A well-kneaded dough also feels soft and slightly tacky, but it no longer sticks to clean, dry hands.

## The windowpane test

Pinch off a piece the size of a walnut. Gently stretch it between your fingers, turning it as you go. If it stretches thin enough to let light through without tearing, the gluten is ready. If it tears early, knead for two more minutes and try again.

The test works for plain white doughs. Wholemeal dough and doughs rich in butter, like our [cinnamon buns](/blog/cinnamon-buns/), tear sooner, so rely on look and feel there.

## Or skip most of the kneading

Time can do much of the work. Our [overnight tin loaf](/blog/overnight-tin-loaf/) uses three sets of folds and a night in the fridge in place of kneading. Wikipedia's page on [kneading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kneading) explains the science.

## Kneading dough in a mixer

A stand mixer with a dough hook kneads a plain white dough in about six to eight minutes on a low speed. Watch it closely, since a mixer can overwork the dough. Stop when the dough pulls away from the sides of the bowl and wraps around the hook, then finish with the windowpane test by hand.

## Common kneading mistakes

The most common mistake is adding too much flour to stop the dough sticking. Each handful makes the loaf drier. Use a light film of oil on the worktop instead, or wet your hands.
