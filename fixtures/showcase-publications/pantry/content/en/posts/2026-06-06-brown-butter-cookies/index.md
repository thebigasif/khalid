Browning the butter is the one extra step that makes these brown butter cookies taste of toffee and toasted nuts. It takes five minutes and a light-coloured pan, so you can see the colour change.

Chill the dough if you can. A day in the fridge gives a thicker cookie with a deeper flavour. For a cookie with no chocolate at all, try our [shortbread fingers](/blog/shortbread-fingers/).

## Ingredients

- 170 g unsalted butter
- 150 g light brown sugar
- 50 g caster sugar
- 1 egg and 1 egg yolk
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 220 g plain flour
- 1/2 teaspoon bicarbonate of soda
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- 200 g dark or milk chocolate, chopped
- Flaky salt, to finish

## Method

1. **Brown the butter.** Melt the butter in a pan over a medium heat and keep it bubbling, stirring, until it smells nutty and the bits at the bottom turn brown. Pour it into a bowl and cool for 15 minutes.
2. **Mix.** Whisk both sugars into the butter, then the egg, yolk and vanilla, until glossy.
3. **Add the dry.** Stir in the flour, bicarbonate of soda and salt, then the chocolate.
4. **Chill.** Cover and chill for at least 1 hour, or up to 2 days.
5. **Shape.** Roll into 16 balls, about 50 g each, and space them well apart on lined trays.
6. **Bake.** Bake at 180°C for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges are golden and the middles still look soft. Sprinkle with flaky salt.
7. **Cool.** Leave on the tray for 5 minutes, then move to a rack.

## Getting chewy brown butter cookies

Take them out early. A cookie keeps baking on the hot tray, and a middle that looks underdone sets as it cools. Brown sugar keeps them chewy, so do not swap it all for white.

Wikipedia's page on the [chocolate chip cookie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate_chip_cookie) tells how it was first made.
