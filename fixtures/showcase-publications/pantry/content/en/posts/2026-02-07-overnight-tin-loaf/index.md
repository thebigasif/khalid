This overnight tin loaf is the easiest bread we know. You stir it together after dinner, leave it in the fridge, and shape and bake it in the morning. The slow, cold rise gives it more flavour than a quick loaf.

It makes good toast and good sandwiches. If you want a bigger project, try our [country sourdough loaf](/blog/country-sourdough-loaf/) next.

## Ingredients

- 500 g strong white bread flour
- 7 g fast-action dried yeast
- 9 g fine salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 320 g warm water
- 25 g soft butter, plus a little for the tin

## Method

1. **Mix.** Stir the flour, yeast, salt and sugar in a large bowl. Add the water and butter and mix to a rough, sticky dough.
2. **Rest and fold.** Cover for 10 minutes, then fold the dough over itself ten times in the bowl. Repeat twice more, 10 minutes apart.
3. **Chill.** Cover the bowl and put it in the fridge overnight, 8 to 14 hours.
4. **Shape.** Butter a 900 g loaf tin. Pat the cold dough into a rectangle, roll it up tightly from the short end and set it in the tin, seam down.
5. **Prove.** Cover and leave somewhere warm until the dough domes just above the rim, about 1 to 1.5 hours.
6. **Bake.** Bake at 220°C for 30 to 35 minutes, until deep golden. Turn it out and tap the base: it should sound hollow.
7. **Cool.** Cool on a rack for an hour before slicing.

## Why the overnight tin loaf needs no kneading

Time does the work of kneading. While the dough sits in the fridge, the gluten lines up by itself, and three sets of folds are enough to give it strength. That is why the dough looks rough in the evening and smooth in the morning.

## Swaps that work

Use 100 g of wholemeal flour in place of the same weight of white, and add 20 g more water. Milk in place of half the water gives a softer crust. A spoon of honey can replace the sugar.
