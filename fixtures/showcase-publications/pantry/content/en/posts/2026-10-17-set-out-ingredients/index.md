Before any bake, set out your ingredients. Every one is weighed into its own bowl or cup and lined up in the order the recipe uses it. Cooks call this mise en place, which is French for putting in place.

It sounds slow, but it saves time. Once you start mixing, you never stop to hunt for the baking powder while the butter melts.

## Why setting out your ingredients helps

Most failed cakes are not about skill. They come from a missed spoon of raising agent, a double dose of salt, or butter that went in cold when it should have been soft. When everything is weighed and in front of you, a missing bowl is easy to see.

It also shows you, before you begin, whether you have enough of everything. Finding out you have one egg after you have creamed the butter is a bad moment.

## Weigh, do not scoop

We write our recipes in grams, and we weigh everything, even water and milk. A cup of flour can weigh anything from 120 g to 160 g, depending on how you fill it. A scale does not change its mind.

A simple digital scale with a tare button is enough. Put the bowl on, press tare, and add the ingredient until you reach the weight.

Here is what one cup of each of our common ingredients weighs, so you can see why cups go wrong:

| Ingredient | One cup, spooned in | One cup, scooped | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain flour | 120 g | 150 g | 25% more |
| Strong bread flour | 125 g | 160 g | 28% more |
| Caster sugar | 200 g | 210 g | 5% more |
| Light brown sugar, packed | 200 g | 220 g | 10% more |
| Cocoa powder | 85 g | 105 g | 24% more |
| Rolled oats | 90 g | 100 g | 11% more |

## Get things to the right temperature

Read the recipe through and check the temperatures. Butter for creaming should be soft enough to dent with a finger. Butter for pastry should be cold. Eggs for cakes mix in better at room temperature, so take them out half an hour before.

Our [brown butter cookies](/blog/brown-butter-cookies/) and [apple pie](/blog/apple-pie/) both work best when you start this way. Wikipedia explains the wider idea of [mise en place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mise_en_place) in restaurant kitchens.
