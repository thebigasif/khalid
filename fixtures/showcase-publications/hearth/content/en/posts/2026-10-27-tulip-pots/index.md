Late October is the best time to plant tulip pots. The soil is cool, so the bulbs root well and are less likely to catch tulip fire, a disease that likes warm, wet autumns. At the nursery we plant about two hundred pots in the last week of the month.

## Why plant tulip pots in layers

A pot planted in layers flowers for longer. Bulbs that flower early go at the top, and late bulbs go at the bottom, deepest down. Each layer comes up through the one above it. Gardeners sometimes call this a bulb lasagne, and the name is a good way to remember the order.

## What you need

- A pot at least 30 cm deep, with holes in the base.
- Peat-free compost mixed with a few handfuls of grit, so water drains away.
- Three kinds of bulbs: small early ones, such as crocus, then early tulips, then late tulips.

## How to plant tulip pots

1. Put 5 cm of compost and grit in the base.
2. Set the late tulips on it, pointed end up, almost touching.
3. Cover them with 5 cm of compost, then set the early tulips on top.
4. Cover again, then push the crocus into the top layer.
5. Finish with 3 cm of compost and water once.

| Layer | Depth from the top | Flowers |
|---|---|---|
| Crocus | 5 cm | March |
| Early tulips | 12 cm | April |
| Late tulips | 20 cm | May |

## Looking after the pot

Stand the pot by a wall, where it is sheltered from the worst rain. Do not water again until you see shoots in late winter, unless the compost is bone dry. A piece of wire mesh on top keeps squirrels from digging.

When the last tulips fade, snip off the heads and let the leaves die back. Many tulips flower less well the next year, so we plant fresh tulip pots each autumn and move the old bulbs into a border.
