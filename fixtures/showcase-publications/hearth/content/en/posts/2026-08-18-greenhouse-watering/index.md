Greenhouse watering is the biggest job at the nursery in summer. In a hot week in July, our four greenhouses can drink ten tonnes of water a day. Since 2018 we have cut the mains water we use by two thirds, and the plants grow better for it.

## Catching the rain

Every roof at the nursery now drains into tanks: the greenhouses, the barn, the shop and even the potting shed. Together they hold sixty cubic metres. In a normal year, that rain covers most of our watering from October to June.

Rainwater is better for plants than tap water because it is softer. It is also the same temperature as the air in the greenhouse, so it does not chill the roots.

## Greenhouse watering from below

Most of our benches are now flood benches. A pump fills a shallow tray under the pots for ten minutes, then the water drains back into the tank to be used again. As a result, each pot drinks only what it needs through the holes in its base.

| Way of watering | Litres a bench uses each week | Leaves stay dry |
|---|---|---|
| Hose from above | 900 | No |
| Drip lines | 450 | Yes |
| Flood bench | 300 | Yes |

Dry leaves matter as much as the water saved. Damp leaves overnight bring mildew and grey mould, so plants watered from below need fewer treatments.

## What we use, year by year

| Year | Mains water, cubic metres | Rain caught, cubic metres |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 1,200 | 150 |
| 2022 | 640 | 520 |
| 2026 | 410 | 610 |

## What you can do at home

You do not need a flood bench. For example, stand pots in a tray of water for half an hour, then let them drain. Water early in the morning, at the base of the plant. A water butt on a shed roof catches a surprising amount, because one heavy shower can fill it.

Plants that need less water help too. Esi's guide to [lavender pruning](/blog/lavender-pruning/) keeps one of the toughest of them in good shape, and a [salad bed by the kitchen door](/blog/salad-bed/) is easy to water little and often. The [Environment Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/environment-agency) publishes advice on saving water at home.
