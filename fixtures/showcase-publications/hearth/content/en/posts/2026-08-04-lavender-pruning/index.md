"Why has my lavender gone woody?" is the question I hear most at the till in summer. The answer is nearly always the same: it was not pruned. The good news is that lavender pruning takes ten minutes a year, and early August is the time to do it.

## Why lavender goes woody

Lavender is a small shrub. Each year it grows new soft shoots from the tips, and the stems below turn to wood. Old wood rarely sprouts again. Leave a plant unpruned for a few years and you get a bare, woody middle with a few tufts of leaves on top.

## When to prune

Prune just after the flowers fade, in late July or early August. The plant still has time to grow new shoots before winter, and those shoots carry next year's flowers. Do not prune hard in autumn or spring, because frost can harm the cut stems.

## Lavender pruning in three steps

1. Gather a bunch of stems in one hand, like a ponytail.
2. Cut them with shears, about 2 cm above the woody part. Leave some green on every stem.
3. Shape the plant into a neat dome as you go round it.

It feels like a lot to take off. The plant will look bare for two weeks, then fill out with fresh grey leaves.

| Plant's age | How much to cut | What to leave |
|---|---|---|
| First year | Just the flower stems | All the leaves |
| Two to ten years | About a third | 2 cm of green |
| Older and woody | Only the flower stems | Replace it in spring |

## A plant that is already woody

A very woody lavender cannot be saved by hard pruning. Instead, take soft cuttings from the tips in late summer and grow new plants. Lavender lives about ten to fifteen years even with good care, so a fresh start is no failure.
