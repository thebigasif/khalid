By September many gardens have little left in flower, but a few late flowers make a real difference to bees. Bumblebee queens are feeding up for winter, and some bees fly until November on mild days.

## What makes a good flower for bees

Bees need to reach the nectar and pollen. Open, simple flowers are best, with one ring of petals round a busy middle. Double flowers, with lots of petals packed together, look full but often give bees nothing at all.

Colour matters too. Bees see blue, purple and yellow well, so asters and sedums are easy for them to find. Red flowers look dark to a bee, though it will still visit a red flower with an open middle.

## Six late flowers for bees

| Plant | Flowers | Height |
|---|---|---|
| Single dahlias | July to frost | 60 to 120 cm |
| Asters | September to October | 40 to 120 cm |
| Sedum | August to October | 40 cm |
| Ivy | September to November | Climber |
| Japanese anemone | August to October | 90 cm |
| Verbena bonariensis | July to October | 150 cm |

Ivy surprises people. Its round flower heads open in autumn, when little else does, and on a sunny day you can hear the bees before you see them.

## Planting late flowers

Most of these plants like sun and soil that drains well. Plant them in groups of three or more, so bees can feed from one patch without flying far. Pots work too: a single dahlia in a large pot by the back door will feed bees for months.

Water new plants once a week for the first month, even in autumn, while their roots spread. After that, most of them need water only in a long dry spell.

## After the flowers

Leave the seed heads standing over winter. Birds eat the seeds, and insects hide in the hollow stems until spring. Cut everything back in March, when you see new shoots at the base.
