If you want to grow food but have little time, start with a salad bed. One metre square, close to the kitchen door, gives you fresh leaves from April to November. You pick a few leaves at a time, and the plants keep growing.

## Where to put your salad bed

Close to the door matters more than you would think. A bed you walk past every day gets picked, watered and weeded. Salad leaves like some shade in summer, so a spot that gets sun only in the morning suits them well.

A raised bed or a large trough works as well as soil in the ground. Fill it with good compost and keep it moist.

## What to sow

Sow little and often. A short row every three weeks means you always have young leaves, and never a glut.

| Leaf | When to sow | Ready to pick |
|---|---|---|
| Loose-leaf lettuce | March to August | 6 weeks |
| Rocket | April to September | 4 weeks |
| Spinach | March to September | 6 weeks |
| Parsley | April to July | 10 weeks |
| Winter purslane | August | 8 weeks |

## How to pick

Take the outer leaves and leave the middle of each plant to grow. This is called cut and come again. A lettuce picked this way gives you leaves for two months, whereas a whole lettuce cut at once gives you one salad.

## Keeping it going

Water in the evening in hot weather, at the base of the plants. Lettuce that runs dry turns bitter and sends up a flower stalk. When a plant does bolt, pull it up and sow again in its place.

## Starting from plants

If sowing feels like too much, start with young plants. Every lettuce and herb on our benches began as a tiny seedling, as Tom shows in [plug plants: six weeks on our benches](/blog/plug-plants/). And when summer ends, the same bed can hold [tulip pots](/blog/tulip-pots/) for spring colour by the door. The [Royal Horticultural Society](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/lettuce/grow-your-own) has a good guide to lettuce.
