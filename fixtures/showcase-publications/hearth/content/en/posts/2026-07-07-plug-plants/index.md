Visitors often ask where our plants come from. Most bedding plants start as plug plants: seedlings grown in trays of small cells, each the size of a thumb. Here is what happens to them in the six weeks before they reach the sales bench.

## Week one: potting on plug plants

The plug plants arrive in trays of 150. Within a day we pot each one into a 9 cm pot of peat-free compost. Three of us can pot about four thousand a day at the long bench in the first greenhouse. We water them in from below and keep them shaded for a few days while they settle.

## Weeks two to four: growing on

The young plants move to the open benches. The vents open at seven every morning and close in the evening, so the plants grow sturdy, not tall and weak. We pinch out the tips of some, such as fuchsias, so they branch and make bushy plants.

| Week | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pot on and shade | Greenhouse one |
| 2 to 4 | Grow on, pinch out | Greenhouse two |
| 5 | Harden off | Cold frames |
| 6 | Label and sell | Sales benches |

## Week five: hardening off

Plants raised under glass need time to get used to wind and cool nights. We move them to cold frames outside, with lids we open in the day and close at night. After a week they are ready for a garden.

## Week six: on the sales bench

Each plant gets a label with its name, how big it grows and whether bees like it. Then it goes out for sale. Any that are not sold in four weeks go into larger pots, or into the hanging baskets we make for the village.

## The year goes round

In autumn the same benches fill with bulbs, and we plant hundreds of [tulip pots](/blog/tulip-pots/) for spring. Come and see the greenhouses for yourself at our [apple day](/blog/apple-day/) in October. The [Horticultural Trades Association](https://hta.org.uk/) has more on how plants are grown in nurseries.
