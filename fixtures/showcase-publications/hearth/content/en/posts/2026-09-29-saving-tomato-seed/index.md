Saving tomato seed is one of the easiest ways to start keeping your own plants. One good fruit gives you a hundred seeds, enough for years. We save seed from six tomatoes at the nursery every September.

## Choose the right tomato

Save seed only from old kinds, often called heritage or open-pollinated tomatoes. Seed from F1 tomatoes does not come true, so the plants you grow will be different from the parent. The seed packet says F1 on the front when a tomato is one.

Pick the best fruit from your healthiest plant. It should be fully ripe, even a little soft.

## Saving tomato seed in four steps

1. Cut the tomato in half across its middle and squeeze the seeds and jelly into a jam jar.
2. Add a splash of water, cover the jar with a cloth and leave it somewhere warm for three days.
3. When a thin white skin forms on top, pour off the skin and the floating seeds. The good seeds sink.
4. Rinse the seeds in a sieve, then spread them on a plate to dry for a week.

The few days in the jar let the jelly round each seed rot away. Without that jelly, the seeds keep better and sprout faster.

## Storing the seed

When the seeds are dry and loose, put them in a paper envelope. Write the name and the year on it. Kept in a cool, dry drawer, tomato seed stays good for four or five years.

## Sowing next spring

Sow the seed indoors in March, on a sunny windowsill.
