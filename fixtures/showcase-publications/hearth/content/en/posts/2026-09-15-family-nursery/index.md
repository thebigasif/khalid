My parents opened Larkfield as a family nursery in April 1968, with one greenhouse, a field of cabbages and a hand-painted sign. This autumn Tom, my son, took over the growing. We sat down together at the old potting bench to talk about what that means to us.

## How the family nursery started

Ruth: My father was a gardener on a big estate. When it was sold, he bought the field behind our cottage and started growing bedding plants to sell at the gate. My mother kept the money in a biscuit tin. I was six, and my job was to water the trays.

Tom: The biscuit tin is still in the office. Nobody is allowed to throw it away.

## What has changed

Ruth: Almost everything. We have four greenhouses now, a shop and a café in the old barn. We stopped using peat in 2019, which was hard for the first year. We also catch rain off every roof to water the plants.

Tom: People ask different questions now. They want plants for bees, food they can grow on a balcony and ways to garden without chemicals. Twenty years ago most of what we sold was bedding.

## What a family nursery still means

Ruth: You still have to be here at seven to open the vents in summer. Plants do not wait for weekends.

Tom: And we still grow most of what we sell ourselves. That is the part I care about most. When someone asks how a plant was grown, we can tell them, because we grew it.
