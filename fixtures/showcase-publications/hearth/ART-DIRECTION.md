# Larkfield Nursery: art direction

Larkfield Nursery is a made-up family plant nursery. Its photographs should look like the nursery's own: plants, flowers, fruit and the tools of the work, close up and in plain daylight, as a gardener would take them on a phone between jobs.

## Tone

- **Plants are the subject.** Flowers, leaves, fruit and benches of pots fill the frame. Hands may appear, but no faces and no posed portraits.
- **Plain daylight.** Soft or bright sun, true colour. No heavy filters and no studio backdrops.
- **Nothing to read.** No signs, prices or labels in the picture.

## Palette

Leaf greens and warm soil browns, with one bright flower or fruit in each picture: red tulips, a red apple, a ripe tomato, purple lavender. These sit well beside the sample brand's moss green and pale sage.

## Subject mix

Of the 9 photographs, 5 are close details and 4 are wider scenes.

- **Close details (5):** a hand holding an apple, a tomato on the vine, a bumblebee in a dahlia, lettuce and parsley in soil, a watering can on a branch.
- **Wider scenes (4):** a bed of tulips, rows of lavender, benches of potted plants, a greenhouse of bedding plants.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead story on the front page | 4:5 | 1600 px wide | Cropped to a tall arch by the template; 4:3 on a phone. Pick a subject in the middle. |
| Cards | 4:3 | 1600 px wide | Cropped by the template under a low arch. Sources are 3:2 or 4:3. |
| Lead picture on a post | 16:10 | 1600 px wide | Cropped by the template under a low arch. A tall source loses its top and bottom. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

## Phone crop

On a phone each picture fills the width. The apple, the tomato and the bee sit in the middle third, so every crop keeps them. The tulips, lavender and greenhouse are even scenes that read the same at any crop.

## Sources and licences

Every photograph comes from the WordPress photo directory through Openverse and `scripts/demo-photo.py`, and every one is under CC0, so none needs a visible credit. `photos.lock` records each one, and `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the photo credits page still name each photographer.
