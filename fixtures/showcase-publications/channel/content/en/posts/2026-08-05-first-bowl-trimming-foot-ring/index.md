Trimming a foot ring turns a heavy bowl into a light one. In the last part of our series, Noor takes the bowl she threw in [part 2](/blog/first-bowl-pulling-walls/), turns it over and carves a clean foot.

## Is the bowl ready?

Trim at [leather hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather-hard). Press a thumbnail into the base: it should leave a mark but not sink in. Too wet, and the clay sticks to the tool in ribbons. Too dry, and the tool chatters and leaves ridges. Wrap bowls in plastic overnight if you cannot trim them the next day.

## Centring the bowl upside down

Put the bowl rim down on the wheel. Spin the wheel slowly and hold a pencil near the side of the bowl. Where the pencil touches, tap the bowl gently towards the middle. Repeat until the pencil touches all the way round. Then press three small coils of soft clay against the rim to hold it.

## Trimming a foot ring step by step

1. Knock on the base and listen. A dull sound means thick clay; a hollow sound means you are close to the floor.
2. Trim the outside wall first, following the curve you threw.
3. Mark the foot ring with the corner of the tool: two circles about a centimetre apart.
4. Carve away the clay inside the inner circle, a little at a time.
5. Smooth the foot with a damp finger while the wheel turns slowly.

Noor stops often to lift the bowl and feel its weight. A finished bowl should feel lighter than it looks.

## What comes next

Let the bowl dry slowly under a loose sheet of plastic for two days, then uncovered. When it is bone dry, it goes into the bisque firing. We show how we pack the kiln in [loading the kiln](/blog/loading-gas-kiln/), and how to glaze the bowl in [three ways to dip glaze](/blog/dip-glaze-three-ways/).

That is the end of the series. Thank you for throwing along with us.
