Dip glaze is the fastest way to glaze a bowl evenly. In this video, Noor glazes three bowls three ways with our [celadon glaze](/blog/celadon-glaze-recipe/) and shows the results after the firing.

## Before you dip glaze

Wipe each bisque bowl with a damp sponge to take off dust. Wax the foot ring with a brush so the glaze will not stick there. Stir the glaze from the bottom of the bucket until no lumps are left. It should coat your hand like single cream.

## Way 1: the one-dip

This is how most [glaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic_glaze) goes on in our studio.

Hold the bowl by the foot with your fingertips, rim down. Push it straight into the glaze, count to three and lift it out. Turn it the right way up straight away so the glaze runs evenly. This is the quickest way, and it suits small bowls that fit in the bucket.

## Way 2: pour inside, dip outside

For a bowl too big to dip whole, pour glaze inside first. Fill the bowl a third full, swirl it once and pour it out in one smooth turn. Let it dry for a minute. Then hold the foot and dip the outside, rim down, to just below the foot.

## Way 3: the double dip

Dip the bowl once, let it dry, then dip only the top third again. Where the two coats overlap, the celadon turns a deeper green. It is the look of the bowls in our thumbnail.

## Cleaning up

Sponge any glaze off the waxed foot. Fill pinholes by rubbing them with a dry finger. Glaze on the foot will weld the bowl to the kiln shelf, so check every one before it goes in.

After the firing, the one-dip bowl came out even and pale, the poured bowl a little darker inside, and the double dip had the band of deep green we hoped for. Tomas shows how the bowls go into the kiln in [loading the kiln](/blog/loading-gas-kiln/).
