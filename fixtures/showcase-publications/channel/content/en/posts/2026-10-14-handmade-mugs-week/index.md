Handmade mugs are what most people ask us for. In this vlog, we film a whole week in the studio, from forty balls of clay on Monday to a shelf of finished mugs the next Sunday.

## Monday and Tuesday: throwing

Noor wedges forty balls of iron red stoneware, each 450 grams. She throws all forty on Monday, cylinders about twelve centimetres tall. By the end of the day, thirty-six are good. Four go back in the clay bucket.

On Tuesday, she trims the feet and pulls handles. We made a separate video on [pulling handles for mugs](/blog/pulling-handles-mugs/), since it is the step people ask about most.

## Wednesday to Friday: drying and bisque

The mugs dry slowly under plastic. Handles dry faster than the body, so Noor wraps each handle in a strip of plastic for the first day. On Friday, Tomas loads them into the bisque firing.

## Saturday and Sunday: glaze and fire

On Saturday, Noor glazes the mugs with our [celadon](/blog/celadon-glaze-recipe/) and a dark iron glaze. Tomas [loads the kiln](/blog/loading-gas-kiln/) and fires it overnight. On Sunday, it cools.

## Handmade mugs by the numbers

| Step | Mugs left | Lost at this step |
|---|---|---|
| Thrown | 40 | 0 |
| Trimmed and handled | 36 | 4 |
| Bisque fired | 35 | 1 |
| Glaze fired | 33 | 2 |

Thirty-three from forty balls of clay is a good week for us. The two lost in the glaze firing had glaze on the foot, which we should have caught.

## What we would change

Next time, Noor will throw forty-four balls, since we lose about one in ten. Tomas will check every foot before the glaze firing, not after. And we will film the handles from above, which shows the thumb groove better. We wrote up the camera side of that in [our filming setup](/blog/filming-pottery-setup/).

Iron red stoneware is a forgiving clay for mugs. It throws well, holds a handle and fires to a warm brown under a clear glaze. You can read more about [stoneware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoneware) and why it suits pots for daily use.
