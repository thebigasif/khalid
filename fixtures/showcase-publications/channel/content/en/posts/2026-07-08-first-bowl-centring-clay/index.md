Centring clay is the step most new potters rush, and it is the reason most first bowls wobble. In this first part of our series, Noor wedges a ball of buff stoneware and centres it on the wheel, slowly enough that you can copy every move.

## Wedging before you sit down

Wedging mixes the clay and pushes out air. A pocket of air can burst in the kiln or throw the clay off centre as it spins. Noor uses the ram's head method: she rocks the clay forward with both palms, lifts it back with her fingers and turns it a little each time. Forty pushes is enough for a ball the size of a grapefruit.

Cut the wedged ball in half with a wire and look at the faces. You want an even colour with no holes. If you see a pocket, wedge again.

## Centring clay in three passes

Wet the wheel head, slap the ball down in the middle and press it so it sticks. Then work in three passes:

1. **Cone up.** Squeeze the clay from the sides with both hands so it rises into a cone.
2. **Cone down.** Press down with the heel of your hand and guide the cone back into a dome.
3. **Hold still.** Brace your elbows on your knees and let the wheel do the work until the clay stops pushing back.

Run the wheel fast for centring, about three quarters of its top speed. Keep your hands wet but not dripping. Too much water turns the outside to slurry, and the clay sags in the next step.

## How to tell when it is centred

Close your eyes and rest one hand lightly on the spinning clay. If it is centred, your hand stays still. If your hand moves in and out, the clay is still off centre, so do one more pass.
