People often ask about filming pottery at the wheel. This post has no video: it is the list we wish we had when we started, written down so you can copy it.

## One camera, mostly still

We film nearly everything with one camera on a tripod. It sits at the height of Noor's shoulder, a little to her left, pointing down at the wheel. From there, you see both her hands and the inside of the pot. A second, small camera looks straight down for trimming and glazing.

We do not move the camera during a take. A still frame lets you watch the clay, not the camera.

## Two soft lights

Clay is shiny when wet, and a hard light makes bright spots that hide the shape. We use two large soft lights:

- one in front, high and to the side, as the main light;
- one behind, low, to show the edge of the pot against the background.

We switched off the ceiling lights. They flickered on camera and made the clay look green.

## Settings for filming pottery

- Frame rate: 25 frames a second, so the wheel does not seem to spin backwards (the [wagon wheel effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon-wheel_effect)).
- Shutter: 1/50 of a second. A faster shutter makes the throwing water look jittery.
- Focus: manual, set on the rim of the pot. Autofocus jumps to Noor's hands.

## Three things that made the biggest difference

1. **A plain background.** We hung a sheet of grey felt behind the wheel. The shelves of pots behind it were lovely but busy.
2. **A sound mat.** A rubber mat under the wheel stopped its hum from reaching the microphone.
3. **Short takes.** We film each step as its own take, so a mistake costs one minute, not twenty.
