Pulling handles is the step that makes a mug feel good in the hand. In this video, Noor pulls a handle from a carrot of clay, attaches it to a leather hard mug and shapes it to fit four fingers.

## Make the carrot

Roll a lump of the same clay as the mug into a short carrot shape, fat at one end. Hold the fat end in one hand, handle down. Wet your other hand.

## Pulling handles step by step

1. Wrap your wet hand loosely round the clay, thumb on one side and fingers on the other.
2. Stroke down in one smooth move. The clay thins and gets longer.
3. Wet your hand again and repeat. After about ten pulls, the strap is long and even.
4. Press your thumb along the strap on the last pull to make a groove. The groove makes the handle look lighter and stiffens it.

Lay the strap over the edge of a table and let it firm up for twenty minutes.

## Attach it so it stays on

Most handles crack off at the joins. Score both the mug and the handle ends with a fork, add a dab of [slip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip_(ceramics)) and press the top join on firmly. Bend the handle into a loop and press the bottom join on. Smooth both joins with a damp brush until you cannot see the line.

## Check the fit

Put four fingers through the loop. You want a finger's width of space between your knuckles and the mug, so hot coffee does not burn them. Noor's handles leave room for four fingers, but a smaller loop for two fingers suits a cup.

Dry the mugs slowly, with a strip of plastic over each handle. You can see a whole batch made this way in [a week of mugs](/blog/handmade-mugs-week/).
