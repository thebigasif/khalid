Pulling walls is where a lump of clay becomes a bowl. In part 2 of our series, Noor opens the clay she centred in [part 1](/blog/first-bowl-centring-clay/), sets the floor and makes three pulls to raise even walls.

## Opening the clay

Slow the wheel to about half speed. Find the middle with your thumbs and press straight down. Stop when about a centimetre of clay is left under your thumbs. To check, push a pin tool through the floor and pinch the clay at the top of the pin.

Then draw your fingers out towards you to widen the floor. A bowl needs a floor about as wide as your palm. Press the floor down with a sponge as you go. This packs the clay and stops S-shaped cracks later.

## Pulling walls in three pulls

Each pull thins the wall and lifts it. Noor uses her fingertips on the inside and a knuckle on the outside, a little below the inside hand.

- **First pull:** start right at the base, squeeze gently and rise slowly. The wall gets about a third thinner.
- **Second pull:** the same move, a little faster. Keep the top narrower than you want, since a bowl opens as you shape it.
- **Third pull:** a light pull to even out the wall. Stop when the wall is about as thick as a pencil.

Slow the wheel a little more for each pull. If the top goes wavy, cut it level with a needle tool and carry on.

## Shaping the curve

Only shape the bowl after the last pull. Push out gently from inside with a rib while your outside hand follows. Wide bowls fall more easily, so shape in small steps and stop while it still looks a little narrow.

Leave the bowl until it is [leather hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather-hard), firm enough to hold but still cool to the touch. In [part 3](/blog/first-bowl-trimming-foot-ring/), Noor trims a foot ring into it.
