This letter is printed first. Every issue of The Proof Sheet goes on a hand press before it goes out by email. We print about forty copies. A few go to readers who asked for one, a few go on the wall of the print shop, and the rest are kept in a box under the stairs.

## Why print at all

Printing first slows us down, and that is the reason this letter is printed at all. Setting a page in type means reading every line twice: once as you set it, letter by letter, and once as you check the proof. Mistakes that slip past a screen do not slip past a compositor's thumb.

It also makes the letter shorter. When every word has to be picked out of a case by hand, you stop writing words you do not need. Our issues have been about the same length since the first one, and we think that is why.

## The proof press

The tool this week is the proof press, a simple flatbed press used to pull a single print to check the type. You ink the type with a roller, lay a sheet over it and run a heavy cylinder across. You can read about [the history of letterpress printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterpress_printing) and the presses that came before ours.

The first print is the proof sheet. We read it with a pencil, mark the errors, fix the type and pull another. Only then does the issue go out.

## Why this letter is printed first

Being printed first changes how an issue feels. A letter is printed with care when every line costs effort. It has an edge and an end. It has a number, a date and a list of what is inside. When we moved to email we kept all of that, because readers told us they liked knowing when an issue was over.

## Letters

A reader asked whether she could get a printed copy. Yes: write to letters@example.com with your address. We send a few every week, in the order you ask. Our [fiftieth issue](/blog/fifty-issues-set-by-hand/) shows the shop where they are printed.
