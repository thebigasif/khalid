Every season we send one special issue with a list of classes, and this is the winter workshop list. Each class below is run by someone we have visited for the letter, and each one has room for beginners. Book early: the small ones fill in days.

## Printing

Ada, whose shop we visited for [our fiftieth issue](/blog/fifty-issues-set-by-hand/), runs four evenings at her hand press in January. You set a short poem in metal type, print twenty copies and take them home. Aprons are provided, because ink does not wash out.

Jonas, the carver from [issue forty-seven](/blog/carving-by-lamplight/), teaches a Saturday on cutting a small wood block for printing. You leave with a block and a stack of prints.

## Textiles

A dyer near the market teaches a day of natural dyes: onion skins for gold, madder root for red and woad for blue. Bring a white wool jumper you are ready to change. You can read about [natural dyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_dye) before you go, so the colours do not surprise you.

The same studio runs a spinning day in February. You learn to spin wool on a drop spindle, then on a wheel. Most people go home with a lumpy skein and a new respect for socks.

## Clay and wood

The potter from [issue forty-five](/blog/a-year-at-the-wheel/) has three wheel classes this winter, each five weeks long. A spoon carving day runs in March, with green wood from a local tree surgeon and a sharp knife each.

## How the winter workshop list works

We do not take money from anyone on the winter workshop list, and nobody paid to be on it. We list classes we would join ourselves. If a class is full, write to the maker and ask to be told about the next one. Most of them keep a list.

## Letters

Several of you asked for workshops for children. Two of the classes above take children over ten with a grown-up, and the spinning day takes them over eight. Ask when you book.
