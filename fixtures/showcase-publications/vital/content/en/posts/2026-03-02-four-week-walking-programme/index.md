Walking is the easiest habit to start: no kit, no booking, just a door. This walking programme builds up over four weeks, from three short walks a week to five longer ones. Go at a pace where you can still talk.

Priya wrote it for people who have not walked much for a while, or who want a gentle way back into moving more. Each week adds a little, so your legs and feet have time to get used to it. Write your walks on a calendar and tick each one off, because seeing the ticks build up is a good reason to keep going.

## The four-week walking programme

| Week | Walks | Each walk | Pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 15 minutes | Easy, chatting |
| 2 | 4 | 20 minutes | Easy, chatting |
| 3 | 4 | 25 minutes | A little brisker for the middle 10 minutes |
| 4 | 5 | 30 minutes | Brisk for the middle 15 minutes |

## Each walk

1. **Start slowly** for the first five minutes, so your body warms up.
2. **Find your pace** for the middle part. You should be able to talk but not sing.
3. **Slow down** for the last five minutes.
4. **Stretch** for a few minutes when you get home, with [five easy stretches for walkers](/blog/after-walk-stretches/).

## Tips from the walking group

- Pick a route you like. A park or a canal path makes the time go faster.
- Walk with a friend, or [join the walking group on Saturdays](/blog/saturday-walking-group/).
- Comfortable shoes matter more than special clothes.
- If you miss a day, just carry on. Repeat a week if it felt hard.

## When the weather turns

Rain and dark evenings are the most common reasons a walking habit stops. So plan an indoor option, such as walking laps of a shopping centre, and keep a waterproof by the door. A short walk in poor weather still counts as one of your walks for the week.

## Keep going

After week four, keep the five walks and choose one longer walk at the weekend. Many people find the programme becomes part of the week without needing a plan. The [Ramblers' advice for new walkers](https://www.ramblers.org.uk/go-walking-hub) has ideas for routes near you.
