This morning mobility routine is the one we teach first at the studio. It takes ten minutes, needs only a mat, and works through the joints that often feel stiff after a night's sleep: the hips, the back and the shoulders.

Move slowly and breathe through your nose. Every move should feel easy. If one does not, make it smaller or leave it out, because the aim is to feel looser, not to push.

## The morning mobility routine, step by step

1. **Cat and cow, for a minute.** On hands and knees, round your back as you breathe out, then let it dip as you breathe in.
2. **Thread the needle.** From hands and knees, slide one arm under the other and rest that shoulder on the mat. Stay for about a minute on each side.
3. **Low lunge.** Step one foot forward, lower the back knee and let your hips sink a little. Hold each side for half a minute or so.
4. **Seated forward reach.** Sit with your legs long and reach towards your feet. Bend your knees as much as you like.
5. **Lying twist.** Lie on your back, drop both knees to one side and look the other way. Then change sides.
6. **Easy squat.** Stand with your feet wide and sit down as far as is comfortable, holding a chair if it helps.

## Making it a habit

The routine works best at the same time each day, for example before breakfast or after brushing your teeth. Roll the mat out the night before, so it is waiting for you when you wake up.

On busy days, do the first three moves only. Three minutes still counts, and keeping the habit matters more than doing every move.

## How it should feel

You should finish warm and a little looser, never sore. A mild stretch is fine; a sharp or pinching feeling is a sign to ease off. Most people find the routine gets easier within two weeks.
