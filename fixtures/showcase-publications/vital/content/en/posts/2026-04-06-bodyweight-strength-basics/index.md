Strength work does not need a gym. This bodyweight strength programme uses four moves, three days a week, for six weeks. Each move has an easier and a harder version, so you can start where you are.

Leave a day between sessions, and warm up first with [the ten-minute mobility routine](/blog/morning-mobility-routine/).

You need a mat or a soft floor, a sturdy chair and a little space. Many people do the sessions on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, so the weekend stays free. Write down how many you managed in each round, since that is the easiest way to see your progress over the six weeks.

## The bodyweight strength session

1. **Squat.** Do 8, rest, and repeat twice more. Easier: sit down to a chair and stand up. Harder: pause at the bottom for two seconds.
2. **Push-up.** Do 6 at a time, three times. Easier: hands on a wall or a table. Harder: hands on the floor.
3. **Bridge.** Lie on your back with knees bent and lift your hips, 10 times in each round. Harder: one leg at a time.
4. **Plank.** Hold for 20 seconds, three times. Easier: knees down. Harder: hold for 40 seconds.

Rest for about a minute between rounds.

## The six weeks

| Weeks | What changes |
|---|---|
| 1 and 2 | Learn the moves with the easier versions |
| 3 and 4 | Add two to each round, or move to the harder version of one move |
| 5 and 6 | Try the harder version of each move you feel ready for |

## How bodyweight strength work should feel

The last two of each round should feel like work, but you should finish with good form. Sore muscles the next day are common at first and fade as you get used to it. Sharp pain is not normal, so stop and rest.

## On the days in between

Rest days matter, because muscles get stronger while they recover. On those days a walk, a stretch or [our balance moves](/blog/balance-practice/) keep you moving without tiring the same muscles.

The [NHS strength exercises page](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/strength-exercises/) shows more moves with pictures, for when you want to add a fifth.
