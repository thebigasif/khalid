After a longer walk, a few minutes of stretching feels good and helps you wind down. These after-walk stretches use a park bench, a wall or a fence. Hold each one for about 20 seconds and keep breathing.

Priya leads them at the end of every Saturday group walk, and most walkers now do them at home too. They suit any level, and none of them asks you to sit or lie on wet grass, so you can do them in any weather and in ordinary clothes.

## The five after-walk stretches

1. **Calf stretch.** Hands on the wall, one foot back, heel down. Then change sides.
2. **Front thigh stretch.** Hold the wall, bend one knee and hold your ankle behind you.
3. **Back thigh stretch.** Rest one heel on a low bench, keep the leg straight and lean forward a little.
4. **Hip stretch.** Step one foot forward into a small lunge and press your hips gently forward.
5. **Side reach.** Stand tall and reach one arm over your head, then the other.

## A few tips

- Stretch while you are still warm, within ten minutes of stopping.
- Never bounce. Ease in and hold.
- A stretch should feel like a gentle pull, never pain.
- Breathe out slowly as you ease into each one.

## Why stretch at the end

After a walk your muscles are warm, so they stretch more easily than at any other time of day. A few quiet minutes also give your breathing and heart rate time to settle before you go indoors.

## On a cold day

In winter, do the stretches indoors as soon as you get home, before you sit down. Keep your coat on for the first two if you feel the cold.
