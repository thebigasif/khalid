Box breathing is one of the simplest ways to slow down. It uses four equal counts, like the four sides of a box. Two minutes is enough, and you can do it at a desk, on a bus or before a difficult call.

Sit or stand comfortably and let your shoulders drop. You can close your eyes if that feels good, or rest them on one spot in front of you. Some people like to trace a square in the air with one finger, one side for each count, which helps keep the rhythm steady while they learn it.

## How to do box breathing

1. **Breathe in** through your nose while you count slowly to four.
2. **Hold** the breath gently for the same count.
3. **Breathe out** through your mouth, again to four.
4. **Pause** with empty lungs for four before the next breath.

Repeat the box six to eight times. If four feels long, use three. If holding feels uncomfortable, leave the holds out and simply breathe in and out evenly.

## When to use it

- Before something that makes you nervous, such as a talk or an interview.
- When you notice your shoulders are up by your ears.
- At the start of your [evening wind-down](/blog/wind-down-routine/) before bed.

## Keep it light

Breathing exercises should feel calm, not effortful. If you feel light-headed, stop and breathe normally for a minute. Nobody needs to know you are doing it, so it works in a meeting or a queue too.

## Practise when you are calm

The pattern is easiest to use under pressure if you have practised it on ordinary days. Try one round each morning for a week, for example while the kettle boils, so the counts become familiar.
