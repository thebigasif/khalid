People often ask why this site is full of short routines and not long lists of rules. The answer is simple: a short routine is something you can do today, in your own home, without buying anything.

## Short routines fit real days

Every routine here takes between two and thirty minutes. Each one says how long it takes, how hard it is, how often to do it and what you need. That is all you need to decide whether to try it.

We would rather you did a five-minute routine every day than a perfect plan once. Short routines are easier to keep, because they fit around work, family and bad weather. They also leave room for rest days, which matter as much as the active ones.

## No promises

We are coaches, not doctors. Our routines are general ideas for moving, breathing and resting well, the kind of common sense a good class teaches. We do not promise results, and nothing here replaces advice from your doctor. Our [about page](/blog/about/) says more.

## Tested in class first

Each routine starts in a class. We teach it for a few weeks, watch what people find hard, and only then write it down. We keep the steps short, give an easier version where it helps, and test the timings with a stopwatch.

## Where to start

- New to all of it? Try [a gentle morning routine](/blog/morning-mobility-routine/) or [five small stretches for your desk](/blog/desk-break-stretches/).
- Want to get outside? [Start walking with a four-week plan](/blog/four-week-walking-programme/).
- Need to slow down? Begin with [a two-minute breathing reset](/blog/box-breathing-reset/), or try [our bedtime routine](/blog/wind-down-routine/).
- Want steadier feet? Do the [balance practice](/blog/balance-practice/) by the kettle.

The [World Health Organization's page on physical activity](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity) explains why a little movement each day is worth it.
