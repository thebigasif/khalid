A good evening often starts well before bed. This wind-down routine takes fifteen minutes and tells your body the day is over. Do it at the same time each night, even at weekends.

Tomás wrote it after years of shift work, when a steady bedtime was hard to keep. It needs nothing special: a warm drink, a pen and paper, and a little floor space. Start it about half an hour before you want to be asleep.

## The wind-down routine

1. **Screens off.** Put your phone on charge in another room, or at least out of reach.
2. **Dim the lights** and make a warm, caffeine-free drink.
3. **Write tomorrow's list,** so the plans leave your head and land on paper.
4. **Stretch slowly** for five minutes: a lying twist, knees to chest, and legs up the wall.
5. **Breathe** with six rounds of [two-minute box breathing](/blog/box-breathing-reset/), or simply breathe out longer than you breathe in.
6. **Read** something calm on paper until you feel sleepy.

## Make the room ready

A cool, dark and quiet bedroom helps. Many people find that a window open a little, thicker curtains or earplugs make a real difference.

## Be patient

A new evening habit takes a few weeks to settle. Keep the order the same each night, because the routine itself becomes the signal to slow down. Do not worry about a night that goes badly.

## If your evenings are busy

On late nights, keep just two steps: screens off and the breathing. Even a five-minute version tells your body that bedtime is coming, and it keeps the habit alive until you have more time.

## Where to learn more

The [Sleep Charity's tips](https://thesleepcharity.org.uk/information-support/adults/sleep-hub/) cover more ideas for a calm evening. If poor sleep goes on for weeks, talk to your doctor, since this routine is a general wellbeing idea and not a treatment.
