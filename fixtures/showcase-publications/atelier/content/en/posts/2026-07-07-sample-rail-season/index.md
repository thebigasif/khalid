Every collection starts on the sample rail, a long rail by the studio window. By July it is full, and most of what hangs there will never be made. This is how a season grows on it.

## Spring: the toiles on the sample rail

In March we sew toiles, first versions of each piece in plain calico. A [toile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toile) shows the shape without the distraction of colour or cloth. We try each one on two or three people of different sizes, mark the changes in pen and sew it again.

## Early summer: the cloth

Once a shape works, we make it in the real cloth. This is where many pieces fail. A coat that looked right in calico can feel heavy in melton, or a trouser can lose its line in linen. We keep the samples that still work and send the rest to the back of the rail.

## July: the edit

In July we take everything off the rail and lay it out on the floor. Tomas styles the pieces into looks, and we ask one question of each: would we wear it every week? Pieces that only work in one outfit come out. This year we started with thirty-one samples and kept eight, which became the [Autumn Winter 2026](/blog/category/autumn-winter-2026/) collection.

## Autumn: the making

From August, the workshop makes each piece in a small run. Most runs are forty pieces or fewer. When a run sells out, we make it again only if the cloth is still available.

## What the sample rail teaches us

The sample rail is where we learn to say no. A piece can be beautiful and still not earn its place, because it does one thing only. The pieces that stay are the ones that work with everything else on the rail, such as the [navy crew](/blog/navy-merino-crew/) and the [saffron coat](/blog/saffron-wool-coat/).

## Come and see it

The sample rail is in the studio all year. If you want to see what we are working on, book a fitting and ask to look. Our [about page](/blog/about/) has the address.
