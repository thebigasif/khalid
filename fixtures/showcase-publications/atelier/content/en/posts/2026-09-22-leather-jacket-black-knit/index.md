Every collection needs one jacket you can put on without thinking. This season it is a leather jacket, cut close and finished plain, worn over a black ribbed knit.

## Vegetable tanning

We chose a vegetable-tanned leather from a small tannery. [Vegetable tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_(leather)) uses bark and plant extracts, and it takes weeks instead of days. The leather starts firm and matte. Within a month it moulds to your arms and takes on a soft shine at the elbows.

## The cut

The jacket has a short body, a stand collar and a zip that sits a little off centre. There are no belts, no buckles and no extra zips. We left the details out so the leather can age without anything to catch or break.

## Styling the leather jacket

For this look I kept it close to a uniform: a black ribbed cotton crew, washed black denim and chelsea boots. It is the outfit you could wear five days in a row and no one would mind. For a softer look, wear the jacket over [a navy knit](/blog/navy-merino-crew/) with flannel trousers.

## Care

- Wipe the leather with a damp cloth when it gets dusty.
- Feed it with a plain leather balm twice a year.
- Hang it on a wide hanger, away from the sun.

Our note on [caring for shirts and shoes](/blog/shirt-shoe-care/) covers the same balm, which also works on the boots.

## How a leather jacket ages

A leather jacket tanned this way costs more and takes longer to make. However, it ages instead of wearing out. Scratches fade when you rub them, and the colour deepens with the sun. In ten years your leather jacket will look like nobody else's, which is the point of buying one.

The leather jacket comes in black and dark brown. Each one is cut by hand in the studio, so allow two weeks for yours.
