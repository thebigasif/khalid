The field coat opens our Autumn Winter 2026 collection, and it is the piece we tested longest. We wanted a coat you could wear to the market on a wet Saturday and to dinner the same night, without changing.

## Why waxed cotton

Waxed cotton keeps out light rain and wind, and it softens with every wear. Ours comes from a small mill that still waxes by hand, so each coat has its own sheen. After a year it creases where you bend, which we think is the point. If you want to know more about the cloth, [waxed cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxed_cotton) has a long history on boats and farms.

## How we cut it

The coat falls just below the hip, with a straight back and two deep patch pockets. The collar stands up when you need it and lies flat when you do not. Inside, the lining is a plain cotton twill in a darker slate, so the coat stays light enough for October.

## How to wear the field coat

In this look, Tomas styled the coat open over a cream lambswool crew. The felt hat and the small leather pouch keep the line simple. On colder days, add [a fine navy knit](/blog/navy-merino-crew/) under it, or swap the hat for a knitted one.

- **Fit:** true to size. Size down if you want it close over a shirt.
- **Care:** brush off mud when dry. Re-wax once a year, as in our note on [choosing a winter cloth](/blog/winter-cloth-guide/).
- **Pairs well with:** straight trousers, boots or loafers. Our [care notes](/blog/shirt-shoe-care/) cover the boots.

## Who the field coat is for

We made the field coat for people who walk to work, ride a bike or spend their weekends outside. It is not a technical coat: it will not keep you dry in a storm. It is a coat for ordinary rain, cold mornings and long days, and it looks better the more you wear it. If you want something warmer, our [saffron wool coat](/blog/saffron-wool-coat/) is made for the coldest weeks.

The field coat comes in slate and olive. Both will be in the studio for fittings from the first week of November.
