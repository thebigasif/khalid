A saffron wool coat is the brightest piece in the collection, and the one people stop us about on the street. Grey months need one strong colour, and this is ours.

## The colour of the saffron wool coat

We dyed the wool to a deep saffron that sits between mustard and orange. It looks warm in daylight and glows under street lights. It goes with black, navy, ink and every shade of grey, so it is easier to wear than it looks.

## The back pleat

The detail that makes the coat is at the back. A single box pleat runs from the yoke to the hem, so the coat moves when you walk and closes up when you stand. It also gives room over a thick knit without making the front look wide.

## Styling notes

For this look I kept everything else dark: a black felt hat, an ink cashmere scarf and a tan leather strap across the body. The coat does the talking. You could also wear it over [the black suit](/blog/black-tailoring/) from look 04 for an evening out.

The wool is boiled, which makes it dense and wind-proof without a lining. It is warm enough for most of the winter. If you want to understand how boiling changes wool, the page on [felt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felt) explains the process well.

- **Fit:** relaxed. Take your usual size.
- **Under it:** a thin knit or a shirt. The [leather jacket look](/blog/leather-jacket-black-knit/) shows the same ribbed crew.
- **Care:** air it after wearing and brush it with a soft clothes brush. Dry clean only when it really needs it.

## Why one bright coat

Most of our customers dress in navy, grey and black, and so do we. A saffron wool coat over those colours changes the whole outfit without any effort. It also makes you easy to find on a dark street, which matters more in December than any trend. If you want a quieter coat, the [waxed field coat](/blog/field-coat/) comes in slate and olive.

The saffron wool coat is made in a run of forty. When it is gone, we will not dye this colour again until next autumn.
