Every autumn, customers ask us which winter cloth to choose for a coat or a jacket. The honest answer is that it depends on how you live. Here is how we think about the three wools we use most.

## Tweed

Tweed is a rough, woven wool, often in a check or a fleck of several colours. It is warm, it hides dirt and it lasts for decades. It is the best winter cloth for a jacket you wear outdoors, on walks or on a bike. [Tweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_(cloth)) comes in many weights, and we use a middle weight that does not feel heavy indoors.

## Flannel

Flannel is soft and slightly fuzzy, brushed after weaving. It drapes well, so it is our first choice for trousers and soft tailoring, such as the trousers in our [black tailoring](/blog/black-tailoring/) look. It is less hard-wearing than tweed, so it suits a jacket you wear in town.

## Melton

Melton is felted and dense, with a smooth face and no visible weave. Wind does not pass through it, so it makes the warmest coats. It also keeps a crisp edge, so a melton coat looks sharp for years.

## How to choose a winter cloth

The table puts the three side by side, with waxed cotton for comparison.

| Cloth | Weight | Warmth | Wind | Wear | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tweed | Middle to heavy | Warm | Some gets through | Lasts for decades | Jackets for walks, cycling and the country |
| Flannel | Light to middle | Mild | Gets through | Wears at the knees and elbows | Trousers and soft tailoring for town |
| Melton | Heavy | Very warm | Stops it | Keeps a crisp edge for years | One coat for the coldest days |
| Waxed cotton | Light | Cool alone, warm over a knit | Stops it | Softens and creases with use | Rain coats and field coats |

- **You walk a lot, or live in the country:** choose tweed.
- **You want something soft for the office:** choose flannel.
- **You need one coat for the coldest days:** choose melton.

Our black tailoring look is cut in a dry wool close to flannel, and the [saffron coat](/blog/saffron-wool-coat/) in a boiled wool close to melton. Whatever you choose, look at the cloth in daylight before you decide. Colours change a lot under shop lights. We keep swatches of every cloth in the studio, next to [the sample rail](/blog/sample-rail-season/), and we post them to you if you ask.

For waxed cotton, which we use on the [waxed field coat](/blog/field-coat/), the rules are different. We will write about it in a later note.
