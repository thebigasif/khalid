Shirt and shoe care takes a few minutes a week, and it doubles how long they last. These are the habits we use in the studio and at home.

## Shirt and shoe care starts with shirts

- **Wash less, and cooler.** Most shirts are clean after one wear if you air them overnight. When you do wash them, thirty or forty degrees is enough.
- **Undo every button,** including the cuffs and the collar points, before washing. This saves the buttonholes.
- **Iron while damp.** Start with the collar, then the cuffs, the sleeves and the body.
- **Fold or hang?** Hang shirts you will wear this week. Fold the rest, as in the picture, with tissue at the collar.

## Leather shoes

Good leather shoes can last twenty years. Three habits make the difference:

1. **Use shoe trees.** Cedar trees hold the shape and draw out damp after each wear.
2. **Rest them.** Do not wear the same pair two days in a row, so the leather can dry.
3. **Polish little and often.** A thin layer of cream polish once a month is better than a thick one twice a year.

When the soles wear through, take them to a cobbler. A shoe that is [welted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_welt) can be resoled many times.

## Knitwear

Knits need their own care. Never hang a knit, because the shoulders stretch. Fold it and keep it in a drawer with a cedar block against moths. Our [merino crew](/blog/navy-merino-crew/) has its own wash notes on the label.

## Why it pays

A good shirt costs as much as a dinner out, and a good pair of shoes as much as a week's shopping. With simple shirt and shoe care, both last five to ten times longer than if you ignore them. That is the cheapest way to dress well, and the kindest to the people who make the clothes. See how a piece starts life in our note on [the sample rail](/blog/sample-rail-season/).
