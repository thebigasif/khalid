The navy merino crew is the quietest piece in the collection. It is also the one we reach for most. A plain knit in a good wool goes with almost everything, so we spent our time on the yarn and the fit.

## The yarn

We use an extra fine merino, spun and knitted in the same town. Fine [merino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merino) is soft enough to wear on bare skin and warm without bulk. It also keeps its shape, so the cuffs and the hem stay neat after many washes.

## Three ways to wear it

1. **Over a checked shirt,** as in this look. Let the collar sit outside the neck and the cuffs show a little.
2. **Under the [field coat](/blog/field-coat/),** for a cold walk. The thin knit fits under any sleeve.
3. **On its own,** with wool flannel trousers and suede shoes, for a day in the studio or the office.

## How the navy merino crew fits

The crew is cut close but not tight. The shoulders are set in, not dropped, so it looks tidy under a jacket. The body is long enough to tuck in, and it does not ride up when you lift your arms.

## Care

Wash it by hand in cool water, or on the wool cycle. Dry it flat, away from the radiator. Fold it, never hang it, as we explain in our [care notes](/blog/shirt-shoe-care/).

## Why navy

Navy is softer than black next to the face, and it goes with brown shoes as well as black ones. That is why we chose it for the first colour of the navy merino crew. It also hides wear and keeps its depth after many washes, while black fades to grey.

The navy crew comes in five sizes. We also knit it in oat and in a deep green, and all three stay in the range from season to season.
