Joshua Tree is known all over the world for its climbing. The granite is rough and full of cracks, and some walls rise for many metres. Without a climber in the frame, it is hard to tell how big they are.

## One small climber

The climber here takes up a tiny part of the frame. That is the point. Your eye finds the person, and suddenly the wall looks huge. A picture of the rock alone would only show texture, with no way to judge its size. A bright shirt helps a great deal: blue or red reads clearly against the pale rock, even when the person is very small in the frame.

## Place them off centre

The person on the rope sits to the right of centre, with plenty of rock to the left. The wall seems to go on beyond the frame. Put a person in the middle and the picture becomes a portrait instead of a picture of a place.

## Ask first

If you photograph people on the rock, ask them first, and never call out while someone is on a hard move. Most are happy to be in a picture, and they may even tell you which route they are on and when they will reach the top.

## Light on granite

The granite of Joshua Tree is pale, and midday sun makes it bright. That suits a picture about size and texture: every crack and hollow shows. For warmer colour, come back in the last hour of the day, when the rock turns gold.

## Climb with care

Climbing is a sport with real risks, and every route needs the right gear and training. The park has [climbing rules and guides](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/planyourvisit/climbing.htm) for anyone who wants to try.
