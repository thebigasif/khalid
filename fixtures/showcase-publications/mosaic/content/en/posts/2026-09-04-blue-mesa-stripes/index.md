The hills at Blue Mesa are made of layers of clay, laid down more than two hundred million years ago. Rain has carved them into rounded mounds, and every layer shows as a stripe. Some are blue, some grey, some purple.

## Soft light for the colours of Blue Mesa

Under hard sun the stripes go pale. High cloud softens the light and brings the colour back. This picture was made under a thin layer of cloud, when the blue bands are strongest and the shadows between the mounds are gentle.

## Fill the frame with pattern

The sky here is plain, so it gets only a thin strip at the top. The hills take up the rest. The stripes run across the frame and carry the eye from one side to the other, with no single subject to stop on. A slightly longer lens flattens the mounds, so the stripes of nearer and further hills line up into one band of colour.

## Walk the loop

A paved trail of about a mile drops down among the hills. From the bottom, the mounds rise around you, and the stripes curve with them. Stay on the path: the clay crust breaks easily and takes years to form again, and footprints stay for seasons.

## Petrified wood on the way

Logs of petrified wood lie along the trail, turned to stone and broken into short pieces. Their colours are as strong as the clay. Leave every piece where it lies: taking any of it is against the law, and a full log looks better in a picture than a gap.

## Before you go

Summer days are hot, and there is no shade on the loop, so go early. The park has a page on [Blue Mesa](https://www.nps.gov/pefo/planyourvisit/blue-mesa.htm). For a deeper canyon in the same kind of country, see [sunrise times at the Grand Canyon](/blog/canyon-sunrise-times/). Lines of another kind are in [dune ridgelines in the morning](/blog/dune-ridgelines-morning/).
