Joshua trees do not flower every year. The Joshua tree bloom needs a wet winter and a cold snap, and even then only some trees flower. In a good spring the tips of the branches carry heavy clusters of pale flowers.

## Get close to the Joshua tree bloom

From a distance, the bloom is a pale knob at the end of a branch. Up close, it is dozens of waxy flowers, each tipped with purple. This picture fills the frame with the flowers and lets the sharp leaves frame them.

## Bright shade, not sun

Direct sun makes the waxy petals shine and hides their colour. This cluster was on the shaded side of the tree, lit by the bright sky. The light is soft, and every petal shows. If the whole tree is in sun, stand so your own shadow falls on the flowers.

## A moth that matters

Joshua trees are pollinated by one small moth, the yucca moth. It lays its eggs in the flowers and carries pollen from tree to tree. Neither can live without the other. The moth was not out when this picture was made, but you can read about it on the park's page on [Joshua trees](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/learn/nature/jtrees.htm).

## When to look

A Joshua tree bloom year is usually known by early March. Rangers and local photographers share news of it quickly, so watch the park's updates from February. Higher valleys flower later than lower ones, so a late visit can still find flowers in the hills.

## Leave the flowers

The flowers are food for many animals, so do not pick them or pull a branch down for a better angle. A longer lens lets you work from the ground.
