At dawn on the South Rim, the light comes in low from the east and picks out the tops of the buttes one by one. Under broken cloud, it moves across the canyon in patches. Knowing the sunrise times for your month lets you be in place before it starts.

## Patches of light

A clear sky lights the whole canyon at once. Broken cloud is better: it lights one butte and leaves the next in shadow. Watch the patches move, and make the picture when one falls on the shape you want.

## Layers in the distance

The canyon is deep and wide, so the far rims look paler and bluer. That haze separates each ridge from the one behind it. A longer lens crops in on the layers and makes the most of them.

## Sunrise times by month

This table gives the sunrise times at the South Rim and a good point to watch from in each season. Times are for the middle of each month, in Arizona time, which does not change in summer.

| Month | Sunrise | Good point | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | 7:35 | Yavapai Point | Sun rises far to the south, lighting the buttes from the side |
| March | 6:40 | Mather Point | Short walk from parking, wide view east and west |
| May | 5:15 | Hopi Point | Long view west along the canyon as the light spreads |
| July | 5:20 | Lipan Point | River in view below, light reaches deep side canyons |
| September | 5:55 | Grandview Point | Quiet, with pine trees to frame the view |
| November | 6:50 | Yaki Point | Low sun and cold, clear air after storms |

## Arrive early

Plan to be at your point thirty minutes before the time in the table. The sky starts to glow well before the sun appears, and parking at the busy points fills fast. The park lists [sunrise and sunset points](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/sunrise-sunset.htm) along the rim. The canyon in snow is in [first snow on the Grand Canyon rim](/blog/first-snow-rim/), and a gentler badland is in [the stripes of Blue Mesa](/blog/blue-mesa-stripes/).
