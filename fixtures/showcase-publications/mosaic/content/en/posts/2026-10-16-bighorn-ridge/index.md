Desert bighorn are hard to find and easy to miss. Their coats match the rock, and they stand very still. This bighorn ram climbed onto a boulder, looked around for a minute, and walked away.

## Wait for the bighorn, do not chase

The sheep was first seen far up the slope. Walking toward it would have sent it over the ridge. Instead, the photographer sat by the road with a long lens and waited. After half an hour the ram came down to the boulders by itself, as if the people by the road were part of the rocks.

## The boulder as a stage

The boulder lifts the animal clear of the brush behind it. Its shape is clean against the softer slope. A lower angle would have put the head against the sky, which is also good, but here the Joshua trees behind tell you where this is.

## High sun is fine sometimes

Most guides say to avoid the middle of the day. The light here is hard, but it shows the texture of the horns and the rough granite. In the desert, animals move when they choose, not when the light is soft, so take the picture you are given.

## Give them room

These sheep need water and quiet, and people near a spring can keep them away from it for a whole day. Keep at least the length of a football pitch between you and any sheep, and never block a path to water. If an animal stops eating to watch you, you are too close.

## Where to look

Rocky slopes near springs and washes are the best places, early in the morning and in the hour before dark. Scan the skyline for a shape that does not move like a rock.
