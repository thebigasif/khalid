The first snow of the winter came in the night. By morning the junipers on the rim were white, and the canyon was full of cloud. Then the cloud began to lift, and the red rock showed through, one butte at a time.

## First snow in front, colour behind

Fresh snow makes a clean white edge across the bottom of the picture. The red rock behind it looks warmer next to the white. Put the snow close to the camera and let the canyon sit further back, so the eye moves from the white trees to the dark rock.

![The viewing platform at Mather Point over a cliff edge, half hidden in falling snow and cloud](mather-point.jpg)

## Wait for a gap

A canyon full of cloud shows nothing. Too clear, and the snow looks flat. The best moments come as the cloud breaks. Stand in one place, watch the gaps move, and be ready when a butte appears. Each gap lasts a minute or two before it closes again.

## Dress for it

The South Rim is more than two thousand metres high. A winter morning there can be far below freezing, and the paths along the rim get icy. Wear boots with good grip, carry spare batteries in an inside pocket, and stay well back from the edge.

## When the first snow comes

Snow can fall on the rim from November to April, but the first storm of the winter is the most striking. The trees still hold it, the light is soft, and the canyon below is often clear by noon. Later storms come on top of old snow, which looks grey and trodden by the time the cloud lifts.

The park lists [winter conditions on the South Rim](https://www.nps.gov/grca/planyourvisit/conditions.htm).
