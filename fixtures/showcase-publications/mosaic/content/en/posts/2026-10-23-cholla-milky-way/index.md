The Milky Way stands almost upright over the desert in late summer. Turn the camera on its side and the band of stars runs from the ground to the top of the frame. A single cholla in front gives the picture something to stand on.

## Why the Milky Way needs a tall frame

Most night pictures are wide. This one is tall because the core of the Milky Way rises nearly straight up at this time of year. The cholla sits low in the frame and the stars fill the rest. On this site it shows as one of the tall tiles, and it holds its shape well there.

## Soft light on the cactus

The cholla was lit with a very dim lamp held far to one side for a moment. The spines catch the light and glow, and the rest of the garden stays dark. Never shine a bright light across the garden: other people are out there making pictures too, and a bright beam spoils every frame they take.

## Settings that work

A wide lens, the aperture fully open and an exposure of about twenty seconds are a good start. Longer, and the stars begin to trail. Focus on a bright star first, using the camera's screen at full zoom, then leave the focus alone for the rest of the night.

## Stay on the path

The Cholla Cactus Garden has a short trail, and the spines break off at a touch. Walk only on the path, even in the dark, and carry tweezers in case a segment catches your boot. Go on a night with no moon, between late spring and early autumn, when the brightest part of the band is up after dark.

The park keeps a page on [night skies at Joshua Tree](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/learn/nature/nightskies.htm). For a wider view of the same sky, see [Jupiter over Queen Valley](/blog/jupiter-queen-valley/). The same garden plants grow near [the bighorn on the ridge](/blog/bighorn-ridge/).
