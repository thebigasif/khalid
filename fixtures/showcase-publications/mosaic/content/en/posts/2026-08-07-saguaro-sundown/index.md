The saguaro is the cactus most people picture when they think of the desert. A tall one can be two hundred years old. At sundown, against a burning sky, it becomes a simple black shape.

## Let the shape speak

A silhouette shows only an outline, so the outline has to be good. This cactus has one tall arm bent upward, and nothing behind it to confuse the shape. Walk around until the arms stand clear of the hills and of any other plant. A low camera helps here, because it lifts the cactus higher against the sky and keeps the busy ground out of the frame.

## Expose for the sky

Set the exposure for the bright sky, not the cactus. The plant and the hills go black, and the colour in the clouds stays strong. If the trunk shows any detail, the sky is too bright, so make the picture a little darker.

## The sun in the frame

Here the sun is just touching the hills. Wait until the last moment, when it is a small point and not a blinding disc. A few seconds later it was gone, and the sky turned red for another ten minutes.

## Finding the right saguaro

Look for a cactus that stands alone on a low rise, so you can put it against open sky. Scout in the afternoon, then come back half an hour before sunset with time to set up. Winter sunsets are often the most colourful, with high cloud catching the light.

## West of Tucson

The western part of the park faces the sunset across open desert. Stay on the trails: young cacti grow in the shade of shrubs and are easy to step on. The park has a page on [things to do](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/planyourvisit/things2do.htm). For the opposite end of the day, see [first light on the dunes at sunrise](/blog/first-light-dunes/).
