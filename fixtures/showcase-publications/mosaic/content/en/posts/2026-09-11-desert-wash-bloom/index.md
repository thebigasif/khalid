After a wet winter, the sandy washes of the low desert turn green and then gold and purple with wildflowers. The bloom lasts a few weeks at most. This wash was full of desert poppies and lupines in the middle of April.

## Wildflowers love cloud

Wildflowers look best on an overcast day. Bright sun makes the yellow petals glare and throws hard shadows under every plant. Under cloud, the colours are even and rich, and you can see every flower in the frame.

## Look down, not across

From eye level, a bloom looks thin, with sand between the plants. Pointing the camera down at the ground fills the frame with flowers. A long lens from a little way back does the same thing, and it keeps your feet off the plants. Kneel at the edge of the wash and try a few heights before you settle on one: a small change in height changes how many flowers overlap in the frame.

## Mixed colours

A field of one colour is pleasant. Two colours that mix, here yellow and purple, make a pattern the eye keeps moving over. Look for a patch where both grow together, and leave the edges of the wash out of the frame.

## When the washes bloom

The lowest washes in the south of the park flower first, often in March. The bloom then climbs into higher ground through April and May. A good year follows steady rain from November to February; a dry winter can mean almost no flowers at all.

## Walk lightly

Flowers in a wash grow where water ran, and the seeds for next year are in the sand. Walk on bare ground and leave every flower where it is.
