# Dry Light: art direction

Dry Light is a made-up photo blog of the desert Southwest. Its photographs should look like one careful walker's year in the dry country: dunes, canyons, night skies, plants and animals, in the light of the edges of the day.

## Tone

- **Light first.** Most pictures are made near sunrise or sunset, at night, or under cloud. Hard midday light is used once, for the bighorn, where it shows texture.
- **One subject a frame.** A ridge, a tree, a cactus, an animal, a climber. The mosaic crops every picture to its tile, so each needs one clear subject that survives a crop.
- **No people's faces.** One climber is seen from far below, small against the rock, to show scale.
- **No signs or brands.** A viewing platform shows in one picture, half hidden by snow.

## Palette

Warm sand and red rock against cool shade and night blue:

- sand, ochre, rust and canyon red;
- dusk blue, night indigo and snow white;
- the green and purple of a spring bloom as the one cool accent by day.

These sit well on the sample brand's sand white page and its dusk indigo accent.

## Subject mix

Of the 15 photographs, 13 lead a post and 2 sit inside a post.

- **Dunes (2):** first light on one ridge, the field two hours later.
- **Night sky (3):** a cholla under the Milky Way, Joshua trees under Jupiter, a dune trail under the Milky Way.
- **Canyons and rock (5):** the Grand Canyon at sunrise and in first snow (two pictures), Blue Mesa's stripes, a climber on granite.
- **Desert life (5):** a bighorn, a Joshua tree in bloom, a wash of wildflowers, Joshua trees in snow, a saguaro at sundown.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large tile | about 5:3 at 1440 px | 1600 px wide | Cut by the template to a fixed row height; the subject sits in the middle third. |
| Tall tile | about 4:5 at 1440 px | 1600 px wide | The same row height on a third of the width, so a landscape is cut to its middle. |
| Small tile | 4:3 | 1600 px wide | Three in a row. |
| Half tile | 3:2 | 1600 px wide | Two in a row. |
| Lead picture on a post | its own | 1600 px wide | Never cropped; a tall picture keeps its shape, at most nearly the height of the screen. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn tiles in one ink on a light tint of it. No photographs of people. |

## Phone crop

On a phone the mosaic is a full-width tile at 3:2, then two half tiles at 4:5, again and again. The tall pictures (the cholla, the dune trail, Mather Point) keep their subject in the middle, so the 3:2 crop of a full tile still shows the cactus or the platform.

## Sources and licences

Fourteen photographs are works of the US National Park Service, in the public domain, from Wikimedia Commons. One, Blue Mesa, is under CC0 1.0. Each was fetched with `scripts/demo-photo.py` and made at most 1600 px wide. `photos.lock` records each one, and `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the photo credits page list them, although neither licence asks for credit.
