The Night Ferry film is out, and people keep asking how we made it with almost no money. The honest answer is a borrowed camera, two return tickets and a lot of goodwill from a ferry crew.

## Why a real crossing for the Night Ferry film

[The single](/blog/night-ferry/) is a song about a late crossing, so we wanted the film to be a real one, not a set. The plan was simple: film the four of us on one evening sailing, from leaving the harbour to the lights of the island coming into view, and cut it to the song.

## Two days on the water

We wrote to the ferry company and explained that we wanted to film on deck, out of the way of passengers, with no lights and no crew. To our surprise they said yes, as long as a member of their staff came with us.

On the first day the sea was too rough to stand up, let alone play. We filmed the harbour and the queue of cars, and went home damp. The second day was calm and grey, which is exactly how the song feels. We filmed the whole crossing in one go, then the drive off the boat in the dark.

## What we used

- **Camera:** a friend's small cinema camera with one lens.
- **Sound:** none. We mimed to the record, which is harder on a rolling deck than it looks.
- **Light:** the boat's own lamps and the last of the daylight.
- **Editing:** three evenings on my laptop in the [practice room](/blog/rehearsal-room/).

## What I learned

Film the boring bits. Our favourite shots are the ones nobody planned: a gull sitting on the rail, the café shutters coming down, Tomasz asleep on a bench. Also, check the weather, then check it again. The song is on [our October album](/blog/glass-weather/), and you can read about the boats that inspired it on the page about [ferries in Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonian_MacBrayne).
