Our rehearsal room is a cellar under a railway arch in the east end of Glasgow. We share it with two other bands and a man who repairs accordions. Every Northlight Parade song since 2021 was worked out here.

## What lives in the rehearsal room

- **A drum kit** that never leaves, so Tomasz brings only his cymbals and snare.
- **Two amplifiers,** a small one for Isla's guitar and a big old one for my bass.
- **A second-hand upright piano** that we tune once a year, whether it needs it or not.
- **A lamp** from a charity shop, because the strip lights made everyone grumpy.
- **Rugs on the floor** to soften the echo off the brick.

## How we write

Isla usually brings a song with the words and the chords. We play it through slowly a few times without talking. Then each of us tries something: a different beat, a bass line that goes against the chords, a new key. We record every run on a phone in the middle of the room, and listen back at the end of the night.

Most songs change shape completely. "The Arches" on [our second album](/blog/glass-weather/) started as a slow waltz and ended up as the loudest song on the record.

## Recording here

The room is too small and too damp to record a whole album, so the last one went to [a church hall in the West End](/blog/church-hall-recording/). It still works for demos and a few final parts. We made [our five-song winter record](/blog/harbour-lights/) here, and Tomasz mixed [the ferry single](/blog/night-ferry/) on the desk in the corner. The songs we take on the road start here too, and you can read how they sound after a week of shows in the [island tour diary](/blog/island-tour-diary/). Trains go overhead every ten minutes, so we learned to play between them.

## Splitting the rent

The rent is split three ways, with a rota on the door. If you are a band in Glasgow looking for space, the [musicians' union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicians%27_Union) keeps a list of rooms, and ours sometimes has a free night on Tuesdays.
