The Harbour Lights EP is out today. It has five songs we wrote last winter, when the band was between albums and the days were very short. It is the first record where I arranged strings for the band, so it is a special one for me.

## Harbour Lights EP track list

1. Harbour Lights (4:02)
2. Ceòl na Mara (3:36)
3. Lamplighter (3:51)
4. Slow Tide (4:44)
5. December Road (3:28)

## The sound of the Harbour Lights EP

We recorded the EP in our [practice cellar](/blog/rehearsal-room/) over four weekends. There was no budget for a studio, so we hung duvets on the walls and borrowed a harmonium from a church in Partick. You can hear it wheezing at the start of "Lamplighter". We kept that on purpose.

The strings are a quartet of friends from the conservatoire. I wrote the parts at the kitchen table and changed half of them on the day, once I heard how the room sounded.

"Ceòl na Mara" is the first song we have sung in Gaelic. Isla grew up speaking it on Lewis, and the song is about her grandmother's house by the shore. The title means "music of the sea".

## Why Harbour Lights is short

We wrote eight songs that winter and kept five. The other three were good, but they were summer songs, and this record is about the dark half of the year. Two of them grew into songs on the next album. The third is still in a notebook.

Five songs also fit on one ten-inch record, which costs less to press than a twelve-inch and looks lovely on a shelf. We wanted something people could give as a present in December, and the timing worked: most of the run sold in the weeks before Christmas.

## Formats

- **Ten-inch vinyl:** a run of 500 in a printed sleeve. A few copies are left at our shows.
- **Download and streaming:** everywhere.

## What came next

The EP led straight into the sessions for [the album we made in a church hall](/blog/glass-weather/), and two of its players came back for that record. If you want to hear where the band started, go back to [the record we made in a mill](/blog/northlight-parade/). The EP is also a good way into [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic) song, if you have never heard any.
