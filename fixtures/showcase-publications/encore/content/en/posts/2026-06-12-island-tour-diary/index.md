This island tour diary covers our short run of shows in early June: five gigs in six days, four ferries, one van and no sleep worth the name. It was the most fun we have had as a band.

## Day one: Skye

We opened in a village hall that seats 120, and about 150 people came. The hall committee made soup for everyone. Tomasz played the whole set on a borrowed drum kit, because ours was stuck in traffic at the Skye Bridge.

## Day two: the long crossing

No show. We took the ferry from Uig and wrote most of a new song in the café, which became "Second Sailing" on [the new record](/blog/glass-weather/). A man at the next table asked if we were a choir.

## Days three and four: Harris and Lewis

Two nights at home for me. The show in Stornoway sold out in a week, and my old music teacher stood in the front row with her arms folded until the third song. Then she danced. On Harris we played in a community hall with the doors open to the beach, and the light did not go until after eleven.

## Day five: Barra

The gig on Barra was meant to be in the hall, but the hall double-booked with a wedding. So we played outside on the pier, with the wedding guests as our crowd. Ruth taught the bride's family the steps for the reel.

## Day six: home

Seven hours in the van to Glasgow. Nobody spoke for the first two.

## What the island tour diary taught us

Small rooms are where the songs make sense. We wrote Glass Weather for places like these, and [the shows this November](/blog/autumn-tour/) go back to two of them. If you want to know why the islands keep pulling us back, read about the [Outer Hebrides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Hebrides), or come to a show and ask me.
