Every Northlight Parade record comes out in small vinyl runs, usually between 300 and 800 copies. People ask why we do not press more, so here is the honest answer, with the numbers.

## What small vinyl runs cost

A pressing plant charges a setup fee for each record, then a price per copy. For us the setup is about the same whether we press 300 or 3,000. So small runs cost more per record, often twice as much. The sleeves, inserts and postage come on top.

That sounds like a bad deal. But a band our size sells most of its vinyl at shows and through our own shop. If we pressed 3,000 records, most of them would sit in Isla's hall cupboard for years, and the money for them would sit there too.

## Why small vinyl runs work for us

- **Less risk.** We can pay for a run of 500 from the money of the last tour, without a loan.
- **Less waste.** Unsold records are still plastic. We would rather press again than throw any away.
- **More care.** With a few hundred copies we can afford better sleeves and a printed insert.

## When we press more

We press again when a run sells out and people still ask for it. Our [first album](/blog/northlight-parade/) sold out in a year, and a second pressing came this spring. The [winter EP](/blog/harbour-lights/) is nearly gone, but we will not press it again; it was always meant to be a winter thing.

## Where the records are made

Pressing plants are busy, and the wait is often three or four months, so we order early. Our next record, [due out in the autumn](/blog/glass-weather/), goes to the plant this summer. If you want to know how a record is made, the page on [phonograph records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph_record) explains the whole process, from the lacquer to the press.
