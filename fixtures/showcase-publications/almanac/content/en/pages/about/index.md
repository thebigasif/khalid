The Field Almanac is a library of long, practical guides to growing food at home and on an allotment. Each guide covers one job in full, from reading your soil to storing apples through winter, so you can come back to it in the same month next year and find exactly what to do.

We are four growers who share a corner of Long Acre Allotments, a site of sixty plots on the edge of Ashby Vale. Our soil runs from heavy clay at the bottom of the hill to light, stony loam at the top, so most of what we write has been tried on both.

## Who writes here

- **Hester Quayle** edits the Almanac and writes the monthly jobs list. She has kept plot 14 for twenty-two years.
- **Bram Hollis** writes about soil, compost, green manure and water. He spent twelve years in a soil testing laboratory.
- **Edith Varga** writes about sowing, seedlings, salad and saving seed. She runs the Long Acre seed library.
- **Rowan Pike** writes about harvesting, storing and dealing with pests. Rowan grew for a farm stall for nine years.

## How the Almanac is laid out

Every guide is split into numbered sections, so you can jump to the step you need and find it again later. Guides that depend on the season name the month in their title or their first lines, and the [monthly jobs list](/blog/november-jobs/) links each job to its full guide.

Good places to start are the [sowing calendar](/blog/vegetable-sowing-calendar/), [how to plan a vegetable plot](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/) and [how to make compost](/blog/make-compost/).

## How we check what we publish

Every guide is read by a second writer before it goes live, and the guide names them. When we give a time or a quantity, it comes from our own plots and notebooks, and we say so. We grow in a temperate climate with frosts from late October to late April, so check your own last frost date before you follow a calendar. We do not publish figures we have not measured, and nobody pays to be mentioned.

## How to reach us

Write to hello@example.com and one of us answers within a week, usually from the shed. The photographs in the Almanac come from photographers who share their work under open licences; the [photo credits](/blog/photo-credits/) page lists each one.
