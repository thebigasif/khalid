To sow seeds indoors, fill a tray or module with fine seed compost, sow two or three seeds per cell at a depth of about twice their own size, water from below and keep them warm until they come up. Then move them to the brightest windowsill you have, turn them every day, and pot them on when they have their first true leaves.

March is the month to sow seeds indoors for most tender and slow crops: tomatoes, peppers, celery and early lettuce. A south or west windowsill is enough for all of them, without a greenhouse or grow lights.

::toc

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Use fresh seed compost; garden soil carries weeds and disease.
- Sow shallowly, and cover most seeds with a fine layer of compost or none at all.
- Warmth brings seeds up; light keeps seedlings short and strong.
- Water from below, and never let the compost dry out or stay soggy.
:::

## What you need

- **Seed trays or modules.** Modules (trays split into cells) are easier: each seedling has its own root ball and is never disturbed. Clean yoghurt pots with holes in the base work too.
- **Seed compost.** It is finer and lower in food than potting compost, which suits young roots. Sieve out any lumps.
- **A clear lid or a plastic bag.** It keeps the compost moist until the seeds come up.
- **Labels and a pencil.** Every tray looks the same after a week.

## Which seeds to sow indoors, and when

You do not need to sow seeds indoors for every crop. Root crops such as carrots and parsnips hate being moved and are best sown straight into the soil. Indoor sowing suits crops that need a long, warm season or that slugs eat as seedlings.

| Crop | When to sow indoors | Warmth to germinate | Days to come up | Plant out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes | Early to late March | 18 to 21°C | 6 to 10 | Late May, after the last frost |
| Peppers and chillies | February to mid March | 21 to 25°C | 10 to 20 | Early June, or keep under cover |
| Celery and celeriac | March | 15 to 18°C | 14 to 21 | May |
| Lettuce | February to April | 10 to 18°C | 4 to 10 | April onwards |
| Leeks | March | 13 to 16°C | 10 to 14 | June, at pencil thickness |
| Courgettes and squash | Late April to mid May | 18 to 21°C | 5 to 8 | Late May to early June |
| Sweetcorn | Late April to May | 16 to 21°C | 7 to 10 | Late May to early June |

Every other crop is in [our calendar of sowing dates](/blog/vegetable-sowing-calendar/), month by month, indoors and out.

## How to sow seeds indoors, step by step

1. **Fill the cells.** Overfill each module with compost, strike off the top with a flat edge, and press it down gently so it sits 1 cm below the rim.
2. **Water first.** Stand the tray in a few centimetres of water until the surface darkens, then let it drain. Watering before sowing stops seeds washing to one side.
3. **Sow.** Put two or three seeds on each cell. Large seeds, such as courgettes, go in singly, on their edge, 1.5 cm deep.
4. **Cover lightly.** Most seeds need a covering about twice their own thickness. Fine seeds, such as lettuce and celery, need light to germinate: press them onto the surface and do not cover them.
5. **Label and cover.** Write the crop and date on a label, then put on the lid or slip the tray into a clear bag.
6. **Keep warm.** Most seeds come up faster on a warm windowsill or the top of a fridge. Check every day.

:::figure{src="cress.jpg" alt="Close view of dense cress seedlings on a windowsill, the easiest crop to sow seeds indoors with, their pale stems and small green leaves lit from behind by daylight"}
Seedlings grow towards the light. Turn the tray a half turn every day, or they lean so far that their stems weaken.
:::

## Once the seedlings come up

As soon as you see the first green loops, take off the lid and move the tray to the brightest place you have. This is the step most often missed, and it is the cause of thin, pale, falling seedlings. A seedling that has to stretch for light never fully recovers.

- **Light:** a south or west windowsill, turned daily. A sheet of card covered in kitchen foil behind the tray bounces light back onto the dark side.
- **Warmth:** once they are up, most seedlings prefer it a few degrees cooler, about 15 to 18°C. Move them off a warm sill above a radiator.
- **Water:** from below, when the compost surface starts to lighten. Soggy compost causes [damping off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damping_off), when seedlings collapse at the base.

## Thin and pot on

When the seedlings have their first true leaves (the second pair, which look like the plant's adult leaves), pull out all but the strongest in each cell. Snip them at the base with scissors rather than pulling, so you do not disturb the one you keep.

If you sowed in an open tray, prick the seedlings out into their own pots now. Hold each one by a leaf, never by the stem, which bruises easily, and lift it with a pencil under the roots. Set it a little deeper in its new pot than it was in the tray.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Brush your hand lightly across the tops of the seedlings once or twice a day. The movement makes them grow shorter, sturdier stems, as wind does outdoors.
:::

## Before they go outside

Plants raised indoors have never felt wind, cold or full sun. Before you plant them out, they need a week or two of [hardening off](/blog/harden-off-seedlings/), when you get them used to the outdoors a few hours at a time.
