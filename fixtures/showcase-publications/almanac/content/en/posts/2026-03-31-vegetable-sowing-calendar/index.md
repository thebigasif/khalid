This vegetable sowing calendar tells you what to sow in each month of the year on a temperate plot, where frosts run from late October to late April. Find the month, sow what is listed, and check the crop table for spacing and how long each crop takes to be ready.

Treat the dates as a guide, not a rule. A cold spring can hold everything back by two weeks, and a plot in a frost pocket may be a month behind one on a sunny slope. Your soil tells you more than the date: if it is cold and sticky, wait.

::toc

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Indoors means a warm windowsill. Under cover means a cold frame, cloche or unheated greenhouse. Outdoors means straight into the soil.
- Most sowing happens from March to June. July and August are for the second crops that see you through autumn.
- Sow little and often for salads, radish and beetroot, so you are never swamped.
:::

## The vegetable sowing calendar, month by month

This table lists the main sowings for each month. A crop can appear in more than one month; sow it in whichever suits your plot.

| Month | Indoors | Under cover | Outdoors |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | Onions from seed, chillies | None | None; plan the plot |
| February | Peppers, chillies, early lettuce, onions | Broad beans, early peas | None on most soils |
| March | Tomatoes, celery, celeriac, leeks, lettuce, cabbages | Lettuce, radish, spinach, salad leaves | Broad beans, peas, parsnips on light soil, first early potatoes |
| April | Courgettes and squash late in the month, sweetcorn, basil | Carrots, beetroot, French beans late in the month | Carrots, beetroot, peas, spinach, chard, radish, lettuce, potatoes |
| May | Runner and French beans, courgettes, squash, cucumbers | None needed | Carrots, beetroot, lettuce, radish, swede, kale, sprouting broccoli |
| June | None needed | None needed | French beans, carrots, beetroot, lettuce, chicory, turnips, fennel |
| July | None needed | None needed | Spring cabbage, kale, beetroot, carrots for autumn, salad leaves, chard |
| August | None needed | None needed | Spring cabbage, winter salad, spinach, spring onions, turnips |
| September | None needed | Winter lettuce, winter salad leaves | Green manure, overwintering onion sets |
| October | None needed | Peas for early pods | Broad beans, garlic, green manure |
| November | None needed | None needed | Garlic, broad beans on light soil |
| December | None needed | None needed | None; order seed |

## How to fit the vegetable sowing calendar to your frost dates

This vegetable sowing calendar assumes a last spring frost in late April and a first autumn frost in late October. To adjust it for your plot:

1. Find your average last [frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost) date. Your local allotment society or a long-standing neighbour will know it better than any map.
2. If it is two weeks later than late April, move every outdoor sowing from March to May two weeks later. Indoor sowings stay the same, but plant out later.
3. If your autumn frosts come early, bring the July and August sowings forward by two weeks.

:::figure{src="seeds.jpg" alt="Seeds for a vegetable sowing calendar spread on a terracotta tile: wrinkled pale peas, speckled beans, white beans, flat squash seeds, tiny round brassica seeds and thin carrot seeds"}
Seed size is the best guide to sowing depth. Large peas and beans go 5 cm down; dust-fine seeds sit on the surface.
:::

## Sow little and often

The commonest mistake with a vegetable sowing calendar is to sow a whole packet in one go. Thirty lettuces ready in the same week is twenty-five too many. For quick crops, sow a short row every two to three weeks instead; our guide to [sowing salad little and often](/blog/sow-salad-little-often/) gives the timings.

::::faq
:::question{text="Is it too late to sow vegetables in June?"}
No. June is a good month for French beans, carrots, beetroot, lettuce, chicory, turnips and fennel, all of which crop before the first frost. Sow kale and spring cabbage in July for winter and spring.
:::
:::question{text="Can I sow straight into the soil instead of indoors?"}
Yes, for most crops, if you wait until the soil has warmed. Root crops, peas, beans, salads and spinach all grow well from outdoor sowings. Tomatoes, peppers, celery and squash need a longer, warmer season than most temperate summers give, so they are best started indoors.
:::
:::question{text="How long does vegetable seed keep?"}
Kept cool, dry and dark, most seed keeps for two to four years. Parsnip, onion and leek seed lose vigour after one year, so buy them fresh. Bean, pea, tomato and squash seed often keeps for five years or more.
:::
