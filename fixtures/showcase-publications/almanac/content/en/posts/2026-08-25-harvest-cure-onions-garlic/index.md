To cure onions and garlic, lift them on a dry day once their leaves have yellowed and fallen over, then lay them out in the sun or hang them somewhere airy for two to three weeks, until the necks are dry and the skins rustle. Potatoes need only a few hours on the soil surface to dry, then a week in the dark to set their skins before you store them.

Curing is the step between harvest and storage. It dries the outer skins and seals the necks, so the crop does not rot or sprout in store. If you cure onions and garlic well, onions keep until April and garlic until the next harvest.

::toc

## When to harvest

| Crop | Signs it is ready | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Garlic, planted in autumn | The lower third of the leaves has turned yellow, the top still green | Late June to July |
| Onions from sets or seed | The leaves have yellowed and the necks have flopped over | July to August |
| Shallots | The leaves have yellowed and the clumps have split apart | July |
| Early potatoes | The flowers have opened; dig one plant to check the size | June to July |
| Maincrop potatoes | The leaves and stems have died back | September |

Garlic is easy to leave too late. If you wait until all the leaves are brown, the outer skins have rotted away and the bulbs split open. Lift it when about six green leaves are left.

## Lift the bulbs gently

1. **Stop watering** two to three weeks before harvest, so the skins start to dry.
2. **Lift on a dry morning.** Slide a fork under the bulbs and lever them gently out of the soil. Do not pull them by the leaves, which tears the neck.
3. **Handle them like eggs.** A bruised onion rots in store. Lay the bulbs down; do not drop or throw them.
4. **Brush off loose soil.** Do not wash them.

## How to cure onions and garlic

- **In fine weather:** lay the bulbs on the soil surface or on a wire rack in full sun for two to three weeks, turning them every few days. Raising them on a rack lets air under them.
- **In wet weather:** cure onions under cover. Bring them in at once. Spread them on staging in a greenhouse or shed with the door open, or hang them in bunches from the roof.
- **Garlic:** tie it in bunches of eight to ten by the stems and hang it in an airy, shady place. Strong sun can scorch garlic.

:::figure{src="onions-drying.jpg" alt="Long rows of lifted onions lying on bare brown soil in a wide field, the way farmers cure onions in fine weather, with a line of trees beyond"}
Onions lifted and left in rows on the soil to dry. On a small plot, a wire rack or an old window frame on bricks does the same job.
:::

The bulbs are cured when the necks are completely dry and thin, the outer skins are papery, and the roots are brittle. Trim the roots, and either cut the stems to 2.5 cm or leave them long for plaiting.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Check each onion before you store it. Any with a thick, soft neck, a bruise or a damaged skin will not keep. Use those first, in the kitchen, within a few weeks.
:::

## How to harvest and cure potatoes

1. **Wait for dry weather.** Lift potatoes on a dry day when the soil is not sticky. Earlies grown from [chitted seed potatoes](/blog/chit-plant-seed-potatoes/) are ready first.
2. **Dig from the side.** Push the fork in at least 30 cm from the stem and lever the soil up, to avoid spearing the tubers.
3. **Leave them on the surface for two to three hours** to dry. Not longer: light turns them green, a sign of [solanine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanine).
4. **Set the skins.** Put them in a dark, airy place, such as paper sacks with the tops open, for a week to ten days. Their skins thicken and any small wounds heal.
5. **Sort.** Use any damaged, speared or green potatoes first.

## Storing the cured crop

| Crop | Where to store | How | Keeps until |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onions | Cool, dry and airy, 0 to 5°C | Plaited, in nets or on slatted trays | March to April |
| Shallots | Cool, dry and airy | In nets or trays | April |
| Garlic | Cool, dry and airy | Plaited or bunched | Late spring, often longer |
| Potatoes | Cool, dark and frost-free, 5 to 10°C | Paper or hessian sacks | March |

Check stores once a fortnight and take out anything soft or sprouting. One rotten onion in a net will spoil its neighbours within a week.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Store onions and potatoes in different places, and keep both away from [stored apples](/blog/pick-store-apples/). Onions give off moisture and gases that make potatoes sprout sooner.
:::

## After the harvest

Onion and garlic beds are often clear by early August, which leaves time for a second crop. Sow salad leaves, spinach or [a quick sowing of green manure](/blog/sow-green-manure/), or plant out leeks and winter brassicas. The cleared bed moves on in the rotation next year; our guide to [planning a vegetable plot](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/) shows what follows. And keep a few of your best garlic bulbs back for planting in October; [planting garlic in autumn](/blog/plant-garlic-autumn/) explains why.
