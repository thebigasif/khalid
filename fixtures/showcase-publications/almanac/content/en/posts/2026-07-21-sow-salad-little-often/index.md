To sow salad little and often, sow a short row or a tray of modules every two to three weeks from March to early September, choosing kinds that suit the season. Each sowing is ready just as the last one starts to run to seed, so you pick fresh leaves every week instead of thirty lettuces at once.

This method is called [succession sowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_planting). July is the month most people stop, and it is the month that matters most: sowings made now give salad from September into November, when shop leaves are dearest.

::toc

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Sow a little every two to three weeks, not a whole packet at once.
- In midsummer, sow in the evening, in shade, and keep the seed bed cool.
- Change what you sow with the season: lettuce in spring and summer, hardy leaves in autumn.
- Pick leaves from the outside of each plant, so it keeps growing.
:::

## Why little and often works

A row of lettuce stays at its best for only two to three weeks. After that, it runs to seed: it grows a tall stem, and the leaves turn bitter. A whole packet sown in April means a glut in June and nothing in July.

If you sow salad a little at a time every few weeks, the harvest spreads out. For a household of two, a row 1.5 m long or a tray of twenty-four modules every three weeks is plenty.

## How much to sow, and how often

| Crop | How much per sowing | Sow every | Sowing season | Ready in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lettuce, hearting | 6 to 8 plants | 3 weeks | March to July | 8 to 10 weeks |
| Lettuce, loose leaf | 1.5 m row | 3 weeks | March to early September | 5 to 6 weeks |
| Rocket | 1 m row | 2 to 3 weeks | March to May, August to September | 4 weeks |
| Radish | 1 m row | 2 weeks | March to September | 4 to 6 weeks |
| Spinach | 1 m row | 4 weeks | March to May, August to September | 6 to 8 weeks |
| Spring onions | 1 m row | 3 to 4 weeks | March to July | 8 to 10 weeks |
| Winter salad leaves | A tray of modules | Once or twice | August to September | 6 to 8 weeks |

Write the dates of each sowing in your notebook, beside [the month-by-month sowing dates](/blog/vegetable-sowing-calendar/). When the seedlings of one sowing come up, it is time to sow the next. That is the simplest timer there is.

## How to sow salad in the ground

1. **Rake the soil fine.** Salad seed is small and needs a crumbly surface.
2. **Draw a shallow drill.** Use the edge of a hoe to make a groove 1 cm deep.
3. **Water the drill.** In summer, fill the drill with water and let it soak in before sowing.
4. **Sow thinly.** Aim for a seed every 2 to 3 cm. Pinch the seed from your palm rather than shaking the packet.
5. **Cover lightly.** Pull dry soil over the drill and press it down with the back of the rake.
6. **Thin.** When the seedlings have two true leaves, thin them to 10 cm apart for leaves, or 25 cm for hearting lettuce. Eat the thinnings.

## Keep summer sowings cool

Lettuce seed has a strange habit: it will not come up when the soil is warmer than about 25°C. In July and August, a sunny bed can be far hotter than that. Many people who sow salad in July fail for this reason alone.

- **Sow in the evening,** so the seed takes up water in the cool of the night.
- **Water the drill with cold water** before sowing.
- **Shade the row** with a plank or a sheet of card for two days, then remove it as soon as the seedlings show.
- **Or sow in modules** in a cool, shady spot, such as the north side of a shed, and plant out at three weeks old.

:::callout{kind=tip}
In the hottest weeks, sow your salad in the shade of taller crops, such as between rows of beans or sweetcorn. The leaves stay sweet for longer there, and they are slower to run to seed.
:::

## Change crops with the season

What you sow should change as the year goes on. Spring and early summer sowings are mostly lettuce, radish and rocket. From late July, switch to crops that cope with short, cool days.

- **Late July to mid August:** loose-leaf lettuce, rocket, spinach, chard, endive and chicory.
- **Mid August to early September:** winter lettuce, lamb's lettuce, land cress, mizuna, mustard leaves and winter purslane.
- **September:** sow in a cold frame or under a cloche for leaves through winter.

## Pick to keep plants cropping

Pick the outer leaves of loose-leaf lettuce, spinach and chard, two or three from each plant, and leave the centre to grow on. A plant picked this way crops for six to eight weeks instead of once. Cut rocket and mustard leaves with scissors 3 cm above the soil, and they will grow again two or three times.

## Keep the pests off

Young salad is the favourite food of slugs, and flea beetles riddle rocket and radish leaves with tiny holes in dry weather. A fine mesh over hoops keeps out flea beetles. For slugs, our guide to [protecting seedlings from slugs and snails](/blog/slugs-without-pellets/) sets out what works. And keep the soil moist; [watering in a dry summer](/blog/water-vegetable-garden/) explains why salad leaves need water more than any other crop.
