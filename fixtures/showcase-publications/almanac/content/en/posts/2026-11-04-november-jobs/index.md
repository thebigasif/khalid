The November jobs on a vegetable plot are about protecting what is still growing and getting the soil ready for spring. Plant garlic and broad beans, harvest leeks, parsnips and winter cabbages, collect fallen leaves for leaf mould, cover bare soil, and check your stored crops every week.

The first hard frosts usually arrive on our plots in November, and the days are short. The November jobs are quiet ones, but the work you do now sets up the whole of next year.

## Harvest

Many crops are at their best now, and some taste sweeter after a frost.

- **Leeks:** lift as you need them. Loosen the soil with a fork first, so they do not snap.
- **Parsnips:** leave them in the ground and dig as needed. Frost turns some of their starch to sugar.
- **Kale, cabbages and sprouts:** pick from the bottom of the stem upwards. Pick off yellow leaves, which harbour disease.
- **Celeriac, swede and late carrots:** lift before hard frost, or cover the row with straw so you can dig them in cold weather.
- **Winter salad:** pick a few leaves from each plant, little and often.

:::figure{src="beetroot.jpg" alt="Freshly lifted beetroot with dark red and soil-brown skins, lying in a shallow wicker basket on weathered wooden boards; lifting the last roots is one of the November jobs"}
Lift the last beetroot before hard frost and store it in a box of damp sand or compost in a shed. Twist off the leaves rather than cutting them, so the roots do not bleed.
:::

## Look after the soil

Bare soil over winter loses its structure and its food in the rain. Cover every empty bed before the end of the month.

1. **Spread compost** 5 cm deep on beds that will hold hungry crops next year, such as potatoes and squash. Our guide to [making compost](/blog/make-compost/) shows how to make your own.
2. **Cover other bare beds** with a sheet of card, a mulch of leaves or a weed-suppressing sheet, weighed down.
3. **Leave the [green manure sown](/blog/sow-green-manure/)** in September and October to grow.
4. **Stay off wet soil.** Lay boards to walk on if you must cross the beds.

## Collect leaves for leaf mould

Fallen leaves make [leaf mould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_mold), a dark, crumbly mulch and the best seed compost there is. It is free, and November is when the leaves come down.

- Rake leaves after rain, when they are damp, and fill old compost sacks or a simple cage of chicken wire.
- Poke a few holes in sacks, tie them, and stack them behind the shed.
- Leave them for one year for a coarse mulch, or two years for fine leaf mould you can sow seeds into.

:::callout{kind=note}
Leaves from oak, beech and hornbeam rot fastest. Leaves from horse chestnut, sycamore and plane are tougher; shred them with a lawn mower first, or mix them with other leaves.
:::

## Protect winter crops

- **Net brassicas** against pigeons, which strip kale and cabbages bare in a hard winter.
- **Stake tall plants** such as Brussels sprouts, which blow over in winter gales.
- **Cover tender crops** with fleece or cloches on frosty nights.
- **Clear away rotting leaves** around plants; they shelter slugs.

## Check your stores

Go through stored crops every week. Take out anything soft, mouldy or sprouting before it spreads to its neighbours.

| Crop | What to look for | Full guide |
|---|---|---|
| Apples | Soft brown patches, wrinkling | [Pick and store apples](/blog/pick-store-apples/) |
| Onions and garlic | Soft necks, sprouting, mould | [Harvest and cure onions](/blog/harvest-cure-onions-garlic/) |
| Potatoes | Soft spots, green skin, sprouts | [Grow and lift potatoes](/blog/chit-plant-seed-potatoes/) |
| Squash | Soft spots near the stalk | None yet |
| Saved seed | Damp or mould in the envelopes | [Save seed](/blog/save-seed-beans-tomatoes/) |

## Clean and plan

Save the last of the November jobs for wet days: clean and oil your tools, wash seed trays and pots for spring, and empty and clean water butts if they are full of leaves. Then sit down with this year's notes. What grew well, what failed, and what did you have too much of? Next January, those notes become your [plan for the plot](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/).
