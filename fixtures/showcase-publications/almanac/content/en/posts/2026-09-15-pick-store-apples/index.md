To store apples through winter, pick only sound, late-ripening apples when they come away from the tree with a gentle twist, then wrap each one in paper and lay them in a single layer in shallow trays somewhere cool, dark, slightly damp and safe from frost. Check them every week or two and remove any that start to rot.

Early apples, ripe in August, do not keep; eat them within a week or two. The apples that store are the late kinds, picked from late September to October, and many of them taste better after a month or two in store than on the day they are picked.

::toc

## When an apple is ready to pick

Each kind of apple ripens at its own time, so watch the tree, not the calendar.

1. **Windfalls.** When a few sound apples drop on their own, the rest are close.
2. **Seed colour.** Cut one apple open. Brown pips mean it is ready; white or pale pips mean wait.
3. **The twist test.** Cup an apple in your palm, lift it slightly and give it a gentle twist. A ripe apple comes away with its stalk. If you have to pull, leave it another week.

:::figure{src="tree.jpg" alt="An old apple tree heavy with small red apples, too many to store apples from every branch, with windfalls scattered across the sunny grass beneath it"}
Windfalls are the first sign that a tree is nearly ready. Use them in the kitchen at once; bruised apples never keep.
:::

## Early, mid and late apples

| Group | Picking time | Keeps for | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early | August to early September | 1 to 2 weeks | Eat fresh from the tree |
| Mid season | Mid September to early October | 4 to 8 weeks | Eat in autumn |
| Late | Early to late October | 3 to 5 months | Store for winter; many improve in store |
| Cooking apples | September to October | 2 to 4 months | Cook and freeze, or store whole |

If you do not know which kind your tree is, a short spell in store tells you. Apples that turn soft and dull within a month are early or mid season kinds; eat them first.

## Pick with care

A bruise you cannot see on the day becomes a soft brown patch within weeks, and it spreads rot to the apples beside it. If you want to store apples, treat every one like an egg.

- Pick on a dry day, by hand, with the stalk still attached.
- Lay the apples in a basket or a bucket lined with a cloth. Never drop them in.
- Keep windfalls, bruised and damaged apples apart, and use them first, for juice, sauce or crumble.
- Leave apples to cool overnight in a shed before you store them.

## How to store apples

1. **Choose the place.** Cool, dark, airy and slightly damp, between 3 and 7°C, and safe from frost and mice. A garage, cellar, shed or unheated room all work. A warm kitchen does not.
2. **Wrap each apple.** Use a square of newspaper or tissue. The paper stops rot spreading from one apple to the next and keeps them from drying out.
3. **Lay them in one layer.** Use slatted wooden trays, fruit boxes or shallow cardboard boxes. Stalks up, not touching.
4. **Keep each kind apart.** Different kinds keep for different times. Label each tray with the kind and the date.
5. **Keep them away from other produce.** Apples give off a gas called [ethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene), which makes potatoes sprout and carrots taste bitter.

:::callout{kind=tip}
No trays? Put five or six wrapped apples in a clear plastic food bag, poke a few holes in it with a pencil, fold the top over loosely, and store the bags in a cardboard box. The bag keeps them from shrivelling.
:::

## Check them every week or two

Go through the trays every week or two. Take out any apple with a soft or brown patch, and check the ones around it. Eat the apples that are starting to wrinkle first. Expect to lose a few: in our shed, about one apple in twenty goes over by February, almost always one that was bruised at picking.

## When you cannot store apples whole

If you have more apples than you can store, there are other ways to keep them.

| Method | How | Keeps for |
|---|---|---|
| Freeze slices | Peel, slice, dip in lemon water and freeze on a tray, then bag | 12 months |
| Freeze as sauce | Cook with a little water, cool and freeze in tubs | 12 months |
| Dry in rings | Slice thinly and dry in a low oven for several hours | 6 months in a jar |
| Juice | Press, then freeze in bottles with space at the top | 12 months |
