To grow climbing beans, build a frame of 2.4 m canes before you plant, sow or plant one bean at the foot of each cane in late May, and guide the young shoots onto the canes. Water them well once the flowers open, and pick the pods every two or three days from July, so the plants keep making more until the first frost.

Climbing beans, both runner and French, give more food from a small patch of ground than almost any other crop. A double row 3 m long feeds a household all summer, with plenty left over to freeze.

::toc

## Runner beans and French beans compared

Both climb, both crop from July and both are grown the same way. The differences are in the taste, the look of the pod and how they cope with heat.

| | Runner beans | Climbing French beans |
|---|---|---|
| Pods | Long, flat and rough, best sliced | Round or flat, smooth, cooked whole |
| Flowers | Red, white or two-coloured; bees love them | Small, white or lilac |
| Heat | Flowers drop in hot, dry spells | Cope better with heat |
| Cold | A little hardier | Need warmer soil to start |
| Height | 2.5 m or more | 1.8 to 2.4 m |
| Harvest | July to October | July to September |

On our plot, we grow both. Runner beans crop more heavily in a cool summer; French beans carry on when a hot spell makes runners drop their flowers.

## Build a frame for climbing beans first

A full-grown row of beans is heavy and catches the wind like a sail. Build the frame before you plant, and build it strong.

1. **Double row:** push two rows of 2.4 m canes into the soil, 60 cm apart, with 20 cm between canes in each row. Lean each pair together and tie them where they cross, about 30 cm from the top.
2. **Ridge pole:** lay a cane along the crosses and tie it to each pair. This locks the frame together.
3. **Wigwam:** for a small bed, push six to eight canes in a circle 1 m across, and tie them together at the top.
4. **Push each cane in 30 cm deep.** Shallow canes blow over in late summer gales, beans and all.

Put the frame at the north end of a bed, or on the north side of the plot, so it does not shade your other crops. Our guide to [planning a vegetable plot](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/) shows where beans sit in the rotation.

## Prepare the soil

Climbing beans have deep roots and need moist soil. The old way, which still works, is a bean trench: in winter, dig a trench along the row a spade deep, fill it with kitchen scraps and spent crops through the spring, and cover it with soil a month before planting. If you have no trench, dig plenty of compost into the row.

## Sow and plant

Beans are tender: frost kills them, and their seeds rot in cold, wet soil. Sow when the soil has warmed, from mid May.

- **In pots:** sow one bean 5 cm deep in each 9 cm pot in early to mid May, indoors. Plant out in late May after [hardening off](/blog/harden-off-seedlings/).
- **Outdoors:** sow two beans 5 cm deep at the foot of each cane in late May or early June. Remove the weaker one if both come up.
- **Gaps:** sow a few extra in pots so you can fill gaps left by slugs.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Young shoots sometimes wander off sideways. Twist each one gently round its cane, anticlockwise when seen from above; beans climb that way and will unwind if you go the other.
:::

## Water and pinch out the tops

Once the plants are growing well, they need little until they flower. From the first flowers, water twice a week in dry weather, a full can for every metre of row, and mulch the soil. Dry soil at flowering is the main reason pods fail to set.

When the plants reach the tops of the canes, pinch out the growing tips. This stops them making a tangle above the frame and makes them branch lower down, where you can reach the pods.

## Pick often

Pick runner beans when they are 15 to 20 cm long and still snap cleanly, before the beans inside make bumps on the pod. Pick French beans when they are pencil-thick. Pick climbing beans every two or three days, even if you have to give some away. If pods are left to ripen, the plant stops flowering.

A healthy row crops from late July until the first frost in October. In the last weeks, leave a few of the best pods to dry on the plant; that is your seed for next year. Edith shows how in her guide to [saving seed from beans, peas and tomatoes](/blog/save-seed-beans-tomatoes/).

## At the end of the season

Cut the plants off at ground level and put the stems on the [compost heap](/blog/make-compost/), but leave the roots in the soil. Beans, like all [legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume), carry small nodules on their roots that hold nitrogen, which feeds the brassicas that follow them in the rotation.
