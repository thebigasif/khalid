To plant garlic in autumn, split a bulb into single cloves, push each clove into well-drained soil with its pointed end up and its tip 2.5 cm below the surface, 15 cm apart in rows 30 cm apart. Plant from October to December, and the cloves will root over winter and grow into full bulbs ready to lift in July.

[Garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic) needs a cold spell to split into cloves. Planted in spring, it often grows a single round bulb instead. That is why autumn is the time to plant garlic, and why garlic is one of the few crops you plant as the rest of the plot is put to bed.

::toc

## Choose your garlic

Buy garlic for planting from a garden supplier, not the supermarket. Shop garlic may be a kind suited to a warmer climate, and it can carry virus or a disease called white rot, which stays in the soil for years.

| Type | Bulb | Flower stalk | Keeps | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Softneck | Many small cloves in layers | No | 6 to 9 months | Long storage and plaiting |
| Hardneck | Fewer, larger cloves around a stiff stem | Yes, a curly stalk called a scape | 4 to 6 months | Flavour; cold winters |
| Elephant garlic | Very large, mild cloves | Yes | 6 to 8 months | Roasting; a leek relative, not true garlic |

Hardneck kinds grow a curly flower stalk in early summer. Cut it off when it curls, so the plant puts its energy into the bulb, and cook it like a spring onion.

## Prepare the soil

Plant garlic only where the soil drains, because it rots in wet ground. It needs a sunny bed with soil that drains well, in the roots and onions bed of [a four-bed vegetable plot plan](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/).

- **On heavy clay,** plant into a raised bed or a ridge of soil 10 cm high, or set each clove on a little sharp sand in its hole.
- **On light soil,** dig in a bucket of compost per square metre a month before planting.
- **Avoid fresh manure,** which makes garlic soft and prone to rot.

:::figure{src="spade-fork.jpg" alt="A garden fork with a worn wooden handle and a spade with a grey grip leaning together against a brown wooden fence, above a strip of freshly dug soil, the tools to loosen a bed before you plant garlic"}
A fork loosens wet soil without smearing it the way a spade does on heavy clay. Loosen the planting row to a fork's depth and leave the surface rough.
:::

## How to plant garlic

1. **Split the bulb** into cloves just before planting. Keep the papery skin on each clove.
2. **Choose the biggest cloves.** Large cloves grow large bulbs. Cook the small ones from the middle.
3. **Make a hole** with a dibber or trowel, 15 cm from the last.
4. **Push in each clove** with the flat base down and the point up, so the tip sits 2.5 cm below the surface.
5. **Firm the soil** gently over the top and label the row.

| Spacing | In the row | Between rows | Depth of the tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garlic | 15 cm | 30 cm | 2.5 cm below the surface |
| Elephant garlic | 20 cm | 30 cm | 5 cm below the surface |
| Onion sets | 10 cm | 30 cm | Tip just showing |

:::callout{kind=tip}
Birds pull newly planted cloves and sets out of the ground to see if they are food. Lay a sheet of fleece or netting over the row for the first three weeks, until the roots hold them in.
:::

## Overwintering onion sets

Some onions are bred to be planted in autumn. Planted in September or October, they root before winter and give bulbs in June, a month or two before spring-planted onions. They do not store as long, but they fill the gap when last year's onions run out.

Plant them as you would garlic, but shallower, with the tip just showing above the soil and 10 cm apart. Check them after frost or heavy rain, and push back any that have lifted.

## Winter and spring care

1. **Weed carefully.** Garlic and onions have narrow leaves and cannot compete with weeds. Hand weed or hoe gently.
2. **Feed in late winter.** In February or March, scatter a little general fertiliser or spread a thin layer of compost along the rows.
3. **Water in dry springs,** until mid June. Then stop, so the bulbs ripen and keep well.
4. **Watch for rust.** Orange spots on the leaves in summer are a fungus called rust. It rarely harms the bulbs; lift a little early if it is bad.

## Harvest in summer

Lift garlic in late June or July, when the lower third of the leaves has turned yellow, and overwintering onions when their leaves have flopped over in June. Our guide to [harvesting and curing onions, garlic and potatoes](/blog/harvest-cure-onions-garlic/) shows how to dry them so they keep. Keep the best bulbs back, and you have your seed garlic for next autumn.
