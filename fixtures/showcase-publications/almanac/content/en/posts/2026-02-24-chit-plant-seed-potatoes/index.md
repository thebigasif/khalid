To chit seed potatoes, stand them in egg boxes with the end that has the most eyes facing up, and keep them somewhere cool and bright for four to six weeks until each has short, dark, sturdy shoots. Then plant them 10 to 15 cm deep from mid-March for first earlies to late April for maincrops, and pull soil up around the shoots as they grow.

Chitting is not essential, but it gives early potatoes a head start of two to three weeks. On a cold plot, that is the difference between new potatoes in June and new potatoes in July.

::toc

## Choose your seed potatoes

[Seed potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_potato) are small tubers grown and certified free of virus for planting. Buy them, rather than planting potatoes from the shop, which may carry disease and are sometimes treated to stop them sprouting. Potatoes come in three groups, sorted by how long they take to grow.

| Type | When to plant | Spacing in the row | Space between rows | Ready to lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First earlies | Mid to late March | 30 cm | 45 cm | June and July, about 10 weeks after planting |
| Second earlies | Early to mid April | 38 cm | 60 cm | July and August, about 14 weeks |
| Maincrop | Mid to late April | 38 cm | 75 cm | September, about 20 weeks |

For a small plot, grow first earlies. They take up the least room for the shortest time, they are lifted before blight usually arrives, and new potatoes taste far better fresh than any you can buy.

## How to chit seed potatoes

1. **Find the rose end.** Each seed potato has one end with more eyes clustered together. That is the rose end, and it goes up.
2. **Set them in egg boxes.** Stand each potato upright in one cup of an egg box or a seed tray.
3. **Keep them cool and bright.** A frost-free shed, porch or unheated room with good light is ideal, at about 10°C.
4. **Wait four to six weeks.** The shoots should be 1.5 to 2.5 cm long, dark green or purple, and firm.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Pale, long, thin shoots mean the potatoes are too warm or too dark. Move them somewhere cooler and lighter. Handle chitted potatoes gently: a broken shoot sets the plant back by a week or more.
:::

## When the soil is ready

Plant when the soil has warmed to about 7°C and is no longer sticky. A soil thermometer is cheap and settles the question. If you have no thermometer, wait until the weeds are starting to grow. On our clay at the bottom of Long Acre, that is usually the first week of April, even for earlies; on the sandy loam at the top, it is the third week of March.

## Planting out, step by step

1. **Prepare the bed.** Potatoes are hungry. Spread a thick layer of compost or rotted manure over the potato bed in [your vegetable plot plan](/blog/plan-vegetable-plot/) a month before planting.
2. **Dig a trench or holes.** Make a trench 10 to 15 cm deep, or use a trowel to dig a hole for each potato.
3. **Place each seed potato rose end up.** Set them at the spacing in the table, taking care not to snap the shoots.
4. **Cover with soil.** Fill in gently and rake the surface level, leaving a slight ridge over the row so you can find it.

## Earth up as the shoots grow

When the shoots are about 20 cm tall, draw soil up around them with a hoe or rake, leaving only the top few leaves showing. Repeat every two or three weeks until the ridge is about 30 cm high or the leaves meet across the rows.

Earthing up does three jobs. It protects young shoots from late frost, it gives the plant more depth to form tubers, and it keeps light off the potatoes, which would otherwise turn them green.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If frost is forecast after the shoots are up and you have earthed up as far as you can, cover the row with a double layer of horticultural fleece overnight. Frosted leaves turn black, and the plant takes two weeks to recover.
:::

## Water and watch for blight

Earlies need water when the flowers open, because that is when the tubers swell. Give each plant a full can once a week in dry weather, at the base. Maincrops need the same through July and August.

Potato blight is a disease that spreads in warm, wet weather, usually from July. It shows as brown patches on leaves with a white edge underneath. If you see it, cut all the leaves and stems to the ground and remove them, then wait two weeks before lifting the tubers so their skins set.

## Lift and store

Lift earlies when the flowers open or soon after: scrape back a little soil and check the size. Lift maincrops when the leaves die back in September, on a dry day.
