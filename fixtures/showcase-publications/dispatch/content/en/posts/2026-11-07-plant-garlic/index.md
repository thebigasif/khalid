Good morning. This week is the last good week to plant garlic before the ground in your pots turns cold and wet. Garlic needs a few weeks of cold to split into cloves, so a clove planted now gives you a fat bulb next summer, and one planted in spring often gives you a single round lump.

## Why plant garlic in November

Garlic grows roots all winter and leaves in spring. The cold tells each clove to divide. A balcony is often a little warmer than open ground, which helps the roots, but it still gets the cold the bulbs need. Last year I planted on the second Saturday of November and lifted twelve bulbs in early July, as I wrote in the [summer harvest count](/blog/balcony-vegetables/).

## Which garlic to buy

Buy seed garlic from a garden centre or a seed company, not the bulbs from the shop. Shop garlic often comes from a warmer country and may carry a virus. Look for a hardneck kind if you like large cloves and a flower stalk you can eat in June, or a softneck kind if you want bulbs that keep until spring.

## How to plant garlic in a box

1. Use a box at least 20 cm deep, with holes in the bottom.
2. Fill it with peat-free compost, a handful of grit for drainage and a little [leaf mould](/blog/leaf-mould/) if you have some.
3. Split the bulb into cloves on the day you plant. Keep the largest ones.
4. Push each clove in, flat end down, so its tip sits 2 cm under the surface.
5. Leave 10 cm between cloves. A 60 cm box takes twelve in two rows.

Water once, then leave it. Keep the best bulb next summer to plant again, as with [saving tomato seed](/blog/saving-tomato-seed/). Rain does the rest until April. If birds pull the cloves up, lay a piece of netting over the box for the first month.

## What comes next

In spring, give the box a liquid feed every two weeks until the leaves start to yellow. When half the leaves are brown, lift the bulbs and dry them for two weeks out of the rain. The RHS has a clear [guide to growing garlic](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/garlic/grow-your-own) if you want more detail.
