Good morning, and welcome to the new readers who joined this week. Each Saturday this letter brings one job for the weekend and the reasons behind it. This week the job is for the bees: a pot of late flowers to feed them into October.

## Why bees need late flowers

Bumblebee queens are feeding up now for their winter sleep, and honeybees are storing the last food for the hive. In towns most gardens are over by September. A few pots of late flowers for bees on a balcony can be the only food in the street.

## Late flowers that bees love

- **Michaelmas daisies** (asters) are covered in bees until October.
- **Sedum**, the tall kind with flat pink heads, flowers into autumn and needs almost no water.
- **Lavender** cut back in August often flowers again now.
- **Ivy** in flower is the most important late food of all. If you have some on a wall, let it flower.
- **Cosmos and verbena** keep going until the first frost if you pick off the dead heads.

Single flowers, with an open centre, are better than double ones, which are packed with petals and hide their pollen.

## Fitting late flowers into pots

You do not need a border. An old wheelbarrow, a crate or one large pot is enough. Mix one tall plant, such as verbena, with a low one, such as sedum, and water well for the first month. Plant now and the same pot flowers again next year. Top the pot with [leaf mould](/blog/leaf-mould/) once the leaves fall, to keep the roots warm in winter.

## Keeping late flowers going

Late flowers last longer when you pick off each flower as it fades, which gardeners call deadheading. A plant that has set seed thinks its job is done and stops. Cut cosmos and verbena just above a pair of leaves, and new buds follow within two weeks. Water in the morning, not the evening, so the leaves dry before the cold nights and mildew does not set in. Feed once a fortnight with a liquid tomato feed until the end of September, then stop, so the plants slow down before frost. On a windy balcony, push a cane in beside the tallest stems.

## What else helps

Leave a saucer of water with a few stones in it, so bees can drink without drowning. Leave seed heads standing in autumn. The Bumblebee Conservation Trust keeps a fuller [list of bee-friendly plants](https://www.bumblebeeconservation.org/gardening-for-bees/), and next month we plant [bulbs in pots](/blog/layered-bulbs/) for the first bees of spring.
