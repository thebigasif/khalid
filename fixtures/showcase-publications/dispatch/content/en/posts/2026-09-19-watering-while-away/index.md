This month's letters all asked the same thing in different words: how do I keep my pots alive while I am away? Watering while away is easier than it sounds. Here are the four answers I give, from the cheapest to the most work. Each one has saved a balcony of mine.

## Before you go

Whatever method of watering while away you choose, do these first. Water everything deeply the evening before you leave. Pick every ripe fruit and every open flower, so the plants are not pushing to ripen more. Move pots out of full sun, into the shade of a wall, and group them together: a crowd of pots keeps its own damp air.

## Four ways of watering while away

1. **The wet towel bath.** For a few days away, stand small pots on old towels in a bath or a deep tray with 3 cm of water. The towels wick water up into the compost.
2. **Bottles.** Fill a plastic bottle, pierce the cap with two small holes, and push it upside down into the compost. A 1 litre bottle lasts a medium pot about four days.
3. **Wicks.** Run a strip of cotton rope from a bucket of water into each pot, buried 5 cm deep. Set the bucket higher than the pots.
4. **A drip kit.** For two weeks or more, a drip kit on a tap timer is worth the money. Mine cost about £30 in 2022 and has run every summer since.

## Ask a neighbour

The best answer is still a neighbour with a key and a watering can. Leave a note of which pots need the most: tomatoes and beans first, herbs last. Pay them in produce. Mine got a bag of [balcony vegetables](/blog/balcony-vegetables/) and a jar of chutney.

## Test your watering while away

Try your method for a weekend before you trust it with a fortnight. Bottles often drain too fast, and wicks too slowly. The Royal Horticultural Society has more on [watering pots and containers](https://www.rhs.org.uk/garden-jobs/watering) if you want it.
