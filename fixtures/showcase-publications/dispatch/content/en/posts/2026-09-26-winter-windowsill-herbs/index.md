Hello. The supermarket basil on your windowsill is probably dying. That is not your fault: it was sown thick and grown fast under lights, and it was never meant to last. Windowsill herbs can last the winter, though, if you pick the right ones and give them what a dark kitchen lacks.

## The windowsill herbs that last

- **Parsley** is the toughest. Sow it now in a deep pot and it crops until spring.
- **Chives** die back in winter, but a clump dug from the garden and potted up in October sprouts again on a cool sill.
- **Mint** grows anywhere, as long as its pot never dries out.
- **Thyme and rosemary** want the brightest, coolest sill you have and very little water.
- **Basil** is the hardest. It needs warmth and light, so keep it for a sunny sill above a radiator, or under a lamp.

## Light is the problem

From November to January a north or east window gives a herb a fraction of the light it had in summer. The plants grow thin and pale and lean towards the glass. Turn the pots a quarter turn every few days, keep the glass clean and move them to the brightest window in the house.

If you love basil, a small grow lamp is worth it. Mine, in the photo above, holds three pots and runs for 14 hours a day on a timer. It costs about as much in power as a kettle boiled once a day.

## Water less than you think

Indoor herbs die of too much water far more often than too little. Water when the top of the compost is dry to your fingertip, and tip out any water left in the saucer after half an hour. Yellow lower leaves are the first sign, as I wrote in [your questions on yellow leaves](/blog/slugs-yellow-leaves-shady-wall/).

## Picking

Pick from the top, just above a pair of leaves, so the plant branches. Never take more than a third at once. For a steady supply, sow a new pot of parsley every two months. The [Garden Organic](https://www.gardenorganic.org.uk/) site has more on growing herbs without peat.
