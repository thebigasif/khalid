Good morning. In March I promised to weigh everything my balcony grew this year, and I did, on the kitchen scales, every time I picked. My balcony is 4 square metres and faces south-west, three floors up. These are the honest numbers for balcony vegetables, the good and the poor.

## The balcony vegetables, crop by crop

| Crop | Pots | Sown or planted | First pick | Weight | Seed and plants | Worth it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cherry tomatoes | 3 | April, from seed | 14 July | 6.8 kg | £2.50 | Yes, every year |
| Climbing beans | 2 | May, from seed | 22 July | 3.1 kg | £2.00 | Yes |
| Garlic | 1 box | November, cloves | 3 July | 12 bulbs | £4.00 | Yes |
| Salad leaves | 2 boxes | March to August | 20 April | 2.4 kg | £3.00 | Yes, the best value |
| Peppers and chillies | 2 | February, from seed | 9 August | 0.6 kg | £1.50 | For the fun of it |
| Strawberries | 1 tower | Plants, last year | 1 June | 1.2 kg | £0.00 | Yes |
| Courgette | 1 | May, a plant | 30 June | 2.9 kg | £3.50 | No, too big |
| Potatoes | 2 bags | March, seed potatoes | 18 June | 3.4 kg | £5.00 | Once was enough |

In 2026 my scales added up to a little over 20 kg of food, plus the garlic, for about £21 of seed and plants, and roughly £15 of compost.

## What worked

Salad leaves gave the most for the least. Two window boxes, sown a pinch at a time every three weeks, kept us in salad from April to September. Cherry tomatoes did best against the warm wall, and I saved seed from the best plant, as Tobias explains in [saving tomato seed](/blog/saving-tomato-seed/).

The peppers ripened late, as they do most years. The tips in [ripening peppers](/blog/ripening-peppers/) saved the last of them. The garlic went in last November, and it is time to [plant garlic](/blog/plant-garlic/) again now.

## What I would not grow again

The courgette took a third of the balcony for less than 3 kg. Potatoes in bags were fun to tip out, but a bag of potatoes from the market costs less than the seed potatoes did. Next year that space goes to a second bean pot.

## The point of counting

Counting changed how I see the balcony. The crops I thought were doing well, such as the courgette, were the ones that looked big. The crops that fed us, such as salad, were the quiet ones. Try it next year. The [Royal Horticultural Society](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables) lists crop yields if you want to compare.
