Every month I answer three of your letters. This month the post bag was full of slugs, so they come first. Keep writing in: reply to this email, and your question may be in the next one.

## "Slugs ate every lettuce. How do I stop them without pellets?"

Slugs feed at night and on wet days, and they hide by day under anything cool and damp. On a balcony or in a yard that usually means under pots and in the gap behind a planter. Here is what works for me:

- Lift your pots onto feet, so there is no damp shade beneath them.
- Go out with a torch after rain and pick them off. Two nights in a row clears most of them.
- Put a ring of copper tape round each pot. It does not stop every slug, but it slows them down.
- Grow lettuce in a hanging basket or a window box. Slugs rarely climb that far.

Avoid slug pellets. They kill the thrushes and frogs that eat slugs for you, and the pellets wash into the soil.

## "My basil leaves are turning yellow. Is it dying?"

Yellow lower leaves on basil nearly always mean too much water and too little warmth. Basil wants to dry out a little between waterings, and it sulks below 12 degrees. Move it to the warmest windowsill you have, water only when the top of the compost feels dry, and pinch off the yellow leaves. Read the issue on [windowsill herbs](/blog/winter-windowsill-herbs/) for the herbs that cope better with a cool kitchen.

## "What can I grow on a wall that gets no sun?"

More than you might think. Ferns, ivy, a climbing hydrangea and the shade-loving fuchsias all do well on a north wall. For food, try mint, chervil, sorrel and lettuce in summer: leafy crops cope with shade far better than fruit. The RHS keeps a list of [plants for shade](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/shade-plants) worth a look.
