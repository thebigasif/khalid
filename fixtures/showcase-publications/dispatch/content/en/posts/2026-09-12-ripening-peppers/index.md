Good morning. My pepper plants are full of fruit, and almost all of it is green. If yours are the same, there are still a few warm weeks left, and ripening peppers is mostly a matter of moving the heat to where the fruit is.

## Why peppers take so long

A pepper is ripe when it is green. It is safe to eat and it has its full size. It only turns red, yellow or orange after weeks more on the plant, which is when it gets sweet. In a short summer the last fruits set too late to colour outside.

## Four ways of ripening peppers

1. **Move the pots.** Put them against the warmest wall you have, the one that is still warm to the touch at dusk.
2. **Pinch out the flowers.** New flowers now will never become fruit. Take them off, so the plant puts its energy into the fruit it has.
3. **Water less.** A little stress tells the plant to ripen what it carries. Keep the compost just moist.
4. **Bring them in.** Before the first frost, bring the whole pot onto a sunny windowsill. Indoors a pepper plant will keep ripening into November.

## The ones that never turn

Pick any that are still green when the plant gives up. Put them in a paper bag with a ripe banana, which gives off a gas called ethylene that helps fruit ripen, and leave the bag somewhere warm. Some will colour. The rest are good green, in a stir fry or roasted.

## Next year

Sow in February, not April, and choose small fruited kinds that ripen early. Save seed from the best fruit, the same way as in [saving tomato seed](/blog/saving-tomato-seed/), if your kind is not an F1 hybrid. My count of [balcony vegetables](/blog/balcony-vegetables/) at the end of the season will show how the peppers did against the rest. The University of Minnesota has a good [note on growing peppers](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers) in a short summer.
