This winter reading is Hanna Lowe's thank-you to the library where she wrote much of her first book. On Wednesday 9 December at 6.30 pm, she will read in the reading room at Corve Street Library in Ludlow.

## What Hanna will read at the winter reading

The evening has two halves. First, two short stories from [Small Hours](/blog/small-hours/), both set on winter nights in a market town much like this one. Then, for the first time anywhere, a passage from the novel Hanna is writing now, about a river warden and a flood.

There will be time for questions afterwards, and the library will have copies of every book for sale and for loan.

## Booking

Entry is free, but the reading room holds fifty people, so please book a place at the library desk or by phone. If the evening is full, the library keeps a waiting list and will call you if a place comes up.

## Why a library

Hanna wrote most of Small Hours at a desk in this library, on her days off from night shifts at the county archive. She has said many times that a free, warm, quiet room with books in it is one of the best things a town can have. You can read about the history of the [public library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_library) and why so many towns built one.

The library is on Corve Street, a short walk from the market square. It is step-free from the street entrance.

## Tea, signing and talk

The library serves tea and mince pies after the winter reading, and Hanna stays to sign books and talk. Last year's reading ran late because nobody wanted to leave. If you would like a signed copy but cannot come, the library desk will take your name and Hanna will sign it on the night. For the new novel itself, see [The river warden](/blog/river-warden-excerpt/).

## Reading groups

Reading groups are especially welcome at the winter reading. If your group would like to come together, tell the library desk when you book, and they will keep a row of seats for you. Hanna is happy to stay after the reading to talk with a group about any of her books, and the library can lend extra copies of Small Hours for groups that want to read it first.
