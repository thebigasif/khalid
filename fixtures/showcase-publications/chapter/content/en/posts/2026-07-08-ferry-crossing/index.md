The last ferry crossing left at eleven, and on a Tuesday in March it carried nobody. Nell liked it that way. She took the money from the empty box, checked the life rings, and stood at the rail while the engine found its rhythm under her feet.

Halfway across, a man came up from the lower deck. She had not seen him board. He was older than she remembered, and thinner, and he had his father's way of standing with one hand in his pocket.

"Nell Morgan," he said. "They told me you were back."

## Twenty years

She knew him at once, and then wished she had not. Owen Price had been her best friend until the summer she was eighteen, when she left the coast without saying goodbye to anyone. She meant to write. In fact she wrote a dozen letters, and posted none of them.

"I'm clearing Dad's house," she said.

"I know. I'm sorry about your father." He looked at the water. "He used to come over on this boat every Sunday. Said it was the only place he could think."

## The end of the ferry crossing

The lights of the far shore came up out of the dark, one by one. Nell brought the ferry in slowly, the way her father had taught her, and the hull bumped once against the old tyres on the jetty.

Owen stepped off without a word. Then he turned back.

"He kept your letters," he said. "The ones you sent him. All of them, in a tin on the dresser."

She never sent her father any letters. She was almost home before she understood what that meant.

## About this excerpt

This chapter comes from the middle of [Salt Road, Hanna's first novel](/blog/salt-road/). The ferry in the book is made up, but small passenger ferries like it still cross estuaries around the coast. You can read about the history of [ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferry) and why some short routes have run for centuries.

The ferry crossing is the turning point of the novel. Before it, Nell is clearing a house. After it, she is trying to find out who wrote to her father in her name, and why. Hanna reads this chapter at most events, and it opens the talk at the [Winter Book Weekend](/blog/winter-book-weekend/).
