The first frost alarm of the year went off at ten past three, and Ruth was in her boots before she was properly awake. Seventy years old, and still the first one out. She stood in the yard with her coat over her nightdress and listened to the orchard. Nothing. No wind, no owl, only the cold settling on everything like a held breath.

Behind her the kitchen light came on. That would be Cass, who had been home for four days and had not slept a full night yet.

## The fires

They lit the old burners along the bottom row, where the cold pooled first. Ruth had told her son for ten years that the burners were a waste of diesel, and for ten years he had bought the diesel anyway. Tonight she was glad of it. The flames came up small and blue, then yellow, and the smoke hung low between the trees.

"Will it work?" Cass asked.

"Some years," Ruth said. "Not every year."

Cass walked the row with the torch, checking each burner. She moved the way her grandfather had, slow and careful, as if the trees could hear her. Ruth did not say so. There would be time for that, or there would not.

## After the first frost

By six the sky was pale over the hill, and the first frost had come and gone. It had taken the blossom on the two oldest Foxwhelps and spared the rest. Ruth counted the damage as the light came up, the way you count change in a dark pocket.

"Twenty trees," she said at last. "We can live with twenty."

Cass laughed, the first time since she had come home. Then she went in to make tea, and left the door open behind her, and the warm air from the kitchen ran out across the yard.

## About this excerpt

This is the opening chapter of [Hanna's new novel](/blog/orchard-year-novel/), lightly cut. Orchard frost burners are a real practice, though most farms now use fans or water sprays. You can read about [frost protection in orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost#Effect_on_plants) and why the coldest air sinks to the lowest ground.

Hanna chose this chapter because the first frost sets the whole year in motion: the lost blossom on the oldest trees decides how much fruit there will be, and the fruit decides whether the farm survives. The [Orchard Year launch](/blog/orchard-year-launch/) opens with a reading of these pages.
