Readers often ask how novels start. For me a novel never starts with a plot. It starts with a place I cannot stop thinking about, and a question about the people who live there.

## How novels start: first, a place

The Orchard Year started with one tree. It was an old cider apple at the bottom of my uncle's orchard, half dead and still fruiting, and every autumn somebody said it should come down. Nobody ever cut it. I wanted to know why, and I wanted to know who would be the one to finally do it.

Salt Road started with a cliff path that had fallen into the sea between one summer and the next. Both books grew outward from that one spot, the way a village grows from a crossroads.

## Then, a notebook

For the first year I only fill notebooks. I write down what people say, what the weather does, what things cost. I copy out old maps and parish records. I do not let myself write a single scene, because once I start writing scenes I stop looking.

When the notebook feels heavy, I read it all in one sitting and mark the pages that make me want to write. Those pages become the first chapter list. Most of it changes, but the list gets me started.

## Then, the long middle

The first draft takes about a year, and I write it badly on purpose. I write in the mornings, by hand, and type it up in the afternoons. The second draft is where the book is really made. I write about that in [A year in one orchard](/blog/cider-farm-notes/).

If you want to try this yourself, start with a place you know well and one question you cannot answer. Many writers keep a [commonplace book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonplace_book) for exactly this kind of gathering. It is an old habit, and a good one.

## What came of it

Every book so far began this way, even the essays in [A Field Guide to Leaving](/blog/field-guide-leaving-essays/), which started as one notebook about a bus route. The newest book is starting the same way now, with a river and a man who looks after it. You can read its first pages in [The river warden](/blog/river-warden-excerpt/).
