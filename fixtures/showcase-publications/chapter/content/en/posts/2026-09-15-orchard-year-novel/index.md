The Orchard Year follows one year on Cae Glas, a cider orchard on the Welsh border that has lost money for six seasons in a row. Its owner, Ruth Pryce, is seventy and tired. Her son wants to sell. Her granddaughter, home from the city after a bad year of her own, wants to try one more harvest.

## What The Orchard Year is about

The novel moves through the orchard's year, from pruning in January to pressing in November. Each part is told by one of the three Pryces, so the reader sees the same trees, the same debts and the same old arguments from three sides. It is a book about work, about what a family owes to a place, and about the small, stubborn hope of planting something you may never see grow.

Readers of [Hanna's coast novel](/blog/salt-road/) will know the valley. This book is set a few miles upriver, forty years later, and two characters from the first novel come back in small parts.

## Where The Orchard Year came from

Hanna grew up on a fruit farm, and she spent a year working in a cider mill while she wrote the first draft. Many of the orchard's details come from that year: the smell of the press, the frost alarms at three in the morning and the old varieties with names like Foxwhelp and Brown Snout. You can read about real [cider apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider_apple) varieties and how they differ from eating apples.

## What early readers say

Booksellers who read the novel before publication called it warm, exact and quietly funny. Several said the orchard itself becomes the fourth main character, which is what Hanna hoped for when she began.

## How the book is built

The novel has four parts, one for each season, and each part has three chapters, one for each Pryce. Hanna wrote it in that order, season by season, so the book follows the real year she spent on the farm.
