For the twelve months I spent writing the first draft of The Orchard Year, I also worked two days a week on a cider farm in the next valley, and kept notes. These are some of my notes from that orchard, tidied up a little.

## Winter on the cider farm

In January we pruned in the cold, three of us to a row. The farmer, who had pruned the same trees for forty years, could tell me which branch to cut from across the field. I could not, and I never really learned. What I did learn was the sound of a good cut, and how tired your arms are by four o'clock. Ruth's hands in the novel are my hands that winter.

## Spring: blossom and frost

In April the blossom came, and with it the frost alarms. We lit burners twice and lost one night's blossom on the low rows anyway. That night became the opening of the novel, which you can read as [The first frost](/blog/first-frost/).

## Autumn: picking up fruit

From September the work was picking up fruit, sorting it and carrying it to the press. Cider apples are not picked from the tree. You wait for them to fall, then gather them from the grass, which is slower and harder on the back than it sounds. The old varieties have wonderful names, and you can read about some of them in this list of [cider apple varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_apple_cultivars).

## What I kept

By the end of the year I had four notebooks, a sore back and a much better book. Most of what I learned never made it onto the page. The orchard is in every sentence anyway, and I think readers can tell.

The farmer read the finished novel last month. He said the frost scenes were right and the money worries were worse than I made them, which I took as praise.
