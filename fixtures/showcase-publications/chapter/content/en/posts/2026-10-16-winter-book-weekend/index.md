At the Borderlands Winter Book Weekend this year, Hanna will give an hour's talk on Saturday 28 November at 2 pm in the Long Tent. Her subject is staying put: why so many of her characters refuse to leave, and what it costs them.

## About the talk at the Winter Book Weekend

Hanna will read short passages from all four of her books, from [Small Hours](/blog/small-hours/) to The Orchard Year, and talk about the border country they share. She will be in conversation with the festival's director, and there will be time for questions from the audience.

After the talk, Hanna signs books in the festival bookshop tent. The bookshop will have every title in stock.

## Tickets

Tickets are on sale now from the festival, with lower prices for students and under-25s. This event sold out last year, so book soon. The festival also offers a weekend pass if you want to stay for the other talks.

## Getting there

Presteigne is a small market town on the border. The festival runs a shuttle bus from Knighton station on both days, and there is parking in the field next to the tents. Bring warm clothes: the tents are heated, but it is November in the hills.

Book festivals like this one began as small gatherings in market towns and now run all year round. You can read about the history of the [literary festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_festival) in Britain and beyond.

## Before you go

The Winter Book Weekend is a good place to start with Hanna's work if you have not read her yet. The talk takes in all four books, and the festival bookshop sells them side by side. Many readers start with [the coast novel](/blog/salt-road/) and read [The ferry crossing](/blog/ferry-crossing/) on the shuttle bus.

## Questions for Hanna

The festival collects questions in advance as well as on the day. If you would like Hanna to answer something about the books, the places or how she writes, leave it with the festival box office before the weekend. She tries to answer every question at the Winter Book Weekend, even if the talk runs a little over its hour, and she stays at the signing table until the last reader has gone.
