Salt Road was Hanna Lowe's first novel. Nell Morgan works on the night ferry across the estuary. When her father dies, she goes back to the coast she left at eighteen to clear his house, and finds that he kept every letter she never answered.

## What Salt Road is about

The novel follows Nell over one wet spring as she sorts the house room by room. Each room brings back a year of her childhood, and each year brings back someone she would rather forget. Slowly she learns why her father stayed on a coast that was washing away, and why her mother left it.

Salt Road is about grief, about the old salt trade road that runs along the shore, and about the stubborn people who stay in places the sea is taking back. It is a slower, sadder book than [the new orchard novel](/blog/orchard-year-novel/), though it ends in the same hope.

## How Salt Road was written

Hanna wrote the first draft over two winters, in a rented room above a harbour café. She walked the coast path most afternoons and talked to the people who worked the ferries. The town in the book is made up, but its eroding cliffs are real. You can read more about [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) and why some shores lose several metres a year.

## What readers say

Salt Road was shortlisted for a first novel prize and chosen by many reading groups. Readers often write to Hanna about their own parents' houses, and about the strange comfort of clearing them.

## Salt Road and the later books

Salt Road is set on the coast, a long way from the orchards of the later novel, but the two books share a family. Nell's cousin runs the cider farm in the later novel, and she comes to the coast for the funeral in chapter three. Readers who start with Salt Road will meet her first, and the later book will feel like a return.
