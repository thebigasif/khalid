Marlbeck will have twelve more Saturday market stalls from 7 November, taking the total to 46, the most in ten years. The town council cut the winter fee for new traders by half this year, and every new pitch was taken within a fortnight.

Market manager Kev Durrant said the square would feel full again. "Five years ago we had gaps all down the east side," he said. "This winter you will have to walk round twice to see it all."

## Who is new on the Saturday market stalls

The new traders include:

- **A cheese stall** from a dairy farm near Ashby Cross.
- **Two bakers,** one selling sourdough and one selling cakes and pies.
- **A plant stall** with winter bedding and houseplants.
- **A repair stall** that sharpens knives and mends watches.
- **Street food,** with two hot food vans at the top of the square.

The other new stalls sell clothes, crafts, secondhand records and fish. The fish stall returns after three years away.

## The winter fee

New traders pay £15 a pitch on Saturdays from November to February, instead of £30. Traders who have stood on the market for a year or more pay £25 all year. The town council expects the cut to cost about £3,000 this winter, and hopes to make it back if the new traders stay. The market's fees are part of the [draft budget](/blog/council-tax-bands/).

## Car-free Saturdays

The town council plans to close the market square to cars on Saturdays from April, so the market can spread across the whole square. Councillors will decide in January after a consultation. Some shops on the square are against the plan. Others, including the reopened [Cobb Street bookshop](/blog/cobb-street-bookshop/), support it.

The market runs every Saturday from 8am to 3pm. A [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) has held a market on this square since 1301, when the town's charter was granted.
