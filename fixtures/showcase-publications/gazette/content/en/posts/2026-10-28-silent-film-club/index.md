A silent film club with a live pianist is coming to the Corn Exchange this winter. The new club, the Marlbeck Picture House, will show a programme of short comedies from the 1920s every other Friday, starting on 13 November.

Founder Rosa Kitteridge, a retired music teacher, will play the piano at every show. "Nobody watches these films in silence," she said. "They were made for a piano, a full hall and people laughing out loud, and that is what we want."

## The silent film club programme

Each evening shows three short films, about 70 minutes in all, with an interval for tea. The first four evenings are:

- **13 November:** comedies about trains, trams and things on wheels.
- **27 November:** comedies set in shops and department stores.
- **11 December:** a Christmas evening, with a film set in the snow.
- **8 January:** comedies about the seaside.

All the films are now in the public domain, so the club can show them freely. Kitteridge has written new piano scores for two of them.

## Tickets

Tickets cost £6, or £4 for under-16s, and include tea and a biscuit. They are sold on the door from 7pm, and the films start at 7:30pm. The Corn Exchange holds 180 people, and the first evening is expected to fill, so the club is selling up to 60 tickets in advance at the Cobb Street bookshop, which [reopens this month](/blog/cobb-street-bookshop/).

## The Corn Exchange

The Corn Exchange was built in 1862 for farmers to sell grain and has been the town's hall for concerts and plays since the 1950s. It is one of the stops on the [Christmas lights](/blog/christmas-lights/) evening, and the Rotary club plans [bonfire night](/blog/bonfire-night-guide/) there every year.

The club hopes to add films for children on Saturday afternoons in the new year. The history of [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film) shows that cinemas once kept a pianist on the staff, and the club hopes to bring that back to Marlbeck.
