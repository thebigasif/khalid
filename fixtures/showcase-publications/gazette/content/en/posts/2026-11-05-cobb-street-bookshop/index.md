The Cobb Street bookshop reopens on Saturday, three months after it closed when its owner of 40 years, Geoffrey Hale, retired. The new owners, Mel and Ade Fashola, have kept the name, the creaking floor and most of the secondhand stock, and added new books and a children's corner.

Mel Fashola said they bought the shop because they could not imagine the street without it. "We met in that shop in 2009," she said. "When the sign went up, we sat at the kitchen table and did the sums for a week."

## What changes at the Cobb Street bookshop

The front room now sells new books, with a table of local history and books by writers from the county. The two back rooms keep Geoffrey Hale's secondhand stock, about 9,000 books, priced as he left them. The old stockroom upstairs has become a children's room with low shelves and a rug.

The shop opens Tuesday to Saturday, 9:30am to 5pm, and on Sundays from 11am to 3pm in December. It will order any book in print for collection the next day.

## The high street

Cobb Street has lost three shops this year, and the bookshop's return was welcomed by its neighbours. Jan Wilmot, who runs the deli next door, said the closure had hit her trade. "People came to town for the bookshop and stopped here for lunch," she said. "When it shut, Tuesdays went quiet."

The town council's plan to make the [market square car-free on Saturdays](/blog/saturday-market-stalls/) has worried some traders, but the Fasholas said they would welcome it. Two cafés and a [new workspace for small firms](/blog/mill-workspace/) are also on the way. Independent shops like theirs make up about two thirds of the town centre, according to the town council's last count of [high street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_street) businesses.

## The opening day

The shop opens at 9:30am on Saturday. Geoffrey Hale will be there from 11am to sign copies of the short history of Cobb Street he wrote in 1998, which the new owners have reprinted. Children who visit on the day get a free book from the children's room.
