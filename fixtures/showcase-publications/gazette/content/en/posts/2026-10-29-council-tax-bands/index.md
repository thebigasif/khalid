Marlbeck Town Council's draft budget for 2027 would raise its share of council tax by 3.5 percent. The town council sets only a small part of the bill: most of it goes to the county and district councils, which publish their own budgets in January. The draft is open for comment until 20 November, and councillors vote on it on 10 December.

The town clerk, Alison Marsh, said the rise covers higher costs, not new spending. "Energy, insurance and wages have all gone up," she said. "This keeps what we already do, plus our share of the flood wall."

## Council tax by band in 2027

This is the town's share only, for a year, if the draft passes unchanged.

| Band | 2026 | Draft 2027 | Change in a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | £68.40 | £70.79 | £2.39 |
| B | £79.80 | £82.59 | £2.79 |
| C | £91.20 | £94.39 | £3.19 |
| D | £102.60 | £106.19 | £3.59 |
| E | £125.40 | £129.79 | £4.39 |
| F | £148.20 | £153.39 | £5.19 |
| G | £171.00 | £176.99 | £5.99 |

Most homes in Marlbeck are in bands B and C. A band D home would pay about 30p a month more.

## Where the money goes

The draft plans to spend £1.18 million in 2027. The largest parts are:

- **Parks and open spaces:** £310,000, including Riverside Park and the play areas.
- **The market and the market square:** £190,000, most of it paid back by [stall fees](/blog/saturday-market-stalls/).
- **The flood wall:** £100,000, the first of three yearly payments to the [flood wall plan](/blog/flood-wall-approved/).
- **Grants to clubs and groups:** £45,000, the same as this year.

## How to comment

Read the draft at the town hall or the [library](/blog/library-saturday-hours/), or online. Send comments to the town clerk by 20 November. The town must set its share before the district council sends out bills in March, as the law on [council tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Tax) requires.
