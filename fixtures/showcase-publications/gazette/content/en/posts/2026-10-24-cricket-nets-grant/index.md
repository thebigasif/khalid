Ashby Cross Cricket Club has won a £24,000 grant for new cricket nets, replacing nets that have stood on the village ground since 1987. The new nets will have an artificial surface and floodlights, so the club can train on spring evenings before the clocks change.

Club chair Anita Shah said the old nets were held together with cable ties. "Every season we patch them, and every season the ball finds a new hole," she said. "The juniors have never had nets that were safe to bat in without a coach watching the gaps."

## Where the money comes from

The money comes from the national sports council's community fund, which pays for sports buildings in small places. The club applied in the spring with letters of support from two primary schools and the village hall committee. It must find £6,000 of its own, which it has raised from a summer quiz, a car boot sale and its members.

The new cricket nets will have three lanes, one with a bowling machine. Work starts in February and should be finished by the first match of the season in April.

## Open to the village

As part of the grant, the club must open the nets to people who are not members. Schools will use them on weekday afternoons in the summer term, and the public can book a lane for £10 an hour on Sunday mornings. The club also plans a girls' team, its first, for the 2027 season.

## The club

Ashby Cross Cricket Club has about 160 members, including 70 juniors, and plays in the county league. It finished third last season, two places behind the leaders. Marlbeck Town's footballers have also had a good autumn, with a run to the [cup quarter-final](/blog/town-cup-quarter-final/). The club's ground is also used for the village fete, and the fete's money this year helped pay for a new library at [Fellgate Primary](/blog/fellgate-school-library/).

Cricket clubs across the country have used grants like this one to keep the game alive in villages, according to the history of [village cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_cricket).
