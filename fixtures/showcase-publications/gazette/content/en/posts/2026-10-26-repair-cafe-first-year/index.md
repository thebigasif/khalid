Marlbeck's repair café mended its 200th item on Saturday, a 40-year-old lamp brought in by a woman who had owned it since her wedding. The café opened in the church hall on Church Walk a year ago and runs on the last Saturday of every month.

Organiser Martin Cole, a retired electrician, said most things only need a small fix. "A loose wire, a blown fuse, a seam that has come apart," he said. "People are about to throw them away, and we fix them over a cup of tea."

## What the repair café fixes

In its first year, the café's 14 volunteers mended:

- 71 electrical items, such as lamps, kettles, toasters and radios;
- 58 pieces of clothing, from zips and hems to a wedding dress;
- 34 bikes and scooters;
- 22 toys;
- 19 other items, including a clock, an umbrella and a garden chair.

About one item in six could not be fixed, usually because a part was no longer made. The volunteers then explain how to recycle it.

## How it works

Bring your broken item to the church hall between 10am and 1pm on the last Saturday of the month. A volunteer fixes it with you, so you can learn how. The repair is free, though the café asks for a donation towards tea and parts. Electrical items are tested for safety before they go home.

## Volunteers wanted

The café most needs people who can fix bikes, because the bike queue is always the longest. It also wants someone who can sharpen tools. Cole said no one needs to be an expert. "Half our volunteers learned here," he said.

The café is one of more than 2,000 [repair cafés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repair_caf%C3%A9) around the world. It now shares the church hall with the library's help sessions, and its volunteers also help with the [tree planting](/blog/northfield-tree-planting/) on the Northfield estate.
