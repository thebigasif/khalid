Fellgate Primary School opened its new school library on Monday, a year after parents set out to raise £18,000 for it. The village school had kept its books on trolleys in a corridor since the old library became a classroom in 2023.

Head teacher Sarah Pennington cut a ribbon of paper chains made by the reception class. "Every child in this school helped pay for this room," she said. "Some of them baked, some of them ran and some of them stayed quiet for an hour, which was the hardest."

## How parents paid for the school library

The parents' association set the target last November. The money came from:

- **Cake sales:** £4,300 from a stall at the school gate every Friday.
- **The Fellgate fun run:** £6,100 in sponsorship, with 140 runners on the village green in May.
- **A sponsored silence:** £1,900, raised by pupils in Years 5 and 6.
- **Local firms and the village fete:** £5,700, with a stall from the [Ashby Cross cricket club](/blog/cricket-nets-grant/).

The district council's community fund doubled the last £1,000, which closed the gap in October.

## What is in the school library

The room has shelves for 3,000 books, low seats and a reading corner with cushions. About 1,200 new books arrived in the summer, chosen by the pupils from lists drawn up by staff. Older pupils run the desk at lunchtime and stamp the books out. The school lends its books to families on Thursday afternoons, so younger brothers and sisters can borrow too.

Parents' association chair Joe Brennan said the next target is smaller. "We want £2,000 for audiobooks," he said. "Some children read better with their ears first."

## Other schools

Marlbeck's other primary schools have kept their libraries, but two have cut opening hours. The town library on Church Walk runs class visits for every primary school in the area and has [extended its Saturday hours](/blog/library-saturday-hours/) from this month. Research on [school libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_library) links a well-stocked library with better reading at age 11, the head teacher said.
