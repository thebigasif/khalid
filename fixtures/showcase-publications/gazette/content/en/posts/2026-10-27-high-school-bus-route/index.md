A new school bus route to Marlbeck High School starts on Monday 2 November, the first day after half term. Route 7S links the Northfield estate and Ashby Cross with the school, so about 180 pupils no longer have to change buses in the town centre.

Parents on the Northfield estate asked for the route for three years. Lucy Carver, whose two children take the bus, said the old journey took 50 minutes for a trip of six kilometres. "They had to stand in the market square in the dark every winter morning," she said. "Now the bus picks them up at the end of the road."

## The school bus route and times

The bus leaves Ashby Cross village hall at 7:55am, stops at four places on the Northfield estate and reaches the school at 8:30am. The return bus leaves the school at 3:25pm. Pupils who stay for clubs can take the town bus, which stops outside the school at 4:30pm. Older students can use the route to reach the [sixth form open evening](/blog/sixth-form-open-evening/) on 12 November.

A term's pass costs £95, the same as on the other school routes. Pupils who live more than three miles from the school, and pupils from families on certain benefits, travel free.

## Who pays for the bus

The county council pays for the route from its school transport budget, with £20,000 a year from the developer of the new homes at Northfield Farm. County councillor Raj Bhatt said the school bus route would be reviewed in July. "If the bus is full, it stays," he said. "If it is half empty, we will look at it again."

## During the bridge closure

The Mill Lane bridge closes from 16 November, so Marlbeck High's buses use a diversion and leave ten minutes earlier. The [bridge closure guide](/blog/mill-lane-bridge-closure/) has the details. The school will send new times to parents by email.

The government's rules for [school transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_bus) say a county must provide free transport over set distances, but routes like this one are a choice the county makes.
