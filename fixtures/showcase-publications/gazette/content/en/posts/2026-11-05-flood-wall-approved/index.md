Marlbeck's flood wall plan was approved on Wednesday evening, two years after the river burst its banks and flooded 61 homes on Riverside and Bridge End. The district council's planning committee voted 9 to 1 for the £4.2 million scheme, which puts a wall and raised banks along 1.4 kilometres of the River Marl.

The committee chair, Councillor Ruth Ellery, said the vote was the easy part. "People on Riverside have waited two winters with sandbags by the door," she said. "Now we have to build it on time and on budget."

## What the flood wall plan includes

The flood wall scheme has three parts, set out in the Environment Agency's design report:

- **A wall along Riverside:** a concrete wall faced in local stone, 1.1 metres high on average, from the Mill Lane bridge to the end of the allotments.
- **Raised banks at Bridge End:** earth banks with a footpath on top, so the riverside walk stays open.
- **Flood gates:** two gates at the slipway and the allotment lane, closed only when a flood warning is in force.

The wall is designed to hold back a flood of the size that, on average, comes once in 75 years. Engineers say it would have kept every home dry in the [floods of November 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood).

## When the work happens

Work on the flood wall starts in March 2027 and runs in two stages. The Riverside wall comes first, so the riverside path between the bridge and the boatyard closes from March to August. The Bridge End banks follow in the autumn. The Mill Lane bridge repairs, which [close the bridge for six weeks from 16 November](/blog/mill-lane-bridge-closure/), are a separate job and will be finished long before.

## Who pays

The Environment Agency pays £3.1 million. The district council pays £800,000 from its reserves, and Marlbeck Town Council adds £300,000 over three years. The town council's share is part of the [draft budget for 2027](/blog/council-tax-bands/), which goes to a vote on 10 December.

## What residents said

Margaret Doyle, who has lived on Riverside for 31 years, watched the flood wall vote from the public seats. "I cried when they put their hands up," she said. "We have had the water in the kitchen twice." The only councillor to vote against, Councillor Neil Barraclough, said he supported the wall but wanted the stone facing dropped to save £400,000. The committee rejected that idea.

Residents can see the full plans at the town hall on weekdays, or at a drop-in session at the Corn Exchange on Saturday 21 November from 10am to 2pm.
