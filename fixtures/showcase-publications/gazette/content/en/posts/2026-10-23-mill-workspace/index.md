The old corn mill at Bridge End will become a mill workspace for small firms, after the district council approved the conversion on Thursday. The plan turns the empty five-storey building into 40 desks, eight small offices, two meeting rooms and a café on the ground floor.

The mill has stood empty since a furniture warehouse moved out in 2018. Its new owners, a group of six local business people, bought it for £410,000 (2025).

## What the mill workspace includes

The ground floor will hold the café and a reception, open to the public. The first and second floors will be open-plan desks, rented by the day or the month. The top floors will hold the offices, each big enough for two to six people. The old loading doors on the river side will become windows.

The building is listed, so the conversion keeps the iron columns, the timber floors and the hoist wheel in the roof. Planners asked for the brick to be cleaned by hand, not blasted.

## Who it is for

Spokesperson Grace Ilori said the town had nowhere for people who work for themselves to meet. "Marlbeck has hundreds of people working from a spare room," she said. "Some of them want a desk, some want a room for clients and most want someone to talk to at lunch."

A desk in the mill workspace will cost £12 a day or £150 a month (2027 prices). The owners hope to open in September 2027 and say the work will create about 30 building jobs and eight jobs in the café and reception.

## The river

The mill sits next to the river, inside the area the new [flood wall](/blog/flood-wall-approved/) will protect. The Environment Agency asked for the ground floor to be built to recover from a flood, with tiled floors and power points set high on the walls.

Research on [coworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coworking) spaces in small towns suggests they bring trade to nearby shops, and two cafés on Bridge End have already asked about a share of the lunch trade.
