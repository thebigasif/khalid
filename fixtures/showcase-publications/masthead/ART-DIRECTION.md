# The Common Room: art direction

The Common Room is a made-up general magazine of culture, cities, nature, food and ideas. Its photographs should look like a good magazine's: one clear subject, calm light and room around it. They show the place or the thing a story is about, not a person posing.

## Tone

- **Places and things first.** Seats, a bench, shelves, bread, a road, a train, a beach. People may appear small or in the background.
- **Everyday settings.** A high street, a market square, a café, a lane in the hills.
- **Natural light.** Morning, dusk or overcast light, true colour, no heavy filters.
- **Little to read.** No logos or large signs; a small price card at a market is fine.

## Palette

Calm, mostly muted colours that sit well with the sample brand's deep blue on cream:

- the greys, browns and greens of streets, shelves and countryside;
- the warm tones of bread, wood and lamplight;
- one bright spot at most, such as a red bicycle or a crate of tomatoes.

## Subjects

Eleven stories across five sections: culture (a small cinema), cities (a bench, night trains, a bicycle), nature (a road in the hills, a beach at low tide), food (a bakery, an allotment harvest, a market) and ideas (a library, a café with a newspaper).

## One ratio per slot

- **Lead story and post lead picture:** 3:2 landscape, as the photographs come. On a phone the full picture shows above the text.
- **Picture cards:** the same 3:2 landscape, shown whole, never cropped to a square.
- **List rows:** a small 3:2 picture beside the text, at least 7rem wide.

All photographs are CC0 or public domain, so none needs a visible credit; the photo credits page still lists each one.
