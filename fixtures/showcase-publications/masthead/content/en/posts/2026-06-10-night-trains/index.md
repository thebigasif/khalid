Ten years ago night trains seemed finished in Europe. Cheap flights had emptied the sleepers, and route after route closed. Now they are coming back. New lines run from Brussels, Paris, Vienna and Stockholm, and many sell out in summer. I spent a month on them to see what has changed.

## What a night on the train is like

You board in the evening, find your berth and watch the city lights thin out. A sleeper compartment holds one to three beds, a small basin and a window. A couchette is cheaper: four or six bunks, with sheets and a pillow. Most trains have a small bar or a trolley. You wake up in another country, often in the middle of a city, with no airport transfer to pay for.

## Five routes worth taking

| Route | Leaves | Arrives | Time on board | Bed from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brussels to Berlin | 19:22 | 08:45 | 13 h 23 min | €89 |
| Paris to Vienna | 19:12 | 10:13 | 15 h 1 min | €119 |
| Vienna to Venice | 21:28 | 08:32 | 11 h 4 min | €69 |
| Stockholm to Narvik | 18:02 | 11:21 | 17 h 19 min | €95 |
| Zurich to Amsterdam | 20:01 | 09:04 | 13 h 3 min | €99 |

Times and prices change with the season, so check before you book. The guide [The Man in Seat 61](https://www.seat61.com/) keeps every route up to date and explains how to book each one.

## What to know before you go

- **Book early.** Beds go on sale three to six months ahead and the cheap ones sell first.
- **Pack light.** There is little room for a large case in a couchette.
- **Bring water and a snack.** Not every train has a bar open all night.
- **Expect delays.** A sleeper that runs an hour late still beats a dawn flight.

## Why it matters

A night train turns travel time into sleep time. It also puts you in the centre of a city, rested, on the first morning. That is worth more than the hours it takes. For more on getting around without a car, read [the bicycle as a test of a good street](/blog/bicycle-city/).
