I waited four years for my allotment. When the letter came, the plot was a tangle of brambles and old carpet. Eleven years later it grows most of the vegetables my family eats from July to October. It has also taught me how to wait, which is a rarer skill.

## The waiting list

In many towns the list for an allotment is longer than the list for a house. Some sites ask people to wait ten years. Councils are meant to provide plots when enough people ask, but land is scarce and new sites are slow. Some towns now split full plots into halves, which shortens the list and suits people who are new to growing.

## The first year

Nobody tells you how much of the first year is digging. I spent most of mine clearing roots and learning which weeds come back. My first harvest was six potatoes and a courgette the size of my arm. It was enough to keep me going.

## What the plot teaches

- **Some things cannot be hurried.** A squash sown in May will not be ready before September.
- **Most of the work is small.** Ten minutes of weeding twice a week beats a whole day once a month.
- **Neighbours are the best teachers.** Every plot holder I know learned from the one next door.
- **Failure is normal.** Slugs, frost and drought take something every year.

## How to get a plot

Put your name down now, even if the list is long. Ask your site whether it offers half plots or a shared plot for beginners. The [National Allotment Society](https://www.nsalg.org.uk/) explains your rights and how to start a new site if your town has none.
