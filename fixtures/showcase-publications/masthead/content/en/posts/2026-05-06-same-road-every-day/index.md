A year ago I made a simple rule. Once a week, whatever the weather, I would walk the same road: four miles out along a lane between the hills, and four miles back. Walking the same road sounds like the opposite of adventure. It turned out to teach me more than any new path.

## The first months

In winter the road was mostly grey. Rain, mud, a few sheep and the same three cars. I almost gave up in February. But by March I had begun to notice small things: which ditch filled first after rain, where the first green showed on the slopes, the day the light lasted long enough to walk home without a torch.

## What changes when you return

When you walk somewhere once, you see a view. When you walk it fifty times, you see a year. I learned when the larks came back, when the bracken turned, and how the same bend looks in fog, frost and full sun. I started to recognise the farmer, then to talk to him.

## How to try it

- **Pick a route close to home,** so it is easy to keep going.
- **Keep it short enough** to walk in any weather, an hour or two at most.
- **Write one line each time,** about anything you notice.
- **Take the same photo** from the same spot each month.

The [Ramblers](https://www.ramblers.org.uk/) list public paths and walking groups across the country, if you want company or a route to start with.

## A year later

I still walk the road every week. It no longer feels like the same walk twice. If you like slow attention to one place, read Daniel's piece on [what the beach shows at low tide](/blog/low-tide/).
