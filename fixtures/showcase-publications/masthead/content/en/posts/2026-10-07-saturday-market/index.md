The Saturday market opens at eight, but the stallholders arrive at six. By the time the first shoppers come, the square is full of crates, awnings and the smell of coffee. I spent a whole Saturday there, from setting up to packing away, to see what keeps a market going.

## The growers

The best stalls sell what was picked that week. On the day I visited, a family from a farm ten miles away had twenty kinds of tomato, sweetcorn still in its leaves and a box of the last runner beans. They have had the same pitch for fourteen years. Most of their customers know them by name.

## The bakers and the cheese stall

Next to them, a small bakery sold out of sourdough by half past ten. Across the square, a cheese stall gave away so many tastes that a queue formed just to try them. The cheesemaker told me tasting is her whole marketing plan. People who try, buy.

## What keeps a market alive

- **Regular stallholders,** who come every week, whatever the weather.
- **A mix of stalls,** so a shopper can do a whole week's shop in one place.
- **Low pitch fees,** so new traders can start small and try an idea.
- **Something to stay for,** such as coffee, music or a bench in the sun.

The [National Association of British Market Authorities](https://www.nabma.com/) works with councils on running markets well, from pitch fees to new traders.

## Packing away

By two o'clock the crates were empty and the square was quiet again. Most of the stallholders were tired but pleased. For the story of one baker who started on a stall like these, read [the corner bakery comes back](/blog/corner-bakery/).
