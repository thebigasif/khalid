Ask people what a public library is for and most will say books. They are right, but only partly. Spend a morning in one and you will see a room that does a dozen jobs at once, most of them never written on the sign outside.

## A morning at the desk

By ten o'clock the tables are full. A student is writing an essay. A man is printing a form for a job. Two women are learning to video call their grandchildren, with a volunteer beside them. A parent is reading to a toddler on a beanbag. Nobody has paid to be there, and nobody has been asked why they came.

That is the quiet point of a library. It is one of the few warm, dry rooms left where you can stay all day without buying anything.

## The jobs a library now does

- **A study room** for people with no quiet space at home.
- **A help desk** for forms, benefits and passports, now that so much is online.
- **A meeting place** for reading groups, language classes and parents with babies.
- **A lender of things,** from laptops to sewing machines in some branches.

## Why this is hard to count

Councils measure libraries by the books they lend. Loans have fallen, so the numbers look bad. But the visits, the help and the hours of warm space are much harder to count, and they are often what people need most. The library body [CILIP](https://www.cilip.org.uk/) has argued for better measures for years.

## What you can do

Use your library, even if you only borrow one book a month. Join its friends group. Tell your councillor why it matters to you, in a sentence or two. For another room a town nearly lost, read our story about [the small cinema that would not close](/blog/small-cinema/).
