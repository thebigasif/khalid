Twice a day the sea pulls back and leaves a second beach behind. At low tide the sand runs out for hundreds of metres, pools fill with crabs and weed, and old posts and wrecks show for an hour or two. I grew up beside such a beach, and I still go back to watch it empty.

## Why the tide goes out

Tides come from the pull of the moon and, to a lesser degree, the sun. As the earth turns, most coasts pass through two high tides and two low tides a day, about six hours apart. The biggest tides, called spring tides, come just after a full or new moon. The smallest, neap tides, come between them.

## What you can find

- **Rock pools,** with anemones, crabs, shrimps and small fish.
- **Sandbars and channels,** which change shape after every storm.
- **Old things,** from wooden groynes to the ribs of a boat.
- **Birds,** which feed on the wet sand as soon as it is uncovered.

## How to explore safely

The sea comes back faster than most people expect, and channels fill behind you before the beach in front does. Check the tide times before you go, and turn back well before low water has passed. The [National Tidal and Sea Level Facility](https://www.ntslf.org/) publishes tide predictions for ports around the coast. Tell someone where you are going, and never cross a channel that is already filling.
