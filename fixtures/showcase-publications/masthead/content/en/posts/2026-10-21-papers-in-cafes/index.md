Most mornings I see the same man in the café on the corner, reading the paper from front to back with one cup of coffee. Print sales have fallen for years, yet reading the paper in a café is a habit that refuses to die. I wanted to know why.

## A paper you can share

Many cafés still buy one or two papers a day and leave them on a rack by the door. Customers pass them between tables. One person reads the news, another takes the crossword, and a third reads the sport. A single paper might be read by twenty people before closing time.

That sharing is part of the appeal. A phone shows each of us our own feed. A paper on a café table shows everyone the same front page, and it often starts a conversation.

## What a paper gives that a phone does not

- **An end.** A paper has a last page, so you can finish it.
- **A choice made by an editor,** not by what you clicked on yesterday.
- **Less hurry.** Nobody refreshes a paper.
- **Something to do with your hands** that is not a screen.

## The cafés that keep it going

The owners I spoke to said the papers cost them little and bring people back. Some keep a week of back issues. One keeps local papers from the last century for regulars to browse. The [British Newspaper Archive](https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/) holds millions of old pages, if you want to read what your town's paper said long ago.

## A habit worth keeping

Read the paper in a café once this week, on paper. Leave it on the table when you go. For another public habit worth keeping, read our essay on [what a public library is for now](/blog/what-a-library-is-for/).
