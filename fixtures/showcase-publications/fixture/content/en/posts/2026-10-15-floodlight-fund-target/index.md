The floodlight fund has reached its target of £18,000. Players, parents, neighbours and the county sports partnership all gave, and the money pays for new LED lights on the main pitch at Larkmoor Fields, where the first team and the [under-12s](/blog/under-12s-open-trials/) train.

## Why we started the floodlight fund

Our old lights went up in 1991. Two of the four poles have failed this year, and the electrician who looks after them says the rest will not last another winter. Without lights, the first team and the juniors could not train on the main pitch after October.

New LED lights use about half the electricity of the old ones, so they will also cut the club's power bill. They light the pitch evenly, which means no more dark corner by the car park where the ball disappears.

## Where the money came from

Most of the money came from donations, large and small, from players, parents and neighbours. Two quiz nights in the clubhouse and the sponsored walk round the Larkmoor reservoir in June added the rest, with a community grant from the county sports partnership. The club's treasurer will publish the full accounts at the annual meeting in May, and anyone can ask to see them before then.

## What happens next

Work starts on 11 January, when the league takes its winter break. The contractor expects it to take three weeks. During that time, training moves to the hard court behind the pavilion.

We will name every donor on a board in the clubhouse, unless you asked us not to. If you would like to see how [floodlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodlight) work, the contractor has offered to show the juniors round on the last day.

Thank you, all of you. This is what a club is. If you want to help with the next project, a new roof for the away dressing room, talk to me at training. New players can read about [pre-season training](/blog/pre-season-training-and-new-kit/).
