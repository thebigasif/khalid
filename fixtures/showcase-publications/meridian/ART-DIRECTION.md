# The Weekly Meridian: art direction

The Weekly Meridian is a made-up journal about architecture and cities. Its photographs should look like an architect's own record of a walk: buildings, streets and materials seen in clear daylight, with straight lines and honest surfaces. The buildings are the subject, never the people who pass them.

## Tone

- **Clear daylight.** Overcast or bright daylight with true colour. No night shots, no heavy filters, no fisheye or tilted frames. A fisheye shot of a Copenhagen junction and a blue-hour view of the Hamburg warehouses were turned down for this reason.
- **Lines and surfaces.** Facades, colonnades, roofs, stairs, kerbs and paving, framed square on or along a strong perspective line. Close details show what a material is made of: brick bond, hammered concrete, crossed timber layers, stone setts.
- **People small and incidental.** People may appear as they would in any street, but no face is the subject. The Copenhagen lead shows people on bikes from behind, as part of the street.
- **Nothing to read as the subject.** No logos, shop signs or posters as the subject. Candidates with a hotel logo across a Barcelona street, a cigarette brand and large lettering on a Hamburg doorway, a car's number plate in the middle of a Copenhagen street, and an exhibition poster beside a concrete stair were turned down. Two leads keep small, incidental lettering that is part of the architecture: the estate name cast into the Karl-Marx-Hof facade, and a small court name on a Barbican wall.

## Palette

Warm masonry and cool sky on neutral grounds:

- the reds, browns and pinks of brick, render and clinker;
- the greys and buffs of concrete, stone and paving;
- the pale gold of new timber;
- blue sky and green trees as the only strong cool colours.

These sit well beside the template's ink on warm ivory and the Meridian sample brand's plum, because the photographs carry warm, earthy colour and leave the accent to the page. Avoid photographs dominated by saturated blue or purple light.

## Subject mix

Of the 30 photographs, 10 are close details and 20 are wider scenes, so a third are close details, as the brief asks.

- **Close details (10):** the Karl-Marx-Hof render and windows, a Flemish bond brick wall, a cross-laminated timber interior, a three-layer timber panel, hammered Barbican concrete, rounded cobbles, an office facade grid, a spiral stair seen from above, the Liège-Guillemins roof ribs, and the Cluny granary roof.
- **Wider scenes (20):** the Turbine Hall, the Tate Modern exterior, the Karl-Marx-Hof front, a Vienna courtyard archway, a Barcelona superblock street, terraced houses, a Copenhagen bridge with raised cycle tracks, the Chilehaus, a Vienna council housing courtyard, the Promenade Plantée arch, the Viaduc des Arts, the Rotterdam cube houses, the Markthal end wall, an avenue of plane trees, Place Dauphine twice, the Speicherstadt canal, a Barbican colonnade, a setts street in Lübeck, and a grass tram track in Freiburg.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post and cards | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Every lead is landscape. The template crops cards to its own ratio from the same file. |
| In-body figure | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width, with a caption. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

The source files are not all 3:2. Of the 18 leads, 11 are 3:2, 4 are 4:3 (Barcelona, the timber interior, the Barbican colonnade, the Freiburg track), and 3 are wider: the terraced houses at about 17:10, the cube houses at about 12:7 and the Vienna courtyard at 16:9. A 4:3 source loses about a tenth at the top and bottom in a 3:2 crop, which costs sky or paving only. The wider sources lose a little at the sides. One body figure, the hammered Barbican concrete, is portrait; it is a texture with no edge to lose, so any crop works, but it shows tall at the text width and is the one figure to watch in the picture review.

## Phone crop

On a phone, the lead picture fills the width and is cropped from the centre. Each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Symmetric views (the Turbine Hall, the Barbican colonnade, the Speicherstadt canal, the plane tree avenue, the Chilehaus point, the Promenade Plantée arch) keep their vanishing point in the middle third.
- Textures and repeats (the office grid, the station roof, the setts, the timber interior) look the same at any crop.
- The Copenhagen bridge has its people on bikes in the middle of the cycle track; a tight crop keeps the track and its granite kerb, which the post is about.
- The terraced houses keep the join between the red and blue houses in the centre.

No photograph carries text over it, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets text over a lead picture, the darker leads (the Turbine Hall, the Barbican colonnade, the Chilehaus) need a scrim to pass AA.
