The Weekly Meridian is a journal about how cities are built and how they change. Four staff writers cover housing, streets, transit, buildings, materials and neighbourhoods, and we publish one long article most weeks.

We write for anyone who cares about the place they live: residents, councillors, students, and people who work in planning, architecture and building. You do not need to be an architect to read us. We explain every technical word the first time we use it.

## Who writes here

- **Helen Ashdown** is our editor. She spent twelve years building affordable homes and writes about housing.
- **Joost Rielle** was a transport planner. He writes about streets, cycling, buses, trams and stations.
- **Anouk Brakel** is a conservation architect. She writes about old buildings and the materials cities are made of.
- **Jonah Kettering** is an urban geographer. He writes about squares, pavements and how neighbourhoods change.

## What we write about

Each section of the journal has a one-word name: Housing, Streets, Transit, Buildings, Materials and Neighbourhoods. Good places to start are [what Vienna's council housing teaches other cities](/blog/vienna-council-housing/), [our guide to reusing old buildings](/blog/reuse-old-buildings/) and [how to read a changing neighbourhood](/blog/read-neighbourhood-change/).

## How we check what we publish

A second writer reads every article before it goes live, and the article names them. When we write about a real city or a well-known building, we visit it or work from public records, and we keep to what is widely documented. When we give advice, we say whether it comes from our own work or from common practice. Nobody pays to be mentioned, and we take no money from developers, builders or suppliers.

## How to reach us

Write to desk@example.com and one of the writers answers within three working days. The Friday letter brings each new article to your inbox. The photographs in the journal come from photographers who share their work under open licences; the [photo credits](/blog/photo-credits/) page lists each one.
