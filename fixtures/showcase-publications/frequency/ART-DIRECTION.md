# Dark Sky Hours: art direction

Dark Sky Hours is a made-up podcast about the night sky for beginners. Its pictures should look like the sky a listener could see, or the things the show talks about: dark landscapes under stars, the Moon and planets, and people at telescopes. Each one is an episode's cover, so it must read at a small square size as well as large.

## Tone

- **The night sky is the subject.** Stars, the Milky Way, a meteor, an aurora, the Moon, Saturn and the space station. People appear small, in silhouette or from behind, at a telescope. No posed portraits.
- **Dark, deep frames.** Most pictures are near-black with a few bright points, which suits the ink console they sit beside. One warm dusk picture, for the live show, breaks the run.
- **Nothing to read.** No signs, logos or captions in the picture.

## Palette

Deep blue-black skies, the white and gold of stars and planets, and the green of the aurora as the one bright colour. These sit well beside the sample brand's indigo and the ink of the console.

## Subject mix

Of the 9 photographs, 4 are space or sky details and 5 are scenes on the ground.

- **Space and sky details (4):** the Moon over Earth's air from orbit, the space station over the Earth, Saturn from the Cassini probe, an aurora over the sea.
- **Ground scenes (5):** a telescope at a star party, the Milky Way over a ridge, a meteor over pines, Joshua trees under stars, rangers beside a telescope at dusk.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Episode row | 1:1 | 1600 px wide | Cropped square by the template, shown from 600 px wide windows up. |
| On air console | 16:9 | 1600 px wide | The newest episode's picture at the top of the console. |
| Episode page | 1:1 on wide screens, 16:9 on phones | 1600 px wide | Beside the title on wide screens, under it on phones. |
| Host mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

## Phone crop

On a phone the episode picture fills the width at 16:9. The telescope, the Moon, the space station and Saturn sit in the middle of their frames, so both the square and the 16:9 crop keep them. The sky pictures are even fields of stars that read the same at any crop.
