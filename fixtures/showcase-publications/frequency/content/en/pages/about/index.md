Dark Sky Hours is a weekly podcast about the night sky for people who look up. Each Thursday, Nadia Ferris and Owen Tully take one question, such as why the Moon looks bigger on the horizon or how the space station stays up, and answer it in under an hour, often with a guest. The show, its hosts, its guests and Fellbrook Observatory are made up, to show the Frequency template.

Each post on this site is one episode with its show notes, or a short note about the show, such as a live recording.

## Who makes the show

- **Nadia Ferris** runs the public nights at Fellbrook Observatory and answers the science questions.
- **Owen Tully** is a back garden astronomer and photographer who asks what a beginner would ask.

The [hosts page](/blog/authors/) lists both of us with every episode we made.

## What we cover

Our episodes fall into five topics: [stargazing](/blog/category/stargazing/), [sky events](/blog/category/sky-events/), [planets and the Moon](/blog/category/planets-and-moon/), [spaceflight](/blog/category/spaceflight/) and [astrophotography](/blog/category/astrophotography/). Good places to start are [your first telescope](/blog/first-telescope/) and [how to find a dark sky](/blog/find-dark-sky/).

## How to follow the show

Search for Dark Sky Hours in your podcast app, or add our [feed](/blog/feed.xml) to any app that takes one. A new episode comes out every Thursday morning. In this demo, each player plays a short tone made for the demo, not a real recording.

## How to reach us

Write to hello@example.com with a question for the show, and we read every one. The photographs on this site are public domain works shared by the people who took them; the [photo credits](/blog/photo-credits/) page lists each one.
