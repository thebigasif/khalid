Most of us live under a sky that glows orange at night. From a town you might see a few hundred stars. Under a truly dark sky you can see thousands, and the Milky Way looks like a cloud. Dark sky ranger Callum Reid tells Owen and Nadia how to find one.

## In this episode

- How light from towns washes out the stars, and how far you need to drive.
- The Bortle scale, which rates a sky from 1, the darkest, to 9, a city centre.
- Dark sky places, parks and reserves that protect their night.
- How long your eyes need in the dark, and why a red torch helps.

## Rate your dark sky on the Bortle scale

The Bortle scale gives a sky a number from 1 to 9. A city centre is an 8 or a 9, where only the brightest stars show. A rural village might be a 4 or 5. Class 1 and 2 skies are rare, and you usually need to be far from any town. Light pollution maps online show the glow around you, so you can pick a spot before you set off.

## Give your eyes time

Your eyes take about twenty to thirty minutes to adjust fully to the dark. One glance at a bright phone screen undoes much of that. Callum's tip is a red torch, or a phone set to a red night mode, since red light spoils your dark vision far less than white.

## Pick the right night

Even the darkest site is washed out by a bright Moon. The best nights are in the week either side of a new Moon, when the Moon is thin or below the horizon. Check the forecast for clear skies, and bring more layers than you think you need.
