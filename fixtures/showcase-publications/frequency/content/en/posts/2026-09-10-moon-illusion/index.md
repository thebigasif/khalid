The moon illusion fools everyone. A full Moon rising over the rooftops can look enormous. A few hours later, high in the sky, it looks small again. The Moon has not changed size. Vision scientist Dr Helen Osei explains why our brains fall for it every time.

## In this episode

- Why the Moon is the same size in the sky wherever it is.
- A simple test you can do with a pencil at arm's length.
- The main ideas for why the illusion happens, and why none of them settles it.
- Why photographs of a huge Moon behind a building are a different trick.

## Test the moon illusion yourself

Hold a pencil at arm's length when the Moon is low, and note how much of the eraser the Moon covers. Do the same when it is high. The two match. The Moon always spans about half a degree of sky. If anything, a rising Moon is a little farther from you, since you are looking across part of the Earth to see it.

## Why we see it bigger

Nobody is quite sure why the moon illusion happens. One idea is that our brains treat the sky as a flattened dome, so a Moon near the horizon seems farther away, and the brain makes it look bigger to match. Another is that trees and houses beside a low Moon make it seem large by comparison. Dr Osei thinks both play a part.

## Those huge Moon photos

The giant Moons behind towers that you see online are real photographs, taken from far away with a long lens. The distance makes the tower look small next to the Moon. The eye never sees it that way.
