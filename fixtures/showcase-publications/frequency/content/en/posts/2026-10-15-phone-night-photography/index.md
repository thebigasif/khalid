Owen took his first good picture of the stars with a phone he already owned and a cheap clamp. This week he walks Nadia through night photography with a phone, from the stand you need to the settings that make the stars sharp.

## In this episode

- Why a phone needs to be perfectly still, and the cheapest way to do it.
- How night mode works: many short pictures stacked into one.
- Setting focus by hand, so the phone does not hunt in the dark.
- Why the darkest sky you can reach beats any camera upgrade.

## Night photography starts with a still phone

A night photo needs the camera to gather light for many seconds. Any movement smears the stars. A small tripod with a phone clamp costs less than a takeaway and makes the biggest single difference. Use the timer, set to three seconds, so pressing the button does not shake the phone.

## Night mode does the hard work

Most recent phones have a night mode that takes many short pictures and stacks them into one. On a tripod, many phones offer a longer exposure, sometimes up to thirty seconds. Turn the flash off, and turn off any setting that brightens the screen for the shot.

## Focus and framing

Phones struggle to focus on stars. If your camera app has a manual or pro mode, set the focus to infinity, the far end of the slider. Put something on the ground in the frame, such as a tree or a hill, so the picture has a sense of place. The [Joshua trees](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/) in this week's picture show how a simple outline lifts a photo of the sky.
