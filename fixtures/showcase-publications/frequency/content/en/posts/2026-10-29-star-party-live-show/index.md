Our first live show is coming. On Saturday 21 November 2026 we will record an episode of Dark Sky Hours in front of an audience at the Fellbrook star party, and then go outside and look at the sky together. Entry to the star party is free.

## What happens at the star party

The show starts at 6 in the evening in the observatory's lecture room and lasts about an hour. Nadia and Owen will answer your questions about the night sky, live. After the recording, local astronomers will have telescopes set up outside, and if the sky is clear you can see the Moon, Saturn and the Andromeda Galaxy.

## Send us your questions

The best questions are the ones beginners are afraid to ask. Send yours to hello@example.com before 14 November, and we will pick the ones for the show. If you are new to all this, our episodes on [choosing a telescope](/blog/first-telescope/) and [dark sky tips](/blog/find-dark-sky/) are a good place to start.

## Getting there

Fellbrook Observatory is on Observatory Lane, a short walk from the bus stop. There are no lights in the car park, on purpose, so bring a torch with a red light. Dress for a cold night, even if the day was mild.

## What to bring

- Warm layers, a hat and gloves. Standing still at a telescope gets cold fast.
- A torch with a red light, or a phone set to a red night mode.
- Binoculars, if you have them. Local astronomers will help you find things with them.
- A folding chair, if you want to stay out for the late planets.

You do not need a telescope of your own. The members of the Fellbrook astronomy club bring theirs and are happy to share the view.

## If the sky is cloudy

The live recording goes ahead whatever the weather. If clouds hide the stars, the club will show the Moon and Saturn on a screen indoors from their own recent pictures, and we will run a longer question time. If the sky clears late, the telescopes come out. Last year the clouds parted at nine, and the space station flew right over the car park. Our episode on [how the space station stays up](/blog/space-station-orbit/) explains how to catch it yourself.
