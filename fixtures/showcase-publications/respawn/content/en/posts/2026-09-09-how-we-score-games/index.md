This page explains how we score games at Lowpoly Post. Every review ends with a score out of 10, shown as a number in a dark square and a health bar of ten cells. A full cell is one point, and a half cell is half a point.

## How we score games: the play comes first

- **At least twenty hours** with the finished game, or the whole game if it is shorter.
- **On the platform most people will use**, and on a second one when we can.
- **With the settings as they come**, then again with the settings a new player would change.

We never score a game from a preview or a demo. If a game changes a lot after launch, we play it again and say so at the top of the review.

## What the scores mean

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| 9 to 10 | One of the best games of its kind. Play it. |
| 7.5 to 8.5 | A very good game with small faults. Most players will enjoy it. |
| 6 to 7 | Good ideas held back by real problems. Read the review first. |
| 4 to 5.5 | Some players will find something here. Most will not. |
| 0 to 3.5 | Broken or not worth your time. |

A score compares a game with others of its kind. A 9 for a puzzle game, such as [The Lantern Archive](/blog/lantern-archive-review/), and a 9 for a racing game mean the same thing: the best of their kind.

## Who decides

How we score games is a team job. The writer suggests a score, and the reviews editor checks it against our other reviews before it goes out. If we disagree, we play the game again. You can read more about [video game reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_review) and how other sites approach them.
