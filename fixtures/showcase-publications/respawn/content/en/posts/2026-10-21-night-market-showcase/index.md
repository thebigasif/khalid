The Night Market showcase is back for a fourth year. The volunteer-run event for small games takes place on 6 and 7 March in Port Ellery's old tram depot, with 60 tables of games you can play, and the organisers say the first day will be free to enter.

The Night Market started as a few tables in a café. Last year it filled the depot, and many of the games we later reviewed, such as [Kitebound](/blog/kitebound-review/), had their first public play there.

## What is new at the Night Market this year

- **A local co-op room**, with sofas and big screens, for games made to be played side by side.
- **Quiet hours** on Saturday morning, with lower sound and lighter crowds.
- **A table for student games**, chosen by a panel of local teachers.

## How to show a game

Any small studio can bring a game to the Night Market. Studios with fewer than ten people can apply for a table until 15 January. A table costs nothing, and the organisers lend screens and power. The cables, they told us, are the hard part: last year the volunteers laid nearly two kilometres of them.

## Why small shows matter

Big trade shows are loud and costly, so most small studios never get a table. Events like the Night Market let a two-person team stand next to its game and watch strangers play it, which teaches more than any survey. Many [indie games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_game) found their first players this way. The organisers told us that a third of last year's tables were a studio's first public showing.

## How to go

Saturday is free. Sunday tickets go on sale in January. We will be there both days, and we will run our own game night in the co-op room. Our guide to [a good couch co-op night](/blog/couch-co-op-night/) is a fair taste of what to expect, and [Hollowmere](/blog/hollowmere-release-date/) began as a Night Market table two years ago.
