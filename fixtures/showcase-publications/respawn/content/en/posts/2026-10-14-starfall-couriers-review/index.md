Starfall Couriers is a game about delivering parcels between the moons of a dying gas giant. You fly a small, slow ship with a cargo hold and a radio, and you carry letters, spare parts and sometimes people to the last few settlements in orbit. This Starfall Couriers review comes from 26 hours on PC.

For its first ten hours it is one of the most beautiful games of the year. After that, it starts to run out of road.

## Flying feels right

The ship handles like a heavy van in space. Every burn of the engine costs fuel, and fuel costs money, so you learn to plan a route that uses each moon's pull to slingshot to the next. Getting a fragile parcel to a far moon with a sliver of fuel left is a real thrill.

The cockpit is lovely. Paper notes stick to the glass, the radio picks up songs from the settlements, and the gas giant fills half the sky.

## Where Starfall Couriers runs out of road

The problem is the map. There are only nine moons, and by hour twelve you have flown every route several times. New parcels bring new stories, but the flying itself stops changing. A late chapter adds storms, which helps for an hour or two.

## A story that tries hard

The writing in Starfall Couriers is sad and sincere. Each settlement is shrinking, and the letters you carry say goodbye more often than hello. Some of it lands. Much of it repeats the same note, and the ending arrives without the weight the game has been building toward.

## Should you play it?

Play the first ten hours of Starfall Couriers, then decide. If planning a perfect route is enough for you, there is plenty here to enjoy. If you want a story that builds to a strong end, you may stop early, as we nearly did.

For something calmer with a stronger finish, try [The Lantern Archive](/blog/lantern-archive-review/), and see [what our scores mean](/blog/how-we-score-games/) for what a 7 means here. The game's slingshot routes borrow a real idea, the [gravity assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_assist) that space probes use to save fuel.
