We have run a couch co-op night at the office every month for a year. It started with two controllers and a pizza, and it now has a waiting list. This guide shares what makes a couch co-op night work, whether you play video games, board games or both.

## Keep it small

Four players is the sweet spot. With more, people wait too long for a turn. With fewer, a stuck player has no one to help them. If more friends come, set up a second screen or a board game on the side.

## Pick games that make people talk

The best co-op games give each player a different job, so they have to talk to win. [Kitebound](/blog/kitebound-review/), where one player runs and the other flies a kite, is a good example despite its faults. Games where everyone does the same thing tend to go quiet.

## Mix the old hands and the new

- **Start with a short, simple game** that a new player can learn in five minutes.
- **Pair a new player with an old hand** for the first round.
- **Let the new player hold the controller.** Advice is fine. Taking over is not.

## Plan the couch co-op night

We play for about three hours, with a break in the middle. The first hour is short games, the second is the main game of the night, and the last is whatever people ask for. Snacks go on a side table, never near the controllers. On a wet evening, a short calm game from our [rainy weekend list](/blog/games-for-a-rainy-weekend/) makes a good warm-up.

## When someone gets stuck

Every game night has a moment when one player keeps failing the same jump. Swap roles, try an easier setting, or move on to the next game. Nobody remembers the level you skipped. They do remember a good evening.
