Kitebound is a platformer for two players tied to the same kite. One player runs and jumps on the ground, and the other steers the kite in the wind above. The string between them is the whole game: pull too hard and the kite falls, let go and the runner drifts off a ledge. This Kitebound review comes from 15 hours, ten of them with a friend on the sofa.

When it works, it is a joy. Too often, it does not.

## The string is a great idea

The best levels make you talk. The runner shouts that a gap is coming, and the kite player climbs to catch the wind at the right moment. Clearing a long canyon on the last gust, both of you laughing, is one of the best co-op moments of the year.

The art is bright and soft, like paper cut-outs. Each of the six worlds has its own weather, and the wind is drawn as moving lines, so you can always see where it blows.

## The Kitebound camera fights you

Both players share one screen, and the camera struggles to keep the kite and the runner in view. In the high levels it zooms so far out that the runner is a speck. We lost more lives to the camera than to the puzzles.

## Alone, it falls flat

You can play Kitebound solo, with a computer friend on the kite. It is fine for an hour. The computer steers well but never makes the mistakes that make co-op funny, and the levels feel long without someone to talk to.

## Should you play it?

Only with a friend in the same room. Kitebound is built for two, and its best levels need both players talking. With the right partner it is worth a weekend, camera and all. Alone, it is a long and lonely climb, and the camera problems are much harder to forgive.
