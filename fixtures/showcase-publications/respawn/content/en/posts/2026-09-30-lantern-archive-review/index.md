The Lantern Archive is a puzzle game set in a vast round library at night. You carry a lantern from desk to desk, and each book you open is a puzzle about light: mirrors, shadows, coloured glass and the order in which you light the lamps. This Lantern Archive review comes from 22 hours, enough to finish it and find most of its secrets.

It is the best puzzle game we have played this year.

## Every puzzle teaches the next one

The Lantern Archive has five wings, and each wing adds one idea. The first is plain light and shadow. The second adds mirrors. By the fifth wing, you are bending light through three colours of glass to read a page that only shows in purple.

What makes it special is the order. No puzzle asks for an idea it has not shown you first, so you never feel stuck because of a rule you could not have known. When you do get stuck, a quiet hint system shows one page of your own notes, never the answer.

## The Lantern Archive is a calm place to think

There is no timer and no failure. The music is soft piano, and the library creaks around you. Between wings, a short scene shows who left the books here and why, and the small story is told well.

## A few small faults

The Lantern Archive is not perfect.

- **Late colour puzzles are hard to read** for players with colour blindness. A setting adds patterns to the glass, but it is hidden in a menu.
- **The mobile version is cramped.** It works, but some desks need careful taps.

## Should you play it?

Yes. The Lantern Archive is clever without being cruel, and it treats your time with care.
