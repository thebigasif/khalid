Cinder Valley is a farming game set on the slopes of a sleeping volcano. You arrive with a cart, a spade and a letter from an aunt you never met, and you spend the next forty hours turning ash into fields. This Cinder Valley review comes from 34 hours with the finished game on PC and six on a console.

It is the most patient game we have played this year. It never rushes you, and it rewards every hour you give it.

## The soil is the star

Most farming games treat soil as a tile that is either wet or dry. Cinder Valley gives every field three numbers: ash, water and life. Ash makes crops grow fast but burns them if you plant the wrong seed. Water washes ash away. Life, from worms, compost and old roots, slowly builds up season by season.

The result is a farm that remembers what you did to it. A field you rushed in spring is thin by autumn. A field you rested and fed for a year grows twice as much. We have not seen soil this deep since the classic [farming games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming_simulation) of the early 2000s.

## Cinder Valley is full of people

The village below your farm has fourteen people, and each one has a small story that runs over the four seasons. None of them is a quest giver with an exclamation mark. You learn what they need by talking to them and watching what they buy at the market.

The writing is warm and short. The baker's long feud with the miller, told in six scenes over a year, is the best thing in the game.

## Where it falls short

- **The last season drags.** Winter has too little to do, and the game knows it: it offers a skip button after the first week.
- **Menus are slow on a console.** Moving seeds between chests takes too many steps with a controller.
- **The map is too big for a walk.** You unlock a fast cart only in year two.

A winter update, Frostfall, is due on 11 December and promises more to do in the cold months. Our [news story on the winter update](/blog/cinder-valley-winter-update/) has the details.

## Should you play it?

Yes, if you like games that move at the speed of the seasons. Cinder Valley is not exciting, and it does not try to be. It is calm, deep and kind, and its soil system will change how you see other farming games. For more slow games, see our [Starfall Couriers review](/blog/starfall-couriers-review/). If you want a calmer puzzle game instead, read our [review of The Lantern Archive](/blog/lantern-archive-review/).
