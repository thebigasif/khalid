Gravel Kings is a rally game about one driver, one car and a long season of forest stages. There are no rivals on the road with you, only the clock, the gravel and your co-driver reading the notes. This Gravel Kings review comes from 30 hours on PC with a wheel and a few on a console with a pad.

It is the most honest rally game we have played in years.

## How Gravel Kings handles the road

Every surface feels different. Dry gravel lets the back of the car slide, wet gravel lets it slide further, and the packed dirt of a farm track grips until it suddenly does not. You feel the change through the wheel and hear it under the tyres.

The stages in Gravel Kings are long, narrow and full of small surprises: a crest that hides a bend, a puddle on the racing line, a bridge with a lip. They feel built by hand, and they reward learning them.

## A career that lets you fail

The Gravel Kings career runs over twelve events. Damage carries from stage to stage, and the service park has only so much time to fix it. One big crash can end your weekend, and the game lets it. It is harsh, but it makes every clean finish feel earned.

## What needs work

- **The menus are slow and dense.** Setting up the car takes too many screens.
- **The pad controls need tuning.** On a console the car feels twitchy until you change the settings.
- **There is little music.** Most of the time, that is fine. Between stages, it feels empty.

## Should you play it?

Yes, if you like driving games that ask you to learn. Gravel Kings will not hold your hand, but it is fair, and its stages are some of the best in the genre.
