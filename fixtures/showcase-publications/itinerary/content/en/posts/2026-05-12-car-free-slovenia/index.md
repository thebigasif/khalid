Car-free Slovenia is simple to plan, because the country is small, the buses run often and the trains go where the buses do not. We spent a week in April 2026 between Ljubljana and the two lakes in the north. This is the plan, day by day.

## Days 1 and 2: Ljubljana

Ljubljana is a capital you can cross on foot in twenty minutes. We walked along the river, climbed to the castle and ate at the open kitchen market on Friday. On day 2 we hired bikes for the Path of Remembrance, a green ring of paths around the whole city.

## Days 3 and 4: Bled

The bus to Bled takes an hour and leaves every hour. We walked the six kilometres around the lake on the first afternoon, then took a rowing boat to the island. On day 4 we walked the Vintgar gorge early, before the tour buses, and climbed Ojstrica for the view of the lake from above.

## Days 5 and 6: Bohinj

Another short bus goes on to Lake Bohinj, which is bigger and quieter than Bled. We took the cable car up Vogel for the last of the snow, then walked to the Savica waterfall. The second day brought rain, so we read in the guesthouse and walked the shore in the evening.

## Day 7: back to Ljubljana

The train from Bohinjska Bistrica runs through the hills to Jesenice, where you change for Ljubljana. We had a last lunch by the river before the airport bus.

## What car-free Slovenia cost us

The week cost us £890 for two in April 2026, without flights. Buses and trains came to £46 in April 2026. Car-free Slovenia also meant no parking fees, which at Bled are high. If you have three days more, go west over the Vršič pass and walk the [Soča trail](/blog/soca-trail/).
