Travel itinerary planning sounds strict, but ours leaves most of each day open. We plan so that we know where we sleep and how we get there, and nothing more. These are the rules we follow for every trip in this diary.

## Start with the nights

We decide how many nights the trip has, then where we sleep each night. We never stay fewer than two nights in a place, so every move gives us at least one whole day there. On our [Georgia itinerary](/blog/georgia-itinerary/) that meant three bases in ten days, not six.

## Move only by day

We look up every leg on the operator's own timetable before we book anything. A move takes half a day, however short it looks on the map, because there is always a bus to wait for and a room to find. On our [Morocco train trip](/blog/morocco-train-trip/) the longest leg took seven hours, so that day had nothing else in it.

## Keep one empty day a week

Every week of a trip has a day with no plan. It catches the late bus, the rain and the place we want to stay in longer. In [car-free Slovenia](/blog/car-free-slovenia/) the empty day fell on the one day of rain, which felt like luck but was the plan working.

## Travel itinerary planning as days

Then we write the plan as days, each with a heading: where we start, where we stay and the one thing we want to see. That is the shape of every trip post on this site. Travel itinerary planning done this way also makes the write-up easy, because the headings are already there.

## Our planning sheet

Our sheet has one row a day, with the place, the bed, the transport and the cost. It is how we know what each trip cost when we write it up. We check it against our [carry-on packing list](/blog/carry-on-packing-list/) the night before we leave. For trains, [The Man in Seat 61](https://www.seat61.com/) is where every plan starts.
