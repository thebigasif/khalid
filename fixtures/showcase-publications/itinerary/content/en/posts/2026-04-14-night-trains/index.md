Night trains save a hotel night and a day of travel, and they are the best way we know to arrive somewhere new. You fall asleep in one country and wake up in another, with the whole day ahead. These are the five sleepers we would take again, from our trips of the last six years.

## Five night trains we liked

1. **Helsinki to Rovaniemi.** Clean two-berth cabins with their own shower, and snow outside the window at breakfast.
2. **Vienna to Venice.** Leave after dinner and wake up crossing the lagoon.
3. **Colombo to Badulla.** The night version of the [hill country train](/blog/hill-country-train/), for when the day seats are full.
4. **Tbilisi to Batumi.** Cheap, slow and friendly, from the capital to the Black Sea, at the end of our [Georgia itinerary](/blog/georgia-itinerary/) if you have two days more.
5. **London to Penzance.** A small bar car and the Cornish coast at dawn.

## What a sleeper cabin is like

Most night trains have three kinds of place. A seat is cheapest, and you sleep badly. A couchette is a bunk in a shared cabin of four or six, with a sheet and a pillow. A sleeper is a cabin of one or two with a real bed, and often a basin. We book a sleeper when we can, because a good night is worth the most on the first day somewhere new.

## How to sleep on night trains

- Book a lower bunk if you can, because it is wider and you can sit up.
- Bring earplugs and an eye mask. Both are on our [carry-on packing list](/blog/carry-on-packing-list/).
- Keep your papers and phone in a small bag in the bunk with you.
- Drink water, since the air in a cabin is dry.
