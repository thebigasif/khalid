The Svaneti trek from Mestia to Ushguli takes four days and passes villages where every family once built its own stone tower. You sleep in a guesthouse each night, so you carry only a day pack. We walked it in July 2026, two months after our [Georgia itinerary](/blog/georgia-itinerary/) took us to Kazbegi.

## Day 1: Mestia to Zhabeshi

The first stage climbs out of Mestia to a ridge with the whole valley below, then drops to Zhabeshi. It is about 15 kilometres. Start early, because the climb faces east and the sun is hot by ten.

## Day 2: Zhabeshi to Adishi

A short road walk comes first, then a long climb through pine forest and over a pass to Adishi, a village of twenty houses and one shop. We arrived in rain and dried our boots by the guesthouse stove while the owner cooked.

## Day 3: Adishi to Iprali

This is the hardest day of the Svaneti trek. You wade the Adishischala river, where a man with a horse will carry you across for a few lari. Then you climb to the Chkhunderi pass under the Adishi glacier. The view from the top is the best of the walk, so stop for lunch there. The path down to Iprali is steep and loose.

## Day 4: Iprali to Ushguli

An easy last stage runs along the valley floor to Ushguli, the highest village in the valley. We had the afternoon to walk among the towers and a last dinner of kubdari, a pie filled with spiced meat.

## What we packed for the Svaneti trek

We sent our big bags ahead by minibus and carried this:

- [x] Rain jacket and a warm layer
- [x] Two litres of water each and a filter
- [x] Sun cream and a hat
- [x] Blister plasters
- [ ] Walking poles (we wished we had them on day 3)

Everything else came from our [carry-on packing list](/blog/carry-on-packing-list/), which we left in the bags we sent ahead.

## What it cost

The four days cost us £410 for two in July 2026, including guesthouses with dinner and breakfast, the river crossing and the minibus back to Mestia. For the route, the [Wikivoyage page on Svaneti](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Svaneti) is a good start.
