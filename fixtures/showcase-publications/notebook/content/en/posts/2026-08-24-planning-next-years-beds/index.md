Late August is when I plan next year's beds. The crops are at their biggest, so I can see what worked, and the seed catalogues have not arrived yet to distract me. My plan is a simple four-year crop rotation, and this note is the version I pin up in the shed.

## Why crop rotation matters

Crops in the same family share the same pests and diseases, and they take the same things from the soil. Grow potatoes in one bed every year and blight and eelworm build up there. Move each family to a new bed each year, and the pests that stayed behind find nothing to eat.

## The four groups

I split my crops into four groups, one for each of my four beds:

1. **Potatoes,** which like a bed rich in compost and break up the soil.
2. **Beans and peas,** which leave nitrogen in the soil for whatever comes next.
3. **Cabbages and kale,** which want that nitrogen and firm ground.
4. **Roots and onions:** carrots, parsnips, beetroot, onions and garlic, which prefer soil that was fed a year before, not fresh.

## The rotation table

Each group moves one bed to the right each year, and after four years it is back where it began.

| Year | Bed 1 | Bed 2 | Bed 3 | Bed 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | Potatoes | Beans and peas | Cabbages and kale | Roots and onions |
| 2028 | Roots and onions | Potatoes | Beans and peas | Cabbages and kale |
| 2029 | Cabbages and kale | Roots and onions | Potatoes | Beans and peas |
| 2030 | Beans and peas | Cabbages and kale | Roots and onions | Potatoes |

## Feeding the beds

The bed that will grow potatoes gets the most compost in autumn, from the [four compost bins](/blog/four-compost-bins/). The roots bed gets only [leaf mould](/blog/leaf-mould-in-two-winters/), because fresh compost makes carrots fork. The cabbage bed gets a dressing of lime every fourth year, which helps against clubroot.

## What does not fit

Squash, courgettes, tomatoes and sweetcorn do not fit neatly, so they go in a fifth, smaller bed that swaps with the potato bed. Perennials such as rhubarb and asparagus stay put. For a longer guide, the [allotment society's rotation sheet](https://www.example.com/allotment-society/crop-rotation) covers five and six bed plans too.
