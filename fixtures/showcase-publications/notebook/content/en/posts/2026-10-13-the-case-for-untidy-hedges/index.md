Untidy hedges are better hedges. That is the whole argument of a short essay in the Hedgerow Notes newsletter this month, and it is the best thing I have read on the subject. It took me ten minutes, and it changed how our allotment society cuts the long hawthorn hedge on the east side.

## Why untidy hedges feed more

A hedge cut hard every autumn loses most of its flowers and berries, because hawthorn and blackthorn flower on wood that grew the year before. Cut it every year and you cut off next year's food. The essay shows this with counts from one farm: a hedge cut every three years carried almost three times as many berries as the same hedge cut every year.

Untidy hedges also give cover. A thick, tall hedge with a wide base shelters hedgehogs, wrens and overwintering insects. A neat box shape has nowhere to hide.

## What we changed on the plot

At the October meeting I read out two paragraphs, and the society agreed to three changes:

- **Cut in late winter, not in autumn.** We now wait until February, so the birds get the berries first. The [fieldfares](/blog/fieldfares-in-the-hawthorn/) came through this week and stripped half the hedge in two days.
- **Cut one side a year.** The east face this winter, the west face next, the top every third year.
- **Leave a wide base.** We stopped strimming the grass at the foot of the hedge. It is now a strip of long grass and nettles, and the frogs moved in by June.

## Who should read it

If you look after a hedge, on an allotment, at a school or at home, read this before you book the cutter. It is short, the numbers are clear, and it does not ask you to do more work, only less, at a better time.

[The essay](https://www.example.com/hedgerow/untidy) is free on the newsletter's site. I liked it enough to send it to everyone on the [spring seed swap](/blog/a-seed-library-worth-copying/) list as well.
