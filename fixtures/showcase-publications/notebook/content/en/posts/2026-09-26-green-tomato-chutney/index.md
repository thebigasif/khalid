Every September there comes a week when the tomato plants are tired, the nights are cold and two kilos of green fruit will never ripen. That week I make green tomato chutney. It is the best use of them, and by Christmas it is better than anything made with red tomatoes.

## The green tomato chutney recipe

This makes about six jars.

- 2 kg green tomatoes, chopped small
- 500 g cooking apples, peeled and chopped
- 500 g onions, chopped
- 250 g sultanas
- 400 g light brown sugar
- 500 ml cider vinegar
- 1 tablespoon salt
- 1 tablespoon mustard seeds
- 1 thumb of ginger, grated

Put everything in a big pan with a heavy base. Bring it slowly to a simmer, stirring until the sugar is gone. Then let it cook, uncovered, for about two hours. Stir it every ten minutes, and more often near the end, when it starts to catch on the bottom.

## How to tell when it is ready

Drag a wooden spoon across the bottom of the pan. If the line stays open for a second before the chutney closes over it, it is done. If vinegar runs into the line straight away, give it another twenty minutes.

## Jars and keeping

Wash the jars and lids in hot soapy water, then dry them in the oven at 140 degrees for fifteen minutes. Fill them while both the jars and the chutney are hot, and close the lids at once.

Leave the chutney for at least six weeks before you open a jar. The vinegar softens and the flavours mix. Unopened, it keeps for a year in a cool cupboard. Once opened, keep the jar in the fridge.

## Why it works

Green tomatoes are sharp and firm, so they hold their shape through two hours of cooking, where red ones turn to sauce. The apples help the chutney set. This year the apples came from [apple day](/blog/apple-day-at-the-orchard/), and the onions were from my own beds, planned in August with the [crop rotation](/blog/planning-next-years-beds/).
