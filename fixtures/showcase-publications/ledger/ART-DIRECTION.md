# The Ledger Review: art direction

The Ledger Review is a made-up journal of personal finance research for ordinary households. Ledger is a text-led template: its lists show no pictures, and a post shows one lead picture under its byline, never cropped. So the photographs here are few and quiet. They should look like the things on a kitchen table on the evening a household sits down to do its sums: paper, pencils, a few coins, a key, a jar, a pair of glasses.

## Tone

- **Calm and plain.** Soft daylight or even studio light, a single subject and plenty of empty ground around it. Nothing dramatic, no hard flash, no low-light blur.
- **Objects, not people.** No face is the subject of a picture. Hands do not appear either; the objects stand for the household.
- **Ordinary things.** Pencils, lined paper, a cash box, preserving jars, a door key, an old hourglass. Nothing that looks like wealth, such as piles of banknotes or gold.
- **Nothing to read.** No brand names, logos, watermarks, price tags or readable text. Banknotes with readable values are never the subject. Candidates turned down for this reason: a key hanging beside an appliance logo, a shop shelf of piggy banks with price labels, a coin jar with a banknote showing its value, a calculator over handwritten sums, a wallet showing a driving licence and a hand holding a single named coin.
- **No symbols of speculation.** A candidate with crypto coins on banknotes was turned down: the Review never discusses or recommends investments of any kind.

## Palette

Paper whites, pencil yellows, pale wood and soft greys, with small warm notes from copper coins, a red-roofed model house and a basket of vegetables. These sit well beside the template's ink green and the Ledger sample brand's petrol blue, because the photographs bring little colour of their own and leave the page colour to the template. Avoid saturated grounds; the one exception is the red cloth under the coins on the account fees post, which is the warmest picture in the set.

## Subject mix

Of the 22 photographs, 17 are close details and 5 are wider scenes, so more than three quarters are close details (the brief asks for at least a third).

- **Close details (17):** a pencil on lined paper, a stack of plain window envelopes, an old cash box with its key, a stack of blank paper and a pen, five stacks of coins, an hourglass, a model house on a calculator, a pencil on a legal pad, empty preserving jars, a red pencil in a metal sharpener, a short leaning stack of coins, an open notebook with a pencil, a pencil, an eraser and a mug on a white table, a door key on a tree stump, reading glasses on a table, coins on red cloth, a basket of vegetables.
- **Wider scenes (5):** knitted jars by a window, a house with green shutters, a desk with a calculator and laptop, a street of terraced houses, a kitchen and dining table.

Only lead pictures appear on the front page and in social cards. The five wider scenes are all either a body figure or the lead of a post whose subject is a room or a desk.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post | As shot, never cropped | 2000 px wide | Ledger shows the lead at the text width in a light frame, uncropped, so each lead keeps its own ratio. |
| Lead picture, social card and feed | 3:2 crop from the centre | 2000 px wide | Used outside the template, such as in link previews. |
| In-body figure | As shot | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width, about 40rem, with a caption. Five posts have one. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn in one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

Of the 17 leads, 7 are 3:2, 7 are 4:3, and three are wider: the payslip paper (10:7), the preserving jars (5:3) and the coins on red cloth (about 16:9). Because Ledger never crops a lead, these differences only change the height of the frame. Within the post column, the tallest lead (4:3) is about 30rem tall on a wide screen, which keeps the first paragraph within reach of the fold.

## Phone crop

On a phone, the lead fills the column width and keeps its ratio, so nothing is cut. For the 3:2 crops used outside the template, each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Single subjects (the cash box, the hourglass, the model house, the key, the glasses, the leaning coins) sit in the middle third and survive a tight crop.
- Repeating or flat subjects (lined paper, the legal pad, stacked paper, the preserving jars, the coins on cloth) look the same at any crop.
- The pencil on lined paper runs across the whole frame; a centre crop keeps its middle and point, which is enough.

No photograph carries text, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets text over a lead, the darker leads (the cash box, the vegetable basket, the coins on red cloth) need a scrim to pass AA.
