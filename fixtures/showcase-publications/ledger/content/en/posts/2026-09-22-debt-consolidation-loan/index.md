A debt consolidation loan is cheaper only if its total cost, including fees, is lower than what you would pay by clearing your current debts over the same time. A lower interest rate is not enough on its own. In our worked example, a loan at 11 in 100 saves about 1,060 over three years against two credit cards, but the same loan stretched over five years costs about 50 more than the cards.

A consolidation loan replaces several debts with one. You borrow enough to pay them all off, then repay the new loan in fixed monthly sums. When I was a debt adviser, I saw it work well for some households and badly for others. The difference was almost always the length of the loan and what happened to the cleared cards.

**This is not financial advice.** The debts, rates and fees are made up. Before you borrow, compare the full cost of any offer in writing, and consider talking to a free debt advice service.

## The debts in this example

| Debt | Balance | Interest a year |
|---|---:|---|
| Credit card one | 5,000 | 22 in 100 |
| Credit card two | 3,000 | 19 in 100 |
| **Total** | **8,000** | |

The household pays 300 a month across both cards: the minimum on the second card and the rest on the first, which charges more. At that rate both cards are cleared in 36 months, and the household pays 2,788 in interest.

## The debt consolidation loan on offer

A lender offers a debt consolidation loan of 8,000 at 11 in 100 a year, with an arrangement fee of 300. The household can choose a term of three, four or five years.

## The full cost, side by side

| Option | Monthly payment | Months | Interest | Fee | Total cost of borrowing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Keep the cards, pay 300 a month | 300 | 36 | 2,788 | 0 | 2,788 |
| Loan over 3 years | 262 | 36 | 1,429 | 300 | 1,729 |
| Loan over 4 years | 207 | 48 | 1,925 | 300 | 2,225 |
| Loan over 5 years | 174 | 60 | 2,436 | 300 | 2,736 |

Over three years the loan saves about 1,060 and the monthly payment falls by 38. That is a good result.

Over five years the monthly payment falls by 126, which feels like a big relief. But interest runs for two more years, so the saving shrinks to about 50. Change the example a little, such as a fee of 400 or a rate of 12 in 100, and the five-year loan costs more than the cards. A lower monthly payment is not the same as a cheaper debt.

## The check, in four steps

You can run this check on any debt consolidation loan offer with a calculator.

1. **Add up what you pay now.** Total balance, each rate and what you pay each month.
2. **Work out how long your current debts take** at your current monthly payment, and the interest you would pay. Our study of [card minimum payments](/blog/credit-card-minimum-payments/) shows this month by month.
3. **Ask for the loan's total amount repayable,** including every fee. Lenders must usually show it before you sign.
4. **Compare like with like.** If the loan runs longer than your current plan, compare total cost, not monthly payment.

Look at the APR as well as the interest rate, because the APR includes most fees. Our guide to [what the APR on a loan tells you](/blog/apr-interest-rate/) explains the difference.

## The risk nobody puts in the table

:::callout{kind=warning}
The biggest risk of a debt consolidation loan is not in the arithmetic. Once the cards are cleared, they are empty and still open. If they fill up again, the household has the loan and new card debt at the same time.
:::

In my years as an adviser, this was the most common way consolidation went wrong. Three habits protect against it:

- **Close or lower the limit on cleared cards,** unless you need one for emergencies.
- **Keep a small emergency fund** so the next repair does not go on a card.
- **Keep paying the old amount.** If you were paying 300 and the loan costs 262, pay the 38 difference into savings, not into spending.

## When a debt consolidation loan is not the answer

A debt consolidation loan only moves debt. It does not reduce it. If your debts are growing because spending is higher than income, a loan will clear them for a while and they will come back. In that case, start with the budget, then the repayment plan.

If you can repay your debts on their current terms in a reasonable time, the simpler path may be to pay them in a set order. We compare the two main orders in [the debt avalanche and snowball on three debts](/blog/debt-avalanche-snowball/). For the [general idea of debt consolidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_consolidation), the reference pages are a useful start.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Compare total cost,** including fees, not the monthly payment.
- **A shorter loan** saved about 1,060 in our example; a longer one saved almost nothing.
- **Cleared cards are a risk,** so close them or lower their limits.
- **A loan moves debt,** it does not reduce it.
:::
