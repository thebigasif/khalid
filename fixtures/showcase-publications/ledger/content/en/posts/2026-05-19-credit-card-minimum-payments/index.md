Paying only the minimum payments on a credit card takes years because the minimum shrinks as the balance shrinks, so most of each payment goes on interest. In our worked example, a balance of 3,000 paid at the minimum takes 176 months, nearly fifteen years, and costs 4,640 in interest. Paying a fixed 150 a month instead clears it in 26 months for 870 in interest.

I saw this pattern often as a debt adviser. People had paid every month, on time, for years, and the balance had hardly moved. Nobody had done anything wrong; the minimum is simply designed to keep the account in good standing, not to clear it. This post shows the arithmetic so you can see it on your own statement.

:::callout{kind=note}
**This is not financial advice.** The balance, rate and minimum rule are made up for the example. Your card's terms are on your statement and in your agreement.
:::

## The card in this example

- **Balance:** 3,000, with no new spending.
- **Interest:** 24 in 100 a year, charged as 2 in 100 each month on the balance.
- **Minimum payment:** 3 in 100 of the balance, or 25, whichever is larger.

Many cards use a rule like this, though the exact numbers vary, and [credit card interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card_interest) is worked out in much the same way everywhere. Some add the month's interest to a smaller share. The pattern below holds for all of them.

## Why minimum payments shrink

The minimum is a share of the balance. As the balance falls, the minimum falls with it, so the next month you pay less, and the balance falls more slowly.

| Month | Interest added | Minimum paid | Balance after payment | Interest paid so far |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 60 | 92 | 2,968 | 60 |
| 2 | 59 | 91 | 2,937 | 119 |
| 3 | 59 | 90 | 2,906 | 178 |
| 12 | 53 | 82 | 2,640 | 679 |
| 24 | 47 | 72 | 2,323 | 1,277 |
| 60 | 32 | 49 | 1,583 | 2,674 |
| 120 | 17 | 26 | 835 | 4,085 |

In the first month, 60 of the 92 paid goes on interest and only 32 reduces the balance. After a year, the balance has fallen by 360, and 679 has gone on interest. After ten years, 835 is still owed. From month 124 the minimum stays at the floor of 25, and the last 800 or so takes another four years.

## What a fixed payment changes

The fix is simple to state: choose a fixed sum and pay it every month, even as the balance falls. The payment then stops shrinking, so more of it reduces the debt each month.

| Monthly payment | Months to clear | Years | Total interest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Minimum only | 176 | 14.7 | 4,640 |
| Fixed 100 | 47 | 3.9 | 1,627 |
| Fixed 150 | 26 | 2.2 | 870 |
| Fixed 200 | 19 | 1.6 | 602 |
| Fixed 300 | 12 | 1.0 | 381 |

A fixed 100, only a little more than the first minimum of 92, cuts the time from nearly fifteen years to under four, and saves about 3,000 in interest. The biggest gain comes from that first step: stop the payment from shrinking.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Set the fixed payment as a standing order or direct debit for a set amount, not "minimum payment". Then check once a year whether you can raise it.
:::

## Reading your own statement

Many card statements now print a box showing how long the balance would take at the minimum and how much interest that would cost. Look for it near the payment details. If yours has none, you can work it out month by month as we did above, or ask the lender.

Check two more things on the statement:

1. **The interest rate,** usually shown as an APR. Our guide to [APR and interest rates](/blog/apr-interest-rate/) explains what that number includes.
2. **Any promotional rate and its end date.** A low rate that ends next month changes the sums quickly.

## When you have more than one card

With several cards, pay at least the minimum on every one, then put your fixed extra on one target card. Which card first is a choice between two methods, set side by side in our study of [the debt avalanche and snowball on three debts](/blog/debt-avalanche-snowball/).

If the total feels too large for any plan, you may want a single loan with a lower rate. That can help, but only when the sums work; we show how to check in [is a consolidation loan cheaper](/blog/debt-consolidation-loan/). And a free debt advice service can look at everything with you, at no cost.

The minimum shrinks for the same reason savings grow: interest is worked out on a balance that changes each month. The other side of that arithmetic is set out in [compound interest explained with one jar](/blog/compound-interest-explained/).
