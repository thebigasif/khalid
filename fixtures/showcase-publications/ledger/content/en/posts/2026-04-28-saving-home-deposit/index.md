Saving a home deposit of 30,000 takes seven and a half years at 300 a month, a little under five years at 500 a month and three years at 800 a month, in our worked example with modest interest. If house prices rise while you save, each of those times gets longer, because the deposit you need grows too. This post sets out both sums so you can plan with the real target, not today's.

The figures use made-up round numbers: a home priced at 300,000 and a deposit of one tenth of the price. Lenders in different countries ask for different deposits, so put your own target in place of ours.

:::callout{kind=note}
**This is not financial advice.** Interest rates, price rises and deposit rules below are assumptions for the arithmetic. Check what lenders where you live actually ask for.
:::

## The target in this example

A deposit, also called a [down payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_payment), is the share of the price you pay yourself; a mortgage covers the rest. In our example the home costs 300,000 and the deposit is one tenth, so the target is 30,000.

A home deposit is not the only cash you need on the day. Legal fees, surveys, taxes and moving costs come on top, and we add them up in [home buying costs beyond the deposit](/blog/home-buying-costs/). Plan for both pots from the start.

## How long a home deposit takes at three saving rates

We assumed the savings earn interest of 3 in 100 a year, added monthly, and that the target stays at 30,000.

| Saving each month | Months to reach 30,000 | Years | Paid in by you | Added by interest |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 | 90 | 7.5 | 27,000 | about 3,200 |
| 500 | 56 | 4.7 | 28,000 | about 2,000 |
| 800 | 36 | 3.0 | 28,800 | about 1,300 |

Interest helps a home deposit more the longer you save, because each payment has longer to grow. At 300 a month it adds about a tenth of the deposit. The idea is set out in [compound interest explained with one jar](/blog/compound-interest-explained/).

## When prices rise while you save

The home deposit target in the table above stands still. In real life it rarely does. If home prices rise, the deposit you need rises with them.

We ran the same sums with prices rising by 3 in 100 a year, so the target grows from 30,000 at the same pace.

| Saving each month | Months with a fixed target | Months with a rising target | Deposit needed by then |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 | 90 | 115 | about 39,800 |
| 500 | 56 | 65 | about 35,200 |
| 800 | 36 | 40 | about 33,100 |

At 300 a month the wait grows by more than two years. The slower you save, the more a moving target costs you, because it has longer to move. That is why small increases in the monthly sum make a large difference.

## Ways to shorten the wait

There is no trick that makes a home deposit appear. There are four honest levers, and the tables above show what each one does.

1. **Save more each month.** Moving from 300 to 500 cut the wait by almost three years in the fixed-target example.
2. **Lower the target.** A cheaper home or a smaller first home lowers the deposit in direct proportion.
3. **Earn a little more interest.** Keep the deposit in a savings account that pays interest and does not tempt you to spend it.
4. **Protect the pot.** Build an emergency fund first, so a car repair never comes out of the deposit.

The fourth lever is easy to forget. Our guide to [how much to keep for emergencies](/blog/emergency-fund-size/) suggests a starter fund of 1,000 before anything else. Cash also loses value when prices rise faster than its interest, which [our study of inflation and cash savings](/blog/inflation-cash-savings/) works through over five years.

## A plan you can write down today

1. Write down the price of the kind of home you want, and the deposit share lenders ask for.
2. Add the buying costs on top, so you know the full cash target.
3. Divide the target by what you can save each month. That is your fixed-target time.
4. Add a margin for rising prices. In our example, about a sixth more time at 500 a month.
5. Check progress once a year and update the target with current prices.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **At 500 a month** a 30,000 deposit took 56 months with a fixed target.
- **Rising prices** stretched that to 65 months.
- **The slower you save,** the more a moving target costs.
- **Protect the deposit** with a separate emergency fund.
:::
