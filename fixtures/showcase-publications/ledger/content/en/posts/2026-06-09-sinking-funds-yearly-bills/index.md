Sinking funds are small pots of money you fill a little each month for a cost you know is coming, such as car insurance or birthday presents. In our worked example, eight yearly costs add up to 3,780, so setting aside 315 a month covers them all without a bad month. The name is old: governments once used a [sinking fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_fund) to pay off a debt slowly, and households use the same idea in reverse.

The costs that wreck a budget are rarely surprises. The car insurance renews every year. Birthdays fall on the same date. What makes them hurt is that they arrive in one lump, in a month with no room for them. A sinking fund spreads each one across the year.

## The difference from an emergency fund

An emergency fund is for costs you cannot predict, such as a lost job or a burst pipe. Sinking funds are for costs you can see on the calendar. Keeping them apart matters: if birthday presents come out of the emergency fund, it will be empty when a real emergency arrives. Our post on [working out an emergency fund](/blog/emergency-fund-size/) covers the other pot.

## List every yearly cost

Start by going through last year's bank statements and writing down every cost that came once or twice a year. This made-up household of two adults and a child found eight.

| Yearly cost | Due in | Amount a year | Set aside each month |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Car insurance | March | 540 | 45 |
| Home insurance | September | 240 | 20 |
| Car service and tyres | May | 420 | 35 |
| Gifts and celebrations | Through the year, most in December | 600 | 50 |
| Dentist and glasses | Through the year | 300 | 25 |
| School trips and uniforms | August | 360 | 30 |
| Holidays | July | 1,200 | 100 |
| Yearly subscriptions | January | 120 | 10 |
| **Total** | | **3,780** | **315** |

Without the list, this household thought its yearly costs were "about a thousand". The real figure was nearly four times that. Most people find the same when they write the list down.

## How much to set aside each month

For a cost a full year away, divide the amount by twelve. For a cost due sooner, divide by the months left until it is due, then drop to the yearly rate once it is paid.

| Cost | Amount | Months until due | Each month until then | Each month after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Car insurance | 540 | 4 | 135 | 45 |
| Car service and tyres | 420 | 6 | 70 | 35 |
| Holidays | 1,200 | 10 | 120 | 100 |

The first year costs more, because you are catching up. From the second year on, each fund only needs its steady monthly sum.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If the catch-up year is too tight, pay one bill the old way this year and start its fund the day after it is paid. Next year it will be ready.
:::

## Where to keep sinking funds

You do not need eight accounts. Most households keep sinking funds in one savings account and track each fund on a list or a spreadsheet. Some banks let you split one account into named pots, which shows the funds without any extra arithmetic.

What matters is that the money is not in your everyday account, where it looks like spare cash.

Check the balance of each fund once a month, on the same day you check your budget. If a fund is short because a bill arrived early or cost more than last year, top it up from the next month's plan rather than from the emergency fund. And when a fund ends the year with money left over, move the surplus to the fund that ran shortest, or let it start next year's pot early.

## Fitting sinking funds into a budget

In a zero-based budget, sinking funds are one line, paid on payday like rent. In our [three budgeting methods compared](/blog/budgeting-methods-compared/), the household set that line at 75 and found it was far too low once it wrote its yearly list. Starting from the real list, the line would be 315.

That number can feel large. Two things make it easier. First, it replaces money you were already spending, just in lumps. Second, the list itself often shows a cost worth cutting, such as a subscription nobody uses.

Housing brings its own yearly costs too, such as building insurance and maintenance, which first-time buyers often miss. We list them in [home buying costs beyond the deposit](/blog/home-buying-costs/).

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Sinking funds** spread once-a-year costs across twelve months.
- **Write the list** from last year's statements; it is usually longer than you think.
- **Divide by the months left,** then by twelve once the cost is paid.
- **Keep them apart** from your emergency fund and your everyday account.
:::
