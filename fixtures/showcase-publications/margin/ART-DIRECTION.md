# Nell Ashby: art direction

Nell Ashby is a made-up essayist who lives on the coast. Each essay has one lead photograph that sets its mood. The photographs are quiet and plain, like the essays: a path, a beach, a window, a table. They show places and things, not people.

## Tone

- **Quiet subjects.** A path, the shore at low tide, a bird, rain on glass, an empty café, wool, tomatoes, two envelopes. One subject per picture, with room around it.
- **Plain light.** Daylight or overcast, true colour. No heavy filters.
- **No faces and nothing to read.** No person is the subject of a picture, and no sign or label a reader could read at card size.

## Palette

Cool sea colours and soft greens that sit beside the sample brand's ink blue and the template's warm paper:

- the greys and blues of sky, sea and wet sand;
- the greens of ferns, weed and garden leaves;
- one warm note at a time: the wood of a table, the blue of the wool.

## Subject mix

Five walking and weather essays (path, tide, bird, rain, winter sea), two on reading and writing (café, letters) and two on home and habit (wool, tomatoes).

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | On a phone |
|---|---|---|
| The lead essay on the front page | 3:2, cropped to fill | The full width of the screen, cropped from the centre |
| An essay's lead picture | The photo's own ratio, 4:3 or 3:2 | The full width, uncropped |
| Rows in lists and related essays | No picture | No picture |

Only the newest essay shows a picture on the front page. The other rows are text, so the front page stays light on a phone.
