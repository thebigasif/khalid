Living by the sea, I learned to read tide tables before I learned the bus times. The beach below my house is only walkable for about three hours either side of low water. Miss that window and the path along the sand is under the sea.

Tide tables are the most honest deadlines I know. The water does not care about my plans. It comes in when it comes in, and the time is printed weeks ahead.

## Reading a week of tide tables

Here is the week I wrote this essay, as my tide tables printed it, and what each low tide meant for the walk.[^1]

| Day | Low water | Height | What it meant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 07:12 | 0.8 m | Walk before breakfast, write after |
| Tuesday | 07:58 | 0.6 m | The best tide of the week |
| Wednesday | 08:41 | 0.6 m | Walk late, start writing at ten |
| Thursday | 09:25 | 0.7 m | Same again |
| Friday | 10:08 | 0.9 m | Walk at lunch, write in the morning |
| Saturday | 10:52 | 1.2 m | The rocks stay covered |
| Sunday | 11:37 | 1.5 m | No beach walk, the cliff path instead |

The low tide moves about forty minutes later each day. Over a fortnight it walks right round the clock.

## What the tide taught me about deadlines

The first lesson was that a fixed time is a gift. When the low water is at 07:58, I do not decide when to walk. The decision is made, and my energy goes into the walk itself.

The second lesson was about drift. A deadline that moves a little each day is easy to ignore until it has moved a long way. Projects drift the same way. So I plan my writing week on Sunday, with the tide tables open, and I put the writing hours around the walks instead of the other way round.[^2]

The third lesson is the plainest one. Some days the beach is closed to you. On a high tide Sunday I take the cliff path, and some weeks the essay does not come. Both come back.

More on the walk itself is in [my morning walk on the coast path](/blog/walking-same-path/), and on bad weather weeks in [a week of rain](/blog/a-week-of-rain/). The UK's official tide predictions come from the [UK Hydrographic Office](https://www.admiralty.co.uk/).

[^1]: Times are for the harbour nearest the house, in local time. Never trust a tide table for a different harbour, even a near one.
[^2]: I keep the week on one index card. If it does not fit on the card, the week is too full.
