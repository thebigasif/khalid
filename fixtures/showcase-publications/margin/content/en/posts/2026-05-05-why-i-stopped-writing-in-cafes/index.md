For ten years I did all my writing in cafés. I had a favourite table, a favourite chair and a coffee order the staff knew before I reached the counter. Last year I stopped writing in cafés, and I want to set down why, because I loved it.

## What writing in cafés gave me

A café is a room full of people who expect nothing from you. That is a rare thing. At home there is always a washing machine to empty. In a café the only job is the page.

There is also the noise. Not silence, but a soft wash of cups and talk that covers the voice in your head saying the sentence is bad. A [2012 study of café noise](https://doi.org/10.1086/665048) found that a moderate level of noise helped people on some kinds of creative task.[^1] I did not need a study. I could feel it.

## Why I stopped

The café changed before I did. It got busy. Then it got a queue, then music, then a sign asking people with laptops to give up their table at lunch. That was fair. It was their café, not my office.

But mostly I changed. My essays got longer and slower, and slow work needs a desk you can leave things on. I wanted my notes spread out. I wanted to stop for an hour, walk, and come back to the same mess. You cannot leave a mess on a café table.[^2]

## Where I write now

I write at the kitchen table, by the window that looks at the sea. I keep a cheap radio on low for the noise. I make one coffee, the way the café made it, and I take the first hour as if I were still out.

If you are thinking about writing in cafés, try it. It is a fine way to start. Just notice when the place stops fitting the work. For more on where the ideas come from, see [why a daily walk feeds the essays](/blog/walking-same-path/), and on writing by hand, [letters I never sent](/blog/letters-i-never-sent/). The winter months, when I write most, are in [the sea in winter](/blog/the-sea-in-winter/).

[^1]: The best known is a 2012 paper in the *Journal of Consumer Research* by Mehta, Zhu and Cheema on ambient noise and creative thinking.
[^2]: I tried once. When I came back from the bathroom my notes had been tidied into a neat pile by a kind waiter.
