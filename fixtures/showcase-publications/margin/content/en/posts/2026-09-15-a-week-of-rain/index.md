It rained for seven days in September. Not showers. Steady, sideways rain off the sea that made the coast path into a stream. For the first time in six years I did not walk for a whole week, and I want to write about what a week of rain did to everything else.

## The first three days of a week of rain

On day one I was glad. A day off. I read in the morning, wrote in the afternoon and felt efficient.

On day two the writing went flat. Every sentence I wrote was correct and dead. I did not connect it to the walk at first. I blamed the essay.

By day three I was restless in a way I recognise from winters as a child. I tidied a cupboard. I rearranged the books by colour, which is a sign that something is wrong.[^1]

## What I did instead of the walk

On day four I tried walking round the house. It sounds silly, and it was. But the rhythm helped a little. On day five I walked to the end of the harbour wall in full waterproofs and came back soaked after ten minutes, and wrote better that afternoon than all week.

That was the lesson of a week of rain. The walk was never about the view. It was about moving my body while my head wandered. Ten minutes in the rain did more than an hour of tidying.[^2]

## Going out anyway

Owen Rees, who keeps [his father's allotment](/blog/my-fathers-allotment/), now writes the weather in his notebook for the same reason. I keep a rain plan instead. On wet days I walk the short loop through the village instead of the dunes, with the hood up and no notebook. It is not the same walk. It does the same job.

The usual walk is in [why I walk one route every day](/blog/walking-same-path/), and the tides that rule it are in [what tide tables teach](/blog/what-tide-tables-teach/). The [Met Office](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/) explains why autumn rain on this coast comes in long spells.

[^1]: The books went back by author the next week. Sorting by colour is pretty for a day and useless for a year.
[^2]: My neighbour saw me come back and asked if I had fallen in the harbour. I had not, but it was close.
