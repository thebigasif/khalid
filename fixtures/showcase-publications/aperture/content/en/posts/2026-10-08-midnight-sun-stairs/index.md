Midnattsoltrappa, the midnight sun stairs, climbs a rocky hill above the village of Lavangen in northern Norway. In June and July people walk up to watch the sun circle the sky without setting. By early August the sun dips below the horizon again, but the evenings stay light until late. This picture was made at half past eight.

## Lines that climb

The stairs run straight up the middle of the frame and disappear among the birches. Their handrails make two lines that meet in the distance, and the eye climbs with them. The grey rock on the left and the green trees on the right frame the stairs like curtains.

## A low camera and a slow shutter

The camera stood on the bottom step, pointing up. At 24 mm the near steps look wide and the far ones narrow, which shows how steep the climb is. The evening light under the trees was soft, so the shutter slowed to 1/15 of a second at f/13. A small tripod held the camera still.

## Light that lasts

In the far north in summer, the golden hour is not an hour. The sun moves along the horizon instead of dropping below it, so warm light can last most of the evening. That gives time to try a picture from several places.

## Walking the midnight sun stairs

The stairs start near the shore and take about twenty minutes to climb. You can read about the [midnight sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_sun) and how far north it reaches. Later the same evening we crossed [a hanging bridge in Spansdalen](/blog/hanging-bridge-night/).

The midnight sun stairs are steep, so take your time and rest on the way. The view from the top looks over the fjord toward the mountains, and in June the midnight sun stairs fill with people waiting for midnight. In August you may have them to yourself. For another path through birch and grass, see [duckboards at the foot of Saana](/blog/duckboards-saana/), and for a misty morning, [morning light in Villotta](/blog/villotta-morning-light/).
