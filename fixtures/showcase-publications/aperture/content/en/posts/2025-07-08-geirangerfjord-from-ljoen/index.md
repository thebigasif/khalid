The Ljøen viewpoint sits on a bend of the mountain road above Hellesylt in western Norway. From here you look straight down Sunnylvsfjorden, and on a clear day you can see where the Geirangerfjord turns off to the south. This picture was made late in the morning, with soft cloud over the peaks.

## Scale in a big view

A fjord seen from above can look flat in a picture. The mountains lose their height and the water loses its depth. What brings the scale back is something small that everyone knows the size of. Here it is the farm shed with its green roof and the red tractor beside it.

The tractor is also the only warm colour in the frame. Everything else is green, blue and grey. A small spot of red pulls the eye, and then the eye travels up the fjord from it.

## The settings

The lens was set to 24 mm, which is close to how wide the scene feels when you stand there. The aperture was f/13 for depth, from the gravel at the bottom to the snow on the far peaks. A shutter of 1/30 of a second is slow for a hand-held picture, so the camera rested on the viewpoint's railing.

## When to see the Geirangerfjord

Morning light falls along the fjord rather than across it, so the water stays dark and green instead of shining. By the afternoon the sun is in front of you. The [Geirangerfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geirangerfjord) is on the World Heritage list, and cruise ships fill it in July, so June mornings are the calmest time.

On the same trip we also stopped below [Kleivafossen in the June melt](/blog/kleivafossen-june-melt/).

The Geirangerfjord is one of the most photographed places in Norway, so it is worth looking for a view that is your own. A working farm in the foreground does that here. It reminds you that people live and work above the Geirangerfjord, not only visit it.
