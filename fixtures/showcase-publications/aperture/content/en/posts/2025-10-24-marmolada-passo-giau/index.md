Passo Giau is a high road pass between Cortina d'Ampezzo and Colle Santa Lucia. From the top you can see the Marmolada, the highest mountain in the Dolomites, with fresh October snow on its north face. This picture was made a little before eleven in the morning, between two banks of cloud.

## A long lens for a big mountain

It sounds strange to use a long lens for a mountain view. But a wide lens makes far things small, and the Marmolada is many kilometres away. At 130 mm the far peak and the near ridge seem to stand close together. The brown grass of the ridge and the white snow behind it become two layers of one picture.

This effect is called compression. It does not really change the shape of things. It only crops in tight, so the far and near parts fill the frame at the sizes they have from where you stand.

## Fast shutter, still air

The shutter was 1/800 of a second at f/8. On a high pass the wind can shake a long lens, so a fast shutter keeps the snow edges crisp. The light was bright, so there was no need to raise the ISO above 100.

## Getting to Passo Giau

The road over the pass is open in summer and early autumn, and there is parking at the top. You can read about the [Marmolada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmolada) and its shrinking glacier. For a quieter morning in the same mountains, see [Morning in Villotta](/blog/villotta-morning-light/).

Passo Giau is also a good place to wait. Cloud moves fast over the passes in autumn, and a peak that is hidden one minute can stand clear the next. Sit with the camera ready rather than walking on. The long lens used here is the same one that caught [reindeer on the shoulder of Saana](/blog/reindeer-saana/), where it kept a respectful distance from the animals instead of pulling in a far peak.
