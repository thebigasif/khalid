Saana is the fell that rises above Kilpisjärvi, in the far north-west arm of Finnish Lapland. From its top, at just over a thousand metres, you look down on Saanajärvi, a deep lake in a bowl of tundra, and across to the fell of Iso-Jehkas. This picture was made at midday, when the cloud broke for a few minutes.

## A panorama of several frames

The view from Saana is far too wide for one frame. This picture joins several frames made side by side, each at 24 mm. The camera was turned upright and moved a little between frames, so each one overlaps the next. Software then joins them into one long picture.

Keep the exposure the same for every frame. If the camera changes it from one frame to the next, the sky shows bands where they join. Here every frame was f/8 at 1/320 of a second.

## Shadows from the clouds

Broken cloud is a gift on a fell top. The shadows of the clouds move across the land and pick out one slope, then another. Waiting for the shadow to leave the lake and lie on the far slope took ten minutes. In that time the lake turned from grey to deep blue.

## The walk up Saana

A marked path climbs Saana from the village, partly on long wooden stairs. It takes about an hour to the top. You can read more about [the fell and its history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saana), a fell the Sámi hold as a sacred place. On the same day we saw [reindeer on the shoulder of Saana](/blog/reindeer-saana/).

Saana changes with the weather more than most places. On a calm day the lake is a mirror; in wind it turns dark and rough. Fog can close the top in minutes, so watch the sky from the path. For the view of the boards at the foot of the fell, see [duckboards at the foot of Saana](/blog/duckboards-saana/).
