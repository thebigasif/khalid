Late in the afternoon on Saana, a small group of reindeer came over the shoulder of the fell. They grazed slowly along the ridge, with only the sky behind them. Every reindeer in Finland belongs to a herder, but in summer they roam free on the fells.

## Keeping a distance

A reindeer that is pushed will run, and running costs it the fat it needs for winter. So this picture was made from well below the ridge, with a long lens at 190 mm. Sitting still and letting the animals come closer works better than walking toward them. They came within about forty metres and never looked up for long.

## Animals on the skyline

From below, the reindeer stand against the sky, so their shapes are clear: the antlers, the legs, the low heads. A picture taken from above would put them against the grass, where their brown coats would disappear.

## Wide open and very fast

The lens was at f/2.8, its widest aperture. That lets in the most light and keeps the grass in front soft, so the animals stand out. Wide open in bright light, the shutter had to be very fast, 1/8000 of a second, the fastest this camera allows. It freezes every step and every turn of a head.

The ISO was 320, set a little high on purpose. The sky was changing fast, and a higher ISO kept the shutter fast even when a cloud crossed the sun.

## Meeting reindeer on the fells

You can read about [reindeer herding in Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer_husbandry_in_Finland). If you meet reindeer on a path, stop and let them move on in their own time. This was the same day as [the lakes below Saana](/blog/lakes-below-saana/).

Reindeer are easiest to find in the early morning and the evening, when they graze on the open slopes. In the heat of the day they often climb to snow patches or windy ridges to escape the flies. Each reindeer wears an ear mark that tells its herder it is theirs. A long lens lets you watch all this without changing what the reindeer do. Another long-lens picture is [Marmolada from Passo Giau](/blog/marmolada-passo-giau/).
