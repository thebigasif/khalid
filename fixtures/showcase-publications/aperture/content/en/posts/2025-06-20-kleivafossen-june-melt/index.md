Kleivafossen is the waterfall most people meet on the path to the Briksdal glacier in western Norway. In early June the snow on the plateau above is melting fast, and the river runs at its fullest. This picture was made at midday from the rocks below the fall, close enough to feel the spray.

## Why such a wide lens

At 16 mm the lens takes in the whole fall and the boulders at its foot. A wide lens makes near things large, so the mossy rock in the lower corner carries as much weight as the white water. That keeps the eye moving through the frame instead of stopping at the brightest part.

The aperture was f/16, so everything from the moss to the trees on the skyline is sharp. The shutter was 1/60 of a second. That is slow enough to soften the water a little, but fast enough that it still has shape. A slower shutter would have turned it into a smooth white sheet, which is pretty but tells you less about the force of the melt.

## Working in the spray

Spray is the real problem at a waterfall. A drop on the front of the lens shows as a soft blur. The fix is simple: keep a dry cloth in a pocket, wipe, take the picture, and wipe again. A lens hood also helps when the wind blows the spray away from you.

## Visiting Kleivafossen

The fall sits beside the road and footpath up Briksdalen, near Olden in Stryn. You can read more about the [Briksdal glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briksdalsbreen) at the head of the valley. Go early in summer for the most water, and wear shoes with a good grip on wet rock.

For another picture of moving water from the same trip, see [the two fjords from Ljøen](/blog/geirangerfjord-from-ljoen/). Every picture on this site is listed by place on the [Norway page](/blog/category/norway/).

Kleivafossen is not the tallest waterfall in the valley, but it is the one you can stand closest to. That closeness is what makes the picture. Later in the summer the flow drops and Kleivafossen breaks into separate threads of water over the rock, which can be lovely too, but it no longer fills the frame the same way. The same wide lens and small aperture also suit [the frozen rapid in Vantaa](/blog/frozen-rapid-vantaa/), where the water is held still by ice instead.
