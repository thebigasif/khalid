Monte Elmo rises above the village of Sesto, or Sexten, at the eastern end of Val Pusteria. Its slopes look across the valley to the Sexten Dolomites, a wall of grey towers and spires. In October the larches on the hillside turn gold, and the grass turns the colour of straw.

## One plain lens

This picture was made with a 50mm lens, the simplest and cheapest lens in the bag. A 50mm lens shows a scene at roughly the size the eye sees it, with no stretching at the edges. That suits a view like this, where the road, the tree and the peaks each need their true weight.

The picture is a panorama, several frames joined side by side. That is how a standard lens makes a wide view without the bent edges of a wide-angle lens.

## Settings for a bright day

At f/10 the lens is at its sharpest, and the depth reaches from the grass to the peaks. A shutter of 1/160 of a second at ISO 100 was all the midday light needed.

## Larch season in the Sexten Dolomites

The larch is the only conifer in the Alps that loses its needles. Before they fall, they turn yellow and then gold, usually in the first half of October. You can read about the [European larch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larix_decidua) and where it grows. The cable car up Monte Elmo runs into the autumn, so the walk to this view is short.

For another view across these mountains, see [Marmolada from Passo Giau](/blog/marmolada-passo-giau/).

The Sexten Dolomites change colour through the day. At noon, as here, the rock is pale grey and the shadows are short. In the evening the same towers turn pink and orange, which is the famous glow of the Dolomites. Both are worth a picture, but the midday view shows the shape of the Sexten Dolomites most clearly.
