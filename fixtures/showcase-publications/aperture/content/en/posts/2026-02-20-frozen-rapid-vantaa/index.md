Hanabölenkoski is a small rapid in the Havukoski part of Vantaa, just north of Helsinki. In a hard frost it becomes a frozen rapid: the spray freezes on every stone and branch. The ice builds up day after day into shapes like coral or frozen waves. This picture was made on a grey February afternoon, with the light already going.

## Close and very wide

The lens was a 14mm, the widest in the bag. At 14 mm you can stand almost on top of the ice and still take in the whole rapid. Near things become very large, so the ice shapes in the front fill half the frame. The dark water in the gaps shows that the river is still moving under all that ice.

## A slow shutter in the cold

The shutter was open for 0.3 seconds. That is too slow to hold by hand, so the camera stood on a small tripod on the bank. In that time the water under the ice moved enough to blur into a smooth sheet, while the ice stayed still and sharp.

Cold drains batteries quickly. A spare battery in an inside pocket, kept warm, saves a winter walk. When you go back indoors, keep the camera in its bag for an hour, so that damp air does not settle on the cold lens.

## A frozen rapid is never the same twice

The shapes on a rapid change with every thaw and frost. A week of mild weather can melt them all. For more [winter pictures](/blog/tag/winter/), see the low light in [Sipoonkorpi](/blog/winter-light-sipoonkorpi/). You can read about [frazil and anchor ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frazil_ice), the kinds of ice that form in moving water.

A frozen rapid is easy to walk past on a grey day, because from a distance it is only white on white. The shapes appear when you get close and low. Kneel by the edge, keep a safe distance from thin ice, and look for one strong shape to put near the lens. Then let the rest of the frozen rapid fall behind it.
