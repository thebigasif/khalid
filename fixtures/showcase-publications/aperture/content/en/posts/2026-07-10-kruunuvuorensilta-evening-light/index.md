Kruunuvuorensilta is the long new bridge across the sea in eastern Helsinki, built for trams, people on foot and cyclists. Its tallest part is a single white pylon held by fans of cables. This picture was made from the shore of Korkeasaari, the zoo island, at a quarter to nine on a June evening.

## Looking up, not across

Most pictures of a bridge are taken from the side, to show its full length. This one looks along the underside instead. The deck runs out of the top corner of the frame and leads the eye to the pylon. The calm sea below doubles the warm light on the concrete.

## Late light in June

In Helsinki in late June the sun sets after ten in the evening. By nine it is low in the north-west, and its light comes in almost flat over the water. Flat light like this turns grey concrete gold and puts long shadows in every rib of the deck.

The shutter was 1/125 of a second at f/10, with the ISO at 200. A 50mm lens kept the lines of the bridge straight, without the bending a wide lens adds at the edges.

## Seeing Kruunuvuorensilta yourself

Korkeasaari is open on summer evenings, and the shore path runs right under the bridge. You can read about [Kruunuvuorensilta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Bridges) and how it links the new districts to the city centre. Another 50mm picture is [the Sexten Dolomites from Monte Elmo](/blog/sexten-dolomites-monte-elmo/).

Kruunuvuorensilta changes the view of the eastern harbour, and for now it is still new enough to look surprising. A picture like this one is also a record: in a few years trams will cross it every few minutes, and the light and the water below will look the same while the bridge becomes ordinary. For another evening in long northern light, see [a hanging bridge in Spansdalen at night](/blog/hanging-bridge-night/), and for a winter day near the city, [low winter light in Sipoonkorpi](/blog/winter-light-sipoonkorpi/).
