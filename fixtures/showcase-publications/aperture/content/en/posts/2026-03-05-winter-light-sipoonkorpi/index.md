Sipoonkorpi is a national park of spruce forest and rock on the edge of Helsinki and Vantaa. In mid February the sun stays low all day. Around four in the afternoon it slips between the trunks and lays a band of gold across the snow. This picture was made in the few minutes that band lasted.

## Why the frame is upright

Most pictures on this site are wide. This one is tall, because the subject is tall: the trunks run from the bottom of the frame to the top. An upright frame also leaves room for the snow in front, where the light falls. The band of light becomes the first thing you see, and the trunks lead the eye up from it.

## Exposing for snow

Snow fools a camera's meter. The meter tries to make everything a middle grey, so bright snow comes out dull and blue. The fix is to add exposure until the snow looks white, but not so much that the bright band loses its detail. Here the ISO went up to 640 to keep a shutter of 1/80 of a second at f/16 in the shade of the trees.

## Walking in Sipoonkorpi

Marked paths in Sipoonkorpi start from several car parks, and some are kept open in winter. You can read about [Sipoonkorpi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipoonkorpi_National_Park). For another cold day near Helsinki, see [a frozen rapid in Vantaa](/blog/frozen-rapid-vantaa/).

Sipoonkorpi is close enough to the city to visit after work in late winter, when the days grow longer each week. The light in this picture lasted less than ten minutes, so it pays to arrive early and choose the spot before the sun reaches it. On a clear day in Sipoonkorpi the shadows of the trunks turn blue on the snow, which gives the gold light even more warmth.
