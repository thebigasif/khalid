Villotta is a small group of houses on the slope above Falcade, in the Biois valley of the Dolomites. At a quarter to eight on a late September morning, the sun had just cleared the ridge. The valley floor was still full of thin mist, and the old house on the hill caught the first light.

## A track to lead the eye

The grassy track in the foreground runs down toward the village and the mist. It gives the eye a path into the picture. Without it, the house and the valley would sit side by side and compete. With it, you start at the house, follow the track down, and end at the far peaks.

The camera was held low, at about knee height, so the track starts right at the bottom edge. A lens setting of 24 mm keeps the house large without bending its walls too much.

## Light in Villotta before it gets hard

Early light is low and warm, and it comes from the side. That side light shows the texture of the wooden balconies and the grass. An hour later the sun is higher and the mist has gone, and the same view looks flat. The shutter was 1/640 of a second at f/11, fast enough to hold the picture by hand without a thought.

## Around Falcade

Falcade is a quiet base for walks below the [Pale di San Martino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_di_San_Martino). In late September the summer walkers have gone and the ski season has not started. For a wider view of these mountains from the same trip, see [Marmolada from Passo Giau](/blog/marmolada-passo-giau/).

Villotta is only a few minutes on foot from the centre of Falcade, so this is a picture anyone staying in the valley can try. Go out before breakfast, walk uphill until the houses of Villotta are below you, and wait for the sun to reach the slope. The mist rarely lasts more than half an hour. For another early start, see [the midnight sun stairs in Lavangen](/blog/midnight-sun-stairs/), where the light lasts far longer.
