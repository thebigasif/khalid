Tern is a note-taking app for people who think in small pieces. You write short notes, link them to each other, and find them again with search that works on your device, with or without a connection. Tern runs on computers, phones and tablets, and it is free on one device, for as long as you like.

Tern Notes is our blog. We write it for anyone who uses Tern, and for anyone who builds software in a small team and wants to compare notes.

## Who makes Tern

Tern is made by nine people. Ines Halloran and Ada Kerrow started it in 2022. Most of us work from home, and we meet in person for a week every two months.

## Who writes here

- **Ines Halloran** is a co-founder and leads design. She writes about the decisions behind Tern's design: what we left out, what we named and why.
- **Ada Kerrow** is a co-founder and leads engineering. She writes the engineering notes on sync, search and storage.
- **Greta Voss** leads product. She plans each cycle and writes most of the release notes.
- **Rafe Morrow** leads support and documentation. He writes about support, interviews and what people teach us.

## What we write about

There are two kinds of post here. Release notes, under [Changelog](/blog/category/changelog/), say what changed in each version of Tern, with a table of every change. Essays, under [Essays](/blog/category/essays/), [How we work](/blog/category/how-we-work/) and [Engineering](/blog/category/engineering/), explain why. Good places to start are [why Tern has no folders](/blog/no-folders/), [how we plan in six-week cycles](/blog/six-week-cycles/) and [how offline search works on your device](/blog/how-offline-search-works/).

## How we check what we publish

Every post is read by a second member of the team before it goes live, and the post names them. Numbers come from our own measurements or our own support inbox, and we say how we got them. Release notes are checked against the release itself, line by line, as part of our [release checklist](/blog/release-checklist/).

## How to reach us

Write to team@example.com and a person on the team answers, usually within a working day. The photographs on this blog come from photographers who share their work under open licences; the [photo credits](/blog/photo-credits/) page lists each one.
