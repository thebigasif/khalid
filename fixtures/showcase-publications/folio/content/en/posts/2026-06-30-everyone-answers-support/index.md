Everyone at Tern answers support email for one morning each week, from the engineers to the founders. It keeps the whole team close to the people who use the app, and it turns support from one person's job into everyone's evidence. I lead support, but I answer fewer than half of the emails myself, and that is how we want it.

When I joined in 2023, I answered every email alone. I was good at it, and that was the problem: the team heard about problems from me, in a weekly summary, after I had already smoothed them out. A summary turns a frustrated person into a tidy line. The rota put the frustration back.

## How everyone answers support email

Each person on the team takes one morning a week, from nine until midday. I cover the gaps, the urgent cases and anything that needs a refund or an account change.

| Day | Morning shift | Afternoon cover |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | An engineer | Rafe |
| Tuesday | Ines or the second designer | Rafe |
| Wednesday | An engineer | Rafe |
| Thursday | Greta | Rafe |
| Friday | Ada or an engineer | Rafe |

On a typical morning, the person who answers support that day replies to about 15 emails. Most are quick: how to export notes, how to move to a new phone, why a note is not syncing. Some are long and hard, and those are the ones that matter most.

## The three rules for whoever answers support

New people on the rota get one page of guidance. It boils down to three rules.

- **Answer the person, not the ticket.** Read the whole email, say what you will do and do it. Never paste a help page without a sentence of your own.
- **Fix it if you can.** Engineers on shift may fix small bugs they find in the same morning, as long as the fix is reviewed.
- **Write down what surprised you.** Every shift ends with a short note in our support log: what came up, what was hard, what we should change.

:::figure{src="notebooks.jpg" alt="Notebooks for the support log of a team where everyone answers support: a white notebook and a brown kraft notebook lying beside a cup of coffee with milk foam and a black pen, seen from above on worn wooden floorboards"}
The support log lives in Tern itself, as one linked note per week. Each shift adds a few lines.

## What the rota has changed

The support log has shaped more of Tern than any planning meeting. Three examples from the last year:

1. **Quick capture.** Engineers on shift read the same story again and again: an idea lost while Tern was opening. That evidence went straight into the spec for [Tern 2.1](/blog/tern-2-1-quick-capture/).
2. **The sync status light.** Ada answered four emails in one morning from people who thought a note was lost when it had not synced yet. Within a week, Tern showed a small light for notes waiting to sync.
3. **The export button.** It used to sit three menus deep. After Greta answered nine export questions in a single shift, it moved to the main menu.

:::callout{kind=stat}
In the year since everyone joined the rota, our [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median) first reply time fell from 11 hours to 3 hours, and the share of emails that needed a second reply fell from 38 percent to 22 percent (June 2025 to June 2026).
:::

## What it costs

A team where everyone answers support pays for it. Each person gives up one morning of their week to support. In a team of nine, that is close to one full person. We think it is the best time we spend, because the alternative is building the wrong thing well.

It also costs some comfort. Answering an angry email about a bug you wrote is not pleasant. But engineers who have answered those emails write fewer of those bugs, and they fix the ones they find faster.

## If you want to try it

Start small. A team where everyone answers support does not need everyone every day. Ask each person to take one shift a month, keep the shifts short and have one person own the hard cases. Most of all, keep a shared log and read it in planning. The value is not in the replies. It is in what the team learns while writing them. Our support log feeds straight into the problem section of every [spec on one page](/blog/one-page-spec/).
