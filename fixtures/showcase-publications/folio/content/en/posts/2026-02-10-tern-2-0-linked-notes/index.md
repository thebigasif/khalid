Tern 2.0 brings linked notes: type two square brackets in any note, choose another note, and the two are joined, with a backlink shown at the foot of the note you linked to. It also brings a rebuilt editor that is faster and steadier on long notes, and it turns tags into notes. Tern 2.0 is free for everyone and out today on every platform.

This is the largest release since Tern began, and linked notes are the heart of it. It took three cycles instead of one, and it changes how many people will organise their notes. Here is what is new and what to expect when you update.

## What is new in Tern 2.0

| Change | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Linked notes | Type two square brackets to link to any note by its title | Everywhere |
| Backlinks | Every note lists the notes that link to it, with the sentence around each link | Everywhere |
| New editor | Rebuilt from scratch, with steady scrolling on notes over 10,000 words | Everywhere |
| Tags become notes | Each tag becomes a note, and each tagged note links to it | On update |
| Link preview | Hover over a link, or press and hold on a phone, to read the start of the note | Everywhere |
| Rename safely | Renaming a note updates every link to it | Everywhere |

## How linked notes work

Type `[[` anywhere in a note and Tern shows a short list of your notes, filtered as you type. Choose one and the link appears as the note's title, underlined. If no note has that title yet, Tern offers to create it, so you can link to an idea before you have written it down.

At the foot of each note, Tern shows its [backlinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backlink): every note that links here, with the sentence that holds the link. Backlinks are often where linked notes give the best surprises. A note about a book you read in March might turn out to be linked from five project notes you wrote since.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Start small. Make one note for something you care about, such as a project or a person, and link to it whenever it comes up. After a week, open it and read its backlinks.
:::

## What happens to your tags

Tern 1 had tags. Tern 2.0 does not. When you update, each tag becomes a note with the tag's name, and each note that had that tag gets a link to it at the end. Nothing is deleted. A note tagged "garden" and "budget" now links to the notes "Garden" and "Budget", and those notes list it in their backlinks.

We know this is a big change. Ines explains our reasons in [why Tern has no folders](/blog/no-folders/), and we will write about how it goes once people have lived with it for a few months.

## The new editor

The old editor struggled with long notes. Scrolling a 20-page note on an older phone could stutter, and very long tables could freeze it for a second. The new editor draws only the part of the note on screen and keeps the rest ready. In our tests on a five-year-old phone, a 15,000-word note now scrolls smoothly, and typing never waits for the screen to catch up.

The new editor also fixes several long-standing bugs:

- Undo now works across paragraphs and after pasting.
- Lists keep their numbers when you move items up or down.
- Selecting text across a link no longer jumps to the link.
- Right-to-left text and mixed languages lay out correctly.

## Before you update

Tern 2.0 updates your notes in place, on your device, the first time it opens. For most people this takes a few seconds. For accounts with more than 20,000 notes it can take a minute, and Tern shows its progress. Your notes are backed up on the device before the update starts, and you can return to them from **Settings** for 30 days.

Thank you to the 600 people on our beta who used Tern 2.0 every day for six weeks in January 2026 and sent us 1,100 problems to fix. We fixed 340 of them before today. The rest are on the list for the next release.
