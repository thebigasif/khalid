Dissolved carbon sampling usually needs a research vessel and a sampler that costs thousands. Our paper in Methods in Coastal Science describes a hand-pumped sampler that anyone can build from hardware-shop parts and use from a small open boat. In our 2025 tests its readings matched a research-grade sampler closely.

## Why cheap carbon sampling matters

Coastal waters change fast with the tide, the wind and the river flow. To understand how much carbon a marsh or a seagrass meadow sends into the water, you need many samples, often from shallow water where a big boat cannot go. A cheap sampler means more samples in more places, taken by more people.

## How the sampler works

The sampler is a length of food-grade tube on a weighted line, a hand pump and a set of glass bottles with gas-tight caps. You lower the tube to the depth you want and pump until three bottle volumes have flushed through. Then you fill the bottle from the bottom up, so no air bubble touches the sample. The full parts list is in the paper's open supplement, and every part can be bought on a high street.

Each bottle is kept cool and dark and reaches the laboratory within a day. That matters, because a warm sample keeps changing after it leaves the water.

## How we tested it

On 18 days through 2025, Rafael took paired samples with the new sampler and with a standard [Niskin bottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niskin_bottle) at the same place and depth. Both sets went to the same laboratory, which did not know which sample came from which method.

- The two methods agreed closely on 94 of 104 pairs.
- The other 10 pairs differed more, all on windy days when the small boat drifted between the two samples.
- One person can take a sample in about four minutes, so a team of two covers a whole estuary in a morning.

## Use it

The design is free to use and change. We have trained two community groups to use it, and their samples now feed into our work on [marsh carbon at Eastfield](/blog/restored-marsh-carbon/). The sampler also rides on the side rail of our [new research boat](/blog/new-research-boat/), so it will be used on every trip from now on.

If you build one, tell us what you changed. Questions about the parts or the method go to Rafael through the [about page](/blog/about/).
