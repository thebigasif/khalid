How close should you set each plug when you restore a marsh? Our paper in Estuarine Field Studies reports a cordgrass planting trial at four spacings on bare mud at Eastfield. Plugs set 30 centimetres apart gave the best mix of survival, spread and cost.

## The cordgrass planting trial

Volunteers planted 2,400 cordgrass plugs in 24 plots in spring 2024. Each plot used one of four spacings. We counted living plugs after one and two growing seasons and photographed each plot from a fixed pole to measure cover.

## Results

The table gives the results after two growing seasons, in 2026.

| Spacing | Plugs per square metre | Survival after 2 years | Cover after 2 years | Cost per square metre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 cm | 44 | 61% | 78% | £22 |
| 30 cm | 11 | 74% | 81% | £6 |
| 45 cm | 5 | 72% | 58% | £3 |
| 60 cm | 3 | 70% | 39% | £2 |

At 15 centimetres, plugs crowded each other and more of them died. At 45 and 60 centimetres, survival was fine, but the gaps were still open after two years, and bare mud washes away in winter storms. At 30 centimetres, cover was as good as the closest spacing for about a quarter of the cost.

## Why spacing matters

Bare patches are where a rebuilt marsh loses carbon and mud. Our [paper on marsh carbon at Eastfield](/blog/restored-marsh-carbon/) found that plant cover explains most of the gap between rebuilt and natural marsh. Getting the spacing right closes those patches sooner.

Height in the tide matters as much. A marsh that sits too low stays wet for too long, and our [fifteen-year marsh elevation record](/blog/marsh-elevation-sea-level/) shows the lowest marsh slowly falling behind the sea.

## Practical advice

- Plant at about 30 centimetres in sheltered mud.
- Plant in spring, after the last hard frost and before the summer heat.
- Plant at the height in the tide where cordgrass already grows nearby. Too low and plugs drown; too high and they dry out.
- Firm the mud around each plug with your heel, so the first tide does not lift it.

We thank the 41 volunteers who planted the trial, and the nursery that grew the plugs of [smooth cordgrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporobolus_alterniflorus). The plot photographs and counts are in the lab's open data archive.
