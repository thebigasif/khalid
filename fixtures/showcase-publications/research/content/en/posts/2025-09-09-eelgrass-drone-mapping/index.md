Drone mapping of seagrass can be simple. Our paper in Methods in Coastal Science shows that a small drone, flown at low tide on a calm, overcast day, takes photographs that a plain colour classifier can map almost as well as two people snorkelling.

## Why map from the air

Snorkel surveys are accurate but slow: two people can cover about one hectare in a morning. A drone covers the same ground in ten minutes. So, if the maps are good enough, we can survey whole meadows every month instead of a few plots a year.

## The drone mapping method

1. Fly a grid at 40 metres, two hours either side of low tide, when the eelgrass lies flat and the water is shallow.
2. Join the photographs into one map.
3. Sort each pixel into eelgrass, bare sand or water by its colour, using a few hundred pixels marked by hand as examples.
4. Check the result against snorkel quadrats laid out on the same day.

The steps and the code are in the open supplement. They run on an ordinary laptop, and the drone is a small consumer model.

## Results

Across 22 flights over six meadows in 2025, the maps agreed with the snorkel survey on 91 percent (2025) of quadrats. Most errors were in deep water, where the colour of eelgrass and of dark sand get close. Overcast days gave better maps than sunny ones, because bright sun leaves glare on the water.

## Update for 2026

In 2026 we mapped the same six meadows every month from April to October. The maps showed the storm damage of 2024 closing up, which is the basis of our paper on [seagrass recovery after grazing](/blog/turtles-seagrass-recovery/). We now launch the drone from the deck of the [new research boat](/blog/new-research-boat/), which reaches the outer meadows at low tide.

## Use it

The method suits any shallow meadow with clear water. You can read more about [eelgrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zostera_marina) itself, and write to Mei through the about page if you want help setting up your first flight.
