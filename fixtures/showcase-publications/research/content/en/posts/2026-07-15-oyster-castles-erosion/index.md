Oyster castles are stacks of small concrete blocks laid in shallow water so that wild oysters settle on them and grow into a reef. In our paper in Shoreline Ecology, oyster castles halved the erosion of the marsh behind them over four years, compared with stretches of the same shore left bare.

## How we tested oyster castles

In 2022 we built six block reefs along the north shore of Wren Bay, each about 20 metres long, with the help of two local shellfish growers. Six matching stretches of shore had no reef. Every three months we measured how far the vegetated bank had moved, using fixed stakes and a laser level.

The growers chose the sites with us. They know where oysters already settle, and a reef only grows if young oysters reach it.

## What we found

- **Less erosion.** Behind the reefs the bank moved back about 9 centimetres a year. Behind bare shore it lost about 19 centimetres a year.
- **Live oysters by year two.** Wild oysters covered most block faces within two summers, so the reefs grew on their own.
- **Calmer water, more mud.** Fine mud settled behind the reefs and in places built the bank up again.

The mud matters as much as the blocks. Lugworms and small crabs moved into it, which our students later counted at the [summer field school](/blog/summer-field-school/).

## Limits

Wren Bay is sheltered. On open coasts with bigger waves the blocks may shift, and the same design may not protect as well. Lukas is now testing heavier blocks on a more exposed shore, and we will report on them in 2027.

Cost is the other limit. Each reef took two days of work for six people and about two tonnes of blocks. That is cheap next to a rock wall, but not free, so a shore needs to be worth protecting.

## Why we care

Living shorelines like these protect the coast and also make habitat. Our earlier paper shows how many [juvenile fish natural reefs shelter](/blog/oyster-reefs-juvenile-fish/), and we are now repeating those counts on the built reefs. You can read more about the idea of a [living shoreline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_shoreline).

The bank measurements, reef photographs and oyster counts are all in the lab's open data archive.
