We have a funded PhD position on seagrass recovery, starting in October 2027. The student will join Mei and the rest of the seagrass team and will study how meadows recover after storms, heat waves and grazing.

## The project

The student will build on our [paper on turtles and seagrass](/blog/turtles-seagrass-recovery/) and our [drone mapping method](/blog/eelgrass-drone-mapping/). The main questions are open. For example, they could include which meadows recover fastest and why, or whether sowing seed helps a damaged meadow come back.

You will spend about a third of your time in the field, a third in the laboratory and a third on analysis and writing. You will also help teach the [summer field school](/blog/summer-field-school/) each June.

## What this PhD position offers

- Three and a half years of funding, with fees and a living allowance.
- Field work from the lab's new research boat and the marine station.
- Training in drone survey, diving and data analysis.
- A friendly lab of eight people that meets every week, and money to present your work at two conferences.

## Who we are looking for

You will have, or expect to have, a degree in ecology, marine biology, geography or a related subject. You will need to be happy working outdoors in all weather. You do not need diving experience, because we train you. Most of all, we want someone curious, careful with data and happy to work in a team.

We welcome applications from people of every background. If you need the work arranged around caring duties or a disability, tell us and we will find a way.

## How to apply

Send a CV and a one-page letter about why this PhD position interests you to Amara through the [about page](/blog/about/) by 15 January 2027. We hold short video interviews in February. Read more about [seagrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrass) if the field is new to you.
