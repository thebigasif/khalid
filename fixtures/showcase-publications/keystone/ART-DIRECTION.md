# Keystone Studio: art direction

Keystone Studio is the blog of a made-up design studio of six people that makes brand identities, signs, wayfinding, packaging and small websites for local clients. Its photographs should look like a tidy studio and the places its work ends up on a clear day: paper, tools, materials and buildings, seen by someone who notices edges, surfaces and colour.

## Tone

- **Clean daylight.** Bright, even, natural light with true colour. No dark moody shots, no night scenes and no coloured stage light. The darkest photograph kept, the heap of wood type, is a body figure and never a lead.
- **Materials and shapes first.** Paper, card, wood, metal, concrete, type and colour fill the frame. A building appears as a surface or a space, not as a landmark. Hands may appear when they show a craft, such as drawing with a ruler, but no face is the subject of a picture.
- **Signs as shapes, never as words.** The concrete arrow is the model: a sign that says where to go without a single letter. Candidates with readable text were turned down: a bakery's price cards, a chalkboard, a market banner, book spines, a theatre's name over its door, red lanterns lettered in Japanese, a ferry seat notice, a paper sample fan with its maker's name and a camera watermark.
- **Nothing to read.** No logos, labels, brand names or watermarks. Two kept photographs carry very small marks that are blurred at full size and unreadable on the page: the cast mark on a lamp base beside the compass, and the maker's name on the rotary cutter beside the cutting mat. Both are named in the report for review.

## Palette

Calm, light grounds with one strong colour at a time:

- warm neutrals of paper, card, kraft, oak and pale concrete;
- white and pale grey of walls, facades and print rooms;
- single strong accents: apple red, pencil yellow, rope teal, lantern orange and pool blue.

This suits Keystone's quiet, framed cards: the photographs bring one colour each and leave the page colour to the template and the sample brand. Avoid photographs dominated by black, saturated purple or busy multicolour scenes; the paint dish and the coloured pencils are the two busiest pictures and both sit on calm grounds.

## Subject mix

Of the 28 photographs, 21 are close details and 7 are wider scenes, so three in four are close details (the brief asks for at least a third).

- **Close details (21):** a rye loaf and knife, a letterpress tray, wood type, red apples, scissors on kraft paper, cream envelopes and cards, a green tape measure, hand tools on floorboards, seedlings in a seed tray, weathered oak, a concrete arrow, a folded white facade, a diving board over water, perforated metal panels, coloured pencils, a dish of mixed paints, a drawing compass, a cutting mat with tools, paper lanterns, a fan of paint colour cards, a coiled mooring rope.
- **Wider scenes (7):** a table and chairs in a bright meeting room, a library hall in a converted church, an empty white studio with cardboard shelving, a ferry deck in mist, an allotment site from the air, a screen-printing room, two people at a table taking workshop notes.

Every post has its own lead photograph. Nine posts carry a second photograph in the body, and the allotment post carries two.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post and cards | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | 20 of the 28 sources are 3:2 already, so the Keystone cards and the framed lead crop almost nothing. |
| In-body figure | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width with a caption. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one graphite ink (#2a2f33) on an 8 percent tint of the same ink: a keystone arch, a direction sign, a box net and a page grid. No photographs of people. |

The sources that are not 3:2: the hand tools (5:4, the hardware lead) lose about a tenth at the top and bottom, which trims the floorboards only. The ferry deck and the compass (4:3) lose about a twentieth top and bottom. The print room (11:8) and the paint dish (4:3) lose very little. The paint colour cards (1:1, the brand guidelines lead) lose a sixth top and bottom, which trims only more cards. The tape measure (about 16:9, the project costs lead) loses a little white ground at the sides. None of these needs a different crop.

## Phone crop

On a phone the lead picture fills the width and is cropped from the centre. Each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Single centred subjects (the loaf, the arrow, the letterpress tray, the print press, the tape measure) survive a tight crop.
- Repeating textures (apples, lanterns, coloured pencils, the allotment grid, the library hall) look the same at any crop.
- Two leads sit off centre and are the ones to watch in the picture review: the compass lies in the lower left third of the workshop tools lead, and the rolled end of the tape measure lies left of centre in the project costs lead. In both, a centre crop keeps most of the subject, but a square crop would cut it.

No photograph carries text, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets text over a lead picture, the light leads (the library hall, the studio, the envelopes, the folded facade) need a dark scrim to pass AA.
