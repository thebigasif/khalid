We built a small hardware website for Pell and Sons in 2026 that answers the five questions customers used to phone about, and that the owners update themselves in a few minutes. It has four pages, loads fast on a phone in the shop's car park and costs less to run each year than one delivery of nails.

| Client | Year | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Pell and Sons Hardware, Eastbridge | 2026 | Website |

## What the shop needed

Pell and Sons has sold tools, paint, keys and garden things on Harbour Road since 1962. Their old site was a single page made years ago by a nephew, with the wrong opening hours and a phone number that no longer worked.

We spent a Saturday morning behind the counter and noted every phone call. In four hours the shop took 23 calls. Almost all of them asked one of five questions.

| Question | Calls | How the new hardware website answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Are you open now? | 7 | Opening hours at the top of every page, with today's hours in bold |
| Do you cut keys? | 5 | A "Services" page listing keys, glass, paint mixing and tool hire |
| Do you have this in stock? | 5 | A short list of what the shop always stocks, and a phone number for the rest |
| Can I park? | 4 | A map with the car park marked, and its height limit for vans |
| Do you deliver? | 2 | Delivery area, days and price on the "Services" page |

## Four pages, nothing more

We could have built an online shop. We did not, because the owners did not want one and their customers did not ask for one. They want to see the tool, ask about it and buy it with advice.

The hardware website has four pages: a home page with hours, address and the five answers in short; a services page; a "what we stock" page; and a contact page with a map. Each page fits on a phone screen with one scroll.

:::callout{kind=note}
A small site is not a lesser site. Four clear pages that are always right do more for a local shop than forty pages that are half out of date.
:::

## A hardware website the owners update

The most important feature of the site is one most visitors never see. The owners update it themselves, from a phone, without calling us.

- **Hours and holidays** are kept in one place. Change them once and every page shows the new times.
- **A notice line** at the top of the home page takes one sentence, such as "Closed Monday for stocktaking".
- **The stock list** is a simple list the owners edit like a note.

We tested this with Ruth Pell, who runs the counter. We gave her a phone and three tasks: change Saturday's hours, add a notice and add "log baskets" to the stock list. She did all three in under four minutes without help, and we changed two labels she found confusing.

## Fast on a phone

Most visitors find the site on a phone, often in the car park. So the hardware website loads in under a second on a weak mobile signal. It has no large photographs at the top, no animations and no tracking scripts. The one photograph on the home page is of the shop front, sized for phones. We followed the plain advice in the [web accessibility guidelines](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) for contrast, text size and links, so it also works for customers who use larger text or a screen reader.

## The brand came along

Pell and Sons had a hand-painted sign but no logo, and we did not make one. We took the lettering and the dark green from the shop sign for the website, the same way we drew the [Fernhill Bakery identity](/blog/fernhill-bakery-identity/) from its window. The site looks like the shop, which is what customers recognise.

## What changed

In the first two months, calls asking "are you open?" almost stopped, and the shop now gets about a dozen messages a week through the contact page, most of them about tool hire. The owners have updated the site 31 times, and they have not called us once to do it.
