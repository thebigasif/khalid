We stay a small design studio of six people because it lets the designer you meet on the first day be the designer who finishes your project. We have been asked to grow many times, and every time we have decided the work is better at this size.

## Who the six are

There are four designers, a studio manager and a junior designer. Each designer leads one kind of work, and everyone helps on everyone else's projects.

- **Mara**, who writes this, leads brand identity.
- **Tobias** leads signage and wayfinding.
- **Hana** leads packaging and print, and runs the [workshop bench](/blog/studio-workshop-tools/).
- **Dev** designs and builds websites.
- **Priya** manages the studio: quotes, schedules, printers, invoices.
- **Joss** joined in 2025 as a junior designer and works across all four kinds of work.

## What a small design studio does well

### The same person from start to finish

In a large agency, a senior person often wins the work and a junior team then does it. In a small design studio, there is nobody to hand it to. The designer who runs your [first client workshop](/blog/first-client-workshop/) draws the work, checks the proofs and stands next to you when the sign goes up.

### Short distances

All six of us sit in one room at Ropewalk Yard. When Tobias needs a colour from Hana's packaging, he turns his chair round. Decisions that take a meeting elsewhere take a minute here.

### We know the town

Almost all our clients are within an hour of the studio. We buy bread from [the bakery whose identity we drew](/blog/fernhill-bakery-identity/), borrow books from Calder Street Library and take the [Tidewater ferry](/blog/tidewater-ferry-timetables/) to the islands. We see our own work every day, so we notice when something is wrong with it.

:::pullquote{by="Mara Quill, founder"}
We see our own work every day on the way to the studio. It is the best quality check we have.
:::

## What we give up

Staying small has costs, and we would rather say so plainly.

| What we give up | Why | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Large national projects | Six people cannot staff a project with forty sites | We pass them to two larger studios we trust |
| More than six projects at once | Each designer can lead about one and a half projects | We keep a waiting list and say how long it is |
| Specialists for everything | We have no photographer, animator or copywriter | We work with three freelancers we have known for years |
| Out-of-hours cover | Nobody is on call at weekends | We plan launches for Tuesdays, never Fridays |

## How we decide what to take on

We say yes to a project when three things are true. It is for a place or business our neighbours use. We can do most of it ourselves. And the client wants to keep using the work for years, not months.

That last test matters most. A sign, a label or a small website should outlive the project that made it. Our post on [what a project costs](/blog/what-a-project-costs/) shows how we price work with that in mind.

## Why not grow a little?

Seven people would still be small. We tried it twice, with a seventh designer for a busy year. Both times the studio began to need meetings to share what used to pass across the room. The [Dunbar number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number) is about friendships, not studios, but a shared table has a limit too, and ours is six.

So the small design studio stays the size it is. When we have more work than we can do, we say so, give a date, and stick to it.
