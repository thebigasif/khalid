We designed 28 pool signs for Saltmarsh Lido in 2026, made from materials that survive sea air, chlorine and summer sun, with large symbols that swimmers can read without their glasses. The lido reopened in May with the new signs, after a two-year restoration.

| Client | Year | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Saltmarsh Lido Trust | 2026 | Wayfinding, signage |

## A hard place for a sign

Saltmarsh Lido is an open-air pool built in 1937, a hundred metres from the sea. The air carries salt, the water carries chlorine and the sun bleaches everything from May to September. The old signs had faded, rusted at the fixings and, in two cases, fallen into the pool.

Swimmers add their own challenge. Many have taken off their glasses, many are children, and everyone is wet. A pool sign must be read from a distance, by blurry eyes, in bright light, by someone in a hurry to get warm.

## Materials that last

We tested materials before we designed anything. Six sample panels spent the winter of 2025 fixed to the lido's plant room wall, facing the sea, and two more sat in a bucket of pool water in our studio.

| Material | After six months by the sea | After six months in pool water | Chosen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Painted aluminium | Small blisters at a cut edge | Paint lifting at the corner | Not used |
| Powder-coated aluminium | No change | Slight dulling | Direction signs on walls |
| Vitreous enamel on steel | No change | No change | Pool depth and safety signs |
| Printed acrylic | Yellowed slightly | Cloudy | Not used |
| Solid-colour laminate | No change | No change | Changing room signs |
| Stainless steel fixings | No rust | No rust | Every fixing |

[Vitreous enamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitreous_enamel) is glass fused onto steel at high heat. It is the material of old railway signs, and it shrugs off salt, chlorine and sun. It costs more, so we used it only where the signs matter most: the pool depths and the safety signs at the water's edge.

The plant room is clad in perforated panels. We matched the direction signs to their grey so they sit quietly on the building.

## Pool signs lead with symbols

At a pool, a symbol does most of the work. A swimmer without glasses can still see a large, simple shape. So every one of the pool signs leads with a symbol, with a short word beneath it.

- **Depths** are shown in large numbers on the pool edge and on the wall above, at every metre of change.
- **No diving** in the shallow end uses the standard symbol, at twice the usual size.
- **Changing rooms, showers, toilets and first aid** each have a symbol and one word.
- **The way out** is shown by a green arrow, the same shape at every exit.

The words use the same open typeface as our library signs, chosen with the tests in [how to choose a typeface for signs](/blog/typeface-for-signs/). We used the standard safety symbols wherever they exist, because swimmers have seen them at other pools. We drew new ones only for things no standard covers, such as the lido's sea-water shower.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Pool depth and safety signs in the UK follow published guidance, and a lido's operator is responsible for them. We worked with the trust's safety adviser on every one, and we recommend any pool does the same before changing its signs.
:::

## Colour in strong sun

Bright sun washes out light colours. We tested our colours outside at noon in July, on the sample panels, and chose a deep blue and a warm white with strong contrast. The same blue appears on the lido's new tickets and website, so the pool signs and the brand agree.

## Testing at the lido

We tested full-size paper copies of all 28 pool signs for a week in April, while volunteers cleaned the pool. Four signs moved, three had their words changed and one was removed. A sign we had put by the café pointed at a door that staff always keep locked, so it now points to the side path instead. Our post on [testing signs with paper copies](/blog/test-signs-on-site/) explains the method.

## What changed

The lido opened on the first Saturday in May with every sign in place. The trust's lifeguards say the depth markings are the clearest they have worked with. The same material tests now guide our other outdoor work, such as the [Wrenfield Allotments signs](/blog/wrenfield-allotment-signs/), and the sign plan method came from [our library project on Calder Street](/blog/calder-street-library-wayfinding/).
