We made new allotment signs and a plot map for Wrenfield Allotments in 2026, so that a new plot holder or a visitor can find any of the 212 plots without help. The signs are oak, cut and fitted by the society's own volunteers from our drawings, and the map lives on a single web page the society keeps up to date.

|---|---|---|
| Wrenfield Allotment Society | 2026 | Signage, website |

## A grid nobody could read

From the air, Wrenfield Allotments is a neat grid. On the ground it is a maze. Paths look alike, hedges hide the view and the old plot numbers were painted on posts that had rotted, leaned or vanished. New plot holders spent their first visit lost, and the committee spent its Saturdays walking people to their plots.

The society had little money and many willing hands. So the brief was clear: design allotment signs the volunteers could make and fit themselves, from materials that last outdoors for twenty years.

## How we planned the allotment signs

We walked every path with the committee secretary and a printed plan. Three things became clear.

1. **Paths needed names, not just plots.** People navigate by paths, like streets. We named the six main paths after trees that grow on the site: Hazel Walk, Damson Row, Elder Path and so on.
2. **Every junction needed a sign.** At each of the 19 junctions, a post now names both paths and shows which plot numbers lie each way.
3. **Plot numbers had to run in order.** The old numbers had been added over sixty years and jumped about. We renumbered all 212 plots so they run along each path, odd on the left and even on the right, like houses on a street.

| Sign | Where | How many | Made from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrance map | At both gates | 2 | Printed aluminium panel in an oak frame |
| Junction post | At every path junction | 19 | Oak post, letters routed into the wood |
| Plot marker | At the corner of every plot | 212 | Short oak stake, number burnt in |
| Notice board | By the main shed | 1 | Oak frame with a cork panel under glass |

## Made to be made by volunteers

Every one of the allotment signs had to be possible with tools the volunteers already had or could borrow. Tobias drew the junction posts so they need only a saw, a drill and a router, and we made a plywood template for the letters so every post matches. The letters follow our rules for a [typeface for signs](/blog/typeface-for-signs/), cut deep enough to stay readable as the oak weathers. A local timber yard gave a good price on green oak, which weathers to silver and does not need paint.

:::figure{src="weathered-wood.jpg" alt="Close-up of two grey, weathered wooden boards with deep cracks and a knot, the silver colour green oak allotment signs turn after a few years outdoors"}
Untreated oak turns this soft silver within a few years. We chose it so the allotment signs would never need repainting.
:::

The plot markers were the biggest job: 212 stakes, each with a number burnt in with a branding iron the society bought for the purpose. Twenty volunteers made and fitted them over three Saturdays in April, with tea and cake from the committee.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If you plan signs for volunteers to make, make the first one yourself, with them, and time it. Our first junction post took four hours. By the tenth, the volunteers made one in ninety minutes.
:::

## A map that stays true

Plots change hands every year, and a printed map is out of date by the autumn. So the entrance maps show only paths, numbers and facilities: water taps, toilets, the shed and the compost bays. Who holds which plot lives online.

Dev built one web page with a map drawn as a simple, accessible graphic, and a list of plots underneath for people using screen readers. The committee secretary updates it from a spreadsheet. Each entrance map carries a short web address so people can check the live version on their phone.

## What changed

The committee says Saturday mornings now go on gardening, not guiding. New plot holders get a welcome letter with their plot number, their path name and the web address, and almost all of them find their plot alone on the first visit.
