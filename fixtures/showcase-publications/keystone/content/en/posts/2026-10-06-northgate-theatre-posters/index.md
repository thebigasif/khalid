We designed and screen printed the theatre posters for Northgate Theatre's 2026 autumn season: six plays, six posters, one shared grid and two inks per poster. The posters run in the theatre's glass cases, in cafés and shops across Eastbridge and as a set the theatre sells in its foyer.

| Client | Year | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Northgate Community Theatre | 2026 | Season identity, print |

## The brief

Northgate is a community theatre of 180 seats, run by volunteers with two paid staff. Each play used to have its own poster, designed by whoever had time. Some were beautiful, some were not, and none looked as if they came from the same theatre.

The theatre asked for a season look that made every poster clearly Northgate's, and that still let each play have its own character. They also had a small budget and a large number of posters to put up.

## One grid, six plays

We built every poster on the same grid. The theatre's name runs along the top in the same place, the dates and booking details sit in a band at the bottom, and the middle belongs to the play.

| Part of the poster | Always the same | Changes with each play |
|---|---|---|
| Top band | Theatre name, season name | Nothing |
| Middle | Size and position of the image area | The image, the play's title, its colour |
| Bottom band | Layout, typeface, booking line | Dates, times and ticket price |
| Inks | Always two | Which two |

Each play has one image, cut from paper and printed flat. A paper boat for the sea comedy, a broken teacup for the family drama, a crown of folded card for the history play. The shapes are simple enough to read from across the street, and they echo each other across the season.

## Why screen print theatre posters

[Screen printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_printing) pushes ink through a fine mesh onto paper, one colour at a time. It suits theatre posters well for three reasons.

- **Strong colour.** Screen ink sits thick on the paper and stays bright in a sunny glass case for months.
- **Good for short runs.** We print 150 of each poster. Screen printing costs little to set up, so 150 is no more expensive per poster than 500 would be by other methods.
- **The look of the thing.** Every poster is slightly different, and the theatre sells them in the foyer as prints. Last season the poster sales paid for the printing.

We print at a shared print studio in the old dye works on Mill Street, where we rent the press by the day.

## Choosing the inks

Every poster uses two inks. One is always a deep ink that holds the text, and the other is the play's colour. Where the two overlap they make a third colour for free.

| Play | Play colour | Text ink |
|---|---|---|
| The Paper Boat | Sea green | Navy |
| A Broken Cup | Warm pink | Dark brown |
| The Lighthouse Keepers | Signal yellow | Navy |
| Crown and Kettle | Brick red | Dark brown |
| Snow on Gull Island | Pale blue | Navy |
| The Winter Fair | Orange | Dark brown |

We printed every colour on the poster paper before we chose, because the same ink looks different on different papers. Our post on [choosing paper stock](/blog/choose-paper-stock/) explains why, and [how we build a colour palette](/blog/build-colour-palette/) explains how we wrote the six colours down so the theatre can reprint them.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If you are planning theatre posters for a whole season, decide the grid first and the images last. A strong shared grid lets each play be different without the season falling apart.
:::

## What changed

The first theatre posters went up in August. The box office says more people now mention a poster when they book, and the foyer sold 140 sets of the season's prints by October. The volunteers can now make posters for one-off events themselves, using the grid and a template we left them, without anyone needing to be a designer.
