We planned the library wayfinding for Calder Street Library in 2025 and 2026, and the 64 new signs went up in February. The building has grown in four stages since 1898, and the new signs give visitors one simple route through all of them, so the staff now answer far fewer questions about where things are.

::toc

## The problem: four buildings pretending to be one

Calder Street Library began as a chapel. A reading room was added in 1934, a children's wing in 1971 and a glass link with a lift in 2009. Each addition brought its own signs, so a visitor met four styles of sign, three names for the same floor and two different numbers for the same room.

The staff counted the questions they were asked at the front desk for two weeks before we started. Out of 1,140 questions, 420 were about finding something: the toilets, the lift, the children's library, the study rooms and the local history room. That count became our measure. Good library wayfinding would cut it, and the count would show by how much.

## How we planned the library wayfinding

[Wayfinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayfinding) is everything that helps people know where they are and where to go next: signs, but also light, colour, landmarks and the shape of the building. We began with the building, not the signs.

### We walked every route

We walked from the street door to each of the twelve places people ask about most, and timed each route. We did it again with a pushchair, again in a wheelchair borrowed from the council, and again with two visitors who had never been inside. Every time someone hesitated, we marked the spot on the plan. Those marks became the places that needed a sign.

### We gave each part of the building one name

The old signs used "Ground floor", "Level 0" and "Street level" for the same floor. We agreed one name for every floor and room with the staff and then used it everywhere: on signs, on the website, on the leaflets and in what staff say at the desk.

| Old names | New name |
|---|---|
| Ground floor, Level 0, Street level | Ground floor |
| Upper floor, Level 1, Gallery | First floor |
| Junior library, Children's wing, Kids | Children's library |
| Room 4, Seminar room, Study 2 | Study rooms |
| Archive, Local studies, Heritage room | Local history room |

### We chose one landmark per floor

People remember places better than numbers, and landmarks are the quiet half of library wayfinding. On each floor we picked a landmark visitors could see from the stairs and the lift: the round desk under the chapel windows, a green wall in the children's library and the old reading room clock. Every sign on that floor points back to its landmark.

## The signs in the library wayfinding

We designed six kinds of sign. Each has one job, so a visitor learns the library wayfinding system after meeting two or three of them.

| Kind of sign | Job | How many | Material |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building directory | Shows every floor and what is on it, at the street door and by the lift | 4 | Painted aluminium on a steel frame |
| Floor directory | Shows the floor you are on, at the top of each stair | 6 | Painted aluminium, wall fixed |
| Direction sign | Points to the next place on a route | 22 | Painted aluminium, hanging or wall fixed |
| Room sign | Names a room at its door, with raised letters and Braille | 24 | Acrylic with tactile letters |
| Toilet and lift sign | Marks toilets, baby change and the lift, with symbols | 6 | Painted aluminium |
| Opening hours | Shows hours and contact details at the entrance | 2 | Printed vinyl on glass |

The room signs have raised letters and Braille at a height a hand finds without searching, between 1.4 and 1.7 metres from the floor. We chose a clear typeface with open letters, and we wrote about why in [how to choose a typeface for signs](/blog/typeface-for-signs/).

:::callout{kind=tip}
Before a single sign was made, we taped full-size paper copies of every sign to the walls for a week and watched what happened. Seven of them moved. Our post on [the week-long test of paper signs](/blog/test-signs-on-site/) explains the method.
:::

## What changed

The staff counted questions again for two weeks in April, at the same time of year. Questions about finding something fell from 420 to 150. The question asked most often is now "where are the study rooms", because the study rooms are booked online, and we are working with the library to add their name to the booking emails.

Library wayfinding is never finished, because a library keeps changing. We left the staff a sign plan, a template for each kind of sign and a short guide to making new ones, so a sign for a new room matches the others without calling us. We wrote more about that habit in [what goes in our brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-handover/). For another building where the signs had to survive more than visitors, read about [the Saltmarsh Lido signs](/blog/saltmarsh-pool-signs/).
