Brinewell Fish Hall is the long brick shed on North Quay where Varrow's boats sell their catch. It has stood there since the harbour was dug, and it still smells of salt, ice and diesel in the best way. Entry is free, and you can buy as little as one fish.

## When to go to Brinewell Fish Hall

Go before nine. The first boats land at about half past five, and by seven the long marble counters are full: mackerel, sardines, hake, crab and, in winter, oysters from the beds past the breakwater. By noon the best is gone and the stall holders are hosing down.

Saturday is busiest. On a weekday you can talk to the sellers, and they will tell you which boat each fish came off.

## What to buy

- **Mackerel**, the cheapest and best fish in the hall from June to October.
- **Brown crab**, sold cooked at the end stall, ready to pick on the harbour wall.
- **Smoked haddock** from the small smokehouse at the back, wrapped in paper.

Most stalls take cards now, but the smokehouse still prefers cash.

## Getting there

Brinewell Fish Hall is at the far end of North Quay, past the lifeboat station. From the station, take bus 2 to the quay stop and walk five minutes along the water. The harbour ferry from The Weirs stops right outside. There is no car park, and the quay is closed to cars until ten on market days, so walking or the bus is quicker. Our note on [getting around Varrow](/blog/getting-around-varrow/) has the times.

## Good to know

The floor is wet and the doors are open to the weather, so wear good shoes and a coat even in summer. There are no seats inside. Take your fish across the road to [Tarry Rope Coffee](/blog/tarry-rope-coffee/), which will let you sit with a paper parcel if you buy a drink.

For a sit-down fish lunch, walk ten minutes along the quay to [Hullwright's Table](/blog/hullwrights-table/), which buys most of its fish here. The [Wikipedia article on fish markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_market) explains how halls like this one work.
