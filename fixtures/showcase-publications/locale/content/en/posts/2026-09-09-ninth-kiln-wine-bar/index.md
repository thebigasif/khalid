Ninth Kiln Wine Bar fills the old glaze store at the end of Glaze Lane, a low room with a vaulted brick ceiling and one long bar. It opens at four, which makes it the best place in town for a quiet glass before dinner and a busy one after.

## The wine at Ninth Kiln Wine Bar

There are about forty wines by the glass, written on a board that changes every week. The staff will pour you a taste of anything before you choose. Most glasses cost less than a cocktail in the harbour bars, and a carafe is good value for two.

If you do not drink wine, there is local cider and a short list of drinks without alcohol, including a good ginger beer made in Varrow.

## Small plates

The kitchen is a single counter, and the plates are small and simple:

- **Salt cod fritters** with a sharp green sauce.
- **Cheese from the hill farms,** with bread from the bakery in Kiln Yard.
- **Pickled mackerel** on toast, with fish from the quay.

Three plates make a light supper for one.

## Getting there

Glaze Lane climbs from the harbour to Kiln Yard, and the bar is at the top, on the left. It is a ten-minute walk from the quay, uphill all the way. Bus 5 stops at the bottom of the lane until midnight. The door is small and painted dark blue, with a lamp over it.

## Good to know

No bookings: come before six for a seat at the bar. It gets loud after nine on Fridays and Saturdays. There is a step down into the room. Card only.

If you want a full dinner after, [Hullwright's Table](/blog/hullwrights-table/) is fifteen minutes downhill. The [Wikipedia page on wine bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_bar) has a short history of rooms like this one.
