Saggar Bakehouse took over the last working kiln in the Kiln Quarter when the pottery closed, and it bakes in the same round brick chamber. The name comes from the clay boxes potters used to protect their pots in the fire. The bread is the best in Varrow, and we have tried them all.

## What to buy at Saggar Bakehouse

- **The kiln loaf,** a dark sourdough with a thick crust that keeps for four days.
- **Cardamom buns,** baked in two batches, at half past seven and nine. Both are usually gone by ten.
- **Rye with seeds** on Fridays only.

A whole kiln loaf costs about the same as two coffees in town. Half loaves are sold after noon.

## The room

There is space for six people at a narrow counter facing the kiln, and two benches outside in the yard. Coffee is simple: filter or a flat white, made with the same beans as [the café on Rope Walk](/blog/tarry-rope-coffee/).

On cold mornings the warmth of the kiln fills the whole yard, and people stand by the door just to warm their hands.

## Getting there

Kiln Yard is the cobbled square at the top of Glaze Lane, ten minutes on foot from the harbour. Look for the round brick kiln with the tall chimney: the bakery is inside it. Bus 5 stops at the bottom of the lane. The yard is closed to cars, and the hire bikes have a rack by the gate.

## Good to know

Card or cash. There is a small step at the door, and the bakers will bring your order out if the step is a problem. Bread can be ordered by phone the day before.

The Kiln Quarter has two more places we like on the same walk: [Clayfold Studios](/blog/clayfold-studios/) for pots and the [Ninth Kiln Wine Bar](/blog/ninth-kiln-wine-bar/) for later in the day. The [Wikipedia page on sourdough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourdough) explains why the loaf keeps so well.
