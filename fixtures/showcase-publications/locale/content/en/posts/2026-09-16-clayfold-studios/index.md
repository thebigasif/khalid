Clayfold Studios is where the Kiln Quarter still makes pots. Twelve potters share the Long Shed, the old drying house of the last pottery, and the front room is a small gallery where you can buy their work. Entry is free, and nobody minds if you only look.

## What you can see at Clayfold Studios

The workshop sits behind a low wall, so you can watch the wheels from the gallery. Thursday and Friday afternoons are the busiest times for throwing. On the first Sunday of each month the big kiln is opened, and the potters show what came out of the fire.

The gallery changes every season. Expect mugs, bowls and jugs for everyday use, and a few larger pieces by the studio's founding potters.

## Buying pots

- **Seconds shelf:** pots with small flaws at half price, by the door.
- **Mugs:** the best gift to take home from Varrow, and easy to pack.
- **Orders:** most potters take orders for sets, ready in about six weeks.

Prices are fair for handmade work. Every piece has the maker's name on its label.

## Getting there

The Long Shed fills the north side of Kiln Yard, opposite the round kiln. Walk up Glaze Lane from the harbour in ten minutes, or take bus 5 to the Kiln Quarter stop. The gallery door faces the yard, and the workshop entrance at the side is for classes only. There are benches in the yard if you want to sit and look at your new mug.

## Good to know

The gallery is step free. There is no café, but [the bakery in the old kiln](/blog/saggar-bakehouse/) is across the yard. Classes run on Saturday mornings and are booked months ahead.

On a wet day, pair this with [Quillon Books](/blog/quillon-books/) in Hillgate. The [Wikipedia page on pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) explains the steps from wet clay to a fired pot.
