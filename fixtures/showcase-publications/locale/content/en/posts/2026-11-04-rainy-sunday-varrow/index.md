It rains in Varrow, often and with feeling. A rainy Sunday in Varrow does not have to be a lost one, though. This is the day we plan for friends who visit in November, with short dashes between warm rooms and nothing more than ten minutes apart.

## Morning: bread and pots

Start at [the bakery in the old kiln](/blog/saggar-bakehouse/) in the Kiln Quarter, where the kiln keeps the whole yard warm. Buy a cardamom bun and eat it at the counter.

Cross the yard to [Clayfold Studios](/blog/clayfold-studios/), which opens at eleven on Sundays. Watch the wheels for a while and look at the seconds shelf by the door.

## Afternoon on a rainy Sunday in Varrow

Take bus 2 up the hill to [Quillon Books](/blog/quillon-books/). The reading chair by the fire is the best seat in town on a wet day.

Walk down the Hillgate Steps, carefully, to [the teahouse halfway down](/blog/tumbledown-teahouse/). Ask for a window seat upstairs and watch the rain run down the steps.

## Evening: noodles by the weir

End the day at [Weirside Noodle Bar](/blog/weirside-noodle-bar/) with a bowl of pork broth noodles, which is open until ten. The harbour ferry stops nearby if your feet are wet.

## If the rain stops

Rain in Varrow often clears by the middle of the afternoon. If it does, climb to the quarry rim in Ashlar Gardens for the view, which is never better than just after a storm. The harbour is bright and the islands look close enough to touch. Come back down before the gates close at dusk.

## What to bring

- A good coat with a hood. Umbrellas do not last long in the harbour wind.
- Shoes with grip for the steps.
- A day ticket for the buses and the ferry.

Our note on [getting around Varrow](/blog/getting-around-varrow/) has the times and fares. The [Met Office](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/) has a forecast for the coast, if you want to know what is coming.
