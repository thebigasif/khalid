Road closures will be in place around North Quay from 20:00 on Friday 30 October until 06:00 on Monday 2 November, while contractors take down the old dockside crane, the harbour board said on Wednesday. Harbour Road will shut completely between Quay Street and the ferry terminal for most of the weekend.

The crane, which has stood on North Quay since 1958, has not worked since 2009. It has to go before phase two of the harbour works starts in January, because the new quay wall will be built where its rails run. A mobile crane will lift it apart in six sections and lower them onto lorries.

## Road closures and times

| Road | Closed from | Open again | Diversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harbour Road, Quay Street to ferry terminal | Friday 20:00 | Monday 06:00 | Via Ropewalk and Mill Lane |
| Quay Street, Ropewalk to Harbour Road | Saturday 06:00 | Sunday 20:00 | Via Market Street |
| North Quay car park | Thursday 18:00 | Monday 08:00 | Use Customs House Square |
| Ropewalk | Open, but parking suspended | | |

During the road closures, buses on routes 3 and 7 will use the diversion through Mill Lane and will not stop on Harbour Road. Temporary stops will be set up on Ropewalk.

## Getting to the ferry

The ferry terminal stays open all weekend, and sailings run as normal. Cars for the ferry can reach the terminal through the diversion, which adds about five minutes while the road closures are in place. Foot passengers can walk through: a marshalled path will run along the landward side of Harbour Road throughout.

:::callout{kind=warning}
The lift itself happens on Saturday and Sunday from 07:00 to 19:00. The cordon around the crane is 50 metres. Please do not try to watch from inside it, however good the view.
:::

## Lifting at night

The heaviest piece, the crane's machinery house, weighs 38 tonnes. Contractors plan to lift it early on Sunday morning, when the wind is forecast to be lightest. Floodlights will be on over the quay from Saturday evening, and residents of Quay Street have been warned to expect noise until midnight on Saturday.

:::figure{src="dusk.jpg" alt="A small harbour town seen across dark water at dusk, its quays lit by orange street lamps and a lit harbour wall in the foreground, the quiet hour when road closures for night work begin"}
Some of the work will be done under floodlights after dark on Saturday, when the quay is quiet.
:::

## What happens to the crane

The harbour board offered the crane to two maritime museums. Neither had room for the whole thing. One section of the jib, about eight metres long, will be kept and put on display on the rebuilt North Quay when the harbour works finish in 2028. The rest will be sold for scrap.

Harbour master Kit Laverty said many people had asked to keep it. "It was the first thing you saw from the ferry for sixty years," he said. "We could not save all of it, but we will keep a piece where people can touch it."

The rest of the timetable for the quay is in our guide to [what phase two of the harbour works means](/blog/harbour-works-phase-two/). This kind of machine is a [mobile crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_crane), which drives in on its own wheels and sets up on outriggers in a few hours.
