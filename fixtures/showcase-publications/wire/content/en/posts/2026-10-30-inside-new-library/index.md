Port Arden's new library opens on Customs House Square at 09:00 today, Friday 30 October, and we spent Thursday afternoon inside the new library with its staff before the doors opened. The building, finished in 2026 for £11.8 million, holds about 64,000 books and brings the town archive, the children's library and a cafe under one roof for the first time.

It replaces the library on Chapel Street, which closed last Saturday after 63 years. Library manager Saira Bell has worked in both. "The old building was loved, but it was a building where you came in, got your book and left," she said. "We want this to be somewhere you stay."

Here is what you will find inside the new library, floor by floor.

::toc

## Ground floor: lending, computers and the cafe

The main doors open onto the lending library, which holds adult fiction, non-fiction, large print books and audiobooks. The shelves are lower than they were on Chapel Street, at about chest height, so you can see across the whole floor from the door.

Along the south wall are twelve public computers and two printers. The computers are free for an hour a day with a library card, and longer if no one is waiting. Printing costs 10p a page in black and white.

The cafe sits by the windows onto the square and is run by the same family that runs the kiosk on the promenade. It opens half an hour before the library each morning.

:::callout{kind=tip}
The self-service machines by the door take back books from any library in the county, not only Port Arden's. Return slips print on request.
:::

## First floor: children and teenagers

The whole of the first floor is for young readers. The picture books and first readers sit at the east end, near the story room, which seats forty and has its own toilets and space for buggies. Story time runs at 10:30 on Tuesdays and Saturdays, with an extra session every weekday in the [school holidays](/blog/half-term-dates/).

At the west end is a room for teenagers, with study tables, games consoles and a door that closes. It was designed with pupils from Port Arden High School, who asked for "somewhere the little ones are not".

## Second floor: the archive and study rooms

The top floor holds the town archive, which moves here from a basement at the Town Hall. It includes harbour board minutes back to 1849, maps of the old harbour, parish records and about 9,000 photographs. Most of it can be seen in the searchroom without an appointment, but older items have to be ordered a day ahead.

Beside the archive are a quiet reading room with 24 desks and two meeting rooms. Groups can book the meeting rooms for £12 an hour in 2026, or free for charities based in the town.

## Opening hours and what is free

|---|---|

| Borrowing books, up to 15 at a time | Free |
| Borrowing audiobooks and films | Free |
| Using the computers and wifi | Free |
| Printing and copying | 10p a page in black and white, 50p in colour |

| Late returns | No fines |

The library opens Monday to Wednesday from 09:00 to 19:00, Thursday from 09:00 to 20:00, Friday from 09:00 to 17:00 and Saturday from 10:00 to 16:00. It is closed on Sundays. The full table, set against the old library's hours, is in our [story on the library opening date](/blog/library-opening-date/).

## How the building was made

The library was designed to be warm in winter without much heating. It sits on the site of the old customs warehouse, and its frame reuses part of the warehouse's concrete floor. Heat pumps in the square warm the building, and the roof has 140 solar panels on its south side.

The design was not popular with everyone. In 2024, 340 people objected to the planning application, most of them worried about the loss of the warehouse's brick front. The council kept the front wall and built the new library behind it.

## How to join the new library

You can join at any desk with proof of your address, such as a bill or a bank letter. Children under 16 join with a parent or carer. If you already have a card from the old library, it works here, and your loans have moved across.

The [public library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_library) is run by the council and is free for everyone who lives, works or studies in Port Arden. Columnist Gil Penhale, who was at the old library's closing, writes about what it meant to the town in [his column on the old library](/blog/old-library-column/).
