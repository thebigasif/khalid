Storm damage from Saturday night's gales has closed 300 metres of Port Arden's promenade, between the lifeboat station and Bay Street, the council said on Sunday morning. Waves broke over the sea wall at high tide, just after 23:00, and threw stones and water across the promenade gardens.

Nobody was hurt. Two homes on Bay Street had water in their ground floors, and the council's flood team was out until 03:00 helping to pump them out.

## What the storm damage looks like

On Sunday morning, the low brick walls around the promenade gardens lay in pieces across the path. A section of railings near the lifeboat station had been bent flat, and a shelter had lost its glass. Stones the size of a fist were strewn across the road at the end of Bay Street.

The sea wall itself held, despite the storm damage around it. Council engineer Priya Nandra, who inspected it at first light, said the damage was "mostly to things built on top of the wall, not to the wall". She added: "That is what we want, if we have to have damage at all."

## What is closed

- **The promenade** between the lifeboat station and Bay Street, until engineers have checked the railings and lamp posts.
- **Bay Street car park**, while the council clears stones and sand.
- **The South Mole**, as a precaution, until Monday.

The stretch of Harbour Road by the fish market, which flooded overnight, reopened at 07:00 on Sunday. The crane removal on North Quay, which had been due to finish on Sunday, will now run into Tuesday, so the [road closures for the crane removal](/blog/crane-removal-road-closures/) last two days longer.

## Why it happened

The gales were stronger and came later than forecast. Gusts reached 71 mph at the harbour mouth at 22:40, during a high spring tide. Engineers call that combination the worst case for the sea wall, as our [storm warning earlier this month](/blog/storm-warning-coast/) explained.

The council's coastal plan says the promenade will be hit by waves more often as sea levels rise. Councillor Helen Marsh, the council leader, said repairs would start this week and that the council would ask the government for help with the cost through its [coastal flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_flooding) defence fund.
