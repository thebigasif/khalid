Phase two of the Port Arden harbour works will start on Monday 11 January and run for about eighteen months, the harbour board confirmed at its meeting on Friday. The work rebuilds the North Quay wall, deepens the ferry berth and moves the fish market's landing bay, at a cost of £21.6 million at 2026 prices.

Phase one, which ended in August, replaced the crumbling South Mole and its lighthouse. This stage of the harbour works touches the parts of the harbour that people use every day, so it will change how you reach the ferry, where boats land their catch and which roads are open. Harbour master Kit Laverty told the board the aim is "to keep the harbour working the whole way through, even when it does not look like it".

This guide explains what is being built, when, and what it means for you.

::toc

## Why the harbour works are needed

The North Quay wall was built in 1887 from stone blocks filled behind with rubble. Surveys in 2023 found that the sea had washed out the fill behind the wall in four places, leaving gaps the size of a car under the quayside. The harbour board closed the end of the quay to heavy lorries that winter, and it has stayed closed since.

The ferry berth has a different problem. The island ferry draws more water than the boat it replaced, and at the lowest spring tides it can touch the bottom while it waits at the ramp. On about twenty days a year, the first sailing is held until the tide rises.

## What phase two builds

- **A new North Quay wall.** A steel wall will be driven into the seabed about three metres in front of the old one, a method called [sheet piling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_pile). The gap is then filled and topped with concrete, so the quay ends up slightly wider than it is now.
- **A deeper ferry berth.** The seabed at the ferry ramp will be dredged by 1.5 metres, so the boat can sail at any tide.
- **A new fish landing bay.** The landing bay moves 60 metres east, next to the fish market, so catches no longer cross Harbour Road on forklifts.
- **New mooring bollards and lighting** along the whole length of North Quay.

## The timetable

The harbour board has split the work into stages so that only one part of the harbour is closed at a time.

| Stage | Dates | Where | What closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | January to April 2027 | East end of North Quay | Quay closed to the public; fish landings move to the old ferry slip |
| 2 | April to August 2027 | Ferry berth | Ferry uses the temporary pontoon on the South Mole |
| 3 | August 2027 to January 2028 | West end of North Quay | Car park on the quay closes; 60 spaces lost |
| 4 | January to June 2028 | Fish landing bay and Harbour Road | Harbour Road one-way for six weeks |

Dredging the berth, in stage 2, will be done at night where possible, so that the ferry timetable does not change.

## What it means for you

### If you take the ferry

From April to August 2027, the ferry will leave from a temporary pontoon on the South Mole, about 400 metres from today's terminal. A free minibus will run from the terminal car park for foot passengers. Cars will queue on the Mole itself. Arden Sound Ferries says sailing times will stay the same as in the [winter ferry timetable](/blog/winter-ferry-timetable/) and the summer one that follows it.

### If you fish from Port Arden

Boats will land at the old ferry slip from January to April. The harbour board has promised a forklift and a cold store at the slip for those months. Fish market manager Bill Ostrander said the fleet had been consulted. "It is not ideal, but it is a plan we can work with," he said.

### If you live or park nearby

Piling is noisy. The contractor will drive piles only between 08:00 and 18:00 on weekdays, and never on weekends. Homes on Quay Street and Ropewalk will get a letter two weeks before piling starts near them, with a phone number to call if the noise or vibration is a problem.

:::callout{kind=warning}
The East end of North Quay will be fenced off from 4 January. Do not climb the fences to fish or watch the work: the quay edge behind them is unsafe.
:::

## What it costs and who pays

| Funder | Amount |
|---|---|
| Government coastal grant | £12.4m |
| Port Arden Harbour Board | £6.1m |
| Port Arden Council | £3.1m |
| **Total** | **£21.6m** |

The council's share appears in its [draft budget for 2027](/blog/council-budget/). The harbour board will pay its part from harbour dues and a loan over twenty years.

## What comes after the harbour works

When phase two ends in mid 2028, the board plans to open the rebuilt North Quay to the public as a walkway, with benches and new railings. A third phase, to repair the old fish market building, has no date or money yet.

The last stage of the harbour works will also carry a new [cycle lane along Harbour Road](/blog/cycle-lane-approved/), which the council approved on 3 November, so the road is dug up once rather than twice.

Before any of that, the old dockside crane on North Quay has to go. It comes down this month; our story on [the road closures for the crane removal](/blog/crane-removal-road-closures/) has the dates.
