The winter ferry timetable between Port Arden and Maddock Island starts on Monday 2 November, Arden Sound Ferries confirmed on Wednesday. Four sailings will run each way on weekdays, down from six in summer, and the first boat of the day moves from 06:15 to 07:00 until the end of March.

The operator says the change follows the pattern of the last three winters, when the early sailing carried fewer than ten cars on most mornings. Friday evenings gain a later last boat, at 20:30 from Port Arden, after islanders asked for a way home from the town's Friday events.

## The winter ferry timetable, sailing by sailing

These times run from Monday 2 November 2026 to Sunday 28 March 2027. The crossing takes about 35 minutes. Check in at the terminal at least 20 minutes before departure with a vehicle, and 10 minutes before on foot.

| Leaves Port Arden | Arrives Maddock Island | Leaves Maddock Island | Arrives Port Arden | Days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 07:00 | 07:35 | 08:00 | 08:35 | Monday to Saturday |
| 10:30 | 11:05 | 11:30 | 12:05 | Every day |
| 14:00 | 14:35 | 15:00 | 15:35 | Every day |
| 17:30 | 18:05 | 18:30 | 19:05 | Every day |
| 20:30 | 21:05 | 21:30 | 22:05 | Fridays only |

On Sundays the first sailing is at 10:30, so there are three return crossings. On Christmas Day and New Year's Day no ferries run.

## What changes for passengers

- **No 06:15 sailing.** Workers on the island who start at 08:00 in town now need the 07:00 from Maddock, which arrives at 07:35.
- **A later Friday boat.** The 20:30 from Port Arden returns from the island at 21:30.
- **Fares stay the same.** Return fares for 2026: £38 with a car, £7.50 on foot. Island residents with a ferry card pay half.
- **Freight goes first.** On the 07:00 and 10:30 sailings, booked freight boards before cars.

## Why the times change in winter

Arden Sound Ferries runs one vessel through the winter while the second goes to dry dock for its yearly survey, which is why the winter ferry timetable has fewer sailings. "With one boat, we have to leave room in the day for weather," said operations manager Kit Laverty. "If we lose a sailing to the wind in the morning, the gap in the middle of the day lets us run it later instead of cancelling it outright."

That spare time matters on this crossing. The ferry is a [roll-on/roll-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roll-on/roll-off) vessel, and its ramps cannot be used safely when the swell at the island slipway passes a set height.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Sailings that are delayed or cancelled are posted on the terminal board and by text message. Sign up at the ticket office with your phone number and the sailings you use most on the winter ferry timetable.
:::
