A storm warning covers Port Arden and the coast from 06:00 on Friday 9 October until 03:00 on Saturday, the national weather service said on Thursday afternoon. Gusts of up to 65 mph are expected in the afternoon, at the same time as one of the highest tides of the year.

The council has asked people to stay off the promenade and the South Mole from midday until the tide has turned, while the storm warning is in force. "The wind on its own we can cope with," said the council's emergency planning officer, Neil Hargreaves. "It is the wind and the high water together that throw waves over the wall."

## When the storm warning is at its worst

The highest risk comes in the two hours either side of high water, when the tide is at its fullest and the wind pushes the sea against the wall. That is the time of a possible [storm surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_surge), when the sea rises above the height the tide tables predict.

| Time on Friday | What to expect |
|---|---|
| 06:00 to 12:00 | Rain, wind rising to gusts of 45 mph |
| 12:00 to 15:00 | Gusts of 55 to 65 mph from the south west |
| 15:40 to 19:40 | Highest risk of waves over the sea wall |
| 17:40 | High tide, 5.9 metres |
| 19:40 to 03:00 | Wind easing to gusts of 40 mph |

## What is closed

- **The promenade** between the lifeboat station and Bay Street closes at 12:00.
- **The South Mole** is shut to walkers and anglers all day.
- **Harbour Road** may close at short notice near the fish market if waves come over the wall.
- **Afternoon ferries** to Maddock Island are likely to be cancelled. Arden Sound Ferries will decide by 11:00.

Schools will stay open. Harbourside Primary, which sits closest to the sea, will keep pupils inside at break.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Do not walk along the sea wall to watch the waves. A single wave can knock an adult off their feet. Watch from the gardens on Cliff Terrace, well back from the railings.
:::

## Sandbags and help

Sandbags are free from the council depot on Mill Lane from 08:00 to 18:00 on Thursday and Friday, and staff will load them into your car. Homes on Bay Street and Water Lane, which flooded in 2023, will get a visit from council staff on Thursday evening.

If water comes into your home, call the council's flood line or, if anyone is in danger, the emergency services. Keep away from flood water: it is often deeper and faster than it looks.

We will post updates through Friday for as long as the storm warning lasts. For the ferry times affected, check the [winter ferry timetable](/blog/winter-ferry-timetable/), which starts in November, and the operator's notices at the terminal.
