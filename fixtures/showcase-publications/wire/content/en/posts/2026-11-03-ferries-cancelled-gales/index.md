Two morning ferries cancelled on Tuesday have cut Maddock Island off from Port Arden until the afternoon, as gales of up to 60 mph hit the harbour, Arden Sound Ferries said at 05:40. Neither the 07:00 nor the 10:30 sailing will run, and the company will decide on the 14:00 sailing by 12:00.

It is the second time in three days that the weather has stopped the ferry. Gales and high water together are the main risk on this coast, as our [storm warning](/blog/storm-warning-coast/) in October explained. The wind was blowing straight into the harbour mouth from the south west at dawn, with a swell of about two metres at the island slipway.

## Ferries cancelled and running on Tuesday

| Sailing | From Port Arden | From Maddock Island | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 07:00 | 08:00 | Cancelled |
| Second | 10:30 | 11:30 | Cancelled |
| Third | 14:00 | 15:00 | Will run, with a delay of up to 30 minutes |
| Fourth | 17:30 | 18:30 | Expected to run |

**Update, 12:10:** The 14:00 sailing will run, the company said, but may leave up to 30 minutes late while the crew checks the ramp at the island. Cars booked on the cancelled morning sailings will board first.

## Who is affected

About 60 islanders cross to the mainland on a normal Tuesday morning, most of them for work, school or hospital appointments. With the ferries cancelled, operations manager Kit Laverty said staff had phoned everyone with a booking. "Nobody likes cancelling, but the ramp cannot be used safely in a swell like this," he said. "We will run the moment it drops."

- **School pupils.** The eleven Port Arden High School pupils who live on the island will be marked present and given work online. A school bus will meet the 15:00 sailing if it runs.
- **Hospital patients.** The hospital has moved island patients' morning appointments to Thursday.
- **Freight.** The fuel lorry for the island's shop and filling station is now booked on Wednesday's 07:00.

## Why gales stop the ferry

The island ferry loads over a hinged steel ramp at each end of the crossing. In a strong swell, the ship rises and falls against the slipway, and the ramp can be damaged or slip while cars are on it. The company's rule is to stop loading when the swell at the island passes 1.5 metres.

The [Beaufort scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_scale) put Tuesday morning's wind at force 9, a severe gale, with stronger gusts.
