Port Arden Council's planning committee approved a protected cycle lane along Harbour Road on Tuesday night, by six votes to three. The lane will run for 1.6 kilometres from the railway station to the ferry terminal, separated from traffic by a low kerb, and should open in spring 2028.

The scheme will cost £2.4 million at 2026 prices, almost all of it from a government grant for walking and cycling that the council won in July 2026. It will be built alongside the last stage of the harbour works, so that Harbour Road is dug up once rather than twice.

## What the cycle lane looks like

The lane will run on the seaward side of Harbour Road, with cyclists going both ways in a strip three metres wide. A raised kerb will keep cars out, a design known as a [segregated cycle lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregated_cycle_facilities). At each of the five side roads, the lane will cross on a raised table with a give-way line for cars.

| Section | Length | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Station to Mill Lane | 450 m | Lane replaces a row of 18 parking spaces |
| Mill Lane to Customs House Square | 380 m | Pavement widened; new crossing at the library |
| Customs House Square to fish market | 420 m | Lane runs behind the bus stops |
| Fish market to ferry terminal | 350 m | Shared with the new North Quay walkway |

The 18 parking spaces will move to the North Quay car park when it reopens after the harbour works. There will be new cycle stands at the station, the library and the ferry terminal, 60 spaces in all.

:::figure{src="bike-rack.jpg" alt="A row of bicycle stands painted in bright rainbow colours against a pale wall, empty, the kind of stands planned beside the new cycle lane"}
Sixty new cycle stands will be fitted along the route, most of them at the station and the ferry terminal.
:::

## Why the committee split

Supporters said the lane would make the ferry and the station safer to reach without a car. Councillor Anwen Price said 400 people cycle along Harbour Road on a summer weekday already, "mixed in with [fish market](/blog/fish-market-hours/) lorries and the ferry traffic, which nobody would design on purpose".

Councillors who voted against it said the loss of parking would hurt Quay Street's shops, including the [new bakery](/blog/new-bakery-quay-street/) that opened last month. Ray Goddard, who runs the hardware shop on Market Street, said he was not against bikes "but against losing another 18 spaces in the same year we froze [parking charges](/blog/parking-charges-vote/) to help trade".

:::callout{kind=note}
The council says the lane will not change bus routes, and lorries for the fish market will still load from Harbour Road between 03:30 and 07:30.
:::

## What happens next

Detailed designs will go on show at the new library in February, and the council will ask residents about the crossings and the kerb height. Building would start in January 2028, when the harbour works reach Harbour Road, and take about four months. We will follow each step, as we follow every council decision under [our council reporting](/blog/council-reporting/) rules.

The seaward side of Harbour Road is also where the weekend's [storm damage](/blog/promenade-storm-damage/) was worst, so engineers will check the sea wall before the design is fixed. Cyclists heading for the boat this week should check for [ferries cancelled by the gales](/blog/ferries-cancelled-gales/) first.

The cycle lane is part of the same plan as the new North Quay walkway, set out in our guide to [what phase two of the harbour works means](/blog/harbour-works-phase-two/).
