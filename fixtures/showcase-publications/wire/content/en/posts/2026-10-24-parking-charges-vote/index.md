Port Arden Council's transport committee voted on Thursday evening to freeze town centre parking charges for 2027, rejecting a plan by council officers to raise them by 20p an hour. The committee also agreed to make the first hour free in every council car park on Saturdays, starting on 5 December.

The vote was 7 to 4. Committee chair Councillor Anwen Price said shops on Quay Street and Market Street had made their case. "They told us, with figures, that every rise in parking charges has been followed by a quieter high street," she said. "We listened."

## Parking charges from January 2027

| Car park | Up to 1 hour | Up to 2 hours | Up to 4 hours | All day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market Street | £1.20 | £2.20 | £4.00 | Not allowed |
| Customs House Square | £1.20 | £2.20 | £4.00 | £7.50 |
| North Quay | £1.00 | £1.80 | £3.20 | £6.00 |
| Westgate (leisure centre) | £0.80 | £1.50 | £2.80 | £4.50 |

These are the same as the charges in 2026. On Saturdays from 5 December, the first hour is free in all four car parks, and longer stays cost the price shown minus the one-hour charge.

North Quay car park closes in August 2027 for the third stage of the [harbour works](/blog/harbour-works-phase-two/), so the council will review all charges again next summer.

## What the freeze costs

Council officers had expected the 20p rise to bring in an extra £180,000 in 2027. The free Saturday hour will cost about £95,000 more in 2027. The committee agreed to find the money by putting off the resurfacing of the Westgate car park for a year.

Opposition councillors on the committee voted against the freeze. Councillor Freya Doyle said the council could not afford it "in the same month it is asking residents for a 3.9 percent council tax rise". The draft [council budget](/blog/council-budget/) is open for comment until 20 November.

:::callout{kind=note}
Blue badge holders park free in every council car park, for as long as they need, and the freeze does not change this.
:::

## What traders said

Five traders spoke at the meeting. Lena Varga, who opens [Tidewater Bakery on Quay Street](/blog/new-bakery-quay-street/) this weekend, said most of her customers would walk or cycle. Others were less sure. Ray Goddard, who has run the hardware shop on Market Street for 26 years, said Saturday trade had fallen by a quarter since 2019. "People drive to the retail park because it is free," he said. "One free hour is not the same, but it is a start."

## What happens next

The decision goes to the full council on 10 December as part of the budget. Councillors can change it there, but committee decisions on [parking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking) are rarely overturned. If the budget passes, the frozen charges apply from 1 January.

**Update, 4 November:** the planning committee has approved a [cycle lane along Harbour Road](/blog/cycle-lane-approved/), which moves 18 parking spaces near the station to North Quay when it reopens.
