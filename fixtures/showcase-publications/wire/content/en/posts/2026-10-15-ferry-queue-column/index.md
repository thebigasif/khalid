Every weekday at 06:45, about thirty people stand in the ferry queue at Port Arden terminal, and almost nobody looks at their phone. I have joined them most weeks for forty years, first as a harbour pilot on my way to work and now as a retired man with a sister on Maddock Island. In all that time, the queue has taught me more about this town than the council chamber has.

## What you learn in the ferry queue

You learn who is ill, because their neighbour is carrying their shopping. You learn whose boat is for sale and whose daughter got into university. You learn, in winter, which of the island's three plumbers is on the mainland that day, and you make a note of it.

You also learn who is new. Newcomers stand a little apart for the first month, holding their tickets. Then one wet morning somebody asks them where they are going, and after that they are part of it.

None of this is planned. The ferry company did not design its car lanes as a meeting place. It is simply what happens when the same people stand in the same place at the same time, five days a week, with nothing to do for twenty minutes but talk.

## Why I worry about the app

Arden Sound Ferries wants to bring in timed boarding next year. You would book a slot on your phone and arrive ten minutes before the boat instead of twenty. I understand why. The queue backs up onto Harbour Road on busy summer days, and nobody enjoys sitting in it in the rain.

But I would ask the company, and the council that licenses it, to count what the queue does before they shrink it. A woman I have known for years told me in the queue last winter that her husband had stopped eating. Two weeks later he was in hospital, and he is well now. I am not sure she would have told me by text.

## What I would keep

I am not against change. The [new winter ferry timetable](/blog/winter-ferry-timetable/) is sensible, and I will be glad of the later Friday boat. But if the company brings in timed boarding, I would ask three things:

1. Keep the waiting room at the terminal open and warm from the first sailing, so foot passengers still have somewhere to stand together.
2. Keep a lane for people who turn up without a booking. Not everyone on the island has a smartphone, or a signal.
3. Ask the people in the queue. Hold a meeting on the island and one in town before deciding.

A town needs a few places where people meet without meaning to. Ours used to be the market, the library and the pub. The market is half the size it was, and the library has just moved. The [mooring bollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollard) the ferry ties up to is about the only thing on the quay older than me. I would like to keep the ferry queue a while longer too.
