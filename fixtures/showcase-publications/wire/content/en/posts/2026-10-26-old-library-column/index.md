The old library on Chapel Street closed its doors for the last time at 17:00 on Saturday, and I was there for the last hour. So were about sixty other people, most of them over sixty, standing among the empty shelves as if waiting for someone to tell us to go home. Eventually, the librarian did.

The building opened in 1963, the year I started school. I learned to read properly there, at a low table under the window, with a librarian called Mrs Fairley who did not believe in children being in a hurry.

## What the old library was for

People talk about libraries as places for books, and they are. But what the old library really taught Port Arden was how to look things up. When I was a boy, a question that could not be answered at home was taken to Chapel Street. How deep is the harbour at low water? When did the last whale come into the bay? Who built our house? Somebody behind the desk would know where to start.

For years, the starting point was the [card catalogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_catalog): rows of wooden drawers, each holding hundreds of typed cards, one for every book. You pulled out a drawer, flicked through the cards and wrote the number on a slip of paper. It was slow, and it was wonderful, because you found three things you had not been looking for on the way to the one you were.

The drawers went in 1998, when the catalogue moved onto a computer. I was sorry then, and I was wrong to be. The computer found things faster. But I notice that the librarians never stopped being the real catalogue. They knew which shelf to try and which old fisherman to ask.

## What I hope the new library keeps

I have been round the new building, and it is very fine. It is warm and bright, and the archive at the top has a view of the whole harbour. I have no complaint about the [new library opening](/blog/library-opening-date/) or the hours, which are longer than the old ones.

I ask only this. Keep the desk staffed. Keep a person there whose job is to help you find things, not just to scan your card. A self-service machine can lend you a book. It cannot tell you that the answer to your question is in a box of harbour board minutes from 1951, on the third floor, and that it will fetch it for you on Tuesday.

## A last look

On Saturday the staff gave away the last of the old library's furniture. I took home a single card drawer, empty, which now sits on my desk and holds my pencils. The label on the front still says what it held: "Harbours. Lighthouses. Navigation."

Nora Fennick will take readers round [every floor of the new library](/blog/inside-new-library/) when it opens on Friday. I will be in the queue at 09:00 with my library card. It is the same card the old library gave me in 1974, and I am told it still works.
