This review of Small Hours is a mixed one, but a fond one. Bea Lund's comedy is set in a laundromat that stays open all night, and every episode covers one shift. The regulars come and go, the machines break, and not much else happens. When it works, it is lovely. When it does not, it is just a room with washing in it.

All ten episodes of *Small Hours* are on Halcyon Play.

## The regulars of Small Hours

Mira Kellett plays Joy, the night attendant, who has worked the late shift for so long that she has forgotten what the sun looks like. Kellett is a revelation. After her sharp detective in [Hollow Creek](/blog/hollow-creek-season-two-review/), she shows a gift for slow, dry comedy that the series leans on heavily.

Dev Arora plays a student who does his laundry every night to avoid his flatmates, and Jonah Pike is a retired taxi driver who comes in for the warmth. Their running argument about the best machine is the show's best joke.

## Where Small Hours is uneven

Each episode is only twenty minutes long, and that helps. However, about half of them rely on a guest star who wanders in with a problem, and those guests are hit and miss. A two-part story about a lost wedding dress is the low point.

The best episodes are the quiet ones, where the regulars simply talk while the machines spin. Episode seven, set during a power cut, is close to perfect.

## Our Small Hours verdict

:::review{item="Small Hours" type="CreativeWorkSeason" rating="3" best="5" author="Tomás Reyes"}
Small Hours is a small, sweet comedy set in an all-night laundromat, with a revelation of a lead in Mira Kellett. The quiet episodes, where the regulars simply talk, are close to perfect. Half the season leans on guest stars who are hit and miss, so watch it one or two at a time.
:::

It is a gentle [sitcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitcom) with a great lead, and it is best watched late, when the house is asleep. It is also the one comedy on [our nine picks for the winter](/blog/winter-watch-list/).
