Hollow Creek season 2 opens exactly where you want it to: in the fog, at dawn, with a body by the stream. Ruth Abernethy's crime series was the surprise hit of last year on Northlight+, and the new season keeps almost everything that worked. It also adds two episodes it did not need.

I watched all eight episodes before writing this review, so I can tell you that the ending is worth it. The road there is uneven.

## The story of Hollow Creek season 2

Owen Strand returns as the tired forest ranger, Callum Reid, now working with a new detective from the city, played by Mira Kellett. A hiker has gone missing in the same valley as last year's case, and the town would rather nobody looked too hard.

Kellett is the season's best addition. Her detective is sharp and impatient, and her scenes with Strand have a dry, spiky rhythm. Dara Fenn is excellent as the hiker's sister, who does her own searching.

## Where it goes wrong

The first three episodes are as good as anything last year. Then the season loses its way. Episodes four and five follow a side plot about a land sale that the finale barely mentions. As a result, the middle of the season drags, and the fog starts to feel like a habit rather than a mood.

## Our Hollow Creek verdict

:::review{item="Hollow Creek, season 2" type="CreativeWorkSeason" rating="3.5" best="5" author="Tomás Reyes"}
Hollow Creek season 2 keeps the fog, the mood and a strong cast, and adds a sharp new detective in Mira Kellett. Two middle episodes about a land sale drag badly, but the last two pull everything together. Fans should watch it; newcomers should start with the tighter first season.
:::

The final scene in the ranger station is the best thing the series has done. Season one is still on Northlight+.
