The nights are long, so our critics made a winter watch list. Some are in cinemas, most are streaming, and every one has a full review on Tenth Row. We picked a mix: three dramas, a thriller, two documentaries, two series and a comedy for when you need one.

## The winter watch list at a glance

| Title | Kind | Stars | Where to watch | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saltglass | Film | 4.5 | In cinemas | 118 min |
| The Quiet Orbit | Film | 4 | Lanternbox | 131 min |
| Night Ferry | Film | 4 | Lanternbox | 109 min |
| Paper Kingdoms | Documentary | 4 | Halcyon Play | 92 min |
| Hollow Creek, season 2 | Series | 3.5 | Northlight+ | 8 episodes |
| The Glasshouse Year | Documentary | 3.5 | Northlight+, Halcyon Play | 87 min |
| Small Hours | Series | 3 | Halcyon Play | 10 episodes |

| Winter Ledger | Series | 2.5 | Lanternbox | 6 episodes |

## For a night in

Start with [Night Ferry](/blog/night-ferry-review/). It is fast, funny and finished in under two hours. If you would rather think than gasp, [The Quiet Orbit](/blog/quiet-orbit-review/) is a slow, gripping space drama that looks best with the lights off.

## For a long weekend

Two series will fill a weekend. [the second season of Hollow Creek](/blog/hollow-creek-season-two-review/) has a wobbly middle but a great ending, and [Small Hours](/blog/small-hours-review/) is a gentle comedy in short episodes. Both are good company on a cold night. [Winter Ledger](/blog/winter-ledger-review/) is the weakest pick, there for Ilse Marrow alone.

## For a quiet afternoon

The two documentaries suit a slow day. [Paper Kingdoms](/blog/paper-kingdoms-review/) follows a mill town through its last year with the mill, and [The Glasshouse Year](/blog/glasshouse-year-review/) spends four seasons with the gardeners of an old glasshouse.

## For a trip to the cinema

Only two films on this list need a big screen. *Saltglass* is the one to see, and [our Saltglass verdict](/blog/saltglass-review/) explains why. [Dust Lines](/blog/dust-lines-review/) is weaker, but its desert pictures deserve a cinema.

## How we chose the winter watch list

Each critic named their five favourite films and series from the past three months, and we kept the nine with the most votes. Every rating comes from the full review, on the five-star scale explained on our [about page](/blog/about/). If you want to know more about the history of watching films at home, read about [home video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_video).
