This review of The Glasshouse Year is easy to write, because the film is easy to like. Tobias Wren spent twelve months with the six gardeners who look after an old glasshouse in a city botanic garden. He films the plants, the people and the weather, and asks very little of any of them.

*The Glasshouse Year* is streaming on both Northlight+ and Halcyon Play.

## What The Glasshouse Year shows

The glasshouse is over a hundred years old, and it leaks. The gardeners spend the year keeping thousands of plants alive inside it while builders repair the roof around them. That gives the film a gentle plot: will the palms survive the winter with half the glass missing?

The best scenes are small ones. A gardener talks to a cactus she has watered for nineteen years. Two apprentices argue about the right way to move a banana tree. The head gardener checks the heaters at three in the morning in a snowstorm.

## How it looks

Wren shoots the glasshouse like a cathedral. Light pours through the panes, and the time-lapse shots of plants turning to the sun are a joy. The music is soft piano, which suits the mood but rarely surprises.

## The weakness

The film is too polite. We learn that the garden's money is running short, and that the city may close the glasshouse to visitors. Wren mentions both and then moves on to another flower. Compare it with [Paper Kingdoms](/blog/paper-kingdoms-review/), which faces a closure head on, and this film feels shy.

## Our verdict on The Glasshouse Year

:::review{item="The Glasshouse Year" type="Movie" rating="3.5" best="5" author="Ines Varga"}
The Glasshouse Year is a calm, kind documentary about six gardeners keeping an old glasshouse alive through a year of repairs. It is lovely to look at, and its small scenes are a joy. It is also too polite, and it steps around the money troubles that threaten the garden.
:::

Watch it on a slow Sunday. It is a calm, kind film about patient work, and it will send you to your nearest [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden). Just do not expect it to ask hard questions.
