This review of Dust Lines comes with a confession: I wanted to love it. Lou Barca's road movie has two fine actors, a great idea and some of the most beautiful desert pictures in years. For its first hour, it nearly earns them. Then the road runs out, and the film keeps driving.

*Dust Lines* is in cinemas now and can also be rented from Marquee Rentals.

## The setup

June Adair plays Wren, a mechanic who agrees to drive a stranger's car across the desert for cash. Rafe Dunmore plays Abel, the stranger, who insists on coming along and will not say what is in the boot.

It is a classic setup, and Barca knows it. The early scenes are funny and tense, as the two of them test each other at petrol stations and roadside motels. Adair is especially good, with a quick, flat delivery that makes Wren seem older than her years.

## Where Dust Lines stalls

Around the hour mark, the mystery of the boot is solved, and the film has nowhere left to go. The last forty minutes drift from one lovely sunset to the next. Dunmore is given a long speech about his father that the film has not earned, and the ending simply stops.

In other words, the pictures are doing all the work. They are very good pictures. Barca and her camera crew shot the whole film in natural light, and the desert has never looked so pale and strange.
