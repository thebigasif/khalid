The short review of Winter Ledger is this: Ilse Marrow is very good, and the series around her is not. Edwin Marsh's six-part period drama looks handsome and sounds expensive. It also moves at the speed of the snow it is set in.

All six episodes are on Lanternbox now.

## The story of Winter Ledger

In a mountain village in the winter of 1891, the merchant who keeps the village accounts dies, and his ledger goes missing. Every family owes him money. His widow, played by Marrow, decides to find the book before anyone else does.

It is a good idea for a series. A ledger that names every debt in a small place is a box of secrets, and the first episode opens it well.

## What goes wrong

After that, Winter Ledger repeats itself. Each episode visits one family, finds a secret, and moves on. The pattern becomes clear by episode three, and nothing breaks it until the finale. Owen Strand, so good as [the ranger in Hollow Creek](/blog/hollow-creek-season-two-review/), is wasted as a stiff priest. Pell Cassidy has fun as the village doctor, but the script gives him one joke.

The dialogue is a bigger problem. Characters explain their feelings in full sentences, and then explain them again to someone else.

## What works

Marrow holds the series together. Her widow is cold, clever and slowly frightened, and she does more with a look across a table than most of the cast do with a page of lines. The snow, the candlelight and the costumes are all lovely, and the final episode finally gives her a scene worthy of her.

## Our Winter Ledger verdict

:::review{item="Winter Ledger" type="CreativeWorkSeason" rating="2.5" best="5" author="Tomás Reyes"}
Winter Ledger looks handsome and has a fine lead in Ilse Marrow, whose cold, clever widow holds every scene. The rest of the series repeats one pattern for six episodes, wastes Owen Strand and explains every feeling twice. Watch it only for Marrow, and only in a slow week.
:::

The final episode is the best of the six. For a better example of the [period drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_piece) on screen this year, look elsewhere. To see Marrow at her best, watch [her role in Saltglass](/blog/saltglass-review/) instead, the film at the top of [our winter picks](/blog/winter-watch-list/).
