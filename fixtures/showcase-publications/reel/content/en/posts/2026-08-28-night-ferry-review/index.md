Here is our review of Night Ferry in one sentence: it is the most fun I have had with a thriller all year. Anouk Delmar's film takes place on a single overnight crossing. A passenger goes missing, the crew says she was never on board, and a tired engineer decides to prove them wrong before the ship docks at dawn.

*Night Ferry* is streaming on Lanternbox now.

## A thriller that knows its job

Delmar keeps it lean. The film runs in close to real time, and almost every scene moves the story forward. There are a few twists, and most of them are fair. One, involving the ship's car deck, made the whole room gasp when I first saw it at a festival.

Teodor Vance plays the engineer, and it is a pleasure to see him run. In [Saltglass, a quiet storm of a film](/blog/saltglass-review/), he was soft and stubborn. Here he is quick, scared and very funny. Rafe Dunmore, so stiff in [Dust Lines](/blog/dust-lines-review/), is much better as a smooth purser who knows more than he says. Celia Brandt steals every scene as a passenger who only wants to sleep.

## The look and sound of Night Ferry

The ferry is a real ship, and Delmar uses every corner of it, from the engine room to the empty bar at three in the morning. The blue deck lights give the film a cold, strange glow. The sound of the engines is always there, low and steady, until the moment it stops.

## Our Night Ferry verdict

:::review{item="Night Ferry" type="Movie" rating="4" best="5" author="Nadia Brennan"}
Night Ferry is a lean, fast and very funny thriller set on one overnight crossing. Teodor Vance runs the film with charm, Celia Brandt steals every scene, and the twist on the car deck is a joy. The final explanation runs long, and one villain's speech should have gone.
:::

Those are small faults in a film that otherwise moves like a train. Watch it tonight. Its opening shot of a running man is one of the examples in our essay on [what makes a great opening scene](/blog/great-opening-scenes/). It is a classic [locked-room mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locked-room_mystery) on the water, made with skill and a sense of fun.
