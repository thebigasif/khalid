# Bench Test: art direction

Bench Test is a made-up review site for camera and audio gear. Its photographs should look like the bench of a small test lab: one object at a time, close, on a plain ground, in clean light. The reader should feel the weight of a microphone grille or the click of a fader before reading a single figure.

## Tone

- **The object is the subject.** A microphone, a lens, a knob, a connector or a light fills most of the frame. Hands may appear when they show a job being done, such as fitting a softbox ring, but no face is the subject of a picture.
- **Product light, not mood light.** Even studio light or soft window light on a plain ground: white, grey card, wood or acoustic panel. A few photographs are darker, low-key product shots (the dynamic microphone, the headphones), but the subject is always lit and sharp. No stage lighting, no coloured gels, no night scenes.
- **Generic or old gear.** Bench Test reviews made-up products, so no photograph may show a real brand. Candidates were chosen at angles or crops where no maker's name or logo can be read, and older or unbranded equipment was preferred. More than thirty candidates were turned down because a logo or model name was readable, among them a pop filter with a maker's name across it, two headphone ear cups, a tripod head, a patch cable and a ball head on a ring light.
- **Nothing to read.** No watermarks, labels or signs. Scale markings that are part of the gear, such as the numbers on a focus ring or a fader's level scale, are allowed, because they carry no name.

## Palette

Neutral and metallic, with one warm accent:

- graphite, black and steel greys from microphone bodies, lens barrels, cables and foam;
- white and pale grey grounds from product tables, backdrops and softbox linings;
- warm wood and the brass or gold of a pin, an insert or a microphone body as the accent.

This sits with Workbench's grey-white bench and single verdict green: the photographs stay neutral and leave colour to the template. Avoid photographs dominated by saturated blue or purple stage light. The one exception is the blue microphone body on the podcast microphone guide, kept because its ground is a calm off-white.

## Subject mix

Of the 25 photographs, 20 are close details and 5 are wider scenes, so four in five are close details (the brief asks for at least half).

- **Close details (20):** a studio microphone in a shock mount, the open lens mount and sensor of a camera, a blue studio microphone with a pop shield, the head of a dynamic microphone, a manual lens with a hood, aperture blades, faders on a mixing desk, knobs and potentiometers, the brass insert of a knob, a shotgun microphone, a lavalier microphone on a shirt, a film camera from above, headphones, a headphone socket, the pins of an XLR plug, jack and XLR plugs, acoustic foam, and three views of a softbox mounting ring being fitted.
- **Wider scenes (5):** a home photo studio with two lights and softboxes, a studio with octagonal softboxes, a product photography setup, a podcast studio with four boom arms, and a video tripod head on a beach.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post and cards | 16:9, 2:1 for the wide post header | 2000 px wide | Workbench crops the featured card to 16:9 and the post's lead to 2:1 from 1280 px. Every lead was chosen in landscape with its subject in the middle band. |
| In-body figure | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width with a caption. All seven body figures are 3:2 or within about 0.1 of it. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

The sources are not all one ratio. Of the 18 leads, 13 are about 3:2, one (the photo studio) is 16:9, one (the lavalier) is 1.63, one (the shotgun microphone) is 2:1 and 1536 px wide, one (the product studio) is 1.26 and one (the manual lens) is 1.17. A 3:2 source loses about a sixth from the top and bottom in a 16:9 crop and a quarter in a 2:1 crop; every 3:2 lead keeps its subject inside that band. The manual lens and the product studio lose the most. The lens sits in the middle of a white ground, so a crop costs only empty space. In the product studio, the top of the overhead softbox and the bottom of the tripod are cut, and the tabletop scene stays whole. These two are the leads to watch in the picture review.

## Phone crop

On a phone, the lead picture fills the width and is cropped from the centre. Each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Single objects (the microphones, the lens, the headphones, the XLR plug, the camera from above) sit in the middle third and survive a tight crop.
- Repeating textures (acoustic foam, faders, knobs) look the same at any crop.
- The shotgun microphone runs diagonally across its frame, so a tight crop keeps the slotted middle of the tube and loses the cable end, which is fine.
- The tripod head sits left of centre on its beach; a centre crop keeps the head and its handle but cuts the left end of the handle. It is the one phone crop to watch.

No photograph carries text, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets text over a lead picture, the dark leads (the dynamic microphone, the headphones, the faders) need a scrim to pass AA.

## Sources and licences

Every photograph comes from Wikimedia Commons through `scripts/demo-photo.py commons`, under CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA. Openverse refused this session's searches, so none came through it. `photos.lock` records each one, and `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the photo credits page credit them. Some credit lines carry the original file title, which can name a real maker or model; the photographs themselves show none.
