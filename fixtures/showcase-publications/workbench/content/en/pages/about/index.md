Bench Test reviews camera and audio gear for people who make videos and podcasts at home or in small studios. We buy or borrow every product, use it for at least two weeks, run it through the same tests on our bench and give it a score out of 10. Every review shows the figures we measured, so you can compare products that we tested months apart.

Bench Test is a made-up publication. The products and the makers we review are made up too, so the names and the figures in our reviews describe no real product.

## Who writes here

- **Oskar Brede** edits Bench Test. He was a broadcast sound engineer for twelve years and writes the buying guides.
- **Signe Halvorsen** reviews cameras, lenses and lights. She shot documentaries in small rooms for ten years.
- **Sol Ferreira** reviews microphones, interfaces, recorders and headphones, and has edited more than 600 podcast episodes.
- **Kit Adebayo** runs the lab and takes every measurement you see in a "Tested" table.

## How our lab tests gear

Our lab is one room with a small treated booth in the corner. Every product goes through the same tests, so a figure in one review means the same as that figure in another.

- **Microphones** read a test script in the booth and in an untreated spare room. We measure the noise floor, the gain a quiet voice needs and how much of the room each microphone picks up.
- **Cameras** film a moving subject on our autofocus course, a colour chart under a reference light and a timed recording until they stop. We note when a camera overheats and how long its battery lasts.
- **Lights** are measured with a light meter at one metre and filmed on the colour chart, so we can report brightness, colour accuracy and fan noise.
- **Interfaces, recorders and headphones** go through calibrated test files and a listening panel of three people who do not know which product they hear.

Our page on [how we test gear](/blog/gear-testing/) sets out each test in full, with the tools we use.

## How we score

Each review ends with a score out of 10. Sound or image quality counts for half of it. Build, ease of use and value for money make up the rest. A score of 8 or more means we would buy it ourselves at its price. A score under 6 means we would not recommend it to anyone.

Makers never see a review before it goes live, and nobody pays to be reviewed. When a maker lends us a product, we say so in the review and send it back afterwards. When a firmware update changes a result, we test again and update the review with the date.
