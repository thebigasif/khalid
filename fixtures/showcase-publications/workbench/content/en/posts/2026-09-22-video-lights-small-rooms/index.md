The best video lights for small rooms are bright through a softbox, accurate on skin and silent near a microphone, and our pick is the Moth 100. If you need to spend less, the Plover L60 is nearly as good but makes daylight only, and the Gorse B30 panel is the best light we tested at the lowest price.

A small room gives you little space to move a light back, so softness and a quiet fan matter more than raw power. We measured all five video lights at one metre, the distance most people can manage in a spare bedroom.

## How we chose the video lights

Each light was measured with our light meter at one metre and filmed on our colour chart to calculate the TLCI, a colour accuracy score for video where 100 is perfect. We measured fan noise at one metre and recorded a short podcast beside each light with a condenser microphone, to hear the fan for ourselves. [The way we measure lights](/blog/gear-testing/) is set out in full on our methods page.

## Moth 100: the best key light for a small room

**Price:** $299 in 2026, with a 60 cm softbox

The Moth 100 is the brightest and most accurate of these video lights, and it can change colour temperature from a warm 2,700 K to a cool 6,500 K. That lets you match a desk lamp or a window at any time of day. In quiet mode its fan measured 20 dB-A, below the hum of our room.

:::proscons
+ Brightest here: 2,900 lux through its softbox at 1 m
+ Bi-colour, from 2,700 to 6,500 K
+ Best colour accuracy, with a TLCI of 97
- Mains power only
- Heaviest at 1.4 kg; needs a sturdy stand
:::

## Plover L60: the best value key light

**Price:** $169 in 2026

Our [Plover L60 review](/blog/plover-l60-review/) found a bright, accurate and quiet light held back by a short power cable and daylight-only output. In a room where you control the light, with the curtains drawn, it does nearly everything the Moth 100 does for much less.

:::figure{src="softbox-assembly.jpg" alt="Several hands fitting a metal mounting ring into the centre of a large octagonal softbox for video lights, seen from inside, its silver lining divided by black ribs"}
A large softbox close to the face gives the softest light. In a small room, a 60 to 90 cm softbox at about one metre is the sweet spot.
:::

## Gorse B30: the best value panel

**Price:** $119 in 2026

The Gorse B30 is a flat bi-colour panel with no fan, so it is silent, and it runs on a camera battery for about 90 minutes. At 1,100 lux it is bright enough as a key light for a face at arm's length, or as a fill light opposite a stronger key. Its colour accuracy of 93 is good, though skin can look a touch green at the warm end.

:::proscons
+ Silent, with no fan
+ Runs on a battery, so it works anywhere
+ Bi-colour at a low price
- 1,100 lux; too dim as a key light beyond 1.5 m
- Slight green cast at 3,200 K
:::

## Linnet Tube 2: the best background light

**Price:** $89 each in 2026

The Linnet Tube 2 is a light stick that can make any colour. It is too dim to light a face on its own, but on a shelf behind you it adds a glow of colour that separates you from the wall. It runs on its own battery, so there is no cable to hide.

## Sedge Mini: a desk light for calls

**Price:** $59 in 2026

The Sedge Mini clips to a monitor and lifts the light on your face for video calls. At 380 lux it is no key light for video, and its colour accuracy of 88 is the lowest here. For calls, it is a big step up from a ceiling bulb.

## Which video light should you buy?

If you can buy only one of these video lights, buy the Moth 100: it is bright through a softbox, matches the colour of any room and stays quiet beside a microphone. On a tighter budget, the Plover L60 does nearly as well with the curtains drawn, and the silent Gorse B30 makes a good fill light.

- **One key light, any room, any time of day:** the Moth 100.
- **One key light on a budget, curtains drawn:** the Plover L60.
- **A silent fill light, or a key light for a small desk:** the Gorse B30.
- **Colour on the background:** one or two Linnet Tube 2s.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Two video lights beat one big light. Put your key light at 45 degrees to one side of your face and slightly above, and a dimmer fill light or a white board on the other side.
:::
