The Kestrel M50 is a pair of closed-back headphones that suits both recording and editing, and it scores 8.0 out of 10. It leaks so little sound that a microphone 15 cm away cannot hear it, and it plays voices evenly enough to edit a podcast on.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 8.0 out of 10
- **Type:** closed-back, over-ear studio headphones, wired
- **Price:** $109 in 2026
- **Best for:** monitoring while you record, and editing speech
- **Skip it if:** you wear headphones for more than two hours at a time in a warm room
:::

## What the Kestrel M50 is

The Kestrel M50 is a pair of wired, over-ear headphones with closed backs. Closed backs keep sound in, which matters when you wear headphones in front of a microphone. The cable is detachable and comes in two lengths, 1.2 m and 3 m, with a 3.5 mm plug and a screw-on 6.3 mm adapter. The ear cups fold flat for travel. They weigh 290 g without the cable.

## Tested

We measured the Kestrel M50 on our headphone rig and tested how much it leaks with a condenser microphone at 15 cm. Three listeners rated it blind against four other pairs. [Our headphone tests](/blog/gear-testing/) are described on our methods page.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Leak into a microphone at 15 cm, loud playback | Below the room noise | Safe to wear while recording |
| Blocking of outside sound | 21 dB at 1 kHz | Good isolation in a noisy home |
| Evenness through the voice range, 200 Hz to 4 kHz | Within 3 dB | Voices sound natural for editing |
| Bass lift below 100 Hz | 4 dB | A little extra weight; easy to allow for |
| Impedance | 38 ohms | Loud from a laptop or phone |
| Volume from a laptop at 70% | Comfortable | No amplifier needed |
| Clamping force | Firm | Stays put; tiring after two hours |
| Weight | 290 g | Light for closed-back headphones |

## How the Kestrel M50 sounds

For speech, the Kestrel M50 is even and clear. Through the range where voices live, it stays within 3 dB, so a guest who sounds harsh in these headphones really is harsh, and you can fix it with confidence. There is a small lift in the deep bass, which makes music more fun but means you should check your low end on speakers before you publish.

Our panel placed it second of five for editing speech, behind a pair costing twice as much.

## Recording with them on

This is where the Kestrel M50 shines. With loud playback and a condenser microphone at 15 cm, our recording showed no trace of the headphones above the room's own noise. Many open or semi-open headphones fail this test, and the leak returns as a faint echo in the microphone.

At 38 ohms they are easy to drive. A laptop, a phone with an adapter and every interface we own, including the [Osprey Two we use on the bench](/blog/osprey-two-review/), played them loudly with room to spare.

:::figure{src="headphone-jack.jpg" alt="A close view of a headphone socket of the kind the Kestrel M50 plugs into, in a white plastic panel with a small printed headphone symbol beside the opening"}
At 38 ohms, these headphones play loudly from any headphone socket, even the weak ones on laptops.
:::

## Comfort

The clamp is firm, which keeps them still when you move your head, and the pads seal well. The cost is warmth. After two hours in a 24 degree room, all three of our testers wanted a break. For a one-hour recording that is fine. For a full day of editing, you may want a lighter, open pair as well.

:::proscons
+ Leaks no measurable sound into a microphone at 15 cm
+ Even through the voice range, so edits translate well
+ Easy to drive at 38 ohms
+ Two detachable cables in the box
- Pads get warm after two hours
- Firm clamp can tire you on long sessions
- Slight bass lift; check low end on speakers
:::

## Who should buy the Kestrel M50

Buy them if you wear headphones while you record, or if you edit speech and want one pair for both jobs. They are the headphones in all three kits in our guide to [building a podcast studio at home](/blog/home-podcast-studio/). If you only edit, in long sessions, an open-back pair will be cooler and more comfortable.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Kestrel M50" type="Product" rating="8.0" best="10" author="Sol Ferreira"}
Closed-back headphones that leak nothing into your microphone and play voices evenly enough to edit on. They are easy to drive and come with two cables. They lose points for warm pads, a firm clamp on long sessions and a small bass lift. For recording and editing a podcast, they are the best value pair we have tested.
:::
