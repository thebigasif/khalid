The Plover L60 is a good first key light for a small video or podcast studio, and it scores 7.9 out of 10. It is bright enough to light a face through a softbox at two metres, its colour is accurate, and its fan stays quiet enough for a microphone a metre away.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 7.9 out of 10
- **Type:** 60 watt point-source LED light, daylight only
- **Price:** $169 in 2026 with a reflector, or $219 with a 60 cm softbox
- **Best for:** a key light for one person on camera
- **Skip it if:** you need to change colour temperature to match a warm room
:::

## What the Plover L60 is

The Plover L60 is a single-colour LED light rated at 60 watts. The LED sits behind a small reflector, and the front takes the common accessory mount that most softboxes and dishes use. A separate control box hangs off the light stand and holds the dimmer, a fan switch and the power socket. It weighs 1.1 kg without the box.

It produces daylight only, rated at 5600 K. There is no way to make it warmer, which matters if you mix it with a desk lamp or window light at sunset.

## Tested

We measured the Plover L60 with a light meter at one metre, then filmed our colour chart under it, as [our light tests](/blog/gear-testing/) describe.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Brightness at 1 m, bare reflector | 7,900 lux | Very bright for its size |
| Brightness at 1 m, through 60 cm softbox | 2,150 lux | Enough for a face at ISO 400 |
| Colour accuracy (TLCI) | 96 | Skin looks true on camera |
| Measured colour temperature | 5,480 K | Slightly warmer than its rating |
| Fan noise at 1 m, quiet mode | 21 dB-A | Inaudible in our podcast tests |
| Fan noise at 1 m, full power | 33 dB-A | Audible on a sensitive condenser |
| Dimmer flicker at 10% | None at 1/1000 s shutter | Safe for slow motion |
| Power cable length | 1.8 m | Too short for most rooms |

## Light quality

Through the 60 cm softbox the Plover L60 gives a soft, even light with a gentle fall-off. Our colour chart came out with a TLCI of 96, so skin tones need no correction. The measured temperature of 5,480 K is a little warm of its 5600 K rating. That is easy to fix with a camera's white balance, and we prefer it to a light that runs cold.

At 2,150 lux through the softbox at one metre, it is bright enough to film at ISO 400 with room to spare. In our [roundup of lights for small rooms](/blog/video-lights-small-rooms/) it was the brightest light under $200 in 2026.

The softbox's mounting ring takes a minute to fit. After that, the whole softbox twists onto the light's front and stays on.

## Noise and handling

In quiet mode the fan dropped to 21 dB-A at one metre, below the hum of our room. We recorded a podcast with the [Larch 24 condenser](/blog/larch-24-review/) one metre away and could not hear the fan in the recording. At full power the fan rises to 33 dB-A and our panel heard it in the pauses, so keep it in quiet mode when you record sound. Quiet mode caps the output at four fifths of full power, which was still more light than we needed.

The control box is the weak spot. Its power cable is only 1.8 m long, so in most rooms you will need an extension lead on the floor. The dimmer knob is smooth and clearly marked, but the display is hard to read from the side.

:::proscons
+ Bright: 2,150 lux through a softbox at one metre
+ Accurate colour with a TLCI of 96
+ Quiet mode keeps the fan out of your recordings
+ Takes the common softbox mount, so accessories are cheap
- Daylight only, so it cannot match a warm room
- Power cable of 1.8 m is too short
- Display is hard to read at an angle
:::

## Who should buy the Plover L60

Buy the Plover L60 if you need one strong key light for a face in a room you can control. With a softbox and a white board on the other side, it is a complete one-light setup. If your room has warm lamps you want to keep in the shot, look for a bi-colour light instead.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Plover L60" type="Product" rating="7.9" best="10" author="Signe Halvorsen"}
A bright, accurate and quiet key light that makes a single face look good through a softbox. It loses points for being daylight only, for a short power cable and for a display that is hard to read. For one person on camera in a small room, it is good value.
:::
