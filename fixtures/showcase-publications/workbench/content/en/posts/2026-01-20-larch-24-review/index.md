The Larch 24 is the best microphone we have tested for a soft, close voice in a quiet room, and it scores 8.4 out of 10. It is warm, very quiet and easy to drive, but it hears every echo of a bare room, so it suits a treated corner better than a kitchen table.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 8.4 out of 10
- **Type:** large-diaphragm condenser, cardioid, XLR
- **Price:** $249 in 2026, with a shock mount
- **Best for:** solo podcasts and voice-over in a treated room
- **Skip it if:** you record in an echoey room or move around while you talk
:::

## What the Larch 24 is

The Larch 24 is a side-address condenser microphone with a 24 mm capsule, the size most studio vocal microphones use. It needs 48 volt phantom power from your interface or mixer. The body is solid metal and weighs 520 g, so it needs a sturdy boom arm. The maker includes a spider shock mount, a foam windscreen and a short XLR cable.

There is one switch on the body: a 10 dB pad for loud sources. There is no bass cut switch, which matters later in this review.

## Tested

We ran the Larch 24 through [our microphone tests](/blog/gear-testing/) in the booth and in an untreated spare room. Each figure is the average of three runs.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Self-noise | 9 dB-A | Very quiet; you will not hear hiss in a pause |
| Gain for a quiet voice at 10 cm | 38 dB | Any interface we own drives it cleanly |
| Room pickup, spare room | 6.1 out of 10 | Hears more room than a dynamic microphone |
| Plosive test, 20 "p" words | 3 pops without windscreen, 0 with | Use the foam or a pop filter |
| Handling noise through shock mount | Low | Desk taps barely register |
| Weight | 520 g | Needs a solid boom arm |

## How the Larch 24 sounds

The Larch 24 has a gentle lift in the upper middle that makes speech clear without sounding thin. Low voices sound full at 15 cm without a muddy build-up, because the proximity effect is mild for a microphone of this size. Our listening panel picked it as the most natural of the four condensers it heard blind in January.

The low self-noise is the headline. At 9 dB-A it is quieter than most microphones at twice the price, so you can record a whisper and raise it in editing without bringing up hiss. Paired with a modest interface such as the [Osprey Two from our interface review](/blog/osprey-two-review/), the chain stayed silent in every pause.

## Where it struggles

A condenser this sensitive hears the room as well as the voice. In our untreated spare room, with a hard floor and a window, the Larch 24 scored 6.1 for room pickup, where the [dynamic Wren D7](/blog/wren-d7-review/) scored 8.9. The echo was not ugly, but it was there, and no setting on the microphone can remove it. Our test of [panels, foam and soft furnishing](/blog/acoustic-foam-tested/) shows how much a treated room changes this result.

The missing bass cut switch is the other weak point. Footsteps and a desk fan came through as a low rumble that we had to filter in editing. Most recording software can do that in one click, but a switch on the body would have saved the step.

:::proscons
+ Very low self-noise of 9 dB-A, so quiet passages stay clean
+ Warm, clear voice sound that our blind panel preferred
+ Easy to drive; 38 dB of gain is enough for a quiet speaker
+ Solid metal body and a good shock mount in the box
- Picks up a lot of room in an untreated space
- No bass cut switch, so rumble needs a filter in editing
- Heavy at 520 g; light boom arms sag
:::

## Who should buy the Larch 24

Buy the Larch 24 if you record alone, sit still, and have a room with some soft furnishing or a few acoustic panels. Voice-over artists and narrators will love its low noise. If two people share a table, or your room has bare walls, a dynamic microphone will give you a cleaner result for less work, as our [podcast microphone buying guide](/blog/best-podcast-microphones/) explains.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Larch 24" type="Product" rating="8.4" best="10" author="Sol Ferreira"}
A quiet, warm large-diaphragm microphone that makes a soft voice sound rich and stays silent in the pauses. It loses points for the room it hears in an untreated space and for the missing bass cut switch. In a calm, furnished room it is the best value condenser we have tested.
:::

For more on why condensers hear so much of a room, see the article on [microphone pickup patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone#Polar_patterns).
