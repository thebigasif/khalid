In our test of the Pike X2 against the Finch C1, the Pike is the better camera for most solo creators, with a score of 8.7 against 8.0. Its autofocus is more reliable and you can change its lens, but the Finch C1 is smaller, has a headphone socket and in 2026 cost $300 less with a lens built in.

Both cameras are made for people who film themselves. The Pike is a small mirrorless camera that takes interchangeable lenses. The Finch C1 is a compact camera with a fixed zoom lens and a 1-inch sensor, larger than a phone's. We used both for three weeks and ran them through the same tests.

## How we tested them

We ran both cameras through our standard tests on the same day: the autofocus course, a recording until each camera stopped, a battery run and our colour chart under a reference light. Then each of us took one camera as our everyday camera for a week and swapped. The [full review of the Pike](/blog/pike-x2-review/) has every result for that camera, and [each of these tests](/blog/gear-testing/) is explained on our methods page.

## Autofocus: the Pike wins

The Pike kept a walking presenter sharp in 97 of every 100 frames, the Finch C1 in 89. The gap grew in dim light: by one lamp in the evening, the compact hunted for focus several times a minute. If you film alone and cannot check every take, this is the most important difference between the two.

## Image quality: the Pike, unless you count the lens

The Pike's larger sensor gives cleaner pictures in low light, with noise showing only from ISO 12800. The Finch C1 shows noise from ISO 3200. But the compact's lens is much brighter than the Pike's kit lens, at f/1.8 against f/3.5, which closes most of that gap at the wide end. Add the [Marten 35mm prime](/blog/marten-35mm-review/) to the Pike and it pulls clearly ahead, with a softer background and cleaner low light.

## Sound: the Finch C1 wins

The compact wins here. Its built-in microphones scored 6.9 with our panel against 5.1 for the Pike, good enough for a quick social clip. More important, it has a headphone socket, so you can hear a crackling cable or wind while you film. On the Pike you have to play a test clip back, or add a [shotgun microphone from our comparison](/blog/shotgun-microphones-compared/) and trust its meters.

## Size and heat: a split

The Finch C1 fits in a jacket pocket and weighs 302 g ready to film. It is the camera you take everywhere. But it stopped with a heat warning after 41 minutes of 4K, where the Pike ran for 72 minutes until the card was full. For long talks or streams, that matters.

## Which should you buy?

Most solo creators should buy the Pike, because its autofocus keeps you sharp when nobody is behind the camera and it does not overheat in long takes. Buy the Finch C1 if you film while travelling, want one small camera that fits a pocket, or record sound on the camera and need to hear it through headphones.

- **You film talking-head videos at a desk, or long takes:** the Pike, on [a steady video head](/blog/video-tripod-heads/).
- **You film while travelling and want one small camera:** the Finch C1.
- **You record sound on the camera with no separate recorder:** the Finch C1.
- **You plan to add lenses over the years:** the Pike.
