The Pike X2 is the camera we recommend to most people who film themselves, and it scores 8.7 out of 10. In our 2026 tests its face tracking kept a walking presenter sharp in 97 of every 100 frames, and since firmware 1.3 it records 4K for more than an hour without stopping.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 8.7 out of 10 (8.1 at launch)
- **Type:** mirrorless camera, APS-C sensor, interchangeable lenses
- **Price:** $899 body only in 2026, or $1,049 with the 16 to 50 mm kit lens
- **Best for:** vlogs, talking-head videos and small interviews
- **Skip it if:** you need a headphone socket or film in heavy rain
:::

## What the Pike X2 is

The Pike X2 is a small camera with an APS-C sensor and the maker's own lens mount. It weighs 415 g with a battery and card, about the same as a large phone in a case. The screen flips out to the side and turns to face you, which is what a vlogger needs. It records 4K at up to 60 frames a second and full HD at up to 120.

There is a microphone socket but no headphone socket. You can only check your sound through the level meters on screen, or by playing a clip back. We cover what that means in practice below.

## Tested

Every figure comes from [our autofocus course and battery test](/blog/gear-testing/), run on firmware 1.3.

## Autofocus you can forget about

Our autofocus course has a presenter walk towards the camera, turn to look at a shelf, then walk back. The Pike X2 held focus on the eyes in 97 of every 100 frames in 4K, and lost the face only when it was turned fully away. When the face came back, the camera found it again in under half a second. In an evening test lit by one lamp, it still kept 91 of every 100 frames sharp.

This is the reason to buy it. Most people who film themselves have nobody behind the camera, and a soft shot is the mistake you only see when you edit.

## Picture quality

Skin tones look natural straight from the camera, with a slight warmth that most viewers like. Under our reference light the colour chart came out close to true, and the flat picture profile gives you room to grade if you want to. In low light, noise stays fine up to ISO 6400 and becomes visible at 12800.

The kit lens is the weak part of the package. It is soft in the corners at its widest setting, and its widest aperture of f/3.5 makes it hard to blur the background. Our [review of the Marten prime lens](/blog/marten-35mm-review/) covers the lens we would add first.

## Sound and the missing headphone socket

The built-in microphones are fine for a quick clip and poor for anything you publish. With a small on-camera microphone in the 3.5 mm socket, sound was clean; our [shotgun microphone comparison](/blog/shotgun-microphones-compared/) names the one we use, and for interviews a [wireless lavalier pair](/blog/lavalier-microphones-compared/) is better still. The trouble is checking it. Without a headphone socket you cannot hear a crackling cable or a wind gust while you film. We now record a five-second test clip and play it back before every take, and we suggest you do the same.

:::callout{kind=note}
We tested the Pike X2 at launch in February and found that it stopped after 23 minutes of 4K. Firmware 1.3 in September fixed this, so we tested again and raised the score. The table above shows both results.
:::

## Who should buy the Pike X2

Buy it if you film yourself, alone, for a video channel or an online course. Pair it with a brighter lens and a small microphone and you have a kit that will last years. If you film interviews with a separate sound recorder, the missing headphone socket matters less. If you are weighing it against a pocket camera, read our [head-to-head with the Finch C1](/blog/pike-x2-vs-finch-c1/).

## Verdict

:::review{item="Pike X2" type="Product" rating="8.7" best="10" author="Signe Halvorsen"}
The most dependable face tracking we have tested at its price, in a small body that no longer overheats. It loses points for the missing headphone socket, a short battery life and a soft kit lens. For anyone who films alone, it is the camera to beat.
:::
