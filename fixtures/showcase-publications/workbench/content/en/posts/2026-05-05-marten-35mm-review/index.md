The Marten 35mm is the first lens we would add to a small camera such as the Pike X2, and it scores 8.2 out of 10. Its bright f/1.8 aperture lets in more than three times as much light as a kit lens, blurs the background behind a face, and it focuses without a sound.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 8.2 out of 10
- **Type:** 35 mm prime lens, autofocus, for APS-C cameras
- **Price:** $279 in 2026
- **Best for:** talking-head videos, interviews and low light
- **Skip it if:** you film yourself at arm's length, which needs a wider lens
:::

## What the Marten 35mm is

The Marten 35mm is a prime lens, which means it does not zoom. On an APS-C camera it frames about the same view as a 50 mm lens on a full-frame camera, close to what your eye sees when you look straight ahead. Its widest aperture lets in a lot of light. It weighs 170 g and takes 52 mm filters.

It comes with a deep hood that blocks stray light and protects the front glass from fingers.

## Tested

We tested the Marten 35mm on the [Pike X2 we reviewed in February](/blog/pike-x2-review/). Sharpness is scored from our test chart, centre and corners, on a scale where 10 is the best lens we have measured on this camera.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Sharpness wide open, centre | 8.6 out of 10 | Sharp enough for 4K wide open |
| Sharpness wide open, corners | 6.9 out of 10 | Soft corners; fine for faces |
| Sharpness at f/4, corners | 8.8 out of 10 | Sharp across the frame stopped down |
| Focus noise, measured at the camera's microphone | Not audible | Safe with the built-in microphone |
| Focus breathing, near to far | 4% change in framing | Barely visible |
| Face tracking with the Pike X2, walking test | 96% of frames sharp | Matches the kit lens |
| Weight | 170 g | Keeps the camera small |

## Image quality of the Marten 35mm

Wide open, the centre is sharp and the corners are soft. For a face in the middle of the frame, that is exactly what you want: the subject is crisp and the background melts into a smooth blur. Stop down to f/4 and the corners sharpen to match the centre, which suits a product shot or a room.

Out-of-focus lights show as smooth, round discs with no hard outline, because the aperture blades are rounded. Colour matched the kit lens closely, so you can mix shots from both without grading.

:::figure{src="aperture-blades.jpg" alt="A close-up of a lens aperture stopped down to a small opening, its curved metal blades forming a near-round hole like the rounded blades of the Marten 35mm"}
Rounded aperture blades keep the opening close to a circle when you stop down, so lights in the background blur into round shapes.
:::

## Focus and handling

Autofocus is fast and silent. We filmed with the camera's built-in microphone and could not hear the motor, even in a quiet room. Face tracking kept 96 of every 100 frames sharp in our walking test, the same as the kit lens. Focus breathing, the small change in framing when focus shifts, was too small to notice in normal use.

The manual focus ring is the weak point. It is narrow, and it turns the focus by a different amount depending on how fast you turn it. That makes a slow, repeatable focus pull hard. If you pull focus by hand for a film look, you will find this frustrating.

:::proscons
+ Bright aperture: over three times the light of a kit lens
+ Sharp centre wide open, sharp corners by f/4
+ Silent autofocus that never reaches your recording
+ Small and light at 170 g
- Soft corners wide open
- Manual focus ring is narrow and changes speed
- Too tight for filming yourself at arm's length
:::

## Who should buy the Marten 35mm

Buy it if you film other people, or yourself from [a tripod with a smooth video head](/blog/video-tripod-heads/) across a desk. It turns a plain room into a soft background and lets you film by one lamp in the evening. If you hold the camera at arm's length to vlog, 35 mm is too tight and a wider lens will suit you better. Our [comparison of two cameras for solo creators](/blog/pike-x2-vs-finch-c1/) shows how it changes the picture.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Marten 35mm f/1.8" type="Product" rating="8.2" best="10" author="Signe Halvorsen"}
A small, bright and silent lens that lifts a talking-head video above what any kit lens can do. It loses points for soft corners wide open and an awkward manual focus ring. It is the best upgrade we have tested for a small mirrorless camera.
:::
