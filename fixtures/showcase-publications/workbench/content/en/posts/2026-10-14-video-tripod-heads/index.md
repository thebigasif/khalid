Of the four video tripod heads we tested for small cameras, the Fulmar F5 gives the smoothest pans for the money and scores 8.4 out of 10. The dearer Gannet 8 is smoother still for heavier rigs, and the cheap Shrike V3 is fine for still shots but stutters on slow pans.

A video head is the part that sits on top of the tripod legs and lets the camera turn and tilt. A photo head locks in place. A video head has drag, a gentle resistance that lets you pan slowly without jerks. That drag is what we tested.

## How we tested the video tripod heads

We mounted a [small mirrorless camera with face tracking](/blog/pike-x2-review/) and a [35mm prime lens](/blog/marten-35mm-review/) on each head, a rig of 0.6 kg. We filmed a slow 30-second pan across a brick wall and measured the jitter, the small jumps in the picture, in pixels at 4K. Then we added weight to find the heaviest rig each head would hold level when tilted forward without the camera drooping. Our page on [how we test and score gear](/blog/gear-testing/) explains the weights behind each score.

## Fulmar F5: the best for most people

**Price:** $189 in 2026

The Fulmar F5 panned with only 0.6 pixels of jitter, smooth enough that viewers will never see a stutter. Its three drag steps cover a slow, deliberate pan and a quick follow of a moving subject. Counterbalance, a spring that stops the camera drooping when you tilt, holds a 3 kg rig steady. Its plate fits the common dovetail clamps, so it swaps with gimbals and sliders.

:::proscons
+ Smooth pans with 0.6 px of jitter
+ Three drag steps and two counterbalance steps
+ Common plate type works with other gear
- 1.1 kg is heavy for a travel kit
- The pan handle is short for slow, precise moves
:::

## Gannet 8: the smoothest, for heavier rigs

**Price:** $329 in 2026

The Gannet 8 is the smoothest head we have tested, at 0.3 pixels of jitter, and its drag and counterbalance are fully variable. It holds a 6 kg rig, enough for a larger camera with a monitor and a microphone. For a small camera like ours it is more head than you need, and at 2 kg it doubles the weight of the kit.

:::proscons
+ Smoothest pans in our test at 0.3 px
+ Variable drag and counterbalance
+ Holds a 6 kg rig comfortably
- 2 kg; heavy to carry
- Expensive for a small camera
:::

## Shrike V3: fine for still shots

**Price:** $99 in 2026

The Shrike V3 holds a small camera steady for a locked-off interview, and its pan handle is long and comfortable. Its drag is fixed and uneven: slow pans showed 2.4 pixels of jitter, which reads as a visible stutter. Buy it only if your camera rarely moves.

## Ptarmigan Mini: for a light camera on a desk

**Price:** $59 in 2026

The Ptarmigan Mini is small and light and fits a tabletop tripod. With our 0.6 kg rig it was near its limit, and slow pans jumped visibly. It suits a phone or a very small camera filming a desk from above.

## Which video tripod head should you buy?

For a small camera, buy the Fulmar F5: it pans smoothly enough that viewers never see a stutter, its drag and counterbalance cover most shots, and its common plate fits other gear. Choose the Gannet 8 for a heavier rig or the smoothest moves, and the Shrike V3 only for interviews where the camera never moves.

- **A small camera, smooth pans, one head for everything:** the Fulmar F5.
- **A larger camera rig, or the smoothest moves:** the Gannet 8.
- **Interviews where the camera never moves:** the Shrike V3.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Set the counterbalance before the drag. Tilt the camera forward and let go; with the right setting it stays where you left it. Then add only as much drag as you need for the speed of your pan.
:::
