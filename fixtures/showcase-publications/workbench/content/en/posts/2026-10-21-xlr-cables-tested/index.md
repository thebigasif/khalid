No, expensive XLR cables do not sound better: in our tests, the cheapest cable and the dearest passed the same signal and our blind panel could not tell them apart. What you pay for is how long a cable survives being plugged, coiled and stepped on, and the cheap Teal Standard was the best value for that.

An XLR cable carries the signal from a microphone to an interface or mixer. It has three wires inside a shield and a locking plug at each end. Because the signal is balanced, the cable cancels most noise it picks up on the way, which is why cheap XLR cables work so well.

## How we tested the XLR cables

We ran a sine sweep from 20 Hz to 20 kHz through each cable into our reference interface and compared it with a 30 cm reference lead. We laid each cable beside a mains extension lead carrying a 2 kW heater to measure hum, and flexed each one by hand at a microphone's gain to hear handling noise. A machine plugged and unplugged each cable 2,000 times. Then three listeners heard the same voice through all six, blind. [The tools on our bench](/blog/gear-testing/) are listed on our methods page.

## What the tests show

Every cable passed the signal unchanged, to within a tenth of a decibel from the lowest bass to the highest treble. At 3 metres, a cable is far too short to change how a voice sounds. The blind panel's picks were spread across three different cables, which is what you would expect if they were guessing.

Hum was low for all six. The cheapest, the Dunlin Basic, let in the most, at -109 dB, still far below anything you would hear in a recording.

The real differences were in build. The Dunlin Basic's plug came loose after 1,140 cycles and it made a scratching noise when flexed, because its shield is thin. The Knot Gold failed at 1,620 cycles despite its gold-plated pins and a price four times the Teal Standard's.

## Our picks

- **Teal Standard:** the best value. Flawless in every test, survived 2,000 plugs and coils neatly.
- **Plover Pro:** the best if your cables live on a floor and get stepped on. Its outer jacket is the toughest and it coils without a kink.
- **Skip the Avocet Studio and the Knot Gold:** they measured the same as the Teal Standard and cost four to six times as much.

:::proscons
+ Teal Standard: perfect in every test at a low price
+ Teal Standard: survived 2,000 plug cycles
- Teal Standard: plugs are plain, with no colour rings to tell cables apart
- Teal Standard: the jacket marks more easily than the Plover Pro's
:::

:::callout{kind=tip}
If you hear hum or crackle, the cable is rarely the cause. Check that the plug is fully locked, move the cable away from power leads, and try a different input before you buy anything.
:::

## Verdict

:::review{item="Teal Standard XLR cable" type="Product" rating="8.3" best="10" author="Kit Adebayo"}
The Teal Standard passed every test as well as a cable six times its price, survived 2,000 plug cycles and coils neatly. It loses points only for plain plugs and a jacket that marks easily. Expensive XLR cables do not sound better; buy a well-built cheap one and spend the rest on your room.
:::
