The Osprey Two is the audio interface we recommend for a podcast with one or two people, and it scores 8.5 out of 10. Its preamps give 66 dB of clean gain, enough to run a quiet dynamic microphone without hiss, and in 2026 it cost $159.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 8.5 out of 10
- **Type:** USB audio interface, two microphone inputs, two outputs
- **Price:** $159 in 2026
- **Best for:** podcasts and streams with one or two XLR microphones
- **Skip it if:** you need more than two microphones at once
:::

## What the Osprey Two is

The Osprey Two is a small box that connects two microphones to a computer over USB. Each input takes an XLR microphone or an instrument cable, with its own gain knob, a pad and phantom power for condensers. On the front there is a headphone socket with its own volume knob, and on the back two speaker outputs. It draws its power from the USB cable, so there is no power supply to carry.

A switch on the front turns on direct monitoring, so you hear yourself without the small delay a computer adds.

## Tested

We measured the Osprey Two with our test files and with a [gain-hungry Wren D7](/blog/wren-d7-review/). Our [noise measurement](/blog/gear-testing/) is described on our methods page.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum gain | 66 dB | Plenty for any dynamic microphone |
| Noise at 58 dB of gain (EIN) | -128 dBu | As clean as interfaces at twice the price |
| Round-trip delay at 64 samples, 48 kHz | 4.1 ms | Fine for live monitoring through software |
| Headphone output into 250 ohm headphones | Loud enough at 60% | Drives hard-to-drive studio headphones |
| Phantom power | 48 V, both inputs | Runs any condenser |
| Gain knob, last tenth of its travel | 14 dB of change | Fine adjustment near the top is hard |
| Size and weight | 18 cm wide, 520 g | Sits on any desk |

## How the Osprey Two sounds

At 58 dB of gain, the level a Wren D7 needs for a quiet voice, the Osprey Two measured -128 dBu of noise. That is about as low as preamps go. We recorded a whispered passage and raised it by 20 dB in editing, and still heard only the room.

The headphone output is strong too. It drove our 250 ohm studio headphones to a comfortable level with the knob just past halfway, where many small interfaces run out of volume.

## Using it

Setting up took two minutes on each of our three computers, with no driver needed on two of them. The gain knobs have a ring of lights around them that turns amber near clipping and red when you clip, so you can set a level at a glance.

The gain knobs are our one real complaint. The last tenth of their travel adds 14 dB, so the setting you want for a dynamic microphone sits in a narrow arc where a small turn makes a big change. It is easy to overshoot and hard to match two hosts exactly.

:::proscons
+ 66 dB of clean gain, with noise of -128 dBu
+ Strong headphone output for demanding studio headphones
+ Clear level lights around each gain knob
+ Powered over USB, with no driver needed on most computers
- Gain jumps 14 dB in the last tenth of the knob's travel
- Only two microphone inputs
- No loopback switch on the box; you set it in software
:::

## Who should buy the Osprey Two

Buy it if you record one or two people with XLR microphones, especially dynamic ones. It is the interface in our mid-budget kit in [our home studio guide](/blog/home-podcast-studio/), and it pairs well with [closed-back headphones that leak nothing](/blog/kestrel-m50-review/). If you need four people in a room without a computer, the [Heron R4 mixer](/blog/heron-r4-review/) records them all to a card.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Osprey Two" type="Product" rating="8.5" best="10" author="Sol Ferreira"}
Preamps as clean as interfaces at twice the price, a strong headphone output and a simple setup make the Osprey Two the easy choice for a small podcast. It loses points for gain knobs that jump near the top and for having only two inputs. It is the interface we recommend most.
:::
