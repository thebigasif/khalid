The best podcast microphone for most people is the Wren D7, a dynamic microphone that ignores the room and cost $129 in 2026. If you record in a quiet, treated room, the Larch 24 condenser sounds richer, and if you want to plug straight into a laptop, the Alder U1 is the USB microphone to get.

We tested nine microphones on our bench this year, and five made this list of the best podcast microphones. Each one went through the same tests, so the figures in the table compare fairly.

## How we chose the best podcast microphones

We judge podcast microphones on speech, not singing. Every microphone read the same script in our treated booth and in an untreated spare room with a hard floor. We measured how much of the room each one picked up, how much gain a quiet voice needs, and how it handled plosives and desk taps. A blind panel of three listeners rated the voice sound. [The tests behind every figure](/blog/gear-testing/) are set out on our methods page.

## Wren D7: the best for most people

**Price:** $129 in 2026, XLR

The Wren D7 is the microphone we would hand to a new podcaster, and our [full Wren D7 review](/blog/wren-d7-review/) explains why. It is a dynamic microphone, so it hears your voice and very little of the room, and it scored 8.9 for room pickup in our spare room. That matters more than anything else for most home studios, where the walls are bare and the neighbour has a dog.

It needs a lot of gain, 58 dB for a quiet speaker at 10 cm, so pair it with an interface that stays clean at high gain.

:::proscons
+ Ignores the room better than any other microphone we tested this year
+ Warm, clear voice sound with a built-in pop filter
+ Tough metal body
- Needs 58 dB of gain for a quiet voice
- Sounds dull if you sit more than 15 cm away
:::

## Larch 24: the best for a quiet, treated room

**Price:** $249 in 2026, XLR

In a calm room, the Larch 24 makes a voice sound richer than any dynamic microphone on this list, and its self-noise of 9 dB-A means a whisper stays clean. It hears the room too, so it only belongs on this list if you have soft furnishing or acoustic panels around you. Our [condenser review from January](/blog/larch-24-review/) has the full figures.

:::proscons
+ Rich, detailed voice sound
+ Very low noise and easy to drive
- Picks up echo in a bare room
- Heavy; needs a strong boom arm
:::

## Rook 7: the best for two hosts at one table

**Price:** $199 in 2026, XLR

The Rook 7 is a broadcast dynamic microphone with a tight pickup pattern. With two hosts at one table, it picked up 4 dB less of the other host than the Wren D7 did, which makes editing much easier. It needs even more gain, 61 dB, and its weight of 760 g calls for a sturdy arm.

:::proscons
+ Best rejection of a second voice in our tests
+ Bass cut and presence switches on the back
- Needs 61 dB of gain
- Heavy at 760 g
:::

## Alder U1: the best USB podcast microphone

**Price:** $79 in 2026, USB and XLR

Of the best podcast microphones for a laptop, the Alder U1 is the simplest. It plugs straight into a laptop over USB and has a headphone socket with a volume knob, so you need nothing else to start. It also has an XLR output, so you can move to an interface later without buying a new microphone. Over USB the sound is a little thinner than the Wren D7, but at this price that is a fair trade.

:::proscons
+ USB and XLR outputs, so it grows with you
+ Headphone socket on the microphone
- Thinner sound over USB than over XLR
- The included desk stand passes on every desk tap
:::

## Fen C3: fine for calls, weak for podcasts

**Price:** $99 in 2026, USB

The Fen C3 is a small USB condenser that sounds clear in a quiet room. It scored 5.4 for room pickup, the worst on this list, and its gain is fixed, so a loud guest can clip it. It is a good upgrade for video calls. For a podcast, the Alder U1 does better for less, which is why the Fen C3 is the last of the best podcast microphones on this list.

## Which of the best podcast microphones should you buy?

Most people should buy the Wren D7, because it sounds clean in an ordinary room with bare walls and costs little. Choose the Larch 24 only if your room is treated and you speak softly, the Rook 7 if two hosts share a table, and the Alder U1 if you want a single cable to a laptop.

- **You record at home in an ordinary room:** the Wren D7.
- **You have a treated room and a soft voice:** the Larch 24.
- **Two hosts share a table:** two Rook 7s.
- **You want one cable to a laptop:** the Alder U1.

:::callout{kind=tip}
The room matters as much as the microphone. Before you spend more, put a rug on the floor and a curtain or a bookcase behind you. Our [room treatment measurements](/blog/acoustic-foam-tested/) show what that changes.
:::

::::faq
:::question{text="Is a dynamic or a condenser microphone better for podcasts?"}
For most home studios, a dynamic microphone. It picks up less of the room, so your voice sounds closer and cleaner without treatment. A condenser sounds richer, but only in a quiet room with soft surfaces.
:::
:::question{text="Do I need an audio interface for a podcast microphone?"}
You need one for an XLR microphone such as the Wren D7. A USB microphone such as the Alder U1 plugs straight into a computer. Our [first-interface pick](/blog/osprey-two-review/) covers a good place to start.
:::
