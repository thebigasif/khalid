The Heron R4 is the simplest way to record a podcast with four people in one room, and it scores 7.6 out of 10. It records each microphone to its own track on a memory card, its preamps stay clean at high gain, but its small screen and deep menus slow you down.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Score:** 7.6 out of 10
- **Type:** four-channel podcast mixer and multitrack recorder
- **Price:** $449 in 2026
- **Best for:** in-person shows with two to four hosts
- **Skip it if:** you mostly record remote guests over the internet
:::

## What the Heron R4 is

The Heron R4 is a desktop mixer with four microphone inputs, each with a fader, a gain knob and a mute button. It records to a memory card, either as one stereo mix or as a separate track for each microphone, so you can fix one person's level later. It also works as a USB audio interface for a computer. There are four headphone outputs, each with its own volume knob.

It runs on mains power or on a USB power bank, which makes it easy to take to a guest's home.

## Tested

We tested the Heron R4 with four [Wren D7 dynamic microphones](/blog/wren-d7-review/), which need a lot of gain and so expose noisy preamps. [How we measure preamp noise](/blog/gear-testing/) is set out on our methods page.

| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum gain | 65 dB | Enough for any dynamic microphone |
| Noise at 58 dB of gain (EIN) | -127 dBu | Clean; no hiss in pauses |
| Recording formats | 48 kHz, 24 or 32-bit float | 32-bit float makes clipping almost impossible |
| Battery life on a 10,000 mAh power bank | 6 h 40 min | A full day of recording |
| Headphone output into 80 ohm headphones | Loud enough at 70% | Fine for every pair we tried |
| Time from power on to recording | 14 s | Quick to start |
| Menu steps to switch on multitrack | 6 | Too many for a common setting |

## How the Heron R4 sounds

The preamps are the best part. At 58 dB of gain, the level a Wren D7 needs for a quiet speaker, the noise measured -127 dBu, which is close to the limit of what physics allows. In a pause, we heard the room and nothing else.

Recording in 32-bit float means a sudden laugh cannot clip the recording, even if the gain was set too high. You can lower it later in editing with no damage. For a show with four excited guests, that is a real safety net.

## Using it

The faders are smooth and long enough to ride a level during a recording, and the mute buttons are large and lit, so a host can cough without fumbling. Four headphone outputs, each with its own volume knob, mean nobody needs a separate headphone amplifier.

The menus are the problem. The screen is small and the setting most people want, a separate track for each microphone, sits six steps deep. Once set, it stays set, but we watched two first-time users give up and record a stereo mix by mistake.

:::callout{kind=warning}
The Heron R4 records one stereo mix by default. Switch on multitrack before your first show, or you will not be able to fix one person's level afterwards.
:::

:::proscons
+ Clean preamps with 65 dB of gain, enough for any dynamic microphone
+ Records each microphone to its own track, in 32-bit float
+ Four headphone outputs with separate volume knobs
+ Runs for a full day on a power bank
- Small screen and deep menus
- Records a stereo mix by default, which catches out beginners
- No easy way to bring in a remote guest over the internet
:::

## Who should buy the Heron R4

Buy it if your show has two to four people in one room and you want to record without a computer. It is excellent for taking to a guest's home. If most of your guests join over the internet, a [two-input USB interface](/blog/osprey-two-review/) and good recording software will serve you better for less.

## Verdict

:::review{item="Heron R4" type="Product" rating="7.6" best="10" author="Sol Ferreira"}
Excellent preamps, separate tracks and 32-bit float recording make the Heron R4 a safe way to record a room full of people. It loses points for a small screen, deep menus and a stereo default that catches beginners. Once set up, it is a dependable machine.
:::

For more on why 32-bit float recording resists clipping, see the article on [audio bit depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_bit_depth).
