I write every morning before I do anything else. I make a pot of tea, open the notebook at the next clean page and fill two or three sides with a fountain pen. Only later, in the evening, do I type any of it up. People ask why I keep handwritten drafts when typing is faster. That is exactly the reason.

## Why handwritten drafts are slower

When I type, my fingers run ahead of my head. I can put down a sentence before I know whether I believe it. A pen holds me back to the speed of one word at a time, and that is about the speed at which I can think clearly. By the time I reach the end of a line, I usually know what the next one should say.

The slowness also makes me choose. If a sentence is going to cost me ten seconds of ink, I want it to earn its place. Typed drafts tend to sprawl, so they need more cutting later. My handwritten drafts come out shorter and plainer.

## Nothing else on the page

A notebook does one thing. It has no messages, no news and no little red numbers. When I write on a screen, the rest of the world is one click away, and I click. On paper, the only way out is to put the pen down, and I rarely do.

## Crossings-out are useful

On a screen, a deleted sentence is gone. On paper it stays, with a line through it, and I can see what I changed my mind about. Some of my best paragraphs began as a crossed-out line that I came back to a week later. In other words, a page full of corrections is a record of the thinking, not just the result.

## What I use

Nothing special: a plain A5 notebook with numbered pages, a steel-nibbed pen that cost less than a paperback, and blue-black ink. I wrote about how I number and index the notebooks in [my notebook system](/blog/notebook-system/), and about the second notebook I keep for other people's sentences in [keeping a commonplace book](/blog/commonplace-book/).

## Typing them up

In the evening I type the morning's handwritten drafts into the computer. This is the first edit, because I leave out what no longer seems true and tighten what does. Most of the posts on this site started this way.

If you want to try it, write by hand for ten minutes a day for one week, about anything at all. The [Royal Literary Fund's writing resources](https://www.rlf.org.uk/resources/) have more ideas for getting started.
