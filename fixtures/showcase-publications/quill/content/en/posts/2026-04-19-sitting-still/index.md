In March I made myself a small promise. Every day for a month, I would spend ten minutes sitting still on the bench at the end of the harbour wall. No phone, no book, no notebook. Just the bench, the water and whatever weather there was.

It sounds easy. For the first week it was the hardest thing I did all day.

## The first week of sitting still

On the first morning I lasted four minutes before I reached for my phone to check the time. On the second I started writing a shopping list in my head. By the fourth day I had noticed that I could not sit without planning something, and that I had not noticed this before.

I kept going because I had told Kit I would, and he would ask.

## What helped

Three small things made sitting still easier:

1. **The same bench at the same time.** I went after breakfast, before writing, so I did not have to decide anything.
2. **A cheap watch.** I set its timer for ten minutes and left my phone in a drawer at home.
3. **Something to look at.** The harbour is never the same twice: the tide, the boats, the light on the water.

## The second half of the month

Somewhere in the third week, the ten minutes began to feel short. I started to notice small things: the order the boats swing round when the tide turns, the same cormorant on the same post each morning, the moment the wind drops before rain.

My writing changed too. The mornings after the bench were slower to start but went further. I think the ten minutes cleared a space that the day had usually filled before I sat down.

## Months later

The month ended weeks ago and I still go most days, with a small [walking bag](/blog/walking-bag-list/) and a flask. Some mornings I cannot settle and I leave early. That is fine, because the habit is the going, not sitting still perfectly. The rain in June tested it, as my [wet June notes](/blog/wet-june-notes/) tell.

If you try it, start with five minutes and pick a place you like to look at. The [NHS page on mindfulness](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/tips-and-support/mindfulness/) has simple ways to begin. I wrote about another quiet habit in [my notebook system](/blog/notebook-system/).
