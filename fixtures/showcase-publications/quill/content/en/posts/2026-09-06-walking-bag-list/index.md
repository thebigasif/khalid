I walk most mornings, usually for an hour along the sea wall and back. Over the years my walking bag has got lighter. Here is everything I carry now, and a few things I have left behind.

## Always in the walking bag

- **A small notebook and a pencil.** Pencil, not pen, because it works in rain and cold.
- **A flask of tea.** Even in summer. A hot drink at the turn is half the reason I go.
- **A light waterproof.** The weather on the estuary changes fast, and the wind is always stronger on the wall.
- **Binoculars.** Small ones, which fold into a pocket. Most of the birds are far out on the mud.
- **The tide times,** written on a card. I check them the night before, but I like having them.
- **A phone,** switched to silent, for emergencies and the occasional photograph.

## In winter, as well

- A hat and gloves, even when I think I will not need them.
- A head torch for the dark mornings in December and January.
- A spare pair of socks in a bag, after one too many walks with wet feet.

I wrote about one of those cold mornings in my [winter estuary walk](/blog/winter-estuary-walk/).

## What I no longer carry

- **A field guide.** I take a note and look the bird up at home.
- **A camera.** I spent more time looking at the screen than at the place.
- **Music.** The estuary is better heard than drowned out.

## Why a light walking bag

A light bag means I go every day, because there is nothing to get ready. It also leaves my hands free and my shoulders loose for the bench at the end, where I spend ten minutes [sitting still](/blog/sitting-still/) before I turn for home. In short, the less I carry, the more I notice.

## Packing it the night before

I pack the walking bag in the evening and hang it on the back door. In the morning I only fill the flask. It sounds like a small thing, but it removes the one excuse I used to have for staying in.
