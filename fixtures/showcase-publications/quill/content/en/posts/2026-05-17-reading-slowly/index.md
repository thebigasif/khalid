For years I raced through books. I finished one a week and could not have told you much about most of them a month later. Working in a library changed that, and reading slowly is now how I keep far more of each book.

Here are the five habits that reading slowly comes down to for me.

## Read at the same time each day

Every evening I give a book half an hour after supper, in the same chair. A fixed time means I do not have to find time. It is already there, and the book is already on the arm of the chair.

## Stop at the end of a chapter

Fast readers push on. I stop at the end of a chapter and sit for a minute with what just happened. Often I flick back a few pages. It is surprising how much you missed the first time.

## Keep a pencil in the book

I mark lines I like with a small tick in the margin, in pencil, only in my own books. In library books I use a slip of paper and copy the line out later. Choosing a line to keep makes me read the page properly. Nell copies hers into a [commonplace book](/blog/commonplace-book/).

## Read some of it aloud

A paragraph read aloud takes three times as long and shows you how it is built. The first page of every new book gets read aloud, and so does any passage I do not understand. My sister keeps [handwritten drafts](/blog/handwritten-drafts/) for much the same reason, since a pen slows the words down.

## Let a book take as long as it takes

The Peregrine took nine days in February, which is a long time for a short book. It needed that. If a book is hard, the answer is usually to read less of it at a time, not to give up.

## What reading slowly changes

After a year of it, I get through about forty books instead of sixty, and I could tell you something true about nearly all of them. You can see what I read in my [February reading log](/blog/february-reading-log/), and where most of it came from in my piece on the [town library](/blog/town-library-mill-street/).
