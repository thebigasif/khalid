February is a short month, but a good one for books. The evenings are still long and dark, and the library is quiet after the January rush. Here is my reading log for the month, with how long each book took and a short verdict.

## The reading log

| Book | Kind | Pages | Days | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Salt Path | Memoir | 288 | 5 | Honest and moving |
| The Peregrine | Nature | 192 | 9 | Read it slowly |
| A Month in the Country | Novel | 112 | 2 | Perfect for a weekend |
| Stoner | Novel | 288 | 6 | Quiet and very sad |
| The Living Mountain | Nature | 112 | 3 | Read it before a hill walk |
| Bird by Bird | Writing | 256 | 7 | Kind advice for writers |

## What stood out

The two short books were the best surprise. A short book read in one or two sittings stays whole in your head in a way a long one rarely does. For that reason, I want to read more of them this year.

The Peregrine was the hardest. It is a book of descriptions of one bird over one winter, and the sentences are thick with detail. I read about ten pages a night and stopped when I stopped taking them in. That pace turned out to be right for it. I wrote more about [taking a hard book slowly](/blog/reading-slowly/) in May.

## Where the books came from

Five of the six came from the [town library on Mill Street](/blog/town-library-mill-street/), where I work. The sixth was a present. Borrowing is the reason I read as much as I do, because a library book has a return date, and a date is a good reason to start.

## Why keep a reading log

I keep the log in the back of a notebook: the title, the dates I started and finished, and one line on what I thought. It takes a minute a book. A year later, the reading log tells me more about that year than my diary does. My sister Nell, who spent much of a [wet June](/blog/wet-june-notes/) reading letters, keeps one too.

## What next

In March I am reading for the Thursday reading group, which has picked a long novel. I will report back on how far we get. If you want ideas of your own, the [Reading Agency's book lists](https://readingagency.org.uk/) are a good place to start.
