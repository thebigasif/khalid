It has rained for twelve days out of the last fourteen. Not storms, just steady grey rain that starts before breakfast and is still going at bedtime. Here are a few notes from a wet June.

## The writing went well in a wet June

Rain is good for work. With no reason to go out, I sat at the table by the window most mornings and wrote more than in any fortnight this year. Two essays are nearly done and a third has started. I think the grey light helps, because nothing outside asks to be looked at.

## The walks were short

I still went out on the sea wall every day, but only as far as the first bench and back. I sat there for my ten minutes all the same, a habit I wrote about in [sitting still](/blog/sitting-still/). The estuary in rain is all one colour, grey water under grey sky with the mud somewhere in between. The swifts did not care. They flew low over the river all fortnight, catching whatever the rain brought down.

It was a long way from the cold, clear mornings of my [winter estuary walk](/blog/winter-estuary-walk/), and yet the curlews were still calling.

## What I read

Kit lent me a book of letters, which suits rainy days, because you can read one and stop. I read a letter or two each afternoon with a cup of tea. He has written about the pleasure of this in [five habits for slower reading](/blog/reading-slowly/).

## Small things I noticed

- The gutter over the back door has a leak that plays one note, the same one, all day.
- The harbour fills with small boats on the first dry hour, as if everyone was waiting.
- My notebook pages curl in the damp, so I now keep them in a box with a bag of rice.
- The library was busier than I have ever seen it, which pleased Kit.

## What a wet June is good for

A wet June is good for finishing things. With the garden too wet to work in and the wall too windy to linger on, I had no reason not to sit down and write. I suspect I will look back on these two weeks as the most useful of the summer.

## The forecast

The forecast says the rain will stop on Thursday. I find I am a little sorry. If you are stuck indoors too, the [Met Office forecast](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/weather/forecast) will at least tell you for how long.
