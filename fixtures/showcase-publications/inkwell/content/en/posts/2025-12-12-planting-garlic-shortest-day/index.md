Every grower in Penhallow knows the saying: plant garlic on the shortest day and lift it on the longest. Like most sayings it is only half right. This issue is about planting garlic, and also about a change at the library: this winter we lend cloves as well as seed.

## Planting garlic in the cold

Garlic needs a cold spell to split into cloves. Planted in spring, it often makes one round bulb instead. So planting garlic any time from October to the shortest day works well here. Push each clove into the soil, pointed end up, with its tip just below the surface, fifteen centimetres apart. Then leave it alone until the green shoots show in February.

## Growing: choosing the best cloves to plant

Garlic does not grow from seed in a garden. Instead, you plant a clove, and it grows into a bulb of the same kind. So save your biggest bulbs to plant, and eat the small ones. Plant only the fat outer cloves, because the thin inner ones make thin bulbs. That way your garlic gets a little better each year. [Wikipedia's garlic page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic) has more on its long history.

## Planting garlic from the tin: Harbour Red

Harbour Red is a hardneck garlic with purple streaks on its skin and a strong taste. It came from a fisherman's wife who grew it in the yard behind the harbour wall for forty years. Hardneck kinds send up a curly flower stalk in June. Cut it off, since the bulb then grows bigger, and fry the stalk in butter.
