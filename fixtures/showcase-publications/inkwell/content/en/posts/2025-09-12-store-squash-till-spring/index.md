This was the best summer for squash in ten years. Long, warm days in August ripened fruit that would usually still be green in October. As a result, sheds all over Penhallow are full, and the question at the gate is the same every day: how do I store squash so it lasts?

## How to store squash till spring

Cure them first. Leave each one in the sun for ten days, or in a warm room, until the skin is hard and a fingernail will not mark it. Keep a short piece of stalk on, because a squash without its stalk rots from the top. Then store squash on a shelf, not touching, somewhere cool and dry. A spare bedroom is better than a damp shed.

Check them every few weeks, and eat any with a soft spot first. A well cured butternut keeps until March, while a thin-skinned courgette type lasts only a month or two.

## Growing: saving seed without crosses

Squash cross easily, since bees carry pollen a long way. To save seed that comes true, Tomasz tapes a female flower shut the evening before it opens. Next morning he pollinates it by hand with a male flower from the same kind, and then tapes it shut again. Finally he ties a label to the stalk so he knows which fruit to keep. [Wikipedia explains hand pollination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-pollination) step by step.

## From the tin: Chapel Butternut

Chapel Butternut is a small butternut that trails only two metres, so it fits a small garden. Each plant gives four or five fruit the size of a bottle. It also keeps well, which is why it is the one to store squash with if you have room for only one kind.
