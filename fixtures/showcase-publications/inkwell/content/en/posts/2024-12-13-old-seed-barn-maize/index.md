Last spring a farmer near Trevose took down a barn roof and found a bundle of maize cobs tied to a beam. His father had hung them there to dry in the early 1980s and never took them down. He brought them to the swap in a feed sack, so this issue is the story of that old seed.

## Will old seed still grow?

Most seed loses its life slowly. Beans and maize kept dry and cool can stay alive for ten years or more, but each year fewer seeds wake up. Forty years is a long time, so we did not expect much. Of two hundred kernels, nine grew. Nine plants, however, were enough to start again.

## Growing: testing old seed on a damp towel

Before you sow old seed, test it first. Roll ten seeds in a damp kitchen towel, put the roll in a plastic bag and keep it somewhere warm. Then check it after a week. If seven sprout, sow as normal. If only two sprout, sow thickly and keep every plant you get, because each one carries part of the variety. [Wikipedia explains germination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germination) in more detail.

## From the tin: Trevose Flint

The nine plants grew tall and gave us small cobs in red, yellow and almost black. Flint maize has a hard skin, so it is ground for meal rather than eaten from the cob. We now have three jars of Trevose Flint, and it goes out to growers for the first time this spring. In return, we ask each grower to keep at least ten plants together, since maize needs a crowd to pollinate well.
