For twenty years the library had nowhere warm to grow. We started tender seed on windowsills all over the village and hoped for the best. This winter, however, twelve volunteers built a greenhouse behind the old chapel from forty reclaimed windows, and this issue is its story.

## A greenhouse from a school's old windows

The windows came from a school that was being refitted. Each was a different size, so the building is a patchwork, wider at one end than the other. The frame is larch from a local mill. It took six Saturdays to build, and finally the last pane went in on the first day of February.

Glass lets us grow peppers, tomatoes and melons to ripe seed, which our summers do not always allow. It also gives the library a warm place to test old seed in winter. [Wikipedia's page on greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse) explains how they hold heat.

## Growing: hardening off greenhouse seedlings

Plants raised under glass are soft. Before they go outside, harden them off: put them out for an hour on a mild day, then a morning, then a whole day, over two weeks. Bring them in if frost is forecast, since one cold night can undo a month of care. Tomasz also uses an old cold frame as a halfway house.

## From the tin: Mabe Pepper

Mabe Pepper is a small, mild chilli that ripens to red even under cool glass. A grower in Mabe kept it for thirty years and gave it to us in a film canister. Until now it struggled on our windowsills, but at last it has a warm home.
