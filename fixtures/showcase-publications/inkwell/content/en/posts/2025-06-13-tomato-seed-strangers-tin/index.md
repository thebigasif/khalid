Last autumn someone left a small tobacco tin on the chapel step. Inside was a twist of paper with about forty grains of tomato seed and one word: grow. We never found out who left it. So this spring we sowed it, and every plant came true.

## Growing a tomato seed with no name

Seed with no name is a puzzle. It might be an old variety someone has kept for years. On the other hand, it might be a cross from a shop tomato, which would not come true. The only way to find out is to grow it, so six growers took four seeds each.

All six had the same plant: tall, with small, sweet, deep red fruit in long trusses. That means the seed was saved with care, from one kind, for years. Someone loved this tomato, and now we do too.

## Growing: saving tomato seed by fermenting it

Tomasz saves his in a jar. First, squeeze the seeds and jelly of a ripe fruit into a jar with a little water. Then leave it for three days until a skin forms on top. Finally, rinse the seeds in a sieve and dry them on a plate. The days in the jar clean off the jelly, because that jelly stops seed from sprouting. [Wikipedia's tomato page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato) has more on how the plant grows.

## From the tin: Doorstep

We have named it Doorstep. It is a cherry tomato that ripens early, even in a grey Cornish summer, and it splits less than most. It also crops well in a pot by the back door, which suits its name. If you know who left the tin, please write to us.
