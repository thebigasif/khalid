# The Seed Tin: art direction

The Seed Tin is the made-up quarterly letter of a seed library in a Cornish harbour village. Each issue's lead picture is the seed or the crop the issue is about. The pictures should feel close, plain and seasonal, like a page from a grower's notebook.

## Tone

- **Seeds and crops, not people.** Beans, pods, squash, garlic, a greenhouse bench. No face is the subject of a picture. Author pictures are drawn marks.
- **Close and true.** Most pictures are close-ups that fill the frame with one thing, in daylight, with no heavy filters.
- **No text in a picture.** The issue's number and title are set in type beside it, so no text sits on a picture.

## Palette

Earthy greens, the creams and browns of dried seed, with one warm note each season:

- the greens of pods, leaves and a greenhouse in spring and summer;
- the creams, golds and browns of dried beans, maize and garlic;
- the deep purple of runner beans and the orange of squash, which echo the sample brand's beetroot red.

## Subject mix

Of the 11 photographs, 8 are close-ups of seed or crops and 3 are wider scenes (a greenhouse, a vine, a shelf of squash).

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latest issue on the front page | 4:3 | 1600 px wide | Cropped to the middle. Every close-up is an even texture, so it crops well. |
| Lead picture on an issue | as shot, about 3:2, 4:3 or 1:1 | 1600 px wide | Shown at the text width, about 36rem. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. |

The archive rows have no pictures, so the front page loads one photograph only.

## Phone crop

On a phone the latest issue's picture fills the width under its words, at 4:3. Lead pictures fill the width.

## Sources and licences

Every photograph comes from Wikimedia Commons through `scripts/demo-photo.py`, under CC0 or in the public domain, so none needs a visible credit. `photos.lock` records each one, and `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the photo credits page list them.
