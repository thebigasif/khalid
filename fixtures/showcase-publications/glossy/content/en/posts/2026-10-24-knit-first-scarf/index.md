Your first scarf can be your very first knitting project. It needs only one stitch, it is straight, and nobody minds if the edges wobble. By the end you will knit without looking.

## What you need for a first scarf

- **Yarn:** about 200 g of a thick wool or wool blend, labelled chunky or aran. A smooth, light colour shows your stitches best.
- **Needles:** one pair of 6 mm or 8 mm needles, as the yarn label suggests. Wooden needles are less slippery for beginners.
- **Scissors and a large blunt needle** for sewing in the ends.

## Cast on

Make a slip knot and put it on one needle. Use the long-tail cast on to add stitches until you have 25 to 30. A scarf this wide is about 20 cm across in chunky yarn.

## The knit stitch

1. Hold the needle with the stitches in your left hand.
2. Push the right needle into the first stitch, from front to back.
3. Wrap the yarn around the right needle.
4. Pull the new loop through and slide the old stitch off.

Knit every stitch of every row. This makes [garter stitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garter_stitch), a soft, ridged fabric that lies flat and looks the same on both sides.

## Joining a new ball

When a ball runs out, start the next at the beginning of a row. Leave tails of 15 cm from both and knit on. Sew the tails in at the end.

## Cast off

When the scarf is as long as you are tall, knit two stitches, then lift the first over the second. Knit one more and repeat to the end. Cut the yarn and pull it through the last loop.

## How long it takes

A chunky scarf takes about ten evenings of an hour each. It is the perfect project for [screen-free evenings](/blog/screen-free-evening/). Wear it on cold mornings, or on [cold lake swims](/blog/cold-lake-swims/) when you climb out of the water.
