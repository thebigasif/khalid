Easy houseplants are the ones that wait for you. They do not sulk when you go away for a long weekend or forget the watering can for a week. These five have lived through our busiest months.

## What makes easy houseplants easy

A forgiving plant stores water in thick leaves or roots, copes with less light than a sunny sill, and grows slowly, so it rarely needs a bigger pot. Most problems with houseplants come from too much water, not too little.

## The five

1. **Spider plant.** It grows fast, makes baby plants on long stems and tells you when it is thirsty by going pale. Water when the top of the soil is dry.
2. **Snake plant.** Stiff upright leaves that store water. It is happy in a dim corner and needs a drink only every two or three weeks.
3. **ZZ plant.** Glossy leaves on fat stems, which grow from a root like a potato. It forgives almost anything except standing in water.
4. **Pothos.** A trailing plant for the top of a shelf. Its leaves droop when it is dry and lift again within hours of watering.
5. **Cast iron plant.** The name says it all. It grows slowly in deep shade and is the last plant standing in a cold hallway.

The [spider plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophytum_comosum) is also one of the easiest to share. Pot up one of its babies and give it to a friend.

## Simple care for busy people

- **Water by the finger.** Push a finger into the soil. If it is dry to the second knuckle, water well, then let it drain.
- **Feed in summer only.** A weak plant food once a month from May to August is plenty.
- **Wipe the leaves.** Dust blocks light. A damp cloth every few weeks keeps them glossy.

## Where to put them

Easy houseplants still need some light. Most of ours live on the open shelf from our story about [a wall the colour of afternoon light](/blog/warm-paint-colours/), a few steps from the window. If you are going away, group them together in a bright room. They keep each other damp.
