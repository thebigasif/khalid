A harbour town weekend is best taken slowly. The town wakes with the boats, eats early and goes quiet when the light goes. If you match its rhythm, two days feel like a week.

## Arrive on Friday evening

Take the train if you can. Most harbour towns are small, and a car spends the weekend in a car park. Hire a bike instead; our notes on [getting around by bike](/blog/town-cycling/) help. Arrive before dark, drop your bag and walk straight to the water. Watch the boats come in and find somewhere for supper that the fishing crews use.

## Saturday: the harbour town weekend starts early

Be on the quay at eight. This is the town's busiest hour: crates of fish, boats being cleaned, and the first coffee at the kiosk by the slipway. Then walk the coast path out of town for an hour, and back again for lunch.

- **Morning:** the quay, the fish market, a coffee by the water.
- **Midday:** a walk on the coast path, with a picnic if the weather is kind.
- **Afternoon:** a swim from the harbour steps, or a nap.
- **Evening:** an early supper, then the walk along the breakwater at sunset.

The breakwater, or [jetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakwater_(structure)), is the best seat in town. It is free, it faces the sunset, and the town lights come on behind you.

## Sunday: one long lunch

Spend Sunday morning in the town's back streets, away from the water. Look for a bakery with a queue. Then book the longest lunch you can, somewhere with a view of the boats, and take the late train home.

## What to pack

One small bag is enough. Our [packing light](/blog/packing-light/) list works for a weekend too: drop half the clothes and keep the swimming things. Bring a hat for the quay; our story on [summer hat care](/blog/straw-hat-care/) explains how to keep a straw one in shape.

## Why slow is better

A short trip tempts you to see everything. In a harbour town there is not much to see, and that is the point. Sit, watch the water and let the tide set the pace.
