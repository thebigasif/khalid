Warm paint colours can make a small room feel lit even on a grey day. We learned this in a rented flat with one window and a long wall that never saw the sun until four in the afternoon.

## Why warm paint colours work in small rooms

A warm colour bounces a yellow light back into the room. Cool greys and blues look calm on a card, but in a dim room they often turn flat and cold. A soft ochre, a clay pink or a honey white holds the glow of a lamp and makes the walls feel closer in a good way.

Light also changes through the day. A north window gives a steady, bluish light. A west window gives a cool morning and a hot gold evening. Look at your room at nine, at one and at five before you choose.

## Test before you buy

1. **Paint large patches.** Use a sample pot and paint a square the size of a pillow, or paint a sheet of card so you can move it.
2. **Look low and high.** Colour looks darker near the floor and lighter near the ceiling.
3. **Live with it for three days.** Your first favourite is rarely the one you still like on day three.

The [colour wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_wheel) helps when you add a second colour. A warm wall sits well with its opposite in small doses, such as a blue vase or a green plant.

## The shelf that made it work

Our yellow went on the wall behind an open shelf. The shelf breaks up the colour, so it never feels like too much. We filled it with plants from our list of [houseplants that forgive a busy week](/blog/easy-houseplants/), a few books and one lamp.

## Paint and the rest of the room

- Keep the ceiling a plain warm white, so the room does not feel low.
- Pick a matt or eggshell finish. Gloss shows every bump in an old wall.
- Choose bulbs marked warm white, about 2700 kelvin, for the evening.

If you rent, ask your landlord first. Many say yes to a soft colour if you offer to paint it back. For more small changes that make a home feel calmer, read our notes on [a screen-free evening](/blog/screen-free-evening/).
