Town cycling is often the quickest way to get anywhere under five kilometres. It is also cheap, quiet and good for your head. You do not need a fast bike or special clothes to start.

## A simple bike for town cycling

- **An upright frame** lets you sit tall and see over the cars.
- **A basket or a rack** carries the shopping, so your back stays dry.
- **Mudguards and a chain guard** keep your clothes clean.
- **Three to seven gears** are enough for most towns.

A second-hand town bike is a good first bike. Ask a local repair shop to check the brakes and tyres before you ride it.

## Planning your routes

Your quickest car route is rarely your best bike route. Look for quiet streets, parks and paths, even if they add a few minutes. Ride them first on a Sunday morning, when the roads are calm.

1. Plan the route on a map with cycle lanes marked.
2. Ride it once with no hurry.
3. Note where you need to stop, turn or cross.

Many towns publish a map of [cycle lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_lane). Some lanes are painted on the road and some are kept apart from traffic; the second kind is calmer for beginners.

## Locks and lights

Use a good D-lock through the frame and the back wheel, and lock to something fixed. Fit front and back lights and use them from dusk, and on grey days too.

## When it rains

A waterproof jacket, a cap under the helmet and mudguards are all you need. Wipe the chain with a dry rag when you get home.

## Make it part of your week

Start with one trip a week, such as the Saturday market. Then add the ride to work on dry days. On days you leave the bike at home, try our notes on [walking for your head](/blog/mindful-walking/). And if your rides take you out of town, [travelling with one bag](/blog/packing-light/) shows how little you need to carry.
