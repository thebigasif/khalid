Lake swimming in the mountains is cold, quiet and unforgettable. The water is clear, the air smells of pine and, for a few minutes, nothing else in your head matters. You do not need to be a strong swimmer, only a careful one.

## Finding a lake for lake swimming

Pick a lake where swimming is allowed and people swim often. Look for a gentle shore of stones or sand, not a steep drop. Check the local rules: some mountain lakes are drinking water or nature reserves, and swimming is not allowed.

- Swim near the shore, never across the lake.
- Never swim alone. Go with a friend who stays on the shore if they do not swim.
- Avoid dams, outflows and boat routes.

## Getting in slowly

Cold water takes your breath away. This is the [cold shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_shock_response), and it passes in about a minute. Walk in slowly, splash your wrists and neck, and breathe out long and slow before you put your shoulders under.

## How long to stay

In autumn the water may be ten degrees or less. A good rule for beginners is one minute in the water for each degree, and less when it is windy. Get out while you still feel good. The cold keeps working after you leave the water.

## Warming up

1. **Dry and dress fast,** top half first, with a hat on.
2. **Drink something warm** from a flask.
3. **Move gently,** such as a short walk, but do not run.
4. **No hot shower straight away.** Warm up slowly over half an hour.

A woollen scarf helps a lot. If you have time this winter, [knit your first scarf](/blog/knit-first-scarf/) for the purpose.

## What to bring

A swimming costume, a towel, a warm hat, wool socks, a flask and a bag for wet things. Our [packing list](/blog/packing-light/) has room for all of it. Make a weekend of it, and walk the shore path slowly afterwards, as in [walking for your head](/blog/mindful-walking/).
