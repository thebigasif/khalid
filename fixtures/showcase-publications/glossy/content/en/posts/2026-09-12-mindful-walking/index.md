Mindful walking is a walk with nowhere to be. It does not count steps or chase a pace. It is twenty minutes in which your only job is to notice where you are.

## Why mindful walking helps

Walking slowly and paying attention gives your thoughts room to settle. Many people find that a problem they carried out of the door looks smaller on the way back. Research on [walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking) links regular walks with better sleep and a calmer mood, and you do not need a park or a forest to begin.

## Five steps

1. **Leave the phone,** or put it on silent at the bottom of a bag.
2. **Slow down.** Walk at the pace you would use with a friend who is telling you a story.
3. **Feel your feet.** For the first minute, notice each step: heel, foot, toes.
4. **Notice three things.** One you can see, one you can hear and one you can smell.
5. **Come back gently.** When your mind wanders to your list, notice it and look up again.

## Where to walk

- A loop around your street, at the same time each day.
- A park at lunchtime, on the path furthest from the road.
- A wood in autumn, when the leaves are loud underfoot.

If you live in town, walking can also be how you get somewhere. Try leaving the bike at home once a week, or read our notes on [getting around by bike](/blog/town-cycling/) for the other days.

## Making it a habit

Tie the walk to something you already do: after breakfast, after the school run, or before supper. Keep it short enough that you never skip it. Twenty minutes is plenty.

On wet days, walk anyway, in a good coat. The rain is part of what you notice. When you are home again, a slow evening helps the calm last. We wrote about ours in [our evenings without screens](/blog/screen-free-evening/).
