Fluffy pancakes need only one good batter and a little patience. Make the batter on Saturday night, and Sunday breakfast is ten minutes away.

## The batter for fluffy pancakes

For eight pancakes:

- 200 g plain flour
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- a pinch of salt and 1 tablespoon sugar
- 2 eggs
- 250 ml milk
- 30 g melted butter

Whisk the dry things in one bowl and the wet things in another. Pour the wet into the dry and stir until just mixed. A few lumps are good. Rest the batter for at least twenty minutes, or overnight in the fridge. The rest is the secret: the flour drinks the milk and the [baking powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking_powder) starts to work.

## Cooking them

Heat a heavy pan on a medium heat and wipe it with butter. Pour in a small ladle of batter. Turn the pancake when bubbles pop on top and the edges look dry, after about two minutes. Cook the other side for one minute more. Keep them warm in a low oven under a tea towel.

## Three toppings

| Topping | What you need | Time | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer fruit | Chopped peach, apple and berries, a spoon of syrup | 5 minutes | A bright morning |
| Brown butter and lemon | 40 g butter cooked until nutty, lemon juice, sugar | 5 minutes | A slow, cold Sunday |
| Salted honey and yoghurt | Thick yoghurt, honey, a pinch of flaky salt | 2 minutes | Something not too sweet |

## Make it a slow Sunday

Pancakes are best eaten at the stove, one at a time, while someone else makes coffee. If you have time after breakfast, start the levain for [your first sourdough loaf](/blog/first-sourdough-loaf/). In autumn, swap the summer fruit for sliced persimmon, as in [our persimmon story](/blog/persimmon-recipes/).

Leftover batter keeps for one day in the fridge. Stir it before you use it, since the flour settles.
