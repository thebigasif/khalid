Straw hat care takes five minutes a season, and a good hat lasts for many summers. Most straw hats are ruined by being sat on, packed flat or left wet, not by age.

## Choosing a hat that lasts

- **Fit first.** Measure around your head just above the ears. A hat that is too tight will stretch and crack; one too loose blows away on the quay.
- **Look at the weave.** A tight, even weave lasts longer and keeps out more sun. Hold it up to the light: fewer pinholes is better.
- **Pick a band you can change.** A ribbon or a shell band, like the one in the picture, can be replaced when it fades.

Straw comes from many plants. A [Panama hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_hat) is woven from palm leaves, while cheaper hats use paper straw, which hates water.

## Straw hat care through the season

1. **Handle it by the brim,** not the crown. Fingers pinch and crack the crown over time.
2. **Brush it.** A soft clothes brush, moved with the weave, removes dust and sand.
3. **Spot clean.** Use a cloth barely damp with water and a drop of mild soap. Never soak a straw hat.
4. **Dry it right way up,** on a bowl or a rolled towel, away from the radiator.

## Reshaping a dented brim

Hold the dent over a steaming kettle for a few seconds, at a safe distance. Shape it with your fingers, then lay it flat under a book until dry. Go slowly; too much steam softens the weave too far.

## Storing it in winter

Stuff the crown with tissue and keep the hat in a box or on a high shelf, upside down on its crown so the brim does not bend. Keep it away from damp cellars.

## Travelling with a hat

Wear it, do not pack it. If you must pack it, fill the crown with socks and put it at the top of the bag. For more on travelling light, read [our one-bag packing list](/blog/packing-light/), or take it on [a slow weekend in a harbour town](/blog/harbour-town-weekend/).
