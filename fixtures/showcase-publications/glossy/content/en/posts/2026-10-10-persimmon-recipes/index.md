Persimmon recipes are a good reason to love late autumn. The fruit arrives when most others are gone, bright orange and sweet as honey when it is ripe.

## Choosing ripe persimmons

There are two main kinds. Squat, round fruit, often sold as Fuyu, can be eaten firm like an apple. Taller, acorn-shaped fruit, often sold as Hachiya, must be soft as jelly before you eat them, or they taste dry and bitter. The bitterness comes from tannins, which fade as the fruit ripens. The [persimmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persimmon) page explains the many kinds in detail.

Buy firm fruit and ripen it in a bowl with a banana. It speeds things up.

## Four persimmon recipes

1. **Autumn salad.** Slice two firm persimmons thinly. Toss with rocket, toasted walnuts and a little goat's cheese. Dress with olive oil and lemon.
2. **Breakfast bowl.** Spoon thick yoghurt into a bowl, add chopped persimmon, a little honey and oats toasted in a dry pan.
3. **Roasted slices.** Cut firm fruit into wedges, toss with a little butter and cinnamon and roast at 200 °C for 15 minutes. Serve with ice cream or on [Sunday pancakes](/blog/fluffy-pancakes/).
4. **Quick jam.** Scoop the flesh of four soft fruit into a pan with 100 g sugar and the juice of a lemon. Simmer for 20 minutes, stirring, until thick. Keep it in the fridge for two weeks.

## Storing them

Keep firm fruit at room temperature and soft fruit in the fridge. Soft persimmon flesh freezes well: scoop it into a box and use it in smoothies or cakes all winter.

## A small autumn ritual

We buy a bag every Saturday in October and eat them slowly through the week, one a day after lunch. They taste best with a slice of fresh bread from [your first sourdough loaf](/blog/first-sourdough-loaf/), a little butter and a cup of tea.
