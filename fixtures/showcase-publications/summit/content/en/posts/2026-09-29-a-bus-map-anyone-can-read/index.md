Most people who do not take the bus say the same thing: they do not know which bus to take. In this workshop, transport designer Grace Mensah shows how to draw a bus map that answers that question in under a minute, and then you draw one for Easthaven.

## What makes a bus map hard to read

Grace has tested bus maps with hundreds of people, from nine-year-olds to visitors who read no English. The same problems come up again and again:

- **Every route drawn the same.** A bus every ten minutes and a bus twice a day look alike on the map.
- **Too many numbers.** A busy street with seven routes becomes a tangle of seven lines.
- **No sense of time.** The map shows where a bus goes, but not how long it takes.

## How the workshop runs

The workshop is three hours long, with a break. You work in groups of four around large printed maps of the town. Grace brings pens, tracing paper and the real timetable data for every route.

| Part | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| The test | 14:00 | Find three journeys on today's map, against the clock |
| Frequent routes | 14:20 | Mark every route with a bus at least every 15 minutes |
| The redraw | 14:50 | Redraw the map with frequent routes bold and the rest thin |
| Break | 15:40 | Tea in the library foyer |
| Time rings | 16:00 | Add rings that show 10, 20 and 30 minutes from the bus station |
| The test again | 16:30 | Swap maps with another group and find the same journeys |
| Wrap-up | 16:50 | Each group shows one change to keep |

## What happens to the maps

Easthaven's bus company has promised to look at every map the workshop draws. Two ideas from last year's summit, bold lines for frequent routes and a stop name on every corner, are now on the real map at the bus station.

The reading room seats 40, and the workshop needs a ticket for Thursday. No drawing skills are needed. You can read more about [transit maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_map) before you come.
