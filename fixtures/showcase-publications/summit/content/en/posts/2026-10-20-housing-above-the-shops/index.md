Walk down almost any high street and look up. Above the shop fronts, many windows are dark. Dev Raman has spent three years counting those empty floors, and his talk makes the case for housing above the shops as the quickest way to add homes to a town centre.

## How much room is there for housing above the shops?

In Easthaven, Dev and his students surveyed every building on the four main shopping streets. Of 312 buildings, 141 had at least one empty floor above the shop. Together, those floors have room for about 400 small homes, without building anything new.

Easthaven is not unusual. Dev has run the same survey in five other towns, and the share of empty upper floors ranged from a third to more than half.

## Why the floors stay empty

The talk walks through the four reasons he hears most often from owners:

- **No separate door.** The only way up is through the shop, so the floor above cannot be let on its own.
- **Fire rules.** A home above a shop needs a safe way out, which the old stairs often do not give.
- **Split ownership.** The shop and the floors above belong to different people, who cannot agree on the cost.
- **Small sums.** For a large landlord, a few flats are not worth the trouble.

## What would unlock them

Each reason has a fix that a council can help with. A shared back stair can serve three buildings in a row. A small grant can pay for a separate street door. A council can buy the upper floors of a whole block and let them as homes.

Dev will show three schemes where this worked, and one where it did not. People living above the shops also bring life to a street after the shops close, which brings the talk back to Ines Albarran's [streets for staying](/blog/streets-are-for-staying/). The [shophouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shophouse), a shop with a home above it, is a very old form of the same idea.
