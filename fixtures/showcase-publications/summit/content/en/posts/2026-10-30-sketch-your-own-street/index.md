Bring a photo of the street where you live, and leave with a plan to make it better. In this two-hour workshop, landscape architect Hanna Lund shows you how to sketch your own street the way designers do, using tracing paper over a photograph, with no drawing skills needed.

## How to sketch your own street

Hanna starts with a short demonstration on one Easthaven street. Then everyone works on their own photo, with Hanna and two of her colleagues moving between tables.

The method has four steps:

1. **Trace what is there.** Draw the kerbs, the doors, the parked cars and the trees, in pencil, over the photo.
2. **Mark where people are.** Where do people stand, sit, wait or hurry? Use a red pen.
3. **Find the spare space.** Look for space that nobody uses: a wide corner, a parking bay, a blank wall.
4. **Draw one change.** One bench, one tree, one wider crossing. Small changes are more likely to happen.

## What you need to bring

Bring one printed photo of your street, taken from the pavement at eye level, at least A4 size. If you forget, Hanna has photos of twenty Easthaven streets to choose from. Pens, paper and tracing paper are on every table.

## What happens to the sketches

At the end, every sketch goes up on the studio wall for the rest of the day. Easthaven council's street team will photograph them all. Last year, two sketches from this workshop led to real changes: a bench outside the post office on Mill Road and a planted corner on Rope Street.

If you want to take your plan further, the council's [street tree](/blog/ten-thousand-street-trees/) team takes requests for new trees from residents. The idea of a [parklet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parklet), a parking space turned into a small public space, is a good one to sketch.

Studio 1 holds 40 at eight tables. The workshop is suitable for anyone over twelve, and children under sixteen come with an adult. It starts after Hanna's own talk at Garden Court and ends in time for lunch before the [closing keynote](/blog/the-fifteen-minute-question/).
