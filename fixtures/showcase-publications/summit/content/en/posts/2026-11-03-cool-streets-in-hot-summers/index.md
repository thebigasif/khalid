On the hottest day of summer 2025, the air on Easthaven's Quay Street was six degrees cooler than on Market Square, four hundred metres away. Landscape architect Hanna Lund wanted to know why. Her talk explains what makes cool streets, with measurements from forty streets across three towns.

## What keeps cool streets cool

Hanna's team fixed small temperature sensors to lamp posts and logged the air every ten minutes for three summers. Then they compared the coolest streets with the hottest ones. Three things made the biggest difference:

- **Shade from trees.** A street under a full canopy was up to seven degrees cooler at head height than an open street. Shade from buildings helped too, but only for part of the day.
- **Pale, open ground.** Dark tarmac stores heat and gives it back all night. Pale paving and planted ground stay cooler.
- **Water you can touch.** A fountain or a shallow rill cools the air only a few metres around it, but people gather there, and it makes a hot day bearable.

## What costs least for the most cooling

Not every street can have big trees. Some have pipes and cables under the pavement, and some are too narrow. Hanna will show cheaper fixes ranked by how much cooling each gave per pound spent. Shade sails over a bus stop, a climbing plant on a south wall and a lighter coat on a playground surface all made a measurable difference.

She will also talk about the people who suffer most in heat: older people living alone, babies, and people who work outdoors. A cool street matters most where they live.
