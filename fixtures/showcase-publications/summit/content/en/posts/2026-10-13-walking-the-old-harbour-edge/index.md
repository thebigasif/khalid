Friday starts outside, with a walk along the old harbour edge. Landscape architect Hanna Lund leads a ninety-minute walk along Easthaven's quays, from the North Quay to the old customs house, and stops at six places to look at what makes a waterfront good to be on.

## The route along the old harbour edge

The walk is about two kilometres on flat paving. It is step-free, but parts of the quay are cobbled.

1. **The pavilion steps,** where the walk starts. Why the town turned its back on the harbour for a century.
2. **The fish quay,** still a working quay. How to share a waterfront with work that is noisy and smelly.
3. **The swing bridge,** where the old rail line crossed. What happens to a town when a crossing closes.
4. **The new flats on Salt Row.** A waterfront you can see but cannot reach.
5. **The slipway,** where children paddle at low tide. The cheapest public space in town.
6. **Garden Court,** behind the customs house, where the walk ends in time for coffee.

## What to look for

Hanna asks walkers to notice three things at each stop: where people sit, where they stop to look at the water, and where they hurry past. By the end of the walk, the pattern is clear. People stay where they can sit with their back to something and the water in front of them.

She will also talk about tides and flooding. The quay has flooded twice in ten years, and any change to the harbour edge has to plan for higher water. A short history of the [harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor) as a type of place is a good read before you come.

## What to bring

Bring a coat and shoes for wet stone. The walk goes ahead in light rain. In a storm warning it moves into the pavilion as an illustrated talk.

Places are limited to 30, so book with your Friday ticket.
