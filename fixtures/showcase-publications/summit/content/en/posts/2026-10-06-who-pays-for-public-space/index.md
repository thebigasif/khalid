Everyone likes a good square. Fewer people agree on who should pay for public space, or who gets to decide what happens in it. Thursday's panel puts three speakers with different answers on one stage: Dev Raman, Ines Albarran and Grace Mensah. Ruth Okoye chairs.

## Three views on paying for public space

Each speaker opens with five minutes on their own view.

- **Dev Raman** argues that new homes above shops should pay towards the street outside, because the street is what makes the homes worth living in.
- **Ines Albarran** argues that councils should pay from the money they save on roads, since a street for people costs less to keep than a street for traffic.
- **Grace Mensah** argues that bus users already pay for much of the street through their fares, and should get a bigger share of it.

Then the panel takes questions from the hall for forty minutes. Ruth will take as many questions as time allows, and every question gets one answer from each speaker, not three speeches.

## Who gets a say

The second half turns from money to voice. A square paid for by a shopping centre may come with rules about who can sit there and what they can do. A square paid for by the council belongs to everyone, but may be the first thing cut when money is short.

The panel looks at three real arrangements: a business improvement district, a community land trust and a square run by a residents' group. Each has given a town a better public space. Each has also left someone out. The idea of [privately owned public space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_owned_public_space) comes up often, so it is worth a look beforehand.
