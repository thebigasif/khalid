# Harbor Kitchen Notes: art direction

Harbor Kitchen Notes is the blog of a made-up meal-kit company. Its photographs should look like the inside of a busy home kitchen and a well-run packing room on a bright day: real food, real tools, nothing styled to look expensive.

## Tone

- **Daylight, not studio light.** Natural or soft window light, with the colour of the food true and warm. No hard flash, no dark moody food shots and no low-light blur.
- **Food first.** The produce or the dish fills the frame. Hands may appear when they show a skill, such as gripping a knife on a sharpening stone, but no face is the subject of a picture.
- **Honest and a little used.** Worn chopping boards, dented pans and wooden spoons dark with use. No props that a home cook would not own.
- **Nothing to read.** No brand logos, labels, watermarks or text in the picture. Candidates with a brand on a knife blade or a meal-kit bag in the corner were turned down for this reason.

## Palette

Warm, saturated produce colours on neutral grounds:

- the reds and oranges of tomatoes, carrots, squash and apples;
- fresh greens of herbs, lettuce, asparagus and pears;
- neutral grounds of wood, white boards, card, steel and grey stone.

These sit well beside the template's deep harbour blue and the Harbor sample brand's warm orange, because the photographs bring their own warmth and leave the page colour to the template. Avoid photographs dominated by purple, black or cold blue grounds.

## Subject mix

Of the 33 photographs, 18 are close details and 15 are wider scenes, so more than half are close details (the brief asks for at least a third).

- **Close details (18):** diced onion, chopped herbs, tomatoes, tomatoes on the vine, apples, pears, garlic, onions, beetroot and turnips, parsley, a lettuce at soil level, carrots, stock vegetables, a whetstone, wooden spoons, roast carrots, roast squash, cherry tomatoes in a crate.
- **Wider scenes (15):** a tray of roast vegetables, an open produce box, a packed box, a kitchen board of produce, a bowl of rice, a pan on the hob, a plate of pasta, a kitchen station, autumn pumpkins on a table, meal-prep boxes, lunch boxes, a bowl of soup, a lettuce field, a skillet of asparagus, a knife on a board in a working kitchen.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post and cards | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Every lead was chosen in landscape at 3:2 or 4:3, so a 3:2 crop loses little. The template crops cards to its own ratio from the same file. |
| In-body figure | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width, about 42rem, with a caption. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

The source files are not all 3:2. Of the 18 leads, 5 are 3:2, 10 are about 4:3, one (the beetroot) is 5:3, one (the parsley crate) is 2:1 and one (the pasta tray) is nearly square. A 4:3 source loses about a tenth at the top and bottom in a 3:2 crop, which none of these subjects needs. The parsley crate fills its frame edge to edge, so a crop from the sides costs nothing. The pasta tray loses about an eighth at the top and bottom and the rim of the bowl touches the edge; it is the one lead to watch in the picture review.

## Phone crop

On a phone, the lead picture fills the width and is cropped from the centre. Each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Single subjects (tomatoes, pears, apples, beetroot, the whetstone, the skillet) sit in the middle third and survive a tight crop.
- Repeating textures (roast vegetables, apples, onions, parsley, the lettuce field) look the same at any crop.
- Scenes with a subject off centre were turned down for lead slots or used in the body, where they show at full width. The packing box lead keeps the tomato tray and the herb box both inside the middle 70 percent.

No photograph carries text, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets text over a lead picture, the darker leads (the whetstone, the herb crate, the skillet) need a scrim to pass AA.
