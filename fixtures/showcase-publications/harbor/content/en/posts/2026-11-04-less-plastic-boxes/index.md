This autumn we took most of the plastic out of our boxes by switching to paper bags for herbs and pantry items, packing sturdy produce loose, and replacing plastic ice packs with ones filled with water inside a paper case. A box that used to hold eleven pieces of plastic now holds two, and both can go in most household [plastic recycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_recycling) collections. We kept those two because, so far, nothing else keeps the food safe.

Our customers asked for this more than for anything else. Here is what we changed, what we kept, and how to recycle every part of a box today.

## What we counted before we started

In August, we unpacked a standard box for two and sorted every piece of packaging on a table. Eleven pieces were plastic:

- herb bags
- a bag for loose salad leaves
- small bags for spices and dried pantry items
- a tray and film for cherry tomatoes
- a bag for mushrooms
- the liner bag around the cold items
- the gel ice packs

Most of it was there out of habit, not need. A carrot does not need a bag. Neither does an onion.

## What we changed

| Item | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Herbs | Small plastic bags | Paper bags |
| Spices and dry pantry items | Small bags | Paper envelopes and bags |
| Cherry tomatoes | Plastic tray with film | Card punnet, no lid |
| Mushrooms | Plastic bag | Paper bag |
| Hardy vegetables | Mixed: some bagged | All loose |
| Ice packs | Gel packs | Water in a sealed paper case |
| Box liner | Bubble liner | Wool and paper liner |

The herb change came first, after a test in our own kitchen showed that [herbs keep longer in paper](/blog/herbs-paper-storage/). That result gave us the confidence to try the rest.

## The plastic we kept

Two pieces stayed as they were:

1. **The bag for loose salad leaves.** Washed leaves lose water fast and wilt in paper within a day. We tried four paper and card options and none kept leaves crisp to the end of the week. The bag we use now is thinner and made from recycled plastic.
2. **The pots for liquids.** Sauces, stock and dressings need a pot that cannot leak in a van. Most household recycling collections take them.

:::callout{kind=note}
We are still testing alternatives for both. When something works as well, we will switch, and we will write about it here.
:::

## How the change affected packing day

Paper bags and loose produce changed how we pack. Heavy, loose vegetables now go in first and form a flat base, with paper bags and soft produce on top. The line is slightly slower, by about thirty seconds a box, because paper bags need folding. You can see the order we pack in [what happens on packing day](/blog/packing-day/).

We also checked that nothing arrived worse. For six weeks we tracked every complaint about damaged or wilted produce. They stayed level, and complaints about herbs fell.

## What to do with each part of your box

- **The cardboard box:** flatten it and recycle it, or keep it for your next delivery and we collect it.
- **Paper bags and envelopes:** recycle them with paper, or reuse them to store herbs and roots.
- **The wool and paper liner:** the wool can go in your compost or garden; the paper case goes with paper.
- **Water ice packs:** cut the corner, pour the water on a plant and recycle the paper case.
- **The salad bag and liquid pots:** check your local collection; most take the pots.

Less plastic is only part of cutting waste. The rest happens in your kitchen: [storing roots so they last](/blog/store-root-vegetables/) and [making stock from your scraps](/blog/stock-vegetable-scraps/) keep more of every box out of the bin.
