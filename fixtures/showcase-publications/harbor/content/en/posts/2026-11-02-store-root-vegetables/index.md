To store root vegetables so they last, cut off any leafy tops, leave the soil on, and keep them cool, dark and dry. Carrots, beetroot, parsnips and turnips keep best in the fridge salad drawer, loose or in a paper bag. Potatoes, onions, garlic and winter squash keep best in a cool, dark cupboard, never in the fridge. Stored this way, most roots keep for weeks and some for months.

If you store root vegetables well, they are the backbone of a winter kitchen, as they are of our winter boxes, and they are the most forgiving produce we send. They are also the produce most often thrown away by mistake, usually because they were stored in the wrong place. Here is where each one should go.

## Three rules to store root vegetables

- **Cut off the tops.** Leafy tops keep drawing water out of the root, so carrots and beetroot go soft within days. Cut them off, leaving a short stub, and cook the leaves separately.
- **Do not wash until you cook.** Soil protects the skin. Washed roots dry out and rot faster. Brush off loose soil and leave the rest.
- **Keep them apart from apples and pears.** Fruit gives off a ripening gas that makes potatoes sprout and carrots taste bitter. Our guide to [apples and pears](/blog/apples-pears/) has more on that.

## How long each root vegetable keeps

These keeping times come from our own cold room and test kitchen, for produce in good condition when it arrives. Use them as a guide, and trust your eyes and fingers over the table. "Fridge" means the salad drawer, which is the most even place in it.

| Vegetable | Where to keep it | How | Keeps for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carrots | Fridge | Tops off, in a paper bag | 3 to 4 weeks |
| Beetroot | Fridge | Tops off, loose | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Parsnips | Fridge | In a paper bag | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Turnips | Fridge | Tops off, loose | 2 weeks |
| Swede | Fridge or cool cupboard | Whole, unwrapped | 3 to 4 weeks |
| Celeriac | Fridge | Whole, unwrapped | 3 to 4 weeks |
| Radishes | Fridge | Tops off, in a damp towel | 1 week |
| Potatoes | Cool, dark cupboard | Paper or cloth bag | 3 to 5 weeks |
| Sweet potatoes | Cool cupboard, not too cold | Loose | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Onions | Cool, dark, airy cupboard | Loose or in a net | 1 to 2 months |
| Garlic | Cool, dark, airy cupboard | Whole bulbs, loose | 2 to 3 months |
| Shallots | Cool, dark, airy cupboard | Loose | 1 month |
| Winter squash | Cool, dry room | Whole, with its stalk on | 1 to 3 months |
| Ginger | Fridge | Unpeeled, in a paper bag | 3 weeks |
| Jerusalem artichokes | Fridge | In a paper bag | 1 to 2 weeks |

## Why potatoes and onions stay out of the fridge

Potatoes stored in the cold turn some of their starch into sugar, a change called [cold-induced sweetening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-induced_sweetening). They taste oddly sweet and go dark when you fry them. Keep them somewhere cool and dark instead, such as a cupboard away from the oven, and keep light off them: light turns potato skins green, and green patches should be cut away before cooking.

Onions need dry air. In the fridge, they pick up moisture, soften and sprout. And keep onions and potatoes in separate places, because onions give off moisture and gases that make potatoes spoil sooner.

:::figure{src="onions.jpg" alt="A heap of yellow onions with dry, papery golden skins, piled loose at a market stall, stored the way to store root vegetables and bulbs: dry and airy"}
Onions keep best loose, in a cool, airy place. Dry, tight skins and a firm neck mean they will last for weeks.
:::

## How to tell when a root has turned

- **Soft or bendy:** it has lost water. It is still fine to cook, especially in soups and stews.
- **Sprouting:** cut the sprouts off potatoes and onions and use them soon.
- **Green patches on potatoes:** cut them away thickly, and throw the potato out if most of it is green.
- **Wet, mouldy or sour-smelling:** compost it, and check its neighbours.

:::callout{kind=tip}
A bendy carrot or parsnip is not wasted. Stand it in a glass of cold water in the fridge for a few hours and it firms up again. Or chop it into the stock bag in your freezer; see [how to make stock from scraps](/blog/stock-vegetable-scraps/).
:::

## Plan your cooking around what keeps

Because roots keep so well when you store root vegetables this way, they belong at the end of the week. Cook the tender greens and herbs first, and save carrots, beetroot and squash for Thursday and Friday. That simple swap is part of [how we plan a week of dinners](/blog/plan-week-dinners/), and it is why our winter boxes put the roots in the last recipes.
