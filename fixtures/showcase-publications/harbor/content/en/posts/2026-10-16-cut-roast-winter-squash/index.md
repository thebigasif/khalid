To cut a winter squash safely, slice a thin piece off one side so it sits flat, then cut down through it with a heavy knife, pushing on the spine with your other palm, never sawing towards your hand. Once it is in halves, scoop out the seeds and cut it into wedges or cubes. Roast it at 200°C, or 180°C with a fan, for thirty to forty minutes, until a knife slides in and the edges are brown.

[Winter squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_squash) is the vegetable people most often leave in the bottom of the box. It looks hard to deal with, and it is hard, which is exactly why it needs a method. Here is the one I teach.

## Choose a good squash

A winter squash should feel heavy for its size, with a dull, firm skin and a dry, woody stalk. Avoid soft patches and cracks. Shiny skin usually means it was picked young and will taste watery.

Whole squash keeps for one to three months in a cool, dry place, which makes it the most forgiving vegetable in an autumn box. Once cut, wrap it and keep it in the fridge for up to five days.

## How to cut a winter squash

1. **Make it stable.** Put a damp cloth under your board. Lay the squash on its side and slice a thin piece off one long side, so it can rest on that flat face without rolling.
2. **Take off the ends.** Cut off the stalk end and the base, a thumb's width from each end.
3. **Halve it.** Stand the squash on its widest flat end. Set the heel of a large, heavy knife in the middle of the top, and press down with your other hand flat on the spine of the knife. Rock gently until the blade is through.
4. **Scoop.** Remove the seeds and stringy middle with a metal spoon.
5. **Cut to size.** Lay each half cut side down and slice it into wedges or 2.5 cm cubes.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Never hold a squash in your hand while you cut it, and never pull a stuck knife towards you. If the blade sticks, take your hands off, lift the squash and knife together, and tap the squash gently on the board until the blade works through.
:::

## To peel or not to peel

You do not have to peel most winter squash. Thin-skinned kinds soften completely in the oven, and the skin is good to eat. Thick, ridged or very hard skins are better peeled, or roasted on and scooped out afterwards.

If you peel, do it after you cut the squash into flat pieces. A vegetable peeler works on a flat wedge; on a whole round squash, it slips.

## Roast it hot, with room on the tray

1. Heat the oven to 200°C, or 180°C with a fan, with a tray inside.
2. Toss the squash in a tablespoon of oil and a good pinch of salt. Add chilli flakes, thyme or cumin if you like.
3. Spread it on the hot tray, cut sides down, with space between pieces.
4. Roast for thirty to forty minutes, turning once, until soft right through and dark at the edges.

:::figure{src="roasted-squash.jpg" alt="Rings of roast winter squash with dark caramelised edges, beside roast aubergine, peppers and a bowl of herb sauce on a white serving platter"}
Thin rings and wedges roast faster than cubes and give more browned edges. Leave the skin on thin-skinned squash; it softens completely.
:::

## Three ways to use roast squash

- **On a salad.** Toss warm squash with bitter leaves, toasted seeds and a sharp dressing.
- **In a soup.** Blend it with stock and a little cooked onion. Squash soup freezes well.
- **With pasta.** Mash a few pieces into the sauce and leave the rest whole.

Roast the seeds too. Rinse them, dry them on a towel, toss them in oil and salt, and roast them for twelve to fifteen minutes alongside the squash.
