The pantry pasta I cook on nights with nothing in is spaghetti tossed with garlic, chilli and olive oil, loosened with a little of the pasta water. It takes fifteen minutes, it needs four things from the cupboard, and it tastes like you planned it. If you keep those four things in, you always have a dinner.

Every cook needs a pantry pasta like this. It is a version of the Italian [spaghetti aglio e olio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti_aglio_e_olio). Plans break: a late train, a forgotten delivery, a fridge that turned out emptier than you thought. This is the one I fall back on, and it is the first recipe I teach anyone who says they cannot cook.

## What you need for two

- 200 g spaghetti
- 4 cloves of garlic
- 1 dried red chilli, or a good pinch of chilli flakes
- 5 tablespoons olive oil
- Salt
- A handful of parsley, if you have it

## How to make it

1. **Boil the water.** Fill a large pan with water, salt it well, and bring it to the boil. Salted water is the only seasoning the pasta itself gets, so be generous.
2. **Slice the garlic.** While the water heats, peel the garlic and slice it thinly. Thin slices turn golden evenly; thick ones burn outside and stay raw inside.
3. **Cook the pasta.** Add the spaghetti and cook it for two minutes less than the packet says.
4. **Warm the oil.** Halfway through, put the oil, garlic and chilli in a wide frying pan over a low heat. Let the garlic sizzle gently until it is pale gold. This takes four to five minutes.
5. **Bring it together.** Scoop out a mug of pasta water, then lift the spaghetti straight into the frying pan with tongs. Add a splash of the water and toss hard over a medium heat for a minute, until the oil and water turn into a light, glossy sauce.
6. **Finish.** Taste for salt, toss in the chopped parsley and eat at once.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Watch the garlic. It goes from golden to bitter brown in under a minute. If it starts to darken before the pasta is ready, take the pan off the heat; the hot oil keeps cooking it.
:::

## Why the pasta water matters

The water the pasta cooked in is full of starch. When you toss it with oil, the starch holds the two together, so the sauce clings to every strand instead of pooling at the bottom of the bowl. Without it, you get oily spaghetti. With it, you get a sauce.

## Five ways to change your pantry pasta

Keep any of these in, and the same fifteen minutes gives you a different pantry pasta each time:

- **A tin of anchovies.** Melt two or three fillets into the oil with the garlic. They disappear and leave a deep, savoury taste.
- **A jar of capers.** Add a spoonful with the pasta water for a sharp, salty bite.
- **Lemon.** Grate the zest over the finished bowl and squeeze in half the juice.
- **Dried breadcrumbs.** Fry a handful in a spoon of oil until crisp and scatter them on top.
- **Frozen peas or spinach.** Drop them into the pasta water for the last two minutes.

## Keep the pantry ready

A pantry pasta works only if the cupboard is stocked. Put spaghetti, garlic, chilli and olive oil on your list whenever you run low, before you run out. It takes the panic out of a plan that breaks, and it is the easiest way to keep a spare night in [your weekly dinner plan](/blog/plan-week-dinners/).
