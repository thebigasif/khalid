Sunday batch cooking works when you cook parts of meals, not whole meals. In two hours you can roast a tray of vegetables, cook a pot of grains, make a sauce and prepare a protein, and those four parts become four quick weeknight dinners. Each dinner then takes fifteen to twenty minutes to finish, and none of them tastes like a reheated leftover.

I tried for years to cook whole dinners on Sunday and freeze them. By Wednesday nobody wanted the stew again. Cooking parts instead keeps the week varied, because the same tray of roast vegetables can become a pasta, a salad and a soup. This is the plan we use in our test kitchen.

::toc

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Cook parts, not meals.** Four parts make four different dinners.
- **Oven first, hob second, cold jobs last.** That order keeps every ring and shelf busy.
- **Cool fast, label everything.** Each part has a date it must be eaten by.
- **Finish fresh.** A weeknight dinner gets one fresh thing: herbs, a squeeze of lemon, a fried egg.
:::

## What to batch cook

Choose one thing from each of these four groups. Together they cover most of what a dinner needs.

- **A tray of roast vegetables.** Use the method in [how to roast vegetables on one tray](/blog/roast-vegetables-one-tray/).
- **A pot of grains.** Rice, farro, barley or lentils. Our guide to [cooking rice in a pan](/blog/cook-rice-pan/) has the water for each kind of rice.
- **A sauce.** A tomato sauce, a green herb sauce or a simple dressing.
- **A protein.** A tray of chicken thighs, a pot of beans, a batch of hard-boiled eggs or a block of baked tofu.

## The two-hour plan

The order matters more than the recipes. Start the slowest jobs first and use the waiting time for everything else.

| Time | Oven | Hob | Counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Heat to 220°C | Rinse grains, start the pot | Chop roasting vegetables |
| 0:15 | Roast vegetables in | Grains simmering | Season chicken or tofu |
| 0:30 | Add chicken or tofu tray | Grains resting | Start the sauce: onion and garlic |
| 0:45 | Turn vegetables | Sauce simmering | Spread grains on a tray to cool |
| 1:00 | Vegetables out to cool | Sauce simmering | Wash up the first round |
| 1:15 | Chicken or tofu out | Eggs on to boil | Make a dressing |
| 1:30 | Oven off | Sauce off to cool | Cool eggs in cold water |
| 1:45 | | | Pack and label every container |
| 2:00 | | | Done |

The plan assumes one oven with two shelves and two rings on the hob. If you have less, drop the eggs; nothing else depends on them.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Line your roasting trays with baking paper. Two hours of cooking leaves enough washing up without scrubbing roasted-on vegetables off a tin.
:::

## How long each part keeps

Batch cooking only saves time if the food is still good when you reach it. These are the times we use in our test kitchen, for food cooled within an hour and kept covered in a fridge at 5°C or below, as the UK Food Standards Agency's [advice on chilling](https://www.food.gov.uk/safety-hygiene/chilling) recommends.

| Part | Keeps in the fridge | Freezes |
|---|---|---|
| Roast vegetables | 4 days | Yes, for soups |
| Cooked rice | 3 days | Yes, in flat bags |
| Other cooked grains | 4 days | Yes |
| Tomato sauce | 5 days | Yes |
| Herb sauce or dressing | 3 days | No |
| Cooked chicken | 3 days | Yes |
| Baked tofu | 4 days | No |
| Hard-boiled eggs, in shell | 5 days | No |

Write the date on every container. Masking tape and a marker are enough.

Parts packed into boxes become lunches as well as dinners. Keep sauces in the box beside the grains, not on top, so the rice stays firm.

## Four dinners from one Sunday

Here is how the four parts become four different meals:

1. **Monday: warm grain bowl.** Grains and roast vegetables, warmed in a pan, with herb sauce and a soft-boiled egg.
2. **Tuesday: pasta.** Tomato sauce warmed with half the roast vegetables, tossed with fresh pasta.
3. **Wednesday: fried rice.** Cold rice fried hard in a hot pan with the chicken or tofu, frozen peas and an egg.
4. **Thursday: soup.** The last roast vegetables blended with stock, with any leftover grains stirred in.

Each takes fifteen to twenty minutes, and each gets one fresh thing at the end. That last touch is what stops batch cooking from tasting like leftovers.

## Make it fit your week

Start small. One Sunday, cook just the vegetables and the grains, and see how much easier Monday and Tuesday feel. Add the sauce the week after. Batch cooking is a habit, and habits grow better from one easy win than from one exhausting afternoon.

This plan sits on top of [the twenty-minute weekly plan](/blog/plan-week-dinners/): plan on Saturday, shop, cook on Sunday. Peelings and trimmings from the afternoon are worth keeping too; [stock from scraps](/blog/stock-vegetable-scraps/) shows what to do with them.
