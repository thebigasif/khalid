To cook rice in a pan so it comes out fluffy, rinse it, measure the water by volume, bring it to the boil, then cover it tightly and cook it on the lowest heat without lifting the lid. Take it off the heat and leave it covered for ten minutes before you fork it through. You do not need a rice cooker, only a pan with a lid that fits.

This is the absorption method, and it is the simplest way to cook rice in a pan. The rice takes up all the water, so there is nothing to drain and nothing to go wrong at the end. In my classes, it is the skill that most often surprises people, because they expect rice to be hard.

## What you need

To cook rice in a pan, you need three things.

- A heavy pan with a tight lid. If the lid rattles, lay a clean tea towel over the pan and put the lid on top of it.
- A cup or mug to measure with. Any size works, as long as you use the same one for the rice and the water.
- A sieve for rinsing.

## How much water for each kind of rice

Different rice needs different amounts of water. These ratios are by volume, cup for cup, and they are the ones we print on every Harbor Kitchen recipe card.

| Rice | Rice to water | Simmer time | Rest time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long grain white | 1 to 1.5 | 12 min | 10 min |
| Basmati | 1 to 1.5 | 10 min | 10 min |
| Jasmine | 1 to 1.25 | 12 min | 10 min |
| Short grain white | 1 to 1.25 | 15 min | 10 min |
| Brown long grain | 1 to 2 | 30 min | 10 min |
| Red or wild blend | 1 to 2.25 | 35 min | 10 min |

One cup of uncooked rice serves two people as a side, or one very hungry person as a main.

## How to cook rice in a pan, step by step

1. **Rinse.** Put the rice in a sieve and run cold water through it, moving the grains with your fingers, until the water runs nearly clear. Rinsing takes off loose starch, which is what makes rice sticky.
2. **Measure.** Tip the rice into the pan and add the water from the table, plus a good pinch of salt.
3. **Boil.** Bring it to the boil with the lid off, so you can see it. Stir once.
4. **Cover and lower.** As soon as it boils, put the lid on and turn the heat down to the lowest setting.
5. **Leave it.** Set a timer for the simmer time. Do not lift the lid and do not stir.
6. **Rest.** Take the pan off the heat, still covered, and leave it for ten minutes.
7. **Fluff.** Lift the lid and run a fork through the rice to separate the grains.

## Why the rest matters

When the timer goes, the grains at the bottom of the pan are softer than the grains at the top. The ten minutes off the heat let the steam even them out. Skip it and you get rice that is wet below and chalky above.

:::callout{kind=warning}
Cooked rice can carry [bacteria that survive cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_cereus). Cool leftover rice quickly, within an hour, spread on a plate. Keep it covered in the fridge, eat it within three days, and reheat it only once. Heat it until it is steaming hot all the way through.
:::

## If something goes wrong

- **Rice still hard, water gone:** sprinkle over two tablespoons of hot water, cover and leave on the lowest heat for five more minutes.
- **Rice soft, water left:** take the lid off and cook on low for two or three minutes until the water steams away.
- **A crust on the bottom:** the heat was too high. Next time, use a smaller ring on the hob or a heavier pan.

## Make a pot for the week

Once you can cook rice in a pan without thinking, cook double. Cool it fast and keep it for a stir fry or fried rice in the next three days. Day-old rice fries better than fresh, because the grains have dried a little.
