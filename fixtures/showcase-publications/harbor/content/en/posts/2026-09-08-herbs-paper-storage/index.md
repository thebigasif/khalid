We pack herbs in paper, not plastic, because paper keeps them fresh for longer. Plastic traps the water herbs breathe out, and wet leaves turn dark and soft within two or three days. Paper takes up that water and lets the leaves breathe, so the same bunch stays green for most of a week.

We did not always pack herbs in paper. Until last year our herbs went in small plastic bags, like most herbs in most shops. Then Ruth noticed that the herby dinners at the end of each week's box got the most complaints, and we went looking for the reason.

## What we tested

Over four weeks we stored the same herbs three ways in an ordinary home fridge: in a plastic bag, in a paper bag, and standing in a glass of water with a loose paper cover. Each day we checked every bunch and noted the last day it was still good to cook with: no slimy leaves, no dark patches, and a fresh smell.

These numbers come from our own test kitchen, with one fridge and herbs from two of our farms. Your fridge and your herbs will differ, but the order of the results should hold.

| Herb | Plastic bag | Paper bag | Glass of water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parsley | 3 days | 6 days | 8 days |
| Coriander | 2 days | 5 days | 6 days |
| Basil | 2 days | 4 days | 5 days, out of the fridge |
| Mint | 3 days | 6 days | 7 days |
| Chives | 4 days | 7 days | 5 days |
| Dill | 2 days | 5 days | 4 days |

A glass of water did best for soft, leafy herbs, but it takes room in the fridge and tips over in a box on a delivery van. Paper came close, and it travels well. So herbs in paper it was.

## Why herbs in paper last longer

Cut herbs keep breathing after they are picked, a process called [transpiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpiration). They take in air and give off water vapour. In a closed plastic bag, that water condenses on the inside of the bag and sits on the leaves. Wet leaves bruise easily and start to rot where they touch.

Herbs in paper last longer because paper does two jobs at once. It soaks up the extra water before it can settle on the leaves, and it lets air move, so the inside of the bag never turns humid. The leaves stay dry but not dried out.

:::figure{src="chopped-herbs.jpg" alt="Herbs kept in paper, then freshly chopped: a pile of dill, rosemary and parsley on a green chopping board, with the tip of a knife at the edge"}
Herbs that stayed dry in storage chop cleanly. Wet, bruised leaves turn to paste under the knife.
:::

## How to store herbs at home

- **Keep herbs in paper until you cook.** If the paper is soaked through, swap it for a dry kitchen towel.
- **Keep basil out of the fridge.** The cold turns its leaves black. Stand it in a glass of water on the counter, out of direct sun.
- **Use the salad drawer for the rest.** It is the most even place in the fridge. Keep herbs away from the back wall, where it is coldest and leaves can freeze.
- **Treat hard herbs differently.** Rosemary, thyme and sage are tougher. Wrapped in paper, they last two weeks or more.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If parsley or coriander has gone limp but not dark, trim the stalks and stand the bunch in cold water for twenty minutes. Most of it will stand up again.
:::

## What this means for your week

Herbs in paper stay fresh, so you can cook the herby dinners later in the week, not only on Monday. That makes planning easier.
