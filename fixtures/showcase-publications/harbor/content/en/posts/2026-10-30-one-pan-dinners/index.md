One-pan dinners work when you add each ingredient in the order it takes to cook: the slowest first, the quickest last. Onions and dense vegetables go in first, then the protein, then soft vegetables, and leaves and herbs at the very end. Get the order right and everything is ready at the same moment, in one pan to wash.

Most one-pan dinners that go wrong do so because everything went in at once. The onions are raw, the courgettes have collapsed, and the chicken is grey. Here is the order we use in our recipes and the reasoning behind it.

## The order for one-pan dinners

1. **Fat and aromatics.** Oil or butter, then onion, leek or shallot. They need time to soften and sweeten, so they lead.
2. **Dense vegetables.** Diced potato, carrot, squash or parsnip. Cut them small so they cook through.
3. **The protein.** Chicken, sausage, firm tofu or fish. Push the vegetables to the edge so the protein touches the hot pan and browns, through the [Maillard reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction).
4. **Spices and garlic.** Add them once there is food in the pan, so they toast without burning.
5. **Soft vegetables.** Peppers, courgette, asparagus, [ripe tomatoes](/blog/choose-ripe-tomatoes/) or green beans.
6. **Liquid, if any.** Stock, a tin of tomatoes or coconut milk, to bring it together.
7. **Leaves and herbs.** Spinach, kale, parsley or coriander, stirred in at the end until they just wilt.

## How long each ingredient needs

These times are for a large frying pan or a shallow casserole over a medium-high heat, with ingredients cut to the size shown. The times are from our own testing on an ordinary home hob.

| Ingredient | Cut | Time in the pan | Goes in at step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onion | Thin slices | 8 to 10 min | 1 |
| Potato | 1.5 cm dice | 15 to 18 min | 2 |
| Carrot | Thin coins | 10 to 12 min | 2 |
| Chicken thigh | 3 cm pieces | 10 to 12 min | 3 |
| Sausage | 2 cm slices | 8 to 10 min | 3 |
| Firm tofu | 2 cm cubes | 8 to 10 min | 3 |
| Pepper | Thin strips | 5 to 6 min | 5 |
| Asparagus | Whole or halved | 4 to 5 min | 5 |
| Cherry tomatoes | Whole | 3 to 4 min | 5 |
| Spinach | Whole leaves | 1 min | 7 |

The slowest ingredient after the onion sets the length of the dinner, and each later ingredient goes in when its own time is left on the clock. For example, with onion, potato, chicken and pepper, the onion softens for eight minutes, then the potato needs about eighteen. The chicken goes in when twelve minutes are left and the pepper when six are left, so dinner takes about half an hour, mostly waiting.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Use the widest pan you own. Food in one layer browns; food in a pile steams. If the pan is full, cook the protein first, set it aside on a plate and add it back at step six.
:::

## Fish and prawns go in last

Fish and prawns cook in three to five minutes, faster than most vegetables. For a one-pan dinner with fish, cook the vegetables almost to the end, then lay the fish on top, cover the pan, and steam it in the last few minutes. Prawns go in with the soft vegetables.

## Taste before you serve

A one-pan dinner gets all its flavour from one pan, so the last minute matters. Taste it, then add one of these:

- **Salt,** if it tastes flat.
- **Lemon juice or vinegar,** if it tastes heavy.
- **Chilli or black pepper,** if it tastes dull.
- **A spoon of butter or good oil,** if it tastes thin.

## Make it from what you have

Once you know the order, you do not need a recipe. Look in the fridge, sort what you find into the seven steps, and cook down the list.
