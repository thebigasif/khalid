To choose ripe tomatoes, smell the stalk end first, then weigh the tomato in your hand and press it gently. A ripe tomato smells green and sweet where the stalk joins, feels heavy for its size and gives a little under your thumb without staying dented. Colour comes last, because many good tomatoes ripen to orange, yellow or striped green.

Late summer is when tomatoes are at their best and their cheapest. It is also when the most disappointing ones reach the shops, picked hard to survive a long journey. Here is how I judge a crate when I buy for our boxes, and how you can do the same at a market stall.

## Smell the stalk end

Tomato flavour lives in the same compounds that give the plant its smell. Hold the tomato to your nose with the stalk end up. A ripe one smells like the leaves of the plant, green and a little sweet. A tomato that smells of nothing will taste of very little, however red it looks.

This is the fastest test, and it works through a paper bag.

## Feel the weight and the give

Pick up two tomatoes of the same size. The heavier one holds more juice and usually more flavour. Then press it gently with your thumb near the shoulder:

- **Hard as an apple:** picked early. It will soften on your counter, but it will not gain much flavour.
- **A slight give that springs back:** ripe. Eat it within two or three days.
- **Soft, with a dent that stays:** overripe. Use it today in a sauce, where texture does not matter.

## Ripe tomatoes are not always red

A deep, even red is what most of us look for, but it can mislead. Some varieties ripen orange or yellow. Others stay green on the shoulders when fully ripe. And tomatoes picked early and kept in warm rooms can turn red without ever getting sweet.

Look instead for a glossy skin with no wrinkles, and a stalk, if there is one, that is green and fresh rather than dry and grey.

:::figure{src="on-the-vine.jpg" alt="Ripe tomatoes, deep red and still joined to their green vine, piled close together"}
Tomatoes sold on the vine keep their smell longer, and the vine itself is the best freshness test: green and bendy means recently picked.
:::

## How to store tomatoes at home

Keep ripe tomatoes out of the fridge, stalk end down, in one layer on a plate. The cold of a fridge stops the flavour developing and turns the flesh mealy within a day or two.

There are two exceptions. A cut tomato goes in the fridge, covered. And a fully ripe tomato that you cannot eat today can go in the fridge for a day or two; take it out an hour before you eat it so it warms up and some of its flavour comes back.

:::callout{kind=tip}
To ripen hard tomatoes, put them in a paper bag with a banana and fold the top over. The banana gives off [ethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene_as_a_plant_hormone), a gas that speeds ripening. Check them every day.
:::

## What to cook with late summer tomatoes

Ripe tomatoes need very little. Slice them thickly, salt them, and let them sit for ten minutes before you add oil. For anything past its best, roast them whole on a hot tray for the last fifteen minutes of [a tray of roast vegetables](/blog/roast-vegetables-one-tray/), where they split and turn sweet. The same smell, weight and feel tests work for most fruit; read [our guide to apples and pears](/blog/apples-pears/) for the autumn version.
