To plan a week of dinners in twenty minutes, pick five meals that share two bases, write one shopping list sorted by where things sit in the shop, and leave one night free for leftovers. That is the whole method. The rest of this guide shows how we do each step in the Harbor Kitchen test kitchen, with a sample week you can copy.

I plan a week of dinners for our test kitchen this way every Sunday, and it is the same thinking behind the five dinners in each Harbor Kitchen box. It works whether you cook for one or for six.

::toc

:::callout{kind=summary}
- **Two bases, five dinners.** Cook a base once, such as a pot of rice or a tray of roast vegetables, and use it twice.
- **One list, sorted by aisle.** One trip, no second jar of something you already have.
- **Five dinners for seven nights.** One night for leftovers, one night free.
- **The plan on the fridge.** Nobody has to ask what is for dinner.
:::

## Start with what your household already eats

The quickest way to plan a week of dinners is to start with meals you already know. Write down three dinners that everyone in your house eats without complaint. These are your anchors. You do not need a recipe for them, and you know roughly what they cost.

Then add two dinners that are a little new. One new recipe a week is enough to stop the plan feeling like a rut, and two keeps it interesting without making every night a test. Do not put a new recipe on Monday, when the week is at its busiest.

## Plan a week of dinners around two bases

A base is something you cook once and use twice. It is the single biggest time saver in weeknight cooking, because the slowest part of most dinners is the part that sits on the hob or in the oven.

Good bases for your week:

- **A pot of rice or another grain.** It carries a stir fry, a grain bowl and fried rice. Our guide to [cooking rice in a pan](/blog/cook-rice-pan/) makes a pot you can keep for three days.
- **A tray of roast vegetables.** It fills a pasta, a salad and a soup. See [how to roast vegetables on one tray](/blog/roast-vegetables-one-tray/).
- **A pot of beans or lentils.** It makes a stew, a soup and a filling for wraps.
- **A batch of sauce.** A tomato sauce dresses pasta on Monday and becomes the base for baked eggs on Wednesday.

Choose two bases, then check that each of your five dinners uses at least one. If a dinner uses neither, swap it for one that does, or move it to the weekend.

## A sample week

Here is a real week from our test kitchen, built on a pot of rice and a tray of roast vegetables, both cooked on Sunday.

| Night | Dinner | Base used | Hands-on time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Pasta with roast vegetables and lemon | Roast vegetables | 15 min |
| Tuesday | Egg fried rice with peas and spring onion | Rice | 15 min |
| Wednesday | Rice bowls with crispy tofu and greens | Rice | 25 min |
| Thursday | Roast vegetable and white bean soup | Roast vegetables | 20 min |
| Friday | Leftovers night | Anything left | 5 min |
| Saturday | Free: a meal out or a change of plan | None | None |
| Sunday | Cook the bases and a slow dinner | New bases | 60 min |

Monday and Tuesday are the fastest on purpose. The rice is used up by Wednesday, inside the three days cooked rice keeps, and the soup on Thursday takes whatever roast vegetables are left.

## Write one shopping list sorted by aisle

One list means one trip. Sort it by where things sit in your shop: fresh produce first, then the fridge, then the shelves, then the freezer. A sorted list takes two minutes longer to write and saves you walking back across the shop for the one thing you missed.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Check the fridge and the cupboard before you write the list, not after. Most [food waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_waste) at home starts with buying a second jar of something already open at the back of the fridge.
:::

Write quantities on the list. "Carrots" leads to a big bag and half of it going soft. "Four carrots" leads to four carrots.

## Leave one night for leftovers

Plan five dinners for a seven-night week. Keep the cupboard ready for a [fifteen-minute pantry pasta](/blog/pantry-pasta/) in case a night goes wrong. One night takes what is left, and one night is free for a meal out, a late day or a change of plan. A plan with no slack breaks the first time a day runs late, and a broken plan is how food ends up in the bin.

Leftovers night is also when the plan tells you how it went. If there is a lot left, you cooked too much of a base. If there is nothing, you can cook a little more next week.

## Keep the plan where you can see it

Write the week on a note on the fridge. When the plan is in front of you, nobody has to ask what is for dinner, and anyone home first can start the base the night before or put the rice on.

At the end of the week, write one line on the back of the note: what worked and what nobody ate. The next time you plan a week of dinners, start from those lines. After a month, those lines are a list of dinners your household loves, and planning takes ten minutes, not twenty.
