Wide bowls are the pots my viewers ask about most, and the pots that fall down most. This week's video is all about shaping a wide bowl from 1.5 kilograms of clay without it collapsing on the last pull.

## Start with a wide, flat floor

A bowl gets its curve from its floor. I open the clay wide and flat, about 15 centimetres across, and compress it three times with a rib. A floor that is compressed and even is the best defence against cracks, as I wrote in my note on [why bowls crack while drying](/blog/why-bowls-crack-while-drying/).

## Pull up before you pull out

The most common mistake is pulling the wall outwards too early. Clay that leans out before it is thin enough has nothing to hold it up. I pull the wall straight up, like a short cylinder, two or three times first. Only on the last pull do I start to lean it out.

## Shaping a wide bowl from the inside

To open the curve, I use a soft rib on the inside and my fingertips on the outside, just below the inside rib. The wheel turns slowly now, about a third of its centring speed. Each pass moves the wall out by a centimetre, never more. When the clay starts to wobble, I stop and let it rest.

## A rim that holds

A wide bowl's rim needs a little extra clay. I keep the top centimetre thicker than the wall and fold it slightly with a chamois leather. A thick rim works like the edge of a hoop: it keeps the whole bowl round while it dries.

## Trimming the foot

Once the bowl is leather hard, I turn it over and trim a foot ring. The foot is about a third of the rim's width, so the bowl stands steady but still looks light. Trimming takes longer than throwing on a bowl this size, so the video covers it in full.

## Try it yourself

Start with 1 kilogram of clay before you try 1.5, because shaping a wide bowl gets harder with every extra 100 grams. If you want help in person, wide bowls are the Sunday project at the [weekend wheel workshops](/blog/weekend-wheel-workshops/).
