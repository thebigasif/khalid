The weekend wheel workshops are back for autumn, with six new dates. Each workshop is two days in the studio for six people, and you take home everything you make once it is fired. You do not need any experience.

## What you will make at the wheel workshops

On Saturday you learn to centre, open and pull, the same steps as my video on [throwing a mug in twelve minutes](/blog/throw-a-mug-in-twelve-minutes/). Most people throw their first mug by mid-afternoon and three or four more by the end of the day.

On Sunday the pots from Saturday are leather hard. You trim their feet, add handles and choose a glaze from the [three we mix ourselves](/blog/mixing-my-own-glazes/). Dev fires everything in the next kiln load, and your pots are ready to collect about three weeks later. We can post them too.

## What to bring

Wear clothes you do not mind getting muddy and shoes that stay on your feet. Keep your nails short, or they will cut into the clay. Bring lunch, or walk to the cafes on the next street. We have aprons, towels and every tool you need.

## How the studio works

The wheel workshops use all six wheels in the studio, so everyone has their own for both days. I teach, and Dev helps at the wheels and looks after the kiln. Your pots dry on the far wall, away from the heater, for the reasons in my note on [why bowls crack while drying](/blog/why-bowls-crack-while-drying/).

Book a place through the [shop](https://www.example.com/tamsin-hale/shop), where each weekend is listed with its places left. If a date is full, write to the studio and we will add you to the waiting list. Gift vouchers for any date are in the shop too.
