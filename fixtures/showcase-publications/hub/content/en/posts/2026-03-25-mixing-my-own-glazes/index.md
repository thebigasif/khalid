I started mixing my own glazes for the studio two years ago, and we have not bought a ready-made glaze since. Mixing glazes yourself costs less, and it lets you change a colour by a small step instead of hoping a shop has the shade you want.

## Why I keep mixing my own glazes

A ready-made glaze is a mystery in a tub. When it crawls or runs, you cannot tell why. When we mix our own, we know every material in the bucket, so we can change one thing and see what it does. Mixing my own glazes also gave Tamsin glazes nobody else sells, because they are part of how people recognise her mugs.

## The three recipes

These are the three glazes on almost everything in the [shop](https://www.example.com/tamsin-hale/shop). All three share one base of 100 parts by dry weight, and we fire all three to cone 6, about 1,220 °C.

| Material | Oat white | Moss green | Iron brown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feldspar | 30 | 30 | 30 |
| Silica | 25 | 25 | 25 |
| Whiting | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| Kaolin | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| Talc | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Tin oxide (extra) | 4 | none | none |
| Copper carbonate (extra) | none | 3 | none |
| Red iron oxide (extra) | none | 1 | 9 |
| Firing | Cone 6 | Cone 6 | Cone 6 |

The extras go on top of the 100 parts. That way the base stays the same, and only the colour changes.

## How we mix a batch

I weigh each dry material on a scale that reads to a tenth of a gram, wearing a dust mask. I add the powder to water, about 90 millilitres for every 100 grams, and stir it with a paddle on a drill for five minutes. Then I pass it twice through a fine sieve.

## Testing before it touches a pot

Every new batch goes on three test tiles first. One tile sits flat, one stands up to show runs, and one has the glaze thick and thin side by side. We fire the tiles with the next [kiln load](/blog/loading-the-kiln/) and keep them on a board by the glaze shelf, so anyone can compare. Raku is the exception: its glazes go through a much faster firing, which Tamsin filmed for her [first raku firing](/blog/first-raku-firing/).

The first time you dip a whole set of cups, it helps to see it done. Tamsin's [glazing day for the autumn cups](/blog/glazing-the-autumn-cups/) shows the dipping, and her video on [throwing a mug](/blog/throw-a-mug-in-twelve-minutes/) shows where those cups begin.
