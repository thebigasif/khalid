When I started selling, one bowl in five cracked on the shelf before it ever saw the kiln. Bowls crack while drying for one reason: one part of the bowl dries and shrinks faster than another, and the clay pulls itself apart. Every fix below slows the fast part down.

## Where bowls crack while drying

Most cracks start in one of two places. An S-shaped crack in the middle of the floor means the base was thicker or wetter than the walls. A crack that runs down from the rim means the rim dried first, while the body below was still soft.

## Habit one: compress the floor

After opening a bowl, I press the floor with a rib, from the middle outwards, three times. This lines up the clay and pushes out water. It takes ten seconds and it stopped almost every S-crack. I show it in the video on [shaping a wide bowl](/blog/shaping-a-wide-bowl/).

## Habit two: even walls

A thick foot under a thin wall is a crack waiting to happen. I trim the foot as soon as the bowl is leather hard, so the base is no thicker than the wall. If you are new to trimming, start with the [twelve-minute mug](/blog/throw-a-mug-in-twelve-minutes/), which has a simpler foot.

## Habit three: dry slowly, upside down

Fresh bowls go under a loose sheet of plastic for the first day. Once the rim is firm, I turn each bowl upside down onto a board. Now the rim and the foot dry at the same pace, and the air reaches the floor from below.

## Habit four: keep the shelves away from the heater

Our drying shelves used to stand next to the studio heater. The side of each bowl that faced the heat dried hours before the other side. Moving the shelves to the far wall cost nothing and saved a dozen bowls a month.

## When a crack still happens

When bowls crack while drying, the crack in the raw clay will not heal in the kiln. It opens wider. So I put cracked bowls straight into the reclaim bucket, and that clay comes back as next month's mugs. The [Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/) has more on drying if you want the science behind it.

The same habits apply to everything we make for the [weekend workshops](/blog/weekend-wheel-workshops/), where every student's bowl dries on the far wall too.
