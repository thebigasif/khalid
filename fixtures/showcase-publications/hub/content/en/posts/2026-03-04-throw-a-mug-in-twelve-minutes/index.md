This week's video shows how I throw a mug in twelve minutes, start to finish. It is not a race. Twelve minutes is simply how long a mug takes once the steps are in your hands, and knowing the steps is what makes the time short.

## Weigh the clay first

Every mug starts as 450 grams of stoneware, weighed on a kitchen scale. Weighing sounds fussy, but it is the cheapest way to make a set that matches. If one ball is 50 grams heavier, that mug will be taller or thicker, and you will see it on the shelf.

I wedge each ball for about a minute to push out air and line up the clay. Then I pat it into a cone, so it sits on the wheel head with no gap underneath.

## Throw a mug: centre, open, pull

Centring takes the first three minutes. I keep the wheel fast, my elbows locked on my knees, and I let the wheel do the work. When the clay stops pushing back against my hands, it is centred.

Opening takes one minute. I press my thumbs down to leave about a centimetre of clay at the base, then pull the floor out to the width of the mug.

Pulling the walls takes most of the rest. I make three pulls, each one slower than the last, and I slow the wheel down as the wall gets thinner. The [potter's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter%27s_wheel) turns about half as fast on the last pull as on the first.

## Shape and finish

The last two minutes are for the rim and the shape. This is where you throw a mug that feels good to drink from, not just one that stands up. I run a strip of chamois leather over the rim to round it, because lips notice a sharp rim before eyes do. Then I cut the mug off with a wire and lift it onto a board.

## What happens next

The mug dries for a day until it is leather hard. Then I trim the foot, which is the subject of a later video on [shaping a wide bowl](/blog/shaping-a-wide-bowl/), and pull a handle. If your bowls or mugs crack while they dry, read my note on [why pots crack while drying](/blog/why-bowls-crack-while-drying/).

You can try all of this with me at a [weekend wheel workshop](/blog/weekend-wheel-workshops/). Most people throw their first mug by Saturday afternoon.
