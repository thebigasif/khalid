Once a season we spend a whole day glazing cups for the shop. Glazing the autumn cups took all of one Tuesday. This autumn it was sixty cups in moss green, all dipped by hand. Glazing cups in a batch this size only works with a steady rhythm, so here is ours.

## Before glazing the autumn cups

The cups came out of the bisque firing the week before. I wiped each one with a damp sponge to remove dust, because dust under a glaze makes it crawl. Then I waxed every foot with a brush, so the glaze would run off it.

The glaze was the moss green from our post on [mixing my own glazes](/blog/mixing-my-own-glazes/). I stirred the bucket for five minutes and checked its thickness with a hydrometer. Moss green wants a specific gravity of about 1.45.

## How I hold and dip each cup

I hold each cup by its foot, upside down, and push it straight into the glaze to the rim. I count to three, lift it out and turn it upright with a flick, so the glaze inside runs back down. A cup takes about ten seconds.

Three seconds gives a coat about as thick as a postcard. Two seconds is too thin and looks patchy after firing. Four seconds runs at the bottom of the cup.

## The checks

After every ten cups I stop and check. Any glaze on the foot comes off with a sponge. Drips get rubbed down with a finger once they are dry. Bare spots from my fingers get a dab of glaze with a brush.

Tamsin checks the first cup of every ten too, because two pairs of eyes catch more than one.

## Into the kiln

Sixty cups is a full kiln and a half. They went into the next two firings, loaded the way I describe in [loading the kiln](/blog/loading-the-kiln/). Fifty-four came out perfect. Six had small pinholes, and those went into the [seconds sale](/blog/seconds-sale-green-bowls/).

If you want to know more about how glaze thickness changes the fired colour, [Digitalfire](https://digitalfire.com/) has a long reference on glaze chemistry.
