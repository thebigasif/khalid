# Tideline: art direction

Tideline is a made-up weekday digest about the Selwick coast, a made-up stretch of coast. Its photographs show the coast the items are about: the sea in a gale, seabirds and waders, kelp under water, a lighthouse, rock pools, a survey boat, dunes and a harbour in fog. Each one sets the scene for an issue's first item; none of them is news in itself.

## Tone

- **The coast as people on it see it.** Weather, water, birds, boats and sand, in plain daylight or the soft light of early morning and evening.
- **Nothing to read.** No signs, prices or names a reader could take for real news. The survey boat carries a small agency mark on its hull, too small to read at the width the picture shows.
- **No faces.** No people are close enough to recognise.

## Palette

Sea greys, teals and blues, with the warm sand of the dunes and rock pools and the orange of a puffin's bill or an oystercatcher's. These sit well beside the sample brand's deep sea teal, its buoy orange and the sea mist desk.

## Subject mix

Of the 10 photographs, 4 are close views and 6 are wider scenes.

- **Close views (4):** puffins on a rock, an oystercatcher on wet sand, kelp and fish under water, a rock pool.
- **Wider scenes (6):** waves over a boardwalk in a gale, a lighthouse on a wooded headland, a survey boat at speed, a calm sea at sunset, a path through dune grass, a harbour in fog.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture on an issue | 2:1 | 1600 px wide | Short and wide at the top of an issue, about 39rem across. The crop sits a little above the middle, so the lighthouse keeps its lantern. |
| Lead picture of the latest issue on the front page | 2:1 | the same file | The same crop, above the latest issue's stamp line. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink: five ticks and two waves. No photographs of people. |

The Brief template shows no pictures in its lists: those are dated rows. Only the latest issue on the front page and each issue's own page show a picture.

## Phone crop

On a phone the picture fills the sheet's width at the same 2:1 ratio. Each subject sits across the middle of the frame: the boat, the bird, the puffins and the lighthouse are near the centre, and the sea, kelp, rock and grass are even textures that read the same at any crop.

## Sources and licences

Every photograph comes from Wikimedia Commons through `scripts/demo-photo.py`: three under CC0 and seven in the public domain, most of them from United States government agencies (NOAA, the Fish and Wildlife Service and the National Marine Sanctuaries). None needs a visible credit. `photos.lock` records each one, and `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the photo credits page list them anyway.
