Six things from the coast this Friday, starting with a survey boat at work in the Wend channel.

## A survey boat maps the Wend channel

A small survey boat is measuring the depth of the Wend channel this week, after the sandbanks moved in the gale. New charts follow in December. Until then, the harbour office asks boats to keep to the marked channel. [Read the survey notice](https://www.example.org/harbour/channel-survey)

## The dredger leaves Brackhaven

The dredger that cleared the inner harbour has finished two weeks early. Boats can use every berth again from Monday. The mud it lifted goes to build up a salt marsh further up the estuary. [See the harbour news](https://www.example.com/harbour/dredging-done)

## The marine lab opens its doors on Saturday

The Ludden Marine Lab holds an open day with tank tours, a plankton microscope and talks every hour. Entry is free, and the lab says the plankton tank is the one children like most. [Plan your visit](https://www.example.org/ludden-lab/open-day)

## Fishing quotas for next year

The fishing association says next year's crab and lobster limits will stay the same. Mackerel limits are not set yet. The association expects the mackerel figure before Christmas. [Read the association's note](https://www.example.net/fishing/quotas)

## A new skipper for the island ferry

Dana Rowe, who has worked on the ferry for fifteen years, takes over as skipper on Monday. She is the first skipper to have grown up on the islands. The crew will mark the day with free tea on the first sailing. [Read the ferry news](https://www.example.net/ferry/new-skipper)

## How sea charts are made

A plain guide to how a survey boat measures depth with sound, and why charts of moving sand need redrawing every year. It has a short animation that shows the sound bouncing off the sea floor. [Read the guide](https://www.example.org/harbour/how-charts-are-made)
