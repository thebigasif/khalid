Six things from the coast this Monday. The Ludden dunes need a hand after the gale, and you can lend one.

## Chestnut fences go up in the Ludden dunes

Rangers are putting up low wooden fences to trap blown sand and rebuild the front dunes the gale flattened. Volunteers help on Wednesdays. Each fence is about knee high, and over a winter it can gather a metre of sand. [Help build the fences](https://www.example.org/rangers/dune-fences)

## The Sunday bus to Ludden starts well

Ninety people used the first Sunday bus to the dunes. The council will decide in March whether to keep it. Most riders were walkers, and some brought dogs and bikes. [Read the first-day count](https://www.example.net/buses/ludden-first-day)

## The morning ferry is back on time

The island ferry's engine is fixed, and the 7:15 sailing runs to time again from today. The company thanked riders for their patience during the week of delays. [See the ferry updates](https://www.example.net/ferry/delays)

## A new map of the coast path

The trust has drawn a new folding map of the 40-mile coast path, with every bench, tap and bus stop on it. It costs five pounds, and the money goes to path repairs. [Order the map](https://www.example.org/coast-trust/path-map)

## The harbour café opens its winter porch

The café at Carrow Point has finished its heated porch, so walkers can sit out of the wind with soup and a view of the point all winter. Dogs are welcome in the porch, and there is a bowl of water by the door. [See the winter menu](https://www.example.com/carrow-cafe/winter)

## Why marram grass holds a dune together

A short piece on the tough grass that holds the Ludden dunes together, grows faster when sand buries it, and needs walkers to keep to the paths. Its roots can reach several metres down into the sand. [Read the short piece](https://www.example.org/rangers/marram-grass)
