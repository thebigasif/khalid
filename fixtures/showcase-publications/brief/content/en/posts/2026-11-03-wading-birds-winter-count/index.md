Six things from the coast this Tuesday, with wading birds back on the estuary in their thousands.

## Wading birds arrive on the Wend estuary

The first winter count found 4,300 wading birds on the estuary mud, most of them oystercatchers, curlews and knots. The count is the highest for October since the trust began counting in 2009. [Read the bird count](https://www.example.org/coast-trust/wader-count)

## The three lido plans are online

Ahead of the public meeting, the council has put the three plans for the old sea pool online, with drawings and costs. One plan restores the pool, one turns it into a rock garden, and one fills it in for a car park. [Compare the three plans](https://www.example.com/council/lido-plans)

## A quiz night for the lifeboat

The Anchor at Brackhaven holds a quiz on Friday to raise money for the lifeboat's new engine. Teams of up to six. Last year's quiz raised enough to buy two new dry suits. [Book a table](https://www.example.com/lifeboat/quiz)

## Hollinmouth beach is clean again

Forty people cleared 60 bags of rubbish from Hollinmouth on Sunday, most of it washed in by the gale. The trust says it was the biggest clean of the year. [See the clean in photos](https://www.example.org/coast-trust/hollinmouth-clean)

## Bird hides open longer in winter

The two hides on the estuary now open from 8 until dusk every day, with a ranger there on weekend mornings. The hides have new benches and a board that shows what was seen that week. [See the opening times](https://www.example.org/rangers/bird-hides)

## How to tell a curlew from a godwit

A short, friendly guide to the long-billed birds of the estuary, with drawings of each one side by side. The quick answer: a curlew's bill curves down, and a godwit's bill is straight or turns slightly up. [Read the guide](https://www.example.org/rangers/curlew-or-godwit)
