Tideline is a short digest about the Selwick coast. Every weekday morning we send five to seven things worth knowing, and on Saturday we send a round-up of the whole week. You should be able to read an issue in about two minutes, over a coffee or on the bus into Brackhaven.

## How Tideline chooses what goes in

We read the notices from the harbour office, the lifeboat station, the council, the Selwick Coast Trust and the Ludden Marine Lab, plus whatever readers send us. Each morning we pick the things that change what someone on the coast might do today: a closed path, a new ferry time, a count that needs volunteers. Good news gets in too, because there is plenty of it, from seabird counts to a café that stays open all winter.

## Why every item fits in a few lines

Each item is a headline, two or three sentences on what happened, and a link to the people who know more. If a story needs more than that, the link is where to read it. We would rather send you to the source than retell it badly, and we never add a link we have not opened ourselves.

## How to send us something

Write to hello@example.com with what is happening, where and when, and a link if there is one. We read everything, and we say thank you in the next issue of Tideline when we use your tip. Photos are welcome too, as long as you took them yourself and are happy for us to share them. Our [Saturday issue on the first autumn gale](/blog/first-autumn-gale/) is a good example of what we look for.

## Who writes it

Ines Calloway edits the weekday briefs. Rory Penhallow writes the Saturday round-up and fills in when Ines is out on the water. Between them they have lived on this coast for more than forty years, and Tideline is the digest they wanted to read themselves. You can read more about both of us on the [about page](/blog/about/).
