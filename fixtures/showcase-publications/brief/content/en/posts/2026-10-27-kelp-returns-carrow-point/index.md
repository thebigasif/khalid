Six things from the coast this Tuesday, starting with the kelp under the water off Carrow Point.

## The kelp beds off Carrow Point are growing back

Divers from the Ludden Marine Lab found kelp covering about a third of the old beds, five years after a warm summer wiped them out. The lab will dive the same spots again in spring to see whether the new growth lasts the winter. [Read the dive report](https://www.example.org/ludden-lab/kelp-report)

## Engineers check the harbour wall after the gale

A survey team will check the outer wall at Brackhaven this week. The end of the pier is closed to walkers until Friday. The harbour board says it expects only small repairs, if any. [See the closure map](https://www.example.com/harbour/wall-survey)

## A seawater tank for Hollinmouth school

The primary school now keeps a small seawater tank of crabs, anemones and limpets, with a rota of pupils to look after it. The class names every new crab and writes a short note about it each week. [Read the school's post](https://www.example.net/hollinmouth-school/rock-pool-tank)

## Winter swimming group grows to 60

The early swimmers who meet at the Ludden slipway now number 60. New swimmers can join on Saturdays, with a buddy for the first month. The group swims at 8 every morning, whatever the weather, and has hot drinks after. [Join the swimmers](https://www.example.com/ludden-swimmers/join)

## Lost and found: a sea kayak

A red sea kayak washed up on Carrow beach after the gale. The harbour office will hold it for a month for its owner. Call the office with the kayak's markings to prove it is yours. [Claim the kayak](https://www.example.org/harbour/lost-and-found)

## Why kelp matters to the whole coast

A short read on how kelp beds calm waves, shelter young fish and store carbon, written for people who have never dived. It also explains why a warm summer can wipe out a bed in a few weeks. [Read the short guide](https://www.example.org/ludden-lab/why-kelp)
