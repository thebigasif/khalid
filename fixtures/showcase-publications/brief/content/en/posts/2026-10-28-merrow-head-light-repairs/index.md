Six things from the coast this Wednesday. The news about the Merrow Head light is good, the ferry news is not.

## New glass for the Merrow Head light

Work starts next week to replace the cracked panes around the lamp. A temporary light on a mast will guide boats until December. The old panes are 60 years old, and two cracked in the gale last week. [Read the repair plan](https://www.example.org/lights/merrow-head-repairs)

## The morning ferry runs 20 minutes late all week

One of the two engines on the island ferry is out for repair. The 7:15 leaves at 7:35 until Saturday. The company says the later sailings are not affected. [See the ferry updates](https://www.example.net/ferry/delays)

## Help wanted to count seal pups

The Selwick Coast Trust needs volunteers for its November seal pup count on the Ludden sandbanks. Training takes one evening. Counters work in pairs from the dunes, with a ranger nearby, and no one goes onto the sand. [Sign up to count](https://www.example.org/coast-trust/seal-count)

## A quieter night sky at Carrow

The council is changing the street lights at Carrow Point to warmer, dimmer lamps, which helps both stargazers and moths. The work starts in January and takes about a month. [Read the council's plan](https://www.example.com/council/carrow-lights)

## A new rescue board at Carrow beach

The lifeboat crew has fixed a bright yellow rescue board and throw line by the steps at Carrow beach, after two swimmers got into trouble there in the summer. Anyone can use it, and a sign beside it shows how in four steps. [See where it is](https://www.example.com/lifeboat/rescue-board)

## Old photos of the lighthouse keepers

The Brackhaven museum has put 80 photos of the last lighthouse keepers and their families online, with their own captions. Some of the families are still on the coast, and the museum asks anyone who knows a face to get in touch. [Browse the photos](https://www.example.net/museum/keepers)
