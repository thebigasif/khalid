# Meridian Atlas: art direction

Meridian Atlas is a made-up travel magazine about cities. Its photographs should look like what a curious walker sees on a good day out: real streets, real markets and real food in daylight, nothing staged for a brochure.

## Tone

- **Warm daylight.** Morning or afternoon sun, or bright open shade. No night shots, no neon, no long-exposure light trails. The one hazy evening shot, Wat Arun, still reads as daylight.
- **Real streets at eye level.** The streets, terraces, platforms and stalls a reader would walk past. A few views from a terrace or a hillside, never from a drone or a skyscraper.
- **Places, not people.** People appear as passers-by, cyclists and shoppers, small in the frame or seen from behind. No face is the subject of a picture.
- **Nothing to read.** No brand logos, shop names, advertising or watermarks a reader could make out. Candidates were turned down for a tram wrapped in an advertisement, a cruise ship's funnel logo, bagel shop and market stall signs, a station poster wall, a fruit sticker brand and a photographer's watermark on a sunset. A few pictures keep small incidental text that cannot be read at card size; they are listed in the review notes below.

## Palette

Warm, sunlit city colour on stone and water:

- terracotta roofs, ochre and pink house fronts and red brick;
- the blue and white of tiles, harbours and summer skies;
- the greens of street trees, artichokes and park paths;
- market colour in small doses: fruit, berries and flowers.

These sit well beside the template's indigo and the Atlas sample brand's magenta, because the photographs bring their own warmth and leave the strong accent to the page. Avoid photographs dominated by grey overcast skies, purple night light or black-and-white.

## Subject mix

Of the 33 photographs, 12 are close details and 21 are wider scenes, so more than a third are close details, as the brief asks.

- **Close details (12):** a stone pavement pattern, a garlic stall, a painted tile panel, artichokes, a paella pan over a fire, salmon soup, a bento box, rice balls and miso soup, a bowl of pho, cups of cut fruit, a flower stall, boxes of berries.
- **Wider scenes (21):** a viewpoint over Alfama, rabelo boats in Porto, a tiled street corner, a square of orange trees, two Yanaka street scenes, cyclists at a Copenhagen crossing, the Long Bien bridge, a lake house from a train, a railway station, Plateau staircases, Victoria Street, the Philosopher's Path, a funicular track, the bay from Cerro Concepción, the Old Market Hall, the Market Square, Wat Arun, an express boat, a Brown Line train over a river, an elevated platform.

Every region has both kinds. Northern Europe has the fewest close details, one (the salmon soup), because its posts are about trains and festivals; a later round could add a herring or a bike detail.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture, post and cards | 3:2 | 2000 px wide, one at 1600 px | Every lead is landscape at 3:2 or 4:3, so a 3:2 crop loses little. The template crops cards to its own ratio from the same file. |
| In-body figure | 3:2 | 2000 px wide | Shown at the text width, about 42rem, with a caption. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink. No photographs of people. |

Of the 17 leads, 11 are 3:2, 5 are 4:3 (Lisbon, the Stockholm Oslo train, Montreal, Valencia and Helsinki) and one, the Chicago river crossing, is slightly wider at 1.55:1. A 4:3 source loses about a tenth at the top and bottom in a 3:2 crop. The lake house lead is 1600 px wide, the source's full size, which is still enough for a lead at the measure but soft on a wide hero.

## Phone crop

On a phone, the lead picture fills the width and is cropped from the centre. Each lead was checked for a centred subject:

- Single subjects sit in the middle third and survive a tight crop: the Old Market Hall façade, the Yanaka corner house, the rabelo boats, Wat Arun's spire, the bento box and the cups of fruit.
- Repeating textures look the same at any crop: the artichokes, the Alfama roofs and the orange trees.
- Leading lines run through the centre: the funicular track, the Philosopher's Path, Victoria Street's curve and the Long Bien bridge.
- Two leads to watch in the picture review. The Plateau staircases sit on the right third, so a tight centre crop keeps the stairs but loses the street. The Brown Line train is small in the middle of a wide river scene, so on a phone it stays in frame but reads small.

No photograph carries text that a reader could read, so no overlay contrast check is needed. If a later design sets the masthead or a title over a lead picture, the darker leads (the Brown Line river crossing and the Philosopher's Path) need a scrim to pass AA.

## Review notes

These pictures passed but were close calls, for the founder to look at in the picture review:

- **Cyclists in Copenhagen (lead):** the buses behind the riders carry the operator's small name on their sides, too small to read at card size.
- **Express boat in Bangkok (figure):** the boat's name is painted on its hull in Thai, small and partly hidden by spray.
- **The Arvika station (figure):** the town's name is on the gable, as it is on any station.
- **The Plateau staircases (lead):** a resident climbs one staircase, seen from the side and small in the frame.
- **The garlic stall in Porto (figure):** two traders stand behind the stall, out of focus; the garlic is the subject.
