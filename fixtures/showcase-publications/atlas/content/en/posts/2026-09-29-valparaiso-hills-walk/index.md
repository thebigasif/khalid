The easiest way to walk the Valparaíso hills is to ride a funicular up and walk down. Take the Concepción funicular up from the lower town, spend the day on the terraces and lanes of Cerro Concepción and Cerro Alegre, then follow the stairways back down to the port.

Valparaíso is a port city built on a narrow strip of flat land and more than forty steep hills, called cerros. Its houses climb them in every colour, linked by stairways, alleys and old funiculars, called ascensores. I grew up partly here, and I still think it is the most rewarding city in South America to walk, as long as you let the funiculars do the climbing.

## Why Valparaíso is built this way

In the late 1800s, Valparaíso was the main port on the Pacific coast of South America, and traders from Britain, Germany and Italy built houses on the hills above it. To reach them, the city built dozens of funiculars, short railways that haul a car up a slope on a cable. Its [historic quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Quarter_of_the_Seaport_City_of_Valpara%C3%ADso) is now a World Heritage site, and several of the funiculars have been restored and still run.

## The route

1. **Ride the Concepción funicular** up from the lower town. It is one of the oldest in the city, and the ride takes about a minute.
2. **Walk the terraces of Cerro Concepción.** The walkways along the edge of the hill look straight down over the port and the bay.
3. **Cross to Cerro Alegre.** The two hills run into each other, and Cerro Alegre has the best of the painted houses and murals.
4. **Visit the fine arts museum** in a grand old mansion on the terrace above the bay, if it is open.
5. **Walk down a stairway** to the lower town, then on to Plaza Sotomayor and the port.

## What to look for on the Valparaíso hills

- **The houses.** Many are clad in painted corrugated iron, which was cheap, light and fire resistant. Much of it is said to have come in on the ships as ballast.
- **The murals.** Whole walls and stairways are painted, some by well-known artists, many by neighbours.
- **The stairways.** Some are painted too, with steps in every colour.
- **The view.** At every corner, a gap between the houses opens onto the bay.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Pay for the funiculars with coins. Several still take cash at a small ticket window, and change can run short in the busy afternoons.
:::

## Getting between the Valparaíso hills

The funiculars only go up and down one slope each, so to move between hills you walk, or come down to the lower town and go up another. The city's old trolleybuses run along the flat streets of the lower town, and local buses and shared taxis climb the hill roads. For a day on Cerro Concepción and Cerro Alegre, you will not need any of them.

## Staying safe

The busiest Valparaíso hills, Concepción and Alegre, are lively by day. As in any port city, keep your phone and camera close in crowds, and do not leave bags on café chairs. After dark, stay on the busy streets of Cerro Concepción and Cerro Alegre and take a taxi to other parts of the city, especially the upper hills.

## When to go

Valparaíso has a mild climate all year. Summer, from December to March, is warm and dry, and busy with visitors from Santiago at weekends. Mornings in any season can start with a sea fog that burns off by noon, so start your walk a little later than you would elsewhere. New Year's Eve brings one of the largest firework displays on the coast, and the hills fill with people watching it.

## Getting there

Valparaíso is about 120 km from Santiago. Buses leave Santiago's main bus stations often throughout the day, and the trip takes about an hour and a half to two hours, depending on traffic.
