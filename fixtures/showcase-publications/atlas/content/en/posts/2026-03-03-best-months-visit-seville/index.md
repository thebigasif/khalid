The best time to visit Seville is from late February to early May, or from late October to November. Those months are warm enough to sit outside but cool enough to walk all day, while July and August are so hot that the city empties in the afternoon.

Seville is one of the hottest large cities in Western Europe in summer, and its calendar is built around that. Choosing when to visit Seville is mostly a choice between spring, with its blossom, festivals and higher prices, and autumn, which is quieter and cheaper. This guide covers each month in turn.

::toc

## Seville month by month at a glance

The temperatures are typical afternoon highs, rounded, from long-term averages. Crowds and prices come from our own visits in four different seasons.

| Month | Typical afternoon | Crowds | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | about 16 °C | Low | Cool, often sunny, the quietest month |
| February | about 18 °C | Low | Oranges on the trees, mild days |
| March | about 22 °C | Medium | Orange blossom, and often Holy Week |
| April | about 24 °C | Very high | Holy Week or the April Fair, high prices |
| May | about 28 °C | High | Warm, bright, patios in flower |
| June | about 33 °C | Medium | Hot afternoons, long evenings |
| July | about 36 °C | Low | Very hot, many locals away |
| August | about 36 °C | Low | The hottest month, some places shut |
| September | about 32 °C | Medium | Still hot at first, easing by the end |
| October | about 26 °C | Medium | Warm and settled, a good walking month |
| November | about 20 °C | Low | Mild, some rain, few visitors |
| December | about 17 °C | Medium | Cool and bright, busy around the holidays |

## Spring: blossom and festivals

From early March, the orange trees that line Seville's streets come into flower, and for a few weeks the whole centre smells of blossom. It is the city at its best and its busiest.

Two festivals set the spring calendar. [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Seville), the week before Easter, fills the streets with processions day and night, and its date moves each year with Easter. The April Fair follows about two weeks later, with a week of dancing, horses and private tents on a fairground across the river. Both are worth seeing once, and both push room prices to their highest of the year, often two or three times the winter rate.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If you want the blossom without the festival prices, look at the weeks just before Holy Week. Check the date of Easter first, as it changes the whole spring calendar.
:::

## Summer: the hot months

In July and August, afternoon temperatures often pass 40 °C. The city adapts: shops close for the afternoon, people stay indoors, and life moves to the late evening. If summer is the only time you can visit Seville, plan your walking for the first hours after dawn and after about 8pm, and spend the middle of the day in museums or your room.

- **Choose a room with air conditioning.** In August it is not a luxury.
- **Carry water** and refill it at fountains and cafés.
- **Expect some closures.** A few family restaurants close for part of August.

## Autumn: the quiet season

By the middle of October, the heat has broken and the crowds have thinned. The light is warm and low, the evenings are long enough to eat outside, and rooms cost much less than in spring. It is our favourite time for walking the city, and it is when we walked the Triana side of the river for this guide.

A tile panel showing the Triana bridge. The old potteries of Triana still make tiles, and their shop fronts are worth the walk across the river.

## Winter: cool, bright and cheap

Winter in Seville is mild by northern standards. Days are often sunny and around 16 °C, nights are cold, and many old buildings have little heating, so pack a warm layer for the evening. In January and February, the oranges hang ripe on the street trees. They are bitter and are picked for marmalade, not for eating.

## Our pick: the best time to visit Seville

If you can choose any time, visit Seville in the second half of October or in early March, outside Holy Week. You get warm days for walking, cool nights for sleeping and fair prices. Stay in or near the old centre, and spend a day crossing the river to Triana.
