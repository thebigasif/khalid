For a first visit, the best Tokyo neighbourhoods to stay in are near a station on the Yamanote line, the loop line that links most of the central districts. Shinjuku and Shibuya suit people who want noise and nightlife, Ueno and Asakusa suit people who want older streets and lower prices, and the area around Tokyo Station suits short stays with early trains.

Tokyo is not one centre but dozens, each built around a station. Where you stay decides how your days feel more than any other choice, because you start and end every day there. This guide compares six Tokyo neighbourhoods that make good bases.

::toc

## Six Tokyo neighbourhoods compared

We stayed in each of these areas over several trips. The prices are rough comparisons for a small double room in a mid-range hotel, not quotes.

| Area | Best for | Getting around | Noise at night | Room prices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shinjuku | Nightlife, food, day trips west | Yamanote line, many other lines | High near the station | High |
| Shibuya | Shopping, cafés, young crowds | Yamanote line, several metro lines | High | High |
| Tokyo Station and Ginza | Short stays, bullet trains | Yamanote line, the bullet trains | Low | High |
| Ueno | Museums, the park, value | Yamanote line, airport train | Medium | Medium |
| Asakusa | Temples, older streets | Metro lines, no Yamanote stop | Low | Medium |
| Yanaka and Nezu | Quiet streets, small inns | Metro, a walk to the Yamanote line | Very low | Medium |

## The Yamanote line, and why it matters

The [Yamanote line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamanote_Line) runs in a loop around central Tokyo and stops at most of the districts a first visit needs. Trains come every few minutes for most of the day. If your hotel is a short walk from one of its stations, you can reach almost anywhere in the centre with one change at most.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Buy a rechargeable transit card at the airport or any large station. It works on the trains, the metro and the buses, and in many shops, and it saves you working out each fare.
:::

## Shinjuku and Shibuya: the busy west

Shinjuku has the busiest station in the world, the tall towers of the west side and a maze of tiny bars to the east. Staying here means you are never short of a meal at midnight, and trains west to the mountains leave from the same station. Shibuya, two stops south, is younger and more about shopping and cafés. Both are loud and bright, and both have the highest room prices on this list.

Pick a hotel a few minutes from the station, not next to it. The difference in noise is large.

## Tokyo Station and Ginza: the practical choice

If you are in Tokyo for two or three nights before taking the bullet train, stay near Tokyo Station. It is calm at night, Ginza is a short walk away, and the trains to Kyoto leave from the platforms below you. It is less fun in the evening than Shinjuku, but it makes early starts easy.

## Ueno and Asakusa: the older east

Ueno has a large park with several of the city's best museums, a busy street market under the railway and a direct train to Narita airport. Rooms here cost less than in the west. Asakusa, a short metro ride away, is built around the Sensō-ji temple and has the feel of an older Tokyo, with low houses and small family restaurants. It has no Yamanote station, so trips west take a little longer.

## Yanaka and Nezu: the quiet choice

Yanaka is one of the few corners of Tokyo that were not destroyed in the 1923 earthquake or the bombing of the Second World War, so its lanes still hold wooden houses, small temples and old shops. Neighbouring Nezu has a lovely shrine and a few small inns. It is the quietest of the Tokyo neighbourhoods on this list and the one we go back to.

## Choosing between Tokyo neighbourhoods

- **Three nights or fewer:** stay near Tokyo Station or Shinjuku.
- **A week, first time:** stay in Ueno or Shinjuku, and spend a day walking Yanaka.
- **A slower trip:** stay in Yanaka or Asakusa and accept a slightly longer ride west.
- **Travelling with a large suitcase:** choose a hotel with a lift and a short, flat walk from a station. Many small stations have long stairs.
