Yes, the Stockholm Oslo train is worth it if you have a day to spare. The direct trains take about six hours, centre to centre, and the line runs through some of the loveliest lake and forest country in Scandinavia, while a flight saves less time than you would think once you add the airports.

We took the train in both directions in early June, when the evenings are long and the lakes are at their brightest. This guide covers the route, how it compares with flying, and how to spend the day on board.

::toc

## The route of the Stockholm Oslo train

The line leaves Stockholm heading west, past Lake Mälaren, then crosses the wide farmland of central Sweden. After Karlstad, on the shore of Lake Vänern, it runs through [Värmland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4rmland), a county of lakes, red farmhouses and pine forest. It crosses into Norway near Charlottenberg, then follows a river valley down past Kongsvinger to Oslo.

:::figure{src="arvika-station.jpg" alt="The red brick railway station at Arvika, a stop on the Stockholm Oslo train, a long two-storey building with a clock in its central gable, seen across a wide paved square under a clear summer sky"}
Arvika, the last large town on the Swedish side. Most direct trains stop here for a minute or two, and the lakes start soon after.
:::

## Train or plane: door to door

The times below are our own, from the centre of Stockholm to the centre of Oslo, on a weekday in June 2026. Your times will differ, but the shape will not.

| Step | By train | By plane |
|---|---|---|
| Getting to the station or airport | about 15 minutes | about 45 minutes |
| Waiting before departure | about 10 minutes | about 1 hour 15 minutes |
| The journey itself | about 6 hours | about 1 hour |
| Getting into the centre | none | about 30 minutes |
| **Door to door** | **about 6 hours 25 minutes** | **about 3 hours 30 minutes** |

The plane is faster by about three hours. But on the train, nearly all of those six hours are usable: you can work, read, eat and watch the view, and nobody asks you to put your laptop away.

## When the Stockholm Oslo train runs

The direct service runs a few times a day, but it has been interrupted for long periods in recent years by track work on both sides of the border. When the direct trains stop, the trip usually means a change at Karlstad and a bus for part of the way. Check the timetable for your dates before you plan around it.

:::callout{kind=note}
Book early for the cheapest seats. The fares on this line rise as the train fills, and the summer Fridays sell out first. When we went, a seat booked six weeks ahead cost about a third of the walk-up fare.
:::

## How to spend six hours on the train

- **Book a window seat.** The lakes come on both sides of the line, so either side will do.
- **Bring food.** The trains we took had a small café, but its choice ran low by the afternoon.
- **Bring a layer.** Scandinavian trains are well heated in winter and well cooled in summer.
- **Watch for the border.** There is no passport check on the train most of the time, only a sign by the track.
- **Use the stops.** At the longer stops, you have time to step onto the platform for some air.

## When to go

June and July are the best months for the view, with light until late in the evening. September brings autumn colour to the birches. In winter, the forest is white and the days are short, so you will see more of it on a morning train than an afternoon one.

## Is it worth it?

Yes, if you have a free day. If your only goal is to get to Oslo, fly. If you want to spend the day watching lakes and forest instead of airport corridors, take the Stockholm Oslo train. It also emits far less than the flight, which is the reason many of our readers give for choosing it.
