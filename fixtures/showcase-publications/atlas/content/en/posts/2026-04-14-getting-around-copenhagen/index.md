Getting around Copenhagen without a car is easy: rent a bike for the flat centre, use the driverless metro for longer trips, and take the S-trains to the suburbs and beaches. One ticket system covers the metro, the trains, the buses and the harbour buses, so you can mix them in a single journey.

Copenhagen is small, flat and built for bicycles, and most visitors never need more than a bike and their feet. When the weather turns, the public transport is good enough that you will not miss a car at all.

## Getting around Copenhagen at a glance

| How | Best for | Runs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bicycle | The centre and nearby districts | Any time | Wide bike lanes on most main roads |
| Metro | The airport, the centre, the outer districts | Day and night | Driverless, trains every few minutes |
| S-train | Suburbs, beaches, day trips | From early morning to after midnight | Bikes ride free on board |
| Harbour bus | Crossing and following the harbour | Daytime | Part of the same ticket system |

## Bicycles: the main way to get around

Copenhagen has some of the best [cycling infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Copenhagen) of any capital. Most main roads have a wide bike lane, separated from the cars by a kerb, with its own traffic lights. The city's bike share and many small rental shops make it easy to join in for a day or a week.

Riding here is safe and calm, but it has rules, and local riders expect you to follow them.

- **Keep right** and let faster riders pass on your left.
- **Raise a hand** before you stop, and point before you turn.
- **Turn left in two stages.** Cross the junction on the right, stop, turn your bike, then cross again with the lights.
- **Use lights after dark.** It is the law, and the police do check.
- **Do not ride on the pavement.** People walking have it to themselves.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Avoid the main bike lanes in the morning and evening rush, from about 7.30 to 9 and from 4 to 6. They fill with commuters riding fast, and a slow visitor holding a map is in the way.
:::

## The metro: fast, driverless and open all night

The metro has no drivers, and its trains run day and night, every few minutes at busy times. The line from the airport reaches the city centre in about a quarter of an hour, which makes it the simplest way in when you land. The circle line opened in 2019 and links the central station, the old town and the districts around the lakes.

## S-trains: out to the suburbs and the sea

The S-trains are the suburban railway. They run on several lines out from the central station to the edges of the city and beyond, and they are the way to reach the beaches to the north and the parks to the west. You can take a bike on board for free, which makes a day out by bike and train easy to plan.

## Harbour buses: the slow, scenic way

Small yellow harbour buses run up and down the inner harbour, stopping at both banks. They are part of the normal ticket system, so they cost no more than a bus. A ride from one end of their route to the other is one of the cheapest ways to see the harbour from the water.

## Tickets: one system for everything

One ticket system covers the metro, the S-trains, the buses and the harbour buses in Greater Copenhagen. You can buy a single ticket for one trip, buy tickets in the official app, or buy a pass for a fixed number of days, which covers the airport too. For a short stay with lots of travel, a pass is simplest. For a stay where you mostly ride a bike, single tickets usually cost less.

Fares are set by zones, and the centre and the airport are close enough that most visitors stay within a few zones. Check the zone count before you buy a single ticket for a trip out of town.

## Getting around Copenhagen on foot

Do not forget your feet. The old town is small, many streets are closed to cars, and a walk along the harbour from the old town to the modern districts in the south is one of the best ways to spend a light evening in summer.
