You can see a lot of the city from the Chicago L train in one day with a single day pass: ride the elevated tracks around the Loop, take the Brown Line north through the old neighbourhoods, ride the Pink Line to Pilsen for lunch, and end the day in Logan Square. Each ride is short, and the trains show you the city from above its streets.

The L, short for "elevated", is Chicago's rapid transit railway. Much of it runs on iron structures above the streets, some more than a century old, and riding it is one of the cheapest ways to see the city. Here is the day we planned and rode in October 2026.

## The day at a glance

| Time | Line | From and to | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9am | Brown | Around the Loop | Ride the elevated ring above downtown |
| 10am | Brown | North to the far end of the line | Watch the neighbourhoods from the tracks |
| Noon | Brown, then Pink | Back to the Loop, out to 18th Street | Lunch and murals in Pilsen |
| 3pm | Pink, then Blue | Back through the Loop to Logan Square | The boulevards and the square |
| 7pm | Blue | Logan Square | Dinner, then the train back |

## How the Chicago L train works

The [Chicago L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_%22L%22) has eight lines, each named for a colour. Several of them meet on the Loop, an elevated ring of track around the downtown business district, which gives the area its name. Two lines, the Red and the Blue, run all night. The Blue Line also runs to O'Hare airport.

Pay with a contactless bank card or phone at the gates, or buy a transit card or a day pass from the machines in the stations. A day pass pays for itself after a few rides, so it suits a day like this one.

:::callout{kind=tip}
On the Brown Line, ride in the first car and stand by the front window. The view down the tracks, between the buildings of the Loop and over the rooftops to the north, is the best free ride in the city.
:::

## Morning: around the Loop and up the Brown Line

Board the Brown Line in the Loop and ride it once around the ring. The train runs one storey above the street, so close to the office windows that you can see the desks. Then stay on as it heads north.

North of downtown, the Brown Line runs past the backs of brick apartment buildings, wooden porches and small gardens, through Lincoln Park, Lakeview and Ravenswood. Near the far end of the line, it comes down to ground level and crosses the North Branch of the Chicago River, as in our lead photograph.

:::figure{src="platform.jpg" alt="A silver Chicago L train standing at an elevated Brown Line platform at Irving Park, with a strip of bright blue paint along the edge of the wooden platform and trees beyond"}
Irving Park, on the Brown Line. Many stations on the line were rebuilt in the 2000s, but the platforms still stand high above the streets.
:::

## Lunch: Pilsen

Ride back to the Loop and change to the Pink Line, which runs south-west to Pilsen. Step off at 18th Street. The station walls are covered in murals, and the neighbourhood around it has been the heart of the city's Mexican community for decades. Walk 18th Street for lunch at a taquería or a bakery, then look for the large murals on the side streets.

## Afternoon and evening: Logan Square

Ride the Pink Line back towards the Loop and change to the Blue Line, which runs north-west, partly underground and partly on elevated track. Step off at Logan Square. The neighbourhood is built along wide, tree-lined boulevards, part of a ring of parks and boulevards laid out in the 1800s. The square itself has a tall column at its centre, and the streets around it are full of places to eat and drink.

The Blue Line runs all night, so you can stay as late as you like and still take the Chicago L train back.

## Riding the Chicago L train well

- **Let people off first,** then board quickly. The doors do not stay open long.
- **Stand on the right** on escalators and stairs.
- **Check the last train.** Most lines stop for a few hours in the middle of the night; only the Red and the Blue run all night.
- **Dress for the platforms.** Elevated platforms are cold and windy from November to March, with little shelter.
