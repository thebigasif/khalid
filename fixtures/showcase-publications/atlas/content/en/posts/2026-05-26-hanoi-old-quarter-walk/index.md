The best way to see the Hanoi Old Quarter is on foot, early in the morning, starting at Hoan Kiem Lake and ending on the Long Bien bridge. The walk is about 4 km, takes three hours with stops, and passes the old trade streets, a covered market and two breakfast counters.

The Old Quarter is a grid of narrow streets north of the lake, crowded with shops, homes and motorbikes. Many streets are still named for what they once sold: Hang Bac for silver, Hang Gai for silk, Hang Ma for paper goods. Most of the year, the mornings are cooler and quieter, and they are when the street kitchens are at their best.

## The Hanoi Old Quarter route at a glance

1. **Hoan Kiem Lake, 6am.** Start on the east shore, where people come to exercise before work.
2. **Breakfast in the streets north of the lake.** A bowl of pho at a pavement counter.
3. **The trade streets.** Walk north through Hang Bac, Hang Gai and Hang Ma.
4. **Dong Xuan market.** The big covered market at the north edge of the quarter.
5. **The Long Bien bridge.** Walk out over the Red River and back.

## Start at Hoan Kiem Lake

[Hoan Kiem Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%C3%A0n_Ki%E1%BA%BFm_Lake) is the centre of old Hanoi. Arrive around 6am and you will share the shore with people doing tai chi, dancing to music from a speaker and walking fast in pairs. Walk to the red wooden bridge that crosses to the small temple on an island at the north end, then head north into the Old Quarter.

## Breakfast: pho on the pavement

Hanoi eats pho for breakfast. Look for a counter with a big pot of broth, a pile of rice noodles and a row of low plastic stools on the pavement, and sit wherever there is space. Point at what the person beside you has if you do not know what to order.

:::figure{src="pho.jpg" alt="A white bowl of beef pho with flat rice noodles and spring onions, with chopsticks lifting the noodles, a plate of cut limes and a small bowl of sliced red chillies beside it, the classic breakfast before a walk in the Hanoi Old Quarter"}
Beef pho with lime and chilli on the side. Squeeze in the lime, add chilli to taste, and eat it while it is too hot.
:::

## Walk the trade streets of the Hanoi Old Quarter

From the lake, walk north into the grid. Many houses here are tube houses: narrow at the front, very deep, with a shop on the street and the family home behind it. Look up above the shop signs to see the old façades, balconies and shutters.

- **Hang Bac** still has silversmiths and jewellers.
- **Hang Gai** sells silk, embroidery and tailoring.
- **Hang Ma** sells paper offerings, lanterns and decorations, and is brightest before the festivals.
- **Ta Hien and the streets around it** fill with bars at night but are calm in the morning.

:::callout{kind=tip}
To cross a busy street in Hanoi, walk at a slow, steady pace and do not stop or run. The motorbikes steer around a walker they can predict. Cross with a local when you can.
:::

## Dong Xuan market

At the north edge of the Hanoi Old Quarter stands Dong Xuan, the largest covered market in the old city. Inside, it is mostly wholesale cloth and household goods, but the streets around it are full of food: fruit, herbs, dried goods and small kitchens. It is a good place for a second breakfast or a coffee.

## Out over the Long Bien bridge

From the market, walk east to the Long Bien bridge, the old iron bridge over the Red River, opened in the early 1900s. Trains still use the middle of the bridge, and the narrow lanes on each side are for motorbikes, bicycles and people on foot. Walk out as far as you like for the view over the river and the banana fields on the islands below, then walk back the way you came.

## When to walk

Hanoi has a cool, often grey winter from December to February and a hot, wet summer from May to September. The best months for walking are October and November, and March and April. In summer, finish the walk by 9am, before the heat.

On Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings, the streets around the lake and some of the Hanoi Old Quarter close to traffic and fill with families walking. If you are in Hanoi at the weekend, come back after dark for a second, very different walk.
