Bangkok river boats are the cheapest and most pleasant way to see the old city. Take the skytrain to the Saphan Taksin station, walk down to the Sathorn pier, and ride the public express boat north up the Chao Phraya river, stepping off at the piers for the Grand Palace, Wat Pho and Wat Arun.

Bangkok's roads are famous for their traffic, but its river runs freely through the middle of the old city. The public Bangkok river boats cost a fraction of a taxi, run every few minutes in the day and show you the city from the water, where it began.

## How Bangkok river boats work

The [Chao Phraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya_River) is served by several kinds of public boat, and they all use the same piers. Each pier has a number and a name, shown on a sign on the pier.

- **Express boats** run up and down the river, stopping at some or all of the piers. Each line flies a coloured flag at the back of the boat.
- **The tourist boat** runs on the main stretch of the river and stops at the piers for the big sights. It costs more than the express boats and comes with a commentary.
- **Cross-river ferries** shuttle from one bank to the other, often from the same piers. They are the way to reach Wat Arun.

## Which express boat to take

The lines and their colours change from time to time, so check the board on the pier. When we rode them in October 2026, the pattern was the same as for many years.

| Boat | Flag | Stops | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local boat | None | Every pier | Short hops, quiet times |
| Main express | Orange | Most main piers | The temples and the old city |
| Faster express | Yellow or green and yellow | Fewer piers | Longer trips, the busy hours |
| Tourist boat | Blue | The main sights | A first ride with commentary |

## A half day by river boat

1. **Start at Sathorn pier,** below the Saphan Taksin skytrain station. It is the easiest place to change from the skytrain to the river.
2. **Ride the orange flag boat north.** The palaces and temples of the old city line the right bank as you head upriver.
3. **Step off at the pier for Wat Pho,** the temple of the reclining Buddha.
4. **Take the cross-river ferry** from the same area to Wat Arun on the far bank. The ride lasts a few minutes.
5. **Ferry back, then walk** to the Grand Palace, or ride one more stop north to the pier closest to it.
6. **Ride on north** if you have time, to the riverside markets and the old neighbourhood of Banglamphu.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Pay on board or at the pier window with small notes and coins. The fares are low, and the conductors on the express boats rarely have change for a large note.
:::

## When to ride Bangkok river boats

The express boats run from early morning to the early evening, but not late at night, so plan your return before dark or by skytrain. The river is busiest in the morning and evening rush, when the boats fill with people going to work. The hours between about 9am and 3pm are calmer.

The best light is late in the afternoon, when the sun drops behind Wat Arun on the west bank. If you can, stay on the river side for sunset, then come back by ferry.

## Staying safe on the boats

- **Hold on.** The express boats move fast and the river is choppy.
- **Keep your bag in front of you** on a crowded boat.
- **Avoid the river in a storm.** In the rainy season, from about May to October, afternoon storms can stop the boats for a while.
- **Be wary of touts** at the piers selling private long-tail boat tours at high prices. The public boats go to the same places.
