Porto prices for a week came to about €640 for one of us (January 2026), not counting the flight. That covered seven nights in a small private room, three meals a day, local transport and a handful of paid sights, and we could have spent less without missing much.

Porto prices are still good value for Western Europe, but the city is not as cheap as it was ten years ago. Rooms in the old centre now cost about what they do in many larger cities, while food and transport are still kind to a small budget. This post sets out what we paid, line by line, so you can plan your own week.

::toc

## How we costed the week

Rosa travelled alone for seven nights in the middle of January, which is low season in Porto. She kept every receipt and noted every card payment, then rounded each figure to the nearest euro. Porto prices change, and summer rooms cost more, so treat each figure as a guide to the size of each cost, not a quote.

:::callout{kind=note}
Every one of these Porto prices is "about", paid in January 2026. In July and August, expect the room to cost about half as much again. Food and transport change much less with the seasons.
:::

## Porto prices for a week: the full costs

| What | How we did it | About |
|---|---|---|
| Room, 7 nights | A small private room with its own bathroom, a short walk from the cathedral | €350 |
| Breakfast, 7 days | Coffee and a pastry at a neighbourhood café | €25 |
| Lunch, 7 days | The fixed lunch menu at small restaurants, with soup, a main dish and a drink | €85 |
| Dinner, 7 days | Two dinners out, five picnic suppers from the market | €70 |
| Transport | A rechargeable card for the metro and buses, plus the airport train both ways | €30 |
| Paid sights | A cellar tour in Gaia, a museum, a tower climb and a river cruise | €55 |

| **Total** | | **about €640** |

The room was more than half of the money. Everything else came to about €290 for the week in January 2026, or about €41 a day.

## Where to save, and where not to

### Eat a big lunch, not a big dinner

Many small restaurants in Porto serve a fixed lunch menu on weekdays: a soup, a main dish, bread and a drink for one price. It was the best value food we found, and it filled us up until evening. Dinner at the same places costs more and comes with no set menu.

### Shop at the Bolhão market

The Bolhão market, in the centre of the city, reopened in 2022 after a long restoration. It sells fruit, vegetables, cheese, bread, fish and cured meat under one roof, and a picnic supper from its stalls cost us about €8 to €10 a night (January 2026).

:::figure{src="garlic-stall.jpg" alt="A heap of white garlic bulbs, with strings of dried stalks, piled on a steel market counter beside a weighing scale at the Bolhão market, a good place to check Porto prices for fruit and vegetables"}
A garlic stall at the Bolhão market. Most stalls sell by weight, and the traders will happily sell you one bulb or one tomato.
:::

### Walk, and use the card for the hills

The old centre is small enough to walk in a day, but it is steep. A rechargeable transport card covers the metro and the buses, and one ride up a hill saved our legs more than once. Walk down to the river, ride back up.

### Pay for one cellar tour, not three

The [port wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_wine) cellars sit across the river in Vila Nova de Gaia. A tour with a tasting is worth doing once. After that, the riverside bars sell a glass for much less, and you can sit outside and watch the boats.

## What a week in Porto looks like, day by day

- **Day 1:** arrive, walk the old centre and the cathedral hill.
- **Day 2:** the Ribeira district and a walk across the upper deck of the Dom Luís I bridge to Gaia.
- **Day 3:** a cellar tour, then the riverside walk west towards the ocean.
- **Day 4:** the Bolhão market in the morning, a museum in the afternoon.
- **Day 5:** a day trip by train up the Douro valley, if the budget allows.
- **Day 6:** Foz, where the river meets the Atlantic, by bus or tram.
- **Day 7:** a last market breakfast and the train to the airport.

:::figure{src="tiled-street.jpg" alt="A corner house in Porto with a façade of blue and white tiles and a wrought iron balcony, beside a quiet street of tall stone buildings in morning light"}
Tiled house fronts are everywhere in Porto, and looking at them costs nothing. The best are on the side streets above the river.
:::

## Is a week in Porto too long?

For most people, a week in Porto is about right if you add a day out of the city. Five days covers the centre and Gaia at an easy pace. With seven, you have time for the Douro valley or the beaches at Foz, and time to sit at a café without checking the clock.
