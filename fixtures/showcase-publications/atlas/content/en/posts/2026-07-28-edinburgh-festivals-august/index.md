The Edinburgh festivals in August are worth it if you book your room months ahead, plan only one or two shows a day and give yourself time away from the Old Town. Several festivals run at once that month, and the streets, theatres and trains are fuller than at any other time of year.

For most of the year, Edinburgh is a compact, walkable city of stone. For three or four weeks in August, it becomes the largest arts festival in the world, with thousands of shows in hundreds of venues. That is thrilling and exhausting in roughly equal parts.

::toc

## The Edinburgh festivals in brief

- **The [Edinburgh Festival Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival_Fringe)** is the big one: an open festival where anyone can put on a show, from comedy to theatre to dance, in rooms of every size.
- **The International Festival** brings invited orchestras, opera and theatre companies to the larger halls.
- **The military tattoo** fills the esplanade in front of the castle every evening with pipes, drums and lights.
- **The book festival** holds talks and readings by writers from around the world.

The Edinburgh festivals overlap for most of the month, so whatever week you choose, all of them are likely to be running.

## Book early, and then book again

Rooms are the hardest part. Many open for booking a year ahead, and by spring the central ones are scarce and costly. When we went in August 2025, a room that cost about £90 a night in May cost about three times as much during the festival.

:::callout{kind=tip}
If central rooms are gone or too costly, look along the train lines. Towns a short train ride away have rooms at normal prices, and the trains run late during the festival. Check the time of the last train before you book a late show.
:::

## Choosing shows without losing your mind

The Fringe programme is the size of a phone book. Do not try to read it all.

1. **Book one or two shows a day,** and leave the rest of the day free.
2. **Mix big and small.** One booked show at a known venue, one gamble on something tiny.
3. **Try the free shows.** Many Fringe shows are free to enter, with a bucket at the end. Pay what you think the show was worth.
4. **Ask people in the queue** what they saw yesterday. It is the best review service in the city.
5. **Do not fill the evenings.** A late dinner after one great show is better than three shows and no dinner.

## Where to escape the crowds

The Royal Mile and the streets around it are the busiest part of the city in August, packed with performers handing out flyers. When it gets too much, these places are a short walk or bus ride away and far calmer.

- **Arthur's Seat:** the old volcano in the park beside the palace. The climb takes under an hour and the view takes in the whole city.
- **Dean Village:** a quiet old mill village on the river, ten minutes from the west end.
- **The Water of Leith walkway:** a path along the river from Dean Village down to Leith.
- **Leith:** the old port, with good places to eat and few festival crowds.
- **Portobello:** the city's beach, a bus ride east, for a swim if you are brave.

## Getting around in August

Walk. The centre is small, and in August the buses are slow in the traffic and the taxis are hard to find. Good shoes matter more than usual: the Old Town is steep, and many of its streets are cobbled. Victoria Street, in our lead photograph, is one of the prettiest and one of the steepest.

## What to pack

August in Edinburgh is mild, with afternoons usually somewhere around 18 °C, and it rains often. Bring a light waterproof jacket, a warm layer for the evening and shoes that can stand up to wet stone. Sunglasses and an umbrella in the same bag is normal here.

## Is it worth it?

Yes, if you love theatre, comedy or music and can plan ahead: the Edinburgh festivals have no equal. If you want to see the castle, the museums and the closes of the Old Town in peace, come in May, June or September instead, when the city is lighter, cheaper and just as beautiful.
