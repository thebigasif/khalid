Helsinki markets come in two kinds: three historic indoor halls and an open-air market on the harbour. You can visit them all in a day on foot. Start at the Market Square and the Old Market Hall beside it for fish and salmon soup, then walk north to Hakaniemi for everyday shopping and west to Hietalahti for lunch and the flea market.

Helsinki markets are where the city eats lunch. They are calm, clean and friendly, and they show the seasons in a way that a supermarket never does: berries in July, mushrooms in August and September, herring in October and smoked fish all year.

::toc

## Helsinki markets at a glance

| Market | Where | Opened | Go for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market Square | The South Harbour | An open market for centuries | Fresh produce in summer, coffee, the ferries |
| Old Market Hall | Beside the Market Square | 1889 | Fish, smoked salmon, salmon soup |
| Hakaniemi hall | Hakaniemi, north of the centre | 1914 | Everyday food shopping, local life |
| Hietalahti hall | Hietalahti, west of the centre | 1903 | Lunch counters, the flea market outside |

## The Market Square

The [Market Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Square,_Helsinki) sits on the South Harbour, with the presidential palace and the city hall on one side and the ferries on the other. It is open all year, with the most stalls from spring to autumn. In summer, the stalls sell new potatoes, peas in the pod, strawberries and blueberries, and the orange tents serve coffee and fried fish.

The ferries to the island fortress of Suomenlinna leave from the square's eastern edge. They are part of the city's public transport, so a normal ticket covers the trip.

## The Old Market Hall

Next to the square stands the old hall, a long brick building from 1889, restored in the 2010s. Inside, a single aisle runs between small wooden stalls selling smoked and salted fish, cheese, bread, reindeer and game, and coffee.

Come here for salmon soup, the dish to try in Helsinki: salmon, potato, leek and dill in a creamy broth, served with dark rye bread and butter. Several stalls serve it at lunch.

:::figure{src="salmon-soup.jpg" alt="A white bowl of creamy Finnish salmon soup with chunks of salmon, potato and carrot, a slice of buttered bread resting on the rim, on a wooden table"}
Salmon soup with rye bread. It is a full lunch on a cold day, and the price includes bread at most counters.
:::

## Hakaniemi

North of the centre, across the bridge, the Hakaniemi hall is the one local people use for their weekly shopping. It reopened in 2023 after a long renovation, with two floors of stalls: food downstairs and crafts and textiles upstairs. On the square outside, an open market runs most days. It feels far less like a sight and far more like a neighbourhood than the halls by the harbour.

## Hietalahti

West of the centre, the Hietalahti hall is smaller and full of lunch counters, with food from around the world. Outside, in the warmer months, the square holds one of the city's best flea markets. Come for lunch, then walk it off down to the sea.

## The October herring market

Every year in early October, fishing boats from the archipelago tie up at the Market Square for the Baltic herring market, a tradition that goes back to the 1700s. They sell herring in every form: pickled in jars with mustard, dill or onion, smoked, fried and fresh. Many families buy a year's supply. If you are in Helsinki then, buy a jar and some dark rye bread for a picnic.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Plan your day around lunch. Many stalls in Helsinki markets close early in the evening, and some close on Sundays, so the markets are a daytime pleasure. Check the opening hours before you go.
:::

## A day around the Helsinki markets

1. **Morning:** the Market Square and the old hall beside it, with coffee.
2. **Late morning:** the ferry to Suomenlinna and back, if the weather is good.
3. **Lunch:** salmon soup at the harbour, or walk to Hietalahti.
4. **Afternoon:** a walk north along the shore to Hakaniemi.
