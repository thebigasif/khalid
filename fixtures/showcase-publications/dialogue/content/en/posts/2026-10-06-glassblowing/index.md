Henrik Dahl has spent twenty-two years glassblowing and working with a flame. He makes drinking glasses, lamp shades and the odd chandelier in a two-room studio in Malmö. In this episode he tells Nadia how he learned to read hot glass by its colour and its weight, and why the best tool in his studio is patience.

## Show notes: what glassblowing teaches you

What we talk about:

- How Henrik started, sweeping the floor of a glassworks at sixteen.
- Why glass has to be turned all the time while it is hot.
- How he prices a glass that took forty minutes and might still crack.
- The week he broke every piece he made, and what he changed after it.

> You cannot hurry glass. You can only notice, a little sooner each year, what it is telling you.

## What we learned about glassblowing

Glassblowing, Henrik says, is mostly watching. Because hot glass changes from second to second, he keeps a piece turning on the pipe and reads its colour, so he knows when to shape it and when to heat it again. For example, a drinking glass goes back into the furnace a dozen times before it is done. He still breaks about one piece a week, and he keeps the worst ones on a shelf as a reminder. As a result, his apprentices learn early that a broken glass is a lesson, not a disaster. ## The transcript

:::toggle{summary="Read the full transcript"}
**Nadia:** Henrik, thank you for having me in the studio. It is very warm in here.

**Henrik:** It always is. The furnace never goes out from Monday to Friday, so this room is summer all year.

**Nadia:** How did you start working with a flame?

**Henrik:** With a broom. I swept the floor at a glassworks when I was sixteen. After a year they let me hold a pipe, and after three years they let me ruin things on purpose.

**Nadia:** On purpose?

**Henrik:** You learn more from a glass that slumps than from one that works. You see the moment it went wrong. Now I can feel that moment coming, most days.

**Nadia:** What do you tell people who want to learn?

**Henrik:** Find someone who will let you watch for a long time. Glass tells you when it is ready, but only if you have learned to listen.
:::
