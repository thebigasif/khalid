Mira Castell says clay will not be hurried, and she has the cracked pots to prove it. She runs a small studio in Porto, where she throws tableware for three restaurants and teaches an evening class. In this episode she tells Nadia how she learned to plan her week around the slowest step: drying. Her case for slow pottery runs through the whole conversation.

## Show notes: slow pottery

What we talk about:

- How a pot dries from the outside in, and why that cracks it.
- Throwing the same bowl two hundred times to learn it.
- Writing a price list that pays for the failures.
- Why she teaches beginners on a slow wheel.

> People think the wheel is the hard part. The hard part is leaving a pot alone for three days.

## What we learned about slow pottery

Slow pottery is not a style, Mira says, but a timetable. Because clay dries from the outside in, she plans every week around the shelves, so throwing happens on Mondays and trimming on Thursdays. For example, a large bowl waits three days under plastic before she touches it again. She also prices for failure: about one pot in eight cracks in the kiln, so the other seven carry its cost. As a result, her prices look high to new customers but keep the studio open. Her advice for beginners is simple. Join a class with a slow wheel, make one shape many times, and keep a notebook of what cracked.

## The transcript

:::toggle{summary="Read the full transcript"}
**Nadia:** Mira, there are pots under plastic sheets on every shelf.

**Mira:** They are drying. If the air gets at them too fast, the rims dry first and pull away from the base.

**Nadia:** So the waiting is part of the work?

**Mira:** It is most of the work. Clay remembers every hurried minute. You see it in the kiln, a week later, as a crack.

**Nadia:** What would you tell someone at their first class?

**Mira:** Make the same bowl until you are bored of it. Then make it twenty more times.

**Nadia:** Do your customers understand the waiting?

**Mira:** Most do, once they see the shelves. So I show them round before they order.
:::
