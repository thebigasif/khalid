We get a lot of listener questions, and some come up every week. Here are the answers to the most common ones, from how we choose guests to where a new listener should start. If yours is not here, write to hello@example.com and we will answer it.

## Listener questions about guests

### How do you choose guests?

We look for people who still do the work with their own hands, whatever the size of their business. Most guests are suggested by listeners. We try to balance crafts, places and experience, so a season has both beginners and people with fifty years at the bench. [This season's guests](/blog/season-two/) show the mix.

### Are guests paid?

Yes, every guest is paid a fee for their time, because an hour of talking is an hour away from paid work at the bench. We also link to their shop, studio or courses on the episode page, so listeners who enjoyed the conversation can buy their work or learn the craft from them.

## How the episodes work

### Why are the episodes so long?

Because the good part of a conversation often comes after forty minutes, when the guest forgets the microphone. We edit each one down, but we never cut it to a fixed length. The length is on every episode page next to the guest's name, so you can choose a short one for a short walk.

### Can I read instead of listening?

Yes. Every episode page has the full transcript, folded away under the chapters, so you can open it and read at your own pace. The transcript marks each speaker by name. It also helps if you want to find a quote again later, or share one line with a friend who makes things.

### Where should I start?

Start with whichever craft you know least about, because the surprise is half the fun. If you cannot choose, try [Ruth Calloway on dry stone walling](/blog/dry-stone-walling/), which listeners mention more than any other episode, or [Henrik Dahl on glassblowing](/blog/glassblowing/), which opened season two.
