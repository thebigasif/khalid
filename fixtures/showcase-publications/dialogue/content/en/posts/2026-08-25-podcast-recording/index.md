People often ask how our podcast recording works in a noisy workshop. The short answer is two microphones, one small recorder and a lot of patience. This note explains the kit we carry, how we set up, and what we do when the furnace roars or the rain gets loud.

## The podcast recording kit we carry

We travel light, because most workshops are small and full of things we should not knock over.

- Two dynamic microphones, which pick up the voice close by and less of the room.
- A small recorder with two inputs, so each voice has its own track.
- Two pairs of closed headphones.
- A blanket, which is the most useful thing in the bag.

All of it fits in one shoulder bag, so we can take a train to a guest and carry everything up a ladder if we have to. The whole kit cost less than a good second-hand guitar.

## How we set up

We record at the bench, not in a quiet office, because guests talk more freely with their work in reach. We put the guest's microphone a hand's width from their mouth, ask them to keep working if they want to, and hang the blanket behind the noisiest machine. Then we record two minutes of the room with nobody talking, which helps the edit later.

## What we do about noise during podcast recording

Some noise belongs in the episode. The hiss of a torch or the tap of a hammer tells you where you are. What we cut is noise that hides words. If a kettle boils or a lorry passes, we stop, wait and ask the question again. For example, in [Henrik Dahl on glassblowing](/blog/glassblowing/) you can hear the furnace all the way through, and we left it in on purpose.

## Editing

Ben edits each conversation down by about a third. He keeps the pauses that matter and cuts the ones that do not. Every guest hears the edit before it goes out, so nothing surprises them on release day. The free [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org/) editor is enough for everything we do.
