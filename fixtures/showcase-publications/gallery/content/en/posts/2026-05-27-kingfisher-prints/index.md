These bird prints were made in Japan in the early 1900s and are now in the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam, which shares them under CC0. The Saltmarsh Bird Reserve asked us for three of them, to sell in its visitor centre: a kingfisher, two doves and a pair of great tits on a maple branch.

## A soft ground behind the kingfisher

The prints have a soft, cloudy ground, made by wiping ink onto the block before each print. A scan turns that softness into noise. We cleaned the noise away without flattening the clouds, which took more care than the birds did.

Two doves, the second print of the set.
:::

## Paper with fibres

We printed on a thin Japanese paper with long fibres, close to the paper the prints were made on. It is too thin to go through most printers, so it is backed with a carrier sheet for printing and taken off after. Each print is then laid flat for a day.

:::figure{src="great-tits.jpg" alt="A tall Japanese print from the kingfisher set: two great tits on a maple branch with green leaves, on a pale peach ground"}
The great tits on a maple branch, the third print.
:::

## The narrow shape

These prints are very tall and narrow, a shape made to hang on a pillar. We kept that shape and sold them with narrow frames. Read more about [Japanese bird and flower prints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kach%C5%8D-e). For a pattern full of birds, see [Strawberry Thief](/blog/strawberry-thief/).

## Why the kingfisher leads

The reserve has kingfishers on its river, and visitors come to see them. So the kingfisher print leads the set and hangs at the door of the visitor centre. It shows the bird at the moment it dives, a moment few people ever see. The kingfisher is also the reserve's logo, so the print is a gift the staff give to every new volunteer. We made a small card of it too, with the same blues, because a card fits in a pocket and a print does not. For more birds in print, see [Haeckel's hummingbirds](/blog/hummingbird-plate/).
