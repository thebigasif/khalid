Utagawa Hiroshige made this woodblock print in 1857 for his series One Hundred Famous Views of Edo. People hurry over the long wooden bridge at Atake as a sudden shower sweeps across the river. The Kanda Paper Company asked us for a small edition to mark a hundred years of making paper.

## Cutting nine blocks

A woodblock print is made from one block for the outlines and one block for each colour. We studied a clean copy and found nine colours. Maren drew each colour as its own layer from the restored scan, and a block cutter in Kyoto cut them in cherry wood. The rain lines are on the key block, cut as fine as the wood allows.

## Printing by hand

Tomas printed the thirty prints over four days, one colour at a time, on paper from the client's own mill. The grey of the river darkens towards the top. That shading is wiped onto the block by hand before each print, so no two skies are quite the same.

## Why Rain at Atake

It is one of the most copied pictures in the world. Vincent van Gogh painted his own copy of it. That makes a new edition hard, since every buyer already knows it well. Read more about [the series and its prints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Hundred_Famous_Views_of_Edo). For two more Japanese prints, see [Red Fuji and the Great Wave](/blog/red-fuji/).

## Showing the edition at Atake

The paper company showed the edition in its shop near the river, not far from where Atake once stood. Each print hung beside a sheet of the paper it was printed on, so visitors could feel it. The rain print sold out in a week. For a pattern made by printing one colour after another, see [Strawberry Thief](/blog/strawberry-thief/).
