Pierre-Joseph Redouté painted the lily family for Les Liliacées, a book printed in parts from 1802. He is best known for roses, but many people think the lily plates are his finest. Old Orchard Seeds asked us to put eight of them on the fronts of their seed packets, starting with this Haemanthus.

## Lily plates made small

The plates are large and a seed packet is small. At that size the fine roots and the hairs on the leaves vanish into a blur. We redrew nothing. Instead we cleaned the plate and then made the darkest lines a touch darker, so they hold at a tenth of the size.

## Room for the name

Each packet carries the plant's name, the sowing months and the price. They sit in a band under the picture, never on it, so the whole plant shows. The band is the same on all eight packets, so the range reads as one family on a shop's rack.

## Eight plants for one year

The eight plants flower one after another from spring to autumn. A gardener who sows all eight has something in flower for most of the year.

Read more about [Redouté and his books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Joseph_Redout%C3%A9). For a calendar of plant plates, see [Merian's Surinam plates](/blog/surinam-plates/).

## Why lily plates

The lily plates have one plant on each page and a lot of empty paper around it. That empty paper gives the packet room to breathe on a crowded rack. The lily plates also share one style, so eight packets look like a family. Old Orchard Seeds now plans a second range for the autumn, with bulbs, and it will use lily plates again, because customers asked for more of them by name.
