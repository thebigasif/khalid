John James Audubon drew every bird in Birds of America at life size, so the book was printed on the largest sheets of its day. The flamingo did not fit even then, so he drew it bent low with its neck curved down. The Saltmarsh Bird Reserve asked us for a poster of it for its new wading bird hide.

## A very large scan

The National Gallery of Art in Washington shares its copy of the plate under CC0, with a scan large enough to print at full size. At poster size the engraved lines still hold. We cleaned the paper and some foxing on the bird's back and left the engraver's lines alone.

## The flamingo pink

Pink is a colour that printers fear. It drifts towards orange or towards purple, and the flamingo's pink is both at once in places. Tomas printed six test strips on three papers before we chose a matt paper with a slight warmth. The pink stays true on it under the reserve's daylight.

## Words on the plate

The plate carries the bird's name and the engraver's credit in small type at the foot. We kept them, under the bird, as they are on the original. The reserve added its own line of text on the poster's margin, never on the picture.

Read more about [Audubon and his book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birds_of_America). For three more birds from the same client, see [the Japanese bird prints](/blog/kingfisher-prints/).

## A flamingo at the door

The poster hangs at the entrance to the hide, at the height of a child's eyes. Visitors stand beside the flamingo to see how tall a real one would be. It is the reserve's best selling poster. Because the hide is damp in winter, we printed the hanging copy on a paper that does not curl, and sealed it behind glass. The copies in the shop are on the same matt paper, so the flamingo looks the same in both places. For sea creatures in a set of prints, see [Haeckel's jellyfish](/blog/jellyfish-plates/).
