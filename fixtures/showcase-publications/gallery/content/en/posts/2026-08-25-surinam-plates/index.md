Maria Sibylla Merian travelled to Surinam in 1699 to draw its plants and insects alive, not as dried samples. Her book of 1705 shows each insect on the plant it eats, with its egg, its young and the adult together. The Glasshouse Garden Trust grows many of her plants under glass, and asked us for a calendar of her plates.

## Twelve months of Surinam plates

We picked twelve plates and put them in the order the plants flower in the Trust's glasshouses. The pineapple opens the year, since it flowers in January there. Each month shows one plate, with the plant's name and the insects on it in a line of small type under the picture.

## A soft paper

Merian's plates were coloured by hand on soft paper. A shiny calendar would have looked wrong, so the calendar is printed on an uncoated paper with a little tooth. Uncoated paper drinks ink, so we made every plate a little lighter before print. Tomas checked each month on press against a proof.

## What we left alone

Some plates have small tears and a printer's number in the corner. We kept those, because they belong to the book. Read more about [Maria Sibylla Merian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sibylla_Merian). For another plant book, see [Redouté's Haemanthus](/blog/lily-plates/).

## Plants that still grow

Every plant in the calendar still grows in Surinam today, and the Trust grows most of them too. Each month's page names the glasshouse where a visitor can see the living plant. The Surinam plates are three hundred years old, and the plants in them are still fruiting under glass. Each month also carries a short note on the insect, written by the Trust's own gardeners, because visitors often ask what the insects are.
