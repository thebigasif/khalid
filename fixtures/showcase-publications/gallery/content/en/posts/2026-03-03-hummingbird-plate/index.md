Ernst Haeckel's plate of hummingbirds is one of the busiest in his book Art Forms in Nature. Dozens of birds perch and hover among leaves, each in a different colour. Kestrel Field Guides asked us for it as the jacket of their new guide to the birds of the Americas.

## A jacket is not a print

A jacket wraps around a book, so the picture must work on the front, across the spine and on the back. We placed the brightest birds on the front panel. The spine falls on a band of leaves with no bird on it, so no bird is cut in half when the book stands on a shelf.

## Gloss on each hummingbird

The jacket is printed on a matt paper. Then a clear gloss varnish goes on top of the birds only, not the leaves. When the book turns in the light, the birds shine like real feathers and the leaves stay soft. Tomas cut a separate plate for the varnish from the restored file.

## Cleaning many small things

A plate with many small subjects is slow to clean. Every bird has its own edge, and the leaves cross behind them. This plate took Maren four days, twice as long as a plate with one large subject.

Read more about [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel). For six more plates from the same book, see [Haeckel's jellyfish](/blog/jellyfish-plates/).

## A hummingbird for every page

Inside the guide, a small detail of the plate opens each chapter, one hummingbird at a time. We cut each hummingbird out of the restored plate with its own leaf behind it. So the jacket and the pages read as one book. The publisher printed a few jackets without the book, on heavier paper, as posters for bookshops. So the hummingbird plate also hangs in shop windows, where it brings people in to look. For a plant plate made small for a packet, see [Redouté's lily plates](/blog/lily-plates/).
