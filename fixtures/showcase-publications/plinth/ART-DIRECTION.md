# Tessel Rowe Architects: art direction

Tessel Rowe Architects is a made-up architecture and interiors practice. Its photographs should look like a practice's own record of finished work: the building or the room, in plain light, with nothing staged. Its plans are drawings made for this publication.

## Tone

- **The building is the subject.** A facade, a room, a stair or a wall fills the frame. People may appear small, as in the old drawing office, but no face is the subject.
- **Plain light.** Daylight or one lamp, true colour, straight verticals where the photograph allows. No dusk shots with every light on, no heavy filters.
- **Little to read.** The library window carries one etched word, which reads as part of the building. The white stair has a small exit sign and the brick housing a small garden sign; both are too small to read at card size. No posters or shop signs.

## Palette

Paper, brick and timber:

- white and off-white render and plaster;
- the reds and browns of brick and oak;
- one or two blues, from the sky over the white house and the cafe bench.

These sit well beside the sample brand's deep green and the template's warm paper.

## Subject mix

Of the 11 photographs, 6 are buildings seen from outside or wide rooms, and 5 are close views of a material, a stair or a corner.

- **Wide (6):** the white house, the open sided shed, the brick housing round a lawn, the living room, the cafe, the old drawing office.
- **Close (5):** the library window, the Georgian doorway, the white stair, the lime plaster wall, the light bulb on a white wall.

## Crops made here

The Georgian doorway carried the photographer's small name mark in its lower corner. It is cropped off, 64 px from the bottom, as its CC BY-SA licence allows, and the credit still names him.

## Phone crop

On a phone each spread's photograph fills the width at 4:3. Every subject sits in the middle third: the house, the doorway, the stair and the bench are centred, and the plaster wall is an even texture.

## Sources and licences
