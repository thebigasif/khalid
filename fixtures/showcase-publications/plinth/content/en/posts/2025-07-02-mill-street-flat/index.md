The Mill Street flat began as one long room on the first floor of a stone mill. It had five tall windows on one side and a blank wall on the other. Our client, a doctor who works nights, wanted a bedroom, a study and a living room, without losing the feeling of one big space.

## Joinery instead of walls

Most of the new divisions in the Mill Street flat are pieces of oak joinery, not walls. A bank of cupboards, two metres high, separates the bedroom from the living room. Its top is open to the ceiling, so light and air pass over it. The study sits behind a sliding screen of oak slats, which can close it off for a video call or open it to the room.

## The kitchen on the window wall

We put the kitchen along the window wall, under the two windows nearest the door. The worktop runs at sill height, so the windows stay clear. The cooker and sink face out over the street, and the tall units sit against the blank wall, where they also hide the boiler and the washing machine.

## A bedroom that stays dark

Because our client sleeps by day, the bedroom needed to be dark at noon. The window there has a lined shutter that folds into the wall, and the cupboard bank has a heavy curtain on a track above it. Closed, the room is dark and quiet; open, it is part of the long room again.

## Finishes in the Mill Street flat

The floor is the mill's own pine boards, sanded and oiled. The walls are lime plaster, left unpainted in the living room. The joinery is oiled [oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak) with brass pulls. Lights hang low over the table and the desk, and the ceiling is left dark, so the room feels lower and warmer at night.

## Cost

The work took eleven weeks and cost a little over half the price of a new kitchen and two new walls in a standard fit out, because the joinery does the work of both.
