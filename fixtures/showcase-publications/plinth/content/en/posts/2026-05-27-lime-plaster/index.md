We have now used lime plaster on three projects in a row: the [house around a garden court](/blog/whitecross-house/), a [flat in an old mill](/blog/mill-street-flat/) and the [harbour cafe in a net store](/blog/net-store-cafe/). This note sets down what we learned, for our clients and for anyone choosing a plaster.

## Why lime plaster

Lime lets a wall breathe. Water in an old stone or brick wall can pass through it and dry out, where a gypsum plaster or a plastic paint would trap it. In old buildings, such as the [Georgian house we repaired](/blog/bow-lane-townhouse/), that is the main reason to use it. In new ones, we use it because it looks and feels softer than gypsum, and it takes a mineral paint well.

## It takes time

Lime sets slowly, by taking carbon dioxide from the air. Each coat needs days to firm up before the next goes on. In the cafe the three coats took five weeks, against about one week for gypsum. Plan the work so other trades are busy elsewhere while it dries, and keep the rooms aired but not heated.

## What lime plaster costs

On our three projects it cost between a quarter and a half more than gypsum, mostly in labour. Fewer plasterers work in lime, so book them early.

| Project | Wall | Coats | Weeks to dry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whitecross House | Blockwork, render outside | 2 | 3 |
| Mill Street flat | Stone, inside | 3 | 4 |
| Net Store cafe | Wood fibre board on stone | 3 | 5 |

## Mistakes we made

On the first job we let the second coat dry too fast in a warm spell, and fine cracks opened. Now we hang damp sheets in hot weather. In the flat we chose a white paint that was not breathable; we stripped it and used a mineral paint instead.

## Where we would use lime plaster again

In any old building with solid walls, always. In a new house, where the walls are built to suit it and the client likes the look. We would not use it in a wet room without care, or where the work must finish fast. Wikipedia explains the history of [lime plaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_plaster) well. Our new hire will spend a week with our plasterer, as the [job post for an assistant](/blog/hiring-architectural-assistant/) explains.
