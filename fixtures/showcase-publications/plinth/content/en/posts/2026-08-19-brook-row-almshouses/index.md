Brook Row is a charity that has housed older people in the town since 1702. Its old homes are too small and too steep for the people who live in them now. We are building six new Brook Row almshouses on its land by the brook, and the work started on site in June.

## Two rows and a garden

The homes sit in two short rows of three, facing each other across a shared garden. Each home has its own front door onto the garden, a small terrace and a back garden of its own. A common room closes the end of the garden, where residents can meet, eat together and hold events.

## Homes on one floor

Every home is on one level, with no steps from the path to the bed. Each has a living room with a kitchen, one bedroom, a shower room and a store for a scooter. Doors are wide, light switches are low, and the windows start low enough to see the garden from a chair.

## How the Brook Row almshouses are built

The walls are the same brick we used for the [reading room on Quarry Lane](/blog/quarry-lane-library/). The roofs are long and low, with deep eaves that shade the windows in summer and keep rain off the doors. Inside, the floors are timber and the ceilings follow the roof up, so even small rooms feel generous. Each home is heated by a heat pump and has solar panels on the south slope of its roof.

## Working with the residents

Before we drew a line, we spent two afternoons with the people who live in the old homes. They asked for a bench by every front door, a shed for tools and a place to hang washing out of sight. All three are in the plan.

## What comes next

The homes should be finished in spring 2027. The charity will choose the first residents in the autumn. We will post again when the roofs are on. [Almshouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almshouse) have a long history in this country, and many towns still build them.
