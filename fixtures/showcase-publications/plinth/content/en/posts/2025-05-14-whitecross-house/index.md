A narrow house is hard to light. Whitecross House stands on a plot only nine metres wide, between two taller neighbours in the old Whitecross part of town. Our answer was to give up part of the plot to a walled garden court and turn the rooms towards it.

## The brief for Whitecross House

The owners, a couple with two teenage children, wanted a kitchen big enough to eat in, a living room away from it, a study and four bedrooms. They also wanted a garden, but the plot had room for only a small one. They worked from home three days a week, so the study had to be quiet and bright.

## A plan around a court

The plan below shows the ground floor. The kitchen and dining room face the street. The stair, a washroom and the study sit in the middle. The living room is at the back and opens fully onto the garden court.

:::figure{src="plan.svg" alt="Ground floor plan of Whitecross House: kitchen and dining at the front, a stair in the middle, the living room opening to a garden court"}
Ground floor. The garden court at the back gives the living room and the study their light.
:::

Upstairs, the four bedrooms all look down into the court. The stair has a roof light over it, so the middle of the house, which would be dark in most narrow plans, is the brightest place on a grey day.

## Render and small windows

The street front has only a few small windows, set where they frame something: the church tower from the kitchen, the sky from the stair. The walls are lime render over blockwork, painted white with a mineral paint. In summer the house stays cool, and in winter the thick walls hold the heat of the low sun. The [render](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stucco) is the same mix the town used on its houses two hundred years ago.

## Living in Whitecross House

The family moved in during the spring of 2024. In the hot summer of 2026 the rooms stayed below 25 degrees with the court doors open at night and the blinds down by day. The court has become the family's favourite room: they eat there from May to September.

## What we learned

A court of 36 square metres feels bigger than a lawn twice the size, because every room borrows it.
