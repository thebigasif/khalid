Every project in our studio starts with drawing by hand. Before anyone opens the computer, we spend a day with tracing paper, soft pencils and the survey of the site. Here is why we still work that way in 2026.

## Why drawing by hand is slow, and why that helps

A computer drawing is exact from the first line. That is its strength later and its weakness at the start. On tracing paper a wall can be thick or thin, here or a little further over. A pencil keeps the first ideas loose, so we try more of them before we choose one.

## Layers of paper

We lay a sheet over the survey and draw. Then another sheet over that, and another. By the end of the day there may be twenty sheets on the table, each a little different. Laid over each other, they show which ideas keep coming back. Those are usually the right ones.

## Clients draw too

At the first meeting with a client we put a roll of paper on the table and hand them a pencil. Most people say they cannot draw, and then draw exactly what they mean: the chair by the window, the table where the family eats. Those sketches go in the project file and we come back to them often. The court at the heart of our [narrow white house](/blog/whitecross-house/) began as a client's sketch of a table under a tree.

## What drawing by hand does not do

Hand drawings are not good at checking. They do not count stairs, test fire routes or work out a floor area. That is the computer's job, and we hand over to it as soon as the plan settles. We also never show a client a hand drawing as a promise: it is a question, not an answer.

## Then the computer

Once the plan settles, Priya draws it up on the computer, and from then on the drawings are exact. The plans in our project posts, such as the [stair for the chapel gallery](/blog/chapel-gallery-stair/), are all redrawn from the first sketches.
