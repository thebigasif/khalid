The chapel on Chapel Row closed in 2018. The town's art society bought it to show local work. The nave was a fine room for pictures, but the upper gallery could only be reached by a narrow stair in a corner. Our job was to make it a chapel gallery anyone could use, with a wide stair and a lift.

## A slot beside the nave

We added a narrow wing along the side of the chapel, four metres wide, and put the entrance, the stair and the lift in it. The old side wall stays as the inner wall of the stair, so you climb past the chapel's own stone and its blocked windows.

## The stair of the chapel gallery

The stair is two metres wide, with shallow steps and a landing halfway, so it is easy to climb and easy to stop on. The walls are smooth white plaster and the steps are pale oak. A long [roof light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylight) runs over the whole stair, so daylight falls down the walls and leads people up. At night, lights hidden in the handrails do the same. The whole stair began as one sketch on tracing paper, as our note on [how each project starts](/blog/drawing-by-hand/) explains.

## The rooms

In the nave we took out the pews and laid a plain oak floor. Pictures hang from a rail at the old gallery height, so nothing is fixed to the old walls. Upstairs, the old balcony is now a second room for small works and talks. The shop and cafe sit under it, at the back of the nave.

## Keeping the chapel a chapel

The art society wanted the building to stay recognisable. We kept the pulpit, the organ case and the painted text above the door. The new wing is built of the same stone, laid plainer, with a flat roof that sits below the chapel's eaves.

## Since the chapel gallery opened

The chapel gallery opened in March with a show of local painters. The art society counted more visitors in its first month than in the whole of the year before, when it borrowed a hall.
