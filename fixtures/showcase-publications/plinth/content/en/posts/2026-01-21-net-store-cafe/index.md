The net store on the quay is a single room with thick stone walls, where fishermen once dried and mended their nets. The harbour trust asked us to make it the Net Store cafe, a place that would stay open all year, not just in summer.

## One long bench

The room is sixteen metres long and only nine wide. We ran a padded bench down the full length of the window wall, with small tables in front of it, so most people sit facing the water. The bench is covered in a blue wool that wipes clean and wears well. Under the seat are drawers for the cafe's spare chairs and the terrace cushions.

## The counter of the Net Store cafe

The counter runs along the back wall, under the old net loft. It is built of oak boards with a zinc top, which will darken and mark over the years like the bar of a harbour pub. The kitchen behind it is small, as the menu is short: soup, bread, fish and cake.

## Warm in winter

Stone walls by the sea are cold. We lined the back wall with wood fibre board and lime plaster, which keeps the room warm and lets the wall breathe. A wood burning stove at the east end gives the room a centre on grey days. The old net loft above is now the store, reached by a ladder stair we made from the loft's own timbers.

## The terrace

On fine days the folding doors open onto the quay, and the terrace doubles the number of seats. Old [net lofts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_loft) like this one stand along much of the coast, and many are empty.

## A year on

The Net Store cafe now opens every day of the year and employs six people from the town. The harbour trust plans to repair the next store along in the same way. The [Mill Street flat](/blog/mill-street-flat/) shows the same idea in a home: one long room with its parts set along the walls. Our [note on lime](/blog/lime-plaster/) has the plaster mix we used.
