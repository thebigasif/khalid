A share index sums up a whole market in one number. When we write that the Varnholm 50 rose 1.8% in a week, we mean the share index rose: a weighted average of the prices of fifty firms. This explainer shows how that average is built. Wikipedia's page on the [stock market index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_index) covers the same idea for real markets.

## Step 1: pick the firms for the share index

The Varnholm 50 holds the fifty largest firms on the Varnholm exchange, measured by their market value: the share price times the number of shares. The small firms index holds the next two hundred.

## Step 2: weigh each firm in the share index

A large firm counts for more than a small one. Each firm's weight is its market value divided by the total of all fifty. The table gives four firms' weights on 28 September 2026, with market values in billions of varns.

| Firm | Market value | Weight in the index |
|---|---|---|
| Northgate Bank | 41 | 9.8% |
| Varnholm Power | 28 | 6.7% |
| Kestrel Machine Tools | 19 | 4.5% |
| Harrow Foods | 12 | 2.9% |
| The other 46 firms | 319 | 76.1% |

So a 10% rise in Northgate Bank moves the index almost 1%, while the same rise in Harrow Foods moves it 0.3%. That is true of the weights in September 2026; they change as prices move.

## Step 3: track the change

Each day, the index takes every firm's price change, multiplies it by the firm's weight and adds them up. The index started at 1,000 in 2001. [The last ten years](/blog/varnholm-50-ten-years/) took it from about 1,400 to 1,720.

## Step 4: the yearly check

Every December, the exchange checks which firms still belong. A firm that has shrunk drops to the small firms index, and a growing one joins. Changes are announced a month ahead, so the firms that leave and join are no surprise.

## Why small firms move differently

The small firms index holds more suppliers, food makers and exporters. News about a [harvest](/blog/harvest-lifts-small-firms/) or [factory orders](/blog/factory-orders-lift-shares/) moves it more than the Varnholm 50, which leans on banks and power firms. That is also why a fall in one sector, such as the miners in [the copper slide](/blog/copper-slide-quiet-week/), barely moves the larger share index.
