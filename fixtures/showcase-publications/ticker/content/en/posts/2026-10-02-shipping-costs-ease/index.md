It costs less to ship goods out of Varnholm than it did a month ago. The freight index, which tracks the price of moving one container to Varnholm's ten largest trading partners, fell for a fifth week. Lower shipping costs helped almost every market: in the week to 2 October 2026 the Varnholm 50 rose 1.2%, copper 2.0% and the varn 0.5%.

## Why shipping costs matter

A firm that sells abroad pays to send its goods there. When shipping costs fall, it keeps more of each sale, or can cut its price and sell more. Varnholm exports machines, grain and copper wire, so cheaper freight helps a large part of the share market. Wikipedia's page on [freight rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_rate) explains how these prices are set.

The table gives the cost of one container on three routes on 2 October 2026.

| Route | Cost of one container | Change in 5 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Varnholm to the north ports | 1,840 varns | -14% |
| Varnholm to the east coast | 2,310 varns | -11% |
| Varnholm to the south islands | 1,120 varns | -9% |

## What brought freight prices down

New ships ordered in 2024 have arrived, and more ships chase the same cargo. Fuel is also cheaper than in the summer of 2026. Neither cause is likely to reverse quickly, because ships take years to build.

## Copper's turn

Copper rose 2.0% in the week to 2 October 2026, its best week in two months. Cheaper shipping lowers the cost of getting copper to the factories that use it. Four weeks later, [a third month of rising orders](/blog/factory-orders-lift-shares/) gave copper another reason to rise, as our guide to [the copper price](/blog/copper-price-factory-orders/) explains.

## Who pays when shipping costs fall

Shipping firms earn less per container, but they carry more. Their shares held up in the week, and their summer results, in our look at [earnings season](/blog/earnings-season-sectors/), were the strongest of any sector.

## Next week

The farm ministry's harvest estimate comes out on Thursday. Grain is the largest cargo through Varnholm's ports, so it matters for freight as well as food.
