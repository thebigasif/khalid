Factory orders in Varnholm rose for a third month in a row, and the share market liked it. In the week to 30 October 2026, the Varnholm 50 gained 1.8%, its best week since June. The small firms index did better still, up 2.6% over the same week of 2026.

## What the factory orders showed

The statistics office said new factory orders rose 1.4% in September 2026. Car parts and machine tools led. Economists had expected a small fall, so the number surprised almost everyone.

Orders matter because they come before output. A firm that wins orders today hires people and buys materials over the next few months. That is why the small firms index, full of suppliers to bigger factories, moved most. For a plain guide to how factories turn orders into goods, see [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) on Wikipedia.

## Which sectors gained from the factory orders

The table shows each sector's change in the week to 30 October 2026, and its share of the Varnholm 50.

| Sector | Weekly change | Share of the Varnholm 50 |
|---|---|---|
| Machine tools | +4.2% | 6% |
| Car parts | +3.6% | 5% |
| Banks | +1.1% | 18% |
| Food makers | +0.4% | 9% |
| Utilities | -0.6% | 7% |

Utilities were the only sector to fall. When investors expect growth, they often sell the steady, slow firms to buy the ones that gain most from it. Banks rose because busy factories borrow more.

## Copper and the varn

Copper rose 1.1% in the week, helped by the same orders: factories use copper wire in almost everything they build. Our explainer on [copper and demand from industry](/blog/copper-price-factory-orders/) sets out the link. The varn edged up 0.3% in the same week of 2026 against the currencies Varnholm trades with most.

## How long can orders keep rising?

Three months of rising orders is a run, not yet a trend. Orders can fall back quickly when customers have stocked up. The next figure comes out on 27 November 2026, and we will set it beside this one in the issue table.
