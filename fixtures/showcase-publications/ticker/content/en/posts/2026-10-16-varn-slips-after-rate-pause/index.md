Varnholm's central bank kept its main interest rate at 3.25% on Tuesday 13 October 2026. It was the first meeting without a rise in more than a year. The rate pause pushed the varn down 1.4% in the week, its largest weekly fall since March 2026, and lifted shares.

## Why a rate pause moves the currency

Money flows to where it earns more. While Varnholm's rates were rising, savers abroad bought varns to earn that interest. A rate pause tells them rates may have peaked, so some sold. Wikipedia's page on [exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate) covers the wider idea. Our explainer on [the yield curve](/blog/yield-curve-explained/) shows how rates for different lengths of time react.

## Why shares rose after the rate pause

For firms, a rate pause is good news. Rates lower than expected mean cheaper loans. In the week to 16 October 2026 the Varnholm 50 rose 0.9% and the small firms index 0.5%.

## What the bank said

The governor said prices were rising more slowly and that the bank would wait and see. She did not say rates would fall. The minutes, out on 4 November 2026, will show how many members wanted another rise.

## Exporters gain from a weaker varn

A weaker varn makes Varnholm's goods cheaper abroad. Exporters, such as machine tool makers, often gain when the currency falls. That is part of why [factory orders](/blog/factory-orders-lift-shares/) matter so much to them, and why [shipping costs](/blog/shipping-costs-ease/) do too.

## What a rate pause does not mean

A pause is not a cut. Banks often hold rates for several meetings before they move either way, so the next change could still be up.
