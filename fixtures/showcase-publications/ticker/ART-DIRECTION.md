# Tallyline: art direction

Tallyline is a made-up weekly markets letter about Varnholm, a made-up country. Its photographs show the real economy behind the numbers: factories, mines, ports, ships, fields, power lines and offices. They are context for the figures, never a promise about them.

## Tone

- **Things that are made, moved or grown.** Each picture shows the part of the economy its post is about: a car line for factory orders, a mine headframe for copper, a harvest for grain, a ship for freight.
- **Plain daylight, true colour.** No trading screens, no charts, no money in piles, no dramatic skies.
- **Nothing to read.** No signs, logos or prices that a reader could take for real data. A candidate for the rate post, a stack of coins on a desk, was turned down for its readable packaging, and an abacus beside a branded calculator for its brand name. The car line has small safety labels, too small to read at the post's width.
- **No faces.** People appear only small, at work.

## Palette

Steel greys and blues of ports, glass and sky, with the gold of wheat and the red of a train. These sit well beside the sample brand's petrol blue and the template's warm paper. Avoid pictures dominated by green, which the template keeps for rising figures.

## Subject mix

Of the 10 photographs, 3 are close or framed details and 7 are wider scenes.

- **Details (3):** a clay mould for casting coins, a car assembly line, a warehouse rack.
- **Wider scenes (7):** a mine headframe, a wheat harvest, a container ship from above, glass office towers from below, a freight train in a valley, power lines over a field, container cranes at a port.

## One ratio per slot

| Slot | Ratio | Source size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead picture on a post | 3:2 | 1600 px wide | Shown at the text width, about 36rem. Sources are 3:2 or 4:3, so a 3:2 crop loses little. |
| Author mark | 1:1 | 160 px SVG | Drawn, one ink colour on a light tint of the same ink: a bar chart and a rising line. No photographs of people. |

The Ticker template shows no pictures on its front page or in its lists: those are the tape, the chart and the issue table. Only the post page shows the lead picture.

## Phone crop

On a phone the lead picture fills the width. Each subject sits in the middle third: the ship, the headframe, the mould and the train are centred, and the wheat, towers, racks and cranes are even textures that read the same at any crop.
