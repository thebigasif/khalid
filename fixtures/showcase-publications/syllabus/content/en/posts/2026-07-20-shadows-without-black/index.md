Black paint makes shadows look like holes. In watercolour it also turns every colour it touches to mud. Real shadows are full of colour. This lesson shows you how to paint them from your three paints.

## What colour is a shadow

Look at a shadow on a sunny day. On white paper or sand it is blue or violet, because it is lit by the blue sky rather than the sun. On grass it is a deep blue green. Indoors under a warm lamp, shadows look cool and bluish.

## The shadow mix

Mix ultramarine with a little red to make a soft violet. Thin it with water. This one mix works as a shadow on almost anything: lay it over a dry colour and the colour shows through, darker and cooler.

## Painting over a dry wash

A shadow must go over a dry layer. If the paper is damp, the shadow spreads and loses its edge. Paint the object first, let it dry fully, then paint the shadow in one stroke. Do not go back into it.

## Look at Homer again

In [Winslow Homer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow_Homer) Bahamas watercolours the shadows under the palms are blue and violet, laid over warm sand. Nothing is black, and the light seems brighter for it.

## The exercise

Put an egg or a white mug on a sheet of paper by a window. Paint it with a pale warm wash and let it dry. Then paint its shadow on the table and on its own side with the violet mix. Compare it with a shadow you paint in grey from the palette.

## Darker darks

For the darkest places, mix the shadow colour stronger, with less water, and add a little more blue. Two thin layers give a deeper, clearer dark than one thick one.
