This is the last lesson. You will plan and paint one finished landscape, from a blank sheet to a picture you can frame. Every step uses something you have already learned, and the video follows Helen through the whole painting.

## Choose a simple landscape

Choose a view with a big sky, a few hills or a shore, and one or two trees or figures. Work from a photo of your own or from a window. Simple subjects make the best first paintings.

## Plan in three values

Make a small pencil sketch in three tones: light, middle and dark. Mark where the lightest light and the darkest dark are. This plan matters more than any brush stroke.

## The order of work

1. Tape the paper down and draw the main shapes lightly.
2. Paint the sky in one go, as in [painting skies](/blog/painting-skies/).
3. When it is dry, paint the distant hills, pale and cool.
4. Paint the middle ground with stronger colour.
5. Paint the near shapes, trees or figures, warm and sharp.
6. Add the shadows with the violet mix from [shadows without black](/blog/shadows-without-black/).
7. Add the darkest darks last, and very few of them.

## Look at a master

[Winslow Homer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow_Homer) beach scenes are a good model for a first landscape. The sky and sand are broad washes. The figures are a few strong shapes. The detail is tiny and only where your eye lands.

## When it is finished

Peel the tape, slowly and away from the painting. Put the picture somewhere you will see it tomorrow. Then compare it with the photo of [your watercolour kit](/blog/watercolour-kit/) from the first lesson. Write the date on the back of the landscape, and the lesson numbers whose skills you used.

## What next

Bring your landscape to one of the [live critique evenings](/blog/live-critique-evenings/). Then paint the same view again, in another light. Every painter learns most from the second try.

## Keep painting

The course ends here, but the practice does not. Paint one small landscape a week, from a window, a park or a photo of your own. Within a month you will see your washes grow cleaner and your colours brighter.
