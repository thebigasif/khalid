A graded wash fades from dark at the top to light at the bottom. Look at a clear sky and you will see one: deep blue overhead, pale near the horizon. This lesson builds on [the flat wash](/blog/flat-wash/), so paint a couple of those first.

## The one change

The method is the same as the flat wash, with one change. After each band, add a brushful of clean water to your mix. The colour gets paler with every stroke, and the bead carries it smoothly down the paper.

## How much water to add

If you add too much water at once, you will see bands. Add a little each time, and work fast enough that the bead never dries. Ten or twelve strokes from top to bottom give a smooth fade on a postcard-sized wash.

## Reversing it

Turn the board upside down and you can grade from light to dark. This is useful for the ground in a landscape, which is often darker in front. In [painting skies](/blog/painting-skies/) you will use both directions in one picture.

## A graded wash in two colours

Once one colour grades smoothly, try two. Start with blue at the top and add a little yellow to the mix as you go down, with water. A sky at sunset fades from blue to a warm glow in just this way.

## The exercise

Paint three graded washes: blue dark to light, red light to dark, and blue into yellow. Label each one with a pencil when it is dry. Keep them in a folder; they are your reference for skies later on.

## Common problems

- **Hard lines across the wash:** the bead dried. Work faster or wetter.
- **No fade at all:** add more water at each step.
- **A dark edge at the bottom:** you did not soak up the last bead.
