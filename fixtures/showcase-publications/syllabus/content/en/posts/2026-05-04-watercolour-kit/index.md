Welcome to First Washes. Before you paint anything, you need a small watercolour kit. Art shops will happily sell you forty colours and a case of brushes. You need far less. This lesson gives you the short list, and it is all you will use for the whole course.

## The three paints in your watercolour kit

You need one warm yellow, one warm red and one blue. A cadmium yellow hue, a cadmium red hue and ultramarine are a good set. Tubes or pans both work. Pans are tidier for a beginner, and tubes are easier for big washes.

Buy artist or student quality from a known maker. The cheapest children's sets hold more filler than colour, and they dry chalky. In [a three colour palette](/blog/three-colour-palette/) Tomás shows how these three colours mix almost every colour you will need.

## The two brushes

Get one large round brush, size 12 or 14, and one small round brush, size 6. A round brush has a belly that holds water and a point for detail. Synthetic hair is fine. Test the point in the shop: dip it in water, flick it once and check that it comes back to a sharp tip.

## One pad of paper

Paper matters more than paint. Buy a pad of 300 gsm watercolour paper, about A4 in size. The next lesson, on [watercolour paper](/blog/watercolour-paper/), explains why weight and cotton change everything.

## What else to gather

- Two jars of water: one to rinse, one kept clean for mixing.
- A white plate or a plastic palette with wells.
- Kitchen paper or a cotton rag.
- Masking tape and a board a little bigger than your paper.
- A soft pencil, HB or 2B.

## What you can leave in the shop

Leave the masking fluid, the special brushes and the boxed sets for now. They solve problems you do not have yet. If you want more colours later, the [Wikipedia page on watercolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolor_painting) lists the classic pigments.

## Your task

Gather your kit and lay it out on a table near a window. Take a photo of it and keep it. At the end of the course you will see how little it took to paint a landscape. A small watercolour kit is easy to carry, too, so you can paint outdoors whenever the light is good.
