Many of you asked for a way to show your work and get advice. From November, Tomás and I will hold live critique evenings once a month, online, for everyone on the course.

## How the critique evenings work

Each evening lasts an hour. Before it, you send us one painting from any lesson. On the night we pick about ten paintings and talk through each one: what works, and one thing to try next time. You can watch, ask questions in the chat, or simply listen.

## Dates

The first three evenings are on Wednesday 18 November, Wednesday 16 December and Wednesday 20 January, at 7 pm UK time. Each one is recorded, so you can watch later if the time does not suit you.

## What to send

Send a clear photo of the painting, taken in daylight, flat and square to the camera. Tell us which lesson it comes from and what you were trying to do. A painting from [the flat wash](/blog/flat-wash/) is as welcome as a [finished landscape](/blog/first-landscape/).

## Friendly and specific critique evenings

Critique can feel frightening. Ours is friendly. We never compare students with each other, and we always end with one practical thing to try. Most of what we say will help everyone watching, not just the painter.

## How to join

Everyone on the course gets the link by email a week before each evening. If you have not signed up for course emails yet, the [about page](/blog/about/) explains how.

## Before your first evening

Watch one recording first if you are nervous; most students say the critique evenings felt easier than they expected. Bring a question, too. The best critique evenings so far were the ones where students asked about a problem they had met in [lifting out](/blog/lifting-out/) or [painting skies](/blog/painting-skies/). We use a simple video call that runs in the browser, and the [Wikipedia page on critique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critique) explains the idea behind it.
