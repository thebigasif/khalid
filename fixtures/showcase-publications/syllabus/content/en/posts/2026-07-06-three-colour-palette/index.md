In [your watercolour kit](/blog/watercolour-kit/) you bought three paints. This lesson shows why a three colour palette is enough. With one yellow, one red and one blue you can mix oranges, greens, purples, browns and greys, and they all belong together in one painting.

## Why fewer colours help

Every extra colour is another way for a painting to fall apart. When every colour comes from the same three, they share something, and the picture holds together. Many painters of the past worked with very few colours for this reason, and the [Wikipedia page on primary colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_color) explains why three can go so far. A three colour palette also costs less and fits in a pocket.

## Laying out a three colour palette

Squeeze or wet your paints in the same places every time: yellow, red, blue, from left to right. Keep a large clean area for mixing. Your hand learns where each colour is, and you stop hunting.

## The colour chart

Draw a grid of four rows and four columns on a sheet. Along the top, paint yellow, red, blue and a mix of all three. Down the side, do the same. In each square, mix the colour of its row with the colour of its column, half and half. Label every square.

You now have sixteen colours from three tubes. Pin the chart near your table. The middle of the chart, the two-colour mixes, gives these:

| Mix | Half and half gives | More of the first | More of the second |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yellow and red | A warm orange | A golden yellow | A brick red |
| Yellow and blue | A soft green | A spring green | A deep sea green |
| Red and blue | A dusky violet | A plum red | A cool indigo |
| All three | A warm brown grey | A sandy brown | A blue grey |

## Strong and weak mixes

Every mix can be made strong, with little water, or weak, with a lot. A strong ultramarine is almost navy. A weak one is a pale sky. Paint each square of the chart once strong and once weak if you have time.

## Where the greys come from

Mix all three colours in roughly equal amounts and you get a grey or a brown. Tip the mix towards blue for a cool grey, towards red for a warm one. These greys are far livelier than grey from a tube, and they are the key to [shadows without black](/blog/shadows-without-black/).
