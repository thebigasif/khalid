A Fernley Moor wetland would turn 20 hectares of drained, wet field back into marsh and pools. In a storm it would hold back about 60,000 cubic metres of water, enough to keep the river out of Fernley's houses on most flood days. The rest of the year it would be home to birds, frogs and dragonflies.

## Why Fernley floods

Fernley has flooded four times since 2015. Each time, rain on the hills ran off fast through ditches dug a century ago to drain the moor. The water reached the village in hours. A wetland slows the water down, so the river rises later and lower. Engineers call this [natural flood management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_flood_management), and it works best high in a valley, where the water first gathers.

## What the Fernley Moor wetland will look like

We will block the old ditches, dig a chain of shallow pools and plant reeds along their edges. A low bank will keep the water away from the farm road. The farmer who owns the land will graze cattle on it in summer, which keeps the grass short for wading birds.

Within two years, the pools will fill with reeds and sedges. Snipe, lapwing and herons use wetlands like this within the first winter. A short path and a bench will let walkers see it without disturbing the birds.

## Working with the village

We held two open evenings in Fernley in spring 2026. Most people asked the same two questions: will it smell, and will it bring midges? It will not smell, because the water keeps moving. Midges stay low in number where frogs, bats and birds feed on them.

The willow for the edges will come from the [ten thousand trees](/blog/ten-thousand-trees/) appeal, and the road crossing nearby will get one of our [otter crossings](/blog/otter-crossings/).
