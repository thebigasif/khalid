Ten thousand trees will go into the banks of the Alder this winter, and you paid for every one. In March 2026 we asked for £40,000. By the end of August 2026, 2,086 of you had given £48,300. Thank you.

Trees on a riverbank keep the water cool, hold the soil together and feed the insects that fish eat. A river in full sun can warm by several degrees on a summer day, and young trout struggle above 20 degrees. The shade of [riparian woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riparian_forest) is one of the simplest ways to keep a river healthy.

## Where the ten thousand trees will go

We picked twelve stretches where the banks are bare and the water gets too warm in summer. Most are on farms, where the farmer has agreed to fence off a strip beside the river. The trees are all local kinds: alder, willow, oak, hazel and hawthorn.

| Stretch | Length of bank | Trees |
|---|---|---|
| Hob Mill to Brackenford | 2.1 km | 3,400 |
| Fernley Moor | 1.6 km | 2,500 |
| Ashby farms | 1.4 km | 2,200 |
| Lower Alder meadows | 1.2 km | 1,900 |

The first stretch matters most. Once [Hob Mill weir](/blog/free-hob-mill-weir/) comes out, the new channel below it will need shade straight away.

## What the extra money does

The extra £8,300 raised in 2026 pays for tree guards against deer and for three years of weeding. Young trees die when grass grows over them, so the weeding matters as much as the planting. Any money left after three years goes to the [Fernley Moor wetland](/blog/fernley-moor-wetland/), which will need reeds and willow of its own.

## Planting days

We plant from November to March, when the trees are dormant. Each planting day starts at 10 in the morning and ends by 2 in the afternoon. We bring the spades, the trees and the tea. You bring boots and gloves. Children are welcome with an adult, and every group plants about two hundred trees in a morning.

## Lead a planting group

We need group leaders for the busiest weekends. Book a place on our [river ranger training](/blog/river-ranger-training/) to learn how to lead a group safely beside water. Our report on [where every pound went](/blog/where-every-pound-went/) shows how earlier planting appeals were spent.
