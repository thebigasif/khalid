Our river clean-up day on 13 September was the biggest yet. 640 volunteers worked on 23 stretches of the Alder and its streams, on foot, in waders and in canoes. By 3 in the afternoon they had pulled out 3.2 tonnes of rubbish.

## What came out on river clean-up day

| What | How much |
|---|---|
| Plastic bags and bottles | 1,870 items |
| Tyres | 41 |
| Shopping trolleys | 3 |
| Traffic cones | 12 |
| Fishing line | 18 kilometres |
| A sofa | 1 |

Fishing line is the worst for wildlife. Birds and otters get tangled in it. Please take old line home and put it in the bins we have fixed at the main fishing spots. Tangled line was one of the reasons we started our [otter crossings appeal](/blog/otter-crossings/), since every otter we lose matters.

## Where the rubbish comes from

Most of what we found had blown or washed in from roads and car parks. Plastic breaks down into small pieces that fish and birds eat, a problem known as [marine debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_debris) once it reaches the sea. Taking it out of the river stops it before it gets there.

## A clue to an oil leak

Near Mill Street, a team found a film of oil on the water. They used one of the [Clean Water Watch](/blog/clean-water-watch/) kits to take a sample and followed the oil upstream to a broken pipe at a yard. The owner fixed it within a week. This is just what the kits are for.

## Thank you

Thank you to every volunteer, to the four schools that sent pupils, and to the council for taking the rubbish away for free. Many of the team leaders trained on our [river ranger days](/blog/river-ranger-training/) in the spring.
