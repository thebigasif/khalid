Every year we show where every pound went, in plain numbers. This is the short version of our report for the year to March 2026. The full accounts are on the [charity register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Commission_for_England_and_Wales), as every registered charity's are.

## Where the money came from

In the year to March 2026 we had £612,400 to spend. Most of it came from people who live in or love the valley.

| Source | Amount | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Gifts from supporters | £286,300 | 47% |
| Grants from trusts and foundations | £174,500 | 28% |
| Gifts left in wills | £82,000 | 13% |
| Farm and business partners | £49,600 | 8% |
| Events and the shop | £20,000 | 3% |

## Where every pound went in 2026

For every pound in the year to March 2026, 84 pence went straight into work on the river. 11 pence went on raising the next pound, and 5 pence on running the trust.

| Spending | Amount |
|---|---|
| River and wetland projects | £356,200 |
| Volunteers and training | £84,700 |
| Surveys and water testing | £72,100 |
| Raising money | £67,400 |
| Running the trust | £30,600 |

## What it bought

- 6.4 kilometres of river opened to fish, with the Brackenford culvert gone.
- 4,100 trees planted on the riverbanks.
- 1,240 volunteers, who gave 18,600 hours between them.
- Monthly water tests at 61 places on the Alder and its streams.

## What we learned

Two things went less well. The planting at Ashby lost a third of its trees to a dry spring, so we now water new trees in their first summer. And our water tests showed that one stream, Fernley Brook, is still too rich in nitrate. That is why we started the Clean Water Watch appeal.

## The year ahead

This year we aim to take out [Hob Mill weir](/blog/free-hob-mill-weir/), dig the [Fernley Moor wetland](/blog/fernley-moor-wetland/) and plant the rest of the [ten thousand trees](/blog/ten-thousand-trees/). We will report on all three in next year's note. Thank you for making it possible.
