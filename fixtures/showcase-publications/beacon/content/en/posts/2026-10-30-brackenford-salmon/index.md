Brackenford salmon were a story the oldest anglers in the valley told. On 14 October, two river rangers filmed three of them leaping the rapids below the old ford. It is the first time anyone has seen salmon there since the 1960s.

## Why the Brackenford salmon came back

In 2024 we took out the Brackenford culvert, a concrete pipe under the farm track that fish could not swim up. We replaced it with a clear-span bridge and let the river find its old bed. Two years on, the gravel below the ford is clean, and the salmon found it.

The [Atlantic salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_salmon) returns to the river it was born in, but some fish stray and explore. Those strays find new places to breed when the way is open. That is what happened here.

## What we saw

The rangers watched the pool for two hours and counted at least three fish, each about 70 centimetres long. On 21 October, Tomasz and a fish biologist found two redds, the nests salmon dig in gravel to lay their eggs. The eggs will hatch in early spring, and the young fish will stay in the river for one or two years before they swim out to sea.

## What it means for Hob Mill

Brackenford is six kilometres below Hob Mill. The fish have now come as far up the Alder as they can. Above the mill lie 14 more kilometres of good gravel that no salmon can reach. That is why our [Hob Mill weir appeal](/blog/free-hob-mill-weir/) matters so much this winter.

## Other wildlife on the rise

Salmon are not the only sign that the river is getting better. This summer our volunteers also counted more nesting birds than ever on the lower river, as our [kingfisher survey](/blog/kingfishers-lower-alder/) shows. Cleaner water and open channels help every animal that lives in or beside the river.

## Thank you to the rangers

None of this would be known without volunteers who walk the river each week. They film, count and write down what they see, and they tell us within hours when something changes. If you would like to join them, Priya runs [river ranger training](/blog/river-ranger-training/) every spring.
