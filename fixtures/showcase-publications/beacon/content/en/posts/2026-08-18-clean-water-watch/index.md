Clean Water Watch will put a testing kit and a trained tester in all 42 villages on the Alder and its streams. A kit lets anyone check a stream in twenty minutes. With one in every village, a sewage leak or a slurry spill is found in days, not months.

## Why we need more eyes on the river

Official tests on the Alder happen four times a year at six places. Pollution often comes and goes between those tests. In 2025, a farm tank leaked into Fernley Brook for nine days before anyone noticed, and it killed fish for two kilometres.

Volunteers who live beside a stream see it every day. Give them a kit and a simple way to report, and they become an early warning for the whole valley. Projects like this are known as [citizen science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_science), and river groups across the country now run them.

## What is in each Clean Water Watch kit

- Strips that test for phosphate and nitrate, the main signs of sewage and fertiliser.
- A meter for how cloudy the water is.
- A tray and a key for river insects. Some insects live only in clean water, so finding them tells you more than a single test.
- A phone app that sends each result to a public map.

At 2026 prices, each kit costs £310 with a year of test strips, and training each tester costs £150. The rest of the money pays for the map and for a part-time coordinator for two years.

## What happens to the results

Every result goes on a public map the day it is taken. When a reading looks wrong, the coordinator checks it and reports it to the water company or the Environment Agency. Priya trains each tester on a [river ranger day](/blog/river-ranger-training/), and testers have already found the source of an oil leak, as our [clean-up day report](/blog/river-clean-up-day/) tells.

## What clean water means for wildlife

Clean water is where everything else starts. The kingfishers that came back to the lower Alder need clear water to hunt, and otters need plenty of fish. Our [kingfisher survey](/blog/kingfishers-lower-alder/) shows what happens when a river gets cleaner.
