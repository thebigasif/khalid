The shadowing technique means listening to a recording and speaking along with it, a moment behind, like a shadow. You copy the sounds, the rhythm and the melody of the sentence as you hear them. It trains your mouth and your ear together, and you can do it alone, anywhere you can talk out loud.

## Why the shadowing technique works

When you read a sentence aloud, you say it the way you think it sounds. When you shadow, you say it the way it really sounds. Over time, your speech takes on the rhythm of the language: where the stress falls, where words run together, where the voice rises. Psychologists have studied it for decades, as the [entry on speech shadowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_shadowing) explains.

## Choose what to shadow

Pick a recording that is short, clear and a little below your level. One speaker is easier than a conversation. Good choices:

- the audio of [graded readers](/blog/graded-readers/) you have already read
- slow news made for learners
- a podcast episode with a transcript

## Shadow in four rounds

1. **Listen** to a short passage, a few sentences long, while you read the transcript.
2. **Mumble along** quietly, with the transcript, a moment behind the speaker.
3. **Speak out loud** with the transcript, copying the rhythm, not just the words.
4. **Speak out loud without the transcript.** This is the round that counts.

Repeat the same passage on the next day before you start a new one.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Record yourself on your phone once a week and compare it with the original. You will hear progress that you cannot hear while you speak.
:::

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Starting with something too hard.** If you cannot follow the speaker with the transcript, you are only making noise. Go down a level.
- **Copying the words but not the tune.** Listen to where the voice rises and falls, and copy that first. The words follow.
- **Changing passages every day.** A passage you have shadowed a few times is where the gains come from. Stay with it until it feels easy.

## Use it before you speak

Shadowing is a good warm-up before a [language exchange](/blog/language-exchange/). Five minutes of shadowing puts the sound of the language in your head, and the first sentences of the meeting come more easily.
