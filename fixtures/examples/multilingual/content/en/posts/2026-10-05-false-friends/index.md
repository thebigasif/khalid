False friends are words that look or sound alike in two languages but mean different things. They feel safe because you think you already know them, which is exactly why they cause mistakes. English, German and Spanish share many roots, so they share many false friends too.

## False friends between English and German

| English word | German look-alike | What the German word means |
|---|---|---|
| gift | Gift | poison |
| become | bekommen | to get, to receive |
| handy | Handy | mobile phone |
| chef | Chef | boss |
| sensible | sensibel | sensitive |

"I will become a coffee" is a classic slip of a German speaker in English. In German, "Ich bekomme einen Kaffee" just means "I'll get a coffee".

## Spanish words that mislead English speakers

| English word | Spanish look-alike | What the Spanish word means |
|---|---|---|
| embarrassed | embarazada | pregnant |
| carpet | carpeta | folder |
| constipated | constipado | having a cold |
| sensible | sensible | sensitive |

An English speaker who says "estoy embarazada" after a small mistake has just announced a pregnancy. To say you are embarrassed, say "me da vergüenza" instead.

## Most similar words are true friends

Do not let false friends make you distrust every familiar word. Most words that look alike mean what you think. "Information", "Information" and "información" are the same idea in all three languages, and so are "hotel", "music" and "nation" with their German and Spanish twins. Linguists call these true friends cognates. They give an adult learner a large head start, so use them freely and check only the ones that seem odd in their sentence.

## Words that trick all three

Some words mislead in all three languages at once. "Actually" means "in fact" in English. German "aktuell" and Spanish "actual" both mean "current". "Eventually" means "in the end" in English. German "eventuell" and Spanish "eventualmente" mean "possibly".

:::callout{kind=tip}
When a new word looks like one you know, look it up anyway, once. If it is a false friend, put it in your [spaced repetition](/blog/spaced-repetition/) deck with an example sentence that shows the real meaning.
:::

## How to spot false friends early

Read a lot, and notice when a sentence does not make sense with the meaning you expected. That moment of surprise is a false friend showing itself. Talking with a partner in a [language exchange](/blog/language-exchange/) helps too, because a native speaker hears the slip at once and can tell you what you said.

Linguists have catalogued many more. The [entry on false friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_friend) is a good place to keep reading.
