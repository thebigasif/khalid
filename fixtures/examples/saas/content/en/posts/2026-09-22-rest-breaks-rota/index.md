Rest breaks are the pauses people take during a shift, and the rest they get between one shift and the next. Both are easy to forget when the shop is busy. The fix is to plan them when you build the rota, not to hope they happen on the day.

This guide uses the UK rules as its example, because they are clear and public. Check the rules where you work, and ask an adviser when you are unsure.

## What the UK rules ask for

The UK government's [page on rest breaks at work](https://www.gov.uk/rest-breaks-work) sets out three kinds of rest for adult workers:

- a break during the working day, if the day is long enough
- daily rest between one working day and the next
- weekly rest, without work, each week or each fortnight

Young workers get more. The page gives the exact hours for each, and it is worth reading in full once.

## Put rest breaks on the rota

:::callout{kind=tip}
Write the break time beside each shift, such as "break at half past twelve". A break with a time on the rota gets taken. A break left to "when it is quiet" does not.
:::

Stagger the rest breaks, so the counter is never empty. Put the longest break in the quietest hour, which your busy-hour map from [the staff rota guide](/blog/staff-rota-guide/) already shows.

## Check the gap between shifts

Read each person's week from top to bottom. Look hard at a late close followed by an early open. That pair is where the daily rest most often breaks.

Rotating patterns make this worse when they turn backwards. The fix is in [our comparison of shift patterns](/blog/fixed-vs-rotating-shifts/): rotate forwards, from early to late.

## On the day

Rest breaks slip most when someone is off sick. When you [cover a gap at short notice](/blog/cover-sick-call/), check that the person covering still gets their break and their rest.

Keep a note of breaks that were missed and why. Two missed breaks in the same slot mean the rota needs another person there, not a tougher manager.
