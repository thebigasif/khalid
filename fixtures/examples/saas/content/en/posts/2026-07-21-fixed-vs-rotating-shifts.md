Fixed shifts keep each person on the same hours every week, while rotating shifts move people through early, late and weekend hours in turn. Fixed shifts suit teams where people want a steady routine. Rotating shifts suit teams where the hard hours must be shared to feel fair.

Many small teams use a mix: a fixed core with a few rotating shifts on top. The table below sets the two side by side, so you can see what each one asks of your people.

## Fixed and rotating patterns compared

| | Fixed shifts | Rotating shifts |
|---|---|---|
| Routine for staff | The same hours each week | Hours change on a cycle |
| Fairness | Good if hard shifts are paid or chosen | Hard hours shared by everyone in turn |
| Planning effort | Low once set | Higher, the cycle must be kept |
| Childcare and study | Easy to plan around | Harder, needs notice of each cycle |
| Cover when someone leaves | Their hours leave a clear gap | The gap moves through the cycle |
| Sleep | Steady | Hard if the cycle jumps from late to early |

## Where rotating shifts go wrong

The common mistake is a fast rotation that runs backwards: a late shift, then an early shift the next morning. People lose sleep, and the short gap may break the rest rules where you work. If you rotate, move forwards: early, then middle, then late. Give each step at least a few days.

Our guide on [rest breaks and rest between shifts](/blog/rest-breaks-rota/) explains how to check the gaps as you plan.

## A mix that often works

:::callout{kind=note}
Ask the team before you choose. Some people want fixed hours for childcare or study, and others are happy to rotate for a fairer share of weekends.
:::

Keep fixed shifts for the people who need them. Rotate only the weekend and the late close among everyone else. Write the cycle down and share it a month ahead, so people can plan.

When you build the week, start with the rotating shifts, because they are the ones people notice. The steps in [how to plan a staff rota in an afternoon](/blog/staff-rota-guide/) put the hard shifts first for that reason.

If the team ends up trading shifts a lot, a [clear swap policy](/blog/shift-swap-policy/) stops the cycle from drifting. To compare the tools that keep a cycle running, see [the spreadsheet against an app](/blog/spreadsheet-vs-scheduling-app/).
