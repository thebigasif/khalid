These are the handouts for our autumn printing course, one section for each evening. They follow what we do in the darkroom, so you can repeat each step at your own pace. The printing course takes you from your first test strip to a finished, toned print.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Evening one: test strips and a straight print.
- Evening two: contrast, with filters and paper grades.
- Evening three: dodging and burning, to lighten and darken parts of a print.
- Evening four: a finished print, washed, dried and flattened.
:::

## Evening one: the test strip

Start every print with a test strip. Set the enlarger for the size you want and focus on the grain. Expose a strip of paper in bands, a little longer each time, then develop it. Choose the band where the important tones look right. Our guide to [the contact sheet](/blog/contact-sheet/) uses the same method.

## Evening two: contrast

Multigrade paper changes contrast with the filter in the enlarger. A low filter gives soft, grey prints. A high filter gives hard, punchy ones. Make the same print with three filters side by side and compare them before you choose.

## Evening three: dodging and burning

Dodging holds back light from part of the print with a card or your hand, so that area comes out lighter. Burning gives one area extra light, so it comes out darker. Keep the tool moving so the edges stay soft. Write each move on a printing map, a rough sketch of the frame with the times marked.

## Evening four: finishing the print

Wash fibre prints for longer than resin-coated ones. Dry them face down on screens, then flatten them under weight. Sign the back in pencil.

## Bring to every printing course session

Gloves, your negatives in sleeves and a notebook. Read [darkroom safety](/blog/darkroom-safety/) again before the first evening. To keep printing after the course ends, [book the darkroom](/blog/darkroom-booking/) as a member.
