A contact sheet is a print of a whole roll of negatives, laid directly on one sheet of photographic paper. Every frame comes out at its real size. It is the quickest way to see what you shot, and the best way to choose which frames are worth enlarging.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Lay the negatives on the paper under glass and expose them under the enlarger.
- Make a test strip first to find the right exposure.
- Mark the frames you like on the contact sheet with a wax pencil.
:::

## Make a contact sheet in six steps

::::howto{name="Make a contact sheet" time="30:00"}
:::step{name="Set up the trays"}
Fill three trays with developer, stop bath and fixer, in that order, and check each is at the temperature on its label.
:::
:::step{name="Set the enlarger"}
Raise the enlarger head until its light covers the whole sheet of paper. Stop the lens down a little for even light.
:::
:::step{name="Make a test strip"}
Under the safelight, lay a strip of paper and the negatives under glass. Cover all but a band, expose, uncover a little more, and repeat.
:::
:::step{name="Develop the test strip"}
Develop, stop and fix it. Pick the band where the clear film edge is just black and the frames show detail.
:::
:::step{name="Expose the full sheet"}
Lay the negatives on a full sheet under the glass, emulsion side down, and expose for the time you picked.
:::
:::step{name="Develop, fix and wash"}
Develop, stop and fix the sheet, then wash it well and hang it to dry.
:::
::::

## Choose the frames to print

Look at the frames with a magnifier. Check focus first, then the light, then the moment. Circle the frames worth printing with a wax pencil, and cross out the near-misses so you do not print them later by accident. The [history of the contact print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_print) is worth a read, too.

## Keep each contact sheet with its negatives

File each contact sheet with its sleeve of negatives, and write the roll's date and subject on the back. Our guide on [how to store negatives](/blog/store-negatives/) shows how.
