You can develop film at home without a darkroom. Black-and-white film only needs darkness for the first minute, while you load it into a light-tight tank. After that, every step happens with the lights on, at the kitchen sink. It is a cheap way to start with film, and a satisfying one.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- You need a changing bag, a developing tank, three chemicals and a thermometer.
- Only loading the film needs darkness. Everything after happens in daylight.
- Follow the time and temperature on your developer's data sheet. Do not guess.
:::

## What you need to develop film

- **A changing bag**, a light-tight bag with sleeves, so you can load the film without a dark room.
- **A developing tank with a reel.** The film winds onto the reel, and the tank keeps light out.
- **Developer, stop bath and fixer.** Developer makes the picture appear, stop bath halts it, and fixer makes it permanent.
- **A thermometer and a measuring jug.** Temperature changes the result more than anything else.
- **Gloves and clips** to hang the film to dry.

## Develop film in seven steps

::::howto{name="Develop black-and-white film at home" time="1:00:00"}
:::step{name="Load the film in the dark"}
Inside the changing bag, open the film, wind it onto the reel and close the tank. Practise first with an old roll in daylight.
:::
:::step{name="Bring the chemicals to temperature"}
Mix the developer as its data sheet says and bring it to the temperature the sheet gives. Keep the other chemicals at about the same temperature.
:::
:::step{name="Develop"}
Pour in the developer and start the timer. Turn the tank gently at the intervals the data sheet gives, then pour the developer out on time.
:::
:::step{name="Stop"}
Pour in the stop bath, or plain water, and turn the tank for the time on its label. This halts the developer at once.
:::
:::step{name="Fix"}
Pour in the fixer and turn the tank as its label says. After fixing, the film is no longer sensitive to light.
:::
:::step{name="Wash"}
Wash the film in clean water, changing the water several times. Leftover fixer slowly damages negatives.
:::
:::step{name="Dry"}
Hang the film in a dust-free room with a clip at each end. Let it dry fully before you cut it.
:::
::::

## Getting the times right

Every film and developer pair has its own time and temperature. Find them on the data sheet from the maker, such as [Ilford's technical data sheets](https://www.ilfordphoto.com/). Write them on a card and keep it by the sink.

Before you start, read [darkroom safety](/blog/darkroom-safety/): the chemicals still need gloves and fresh air. If you have not chosen a film yet, [choosing a film stock](/blog/choose-film-stock/) helps.
