Google ratings are the stars and short comments customers leave on your map listing. They help a local business twice. They help people nearby decide to trust you, and Google uses them when it ranks local listings. Most happy customers will leave one if you ask at the right moment and make it easy. This guide shows how to do both without feeling pushy.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Ask in person, at the moment the customer is pleased.
- Follow up the same day with a direct link to your rating form.
- Reply to every rating, and never offer anything in return for one.
:::

## When to ask for Google ratings

Ask at the moment the customer says thank you. For a physio, that is when a patient says the pain has eased. For a plumber, it is when the water runs hot again. A request at that moment feels natural, because you are answering their thanks.

## How to ask

:::pullquote
"I am glad that helped. Would you mind saying so on Google? It helps other people nearby find us."
:::

Then send the direct link to your rating form by text or email the same day. Every extra click loses people. The link is in your [Google Business Profile, set up with our checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist/).

If you send a welcome or thank-you email already, add the link there. Clara's guide to [a welcome email people read](/blog/welcome-email/) shows where it fits.

## What not to do

:::callout{kind=warning}
Never offer a discount, a gift or a prize for a rating, and never write one for yourself. Both break Google's rules, and in the UK the Competition and Markets Authority treats fake reviews as unlawful.
:::

The [Competition and Markets Authority](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/competition-and-markets-authority) publishes guidance for businesses on consumer law, including fake reviews.

## Reply to every rating

Thank people for good Google ratings by name. Answer poor ones calmly, once, and offer to talk offline. Readers judge you on the reply more than on the complaint, so a calm answer to a hard comment can win trust.

Keep a short list of replies you can adapt, so answering takes a minute, not an evening. Write each one fresh enough that it does not read as a template.

At [the physio clinic we worked with](/blog/work/physio-clinic-booking-page/), the front desk asks at the end of the last session in a course.
