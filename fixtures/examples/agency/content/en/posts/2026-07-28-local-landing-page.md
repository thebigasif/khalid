A local landing page is a page on your website about one service in one place, such as boiler repair in a nearby town. Done well, it is the page a nearby customer lands on from search or from your map listing. Done badly, it is the same text with a new town name, and search engines learn to ignore it.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Write one page for each place you really serve, not every town on the map.
- Put something on each page that is true only of that place.
- Make the next step obvious: a phone number, a form or a booking link.
:::

## What makes a local landing page useful

Ask what a customer in that town needs to know before they call you. Then answer it on the page.

- **How you get there.** How long it takes, and whether you charge for travel.
- **Work you have done there.** A short note on a job nearby, with the client's permission.
- **Local details.** Parking, access, or common problems with the local housing.
- **Who will come.** A name and a photo of the person who covers that area.

Google's guide to [creating helpful, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks the same question in its own words: would a reader leave satisfied?

## What to leave off

Leave off long lists of towns, and paragraphs that repeat the town name. Leave off pages for places you do not serve. A few strong pages beat many weak ones.
