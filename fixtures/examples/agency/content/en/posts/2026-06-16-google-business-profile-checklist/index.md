A Google Business Profile is the listing that shows your business on Google Maps and beside local search results. For a clinic, a trade or a shop, it is often the first thing a nearby customer sees, before your website. This checklist covers what to set up once and what to check each month.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Fill in every field that is true for your business, and nothing that is not.
- Pick the one main category that matches what customers search for.
- Add real photos and answer every review, good or bad.
- Check the profile once a month, because anyone can suggest changes to it.
:::

## Set up your Google Business Profile once

- **Name.** Your real business name, as it appears on your sign. Adding a town or a service to the name breaks Google's rules.
- **Main category.** The one that matches what customers search for, such as "Physiotherapist" rather than "Health".
- **Address or service area.** An address if customers visit you, a service area if you go to them.
- **Hours.** Opening hours, plus holiday hours before each bank holiday.
- **Services.** Each service in plain words, with a short line on what it includes.
- **Photos.** The outside, so people recognise it, the inside, and the team at work.

Google's own [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) list what the name and address may include. Read them once before you change anything.

## Check it each month

Anyone can suggest an edit to a profile, and Google sometimes accepts them. Once a month, open the profile and check the name, hours, phone number and website link. Then answer any reviews you have missed.

Ratings matter for both trust and ranking. Our guide on [how to earn more ratings](/blog/google-ratings/) gives a way to ask that does not feel pushy.

## Link the profile to the right page

Point the website link at the page that best matches the main category, not always your home page. A plumber whose main category is "Plumber" should link to a page about plumbing in their area.

If you do not have that page yet, read [how to write a local landing page](/blog/local-landing-page/) first. Our [case study of a family plumber](/blog/work/plumber-service-pages/) shows the profile and the pages working together.
