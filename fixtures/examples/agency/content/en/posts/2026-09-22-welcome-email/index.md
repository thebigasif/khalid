A welcome email is the first message a person gets after they join your list. It arrives when they still remember signing up, so it is the email they are most likely to open. A good welcome email tells them what to expect and gives them a reason to open the next one.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Send it straight away, from a person's name.
- Say what you will send and how often, in one line.
- Give them something they can use today, and ask one question back.
:::

## What to put in a welcome email

1. **A plain subject line.** "Welcome, and what to expect" works. Clever subject lines get skipped.
2. **Who it is from.** A real name, such as the owner, not "no-reply".
3. **What they will get.** One line: a short letter each month with offers and news from the shop.
4. **A gift for today.** A tip, a short guide or an offer they can use this week.
5. **One question.** "What brought you to us?" Replies teach you more than any report.

## Keep it short

Write it as a note, not a brochure. Three short paragraphs and a sign-off are enough. If you want to add more, save it for the second email.

Read it aloud before you send it. If a sentence sounds like an advert, say it the way you would at the counter, and write that down instead. People forgive a plain email. They delete one that sounds like it came from a machine.

## Test it on yourself

Sign up to your own list with a personal address. Check that the welcome email arrives within a minute, that the sender name is right, and that every link works on a phone. Then ask a friend to sign up too, and tell you honestly what they expected to get.
