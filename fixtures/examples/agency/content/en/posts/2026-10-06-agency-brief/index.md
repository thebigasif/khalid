An agency brief is a short document that tells a marketing agency what you want to achieve and what they have to work with. A clear one saves weeks of back and forth. It also lets you compare agencies fairly, because each one answers the same question. This guide shows what to put in a brief that fits on one page.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- One page is enough: the goal, the customer, the budget and the deadline.
- Say how you will judge success before the work starts.
- Share what you have tried before, including what failed.
:::

## What goes in an agency brief

- **The goal.** One sentence. "More bookings for new patients on weekdays" beats "raise awareness".
- **The customer.** Who you want to reach, where they live and what they care about.
- **What you have.** Your website, your map listing, your list, your photos, and who on your team can help.
- **What you have tried.** Past campaigns, and what you learned from them.
- **Budget and timing.** A range is fine. A missing budget leads to proposals you cannot use.
- **How you will judge it.** The signs that would tell you the work is going well.

## What to leave to the agency

Leave the how to them. Your agency brief should describe the problem, not the solution. If you ask for "a newsletter", you get a newsletter. If you ask for "more repeat visits from past customers", you may get a better idea.
