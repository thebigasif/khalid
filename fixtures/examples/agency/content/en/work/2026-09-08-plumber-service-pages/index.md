This case study is about service pages for a family plumbing firm of three people. Their website had a single page that listed every job they did, from dripping taps to new bathrooms. They wanted more boiler work and fewer small callouts, and they asked us how their website could help.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- A single page for every service could not match what people search for.
- We wrote separate service pages for the work the firm wants more of.
- We tidied the map listing and pointed it at the boiler page.
:::

## What we found

The firm's map listing linked to the home page, and its main category was a broad one. The list of jobs on the website gave each service a line, so no page answered a question in depth. Their reviews were strong but rarely mentioned boilers, because they rarely asked boiler customers.

## The service pages we wrote

1. **Three service pages.** Boiler servicing, boiler repair and bathroom fitting, the work the firm wants more of. Each page says what the job involves, how long it takes and who will come.
2. **The map listing.** We set the main category to match boiler work and linked the listing to the boiler repair page, using our [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist/).
3. **Ratings.** The firm now asks boiler customers for a rating on the day, with the method in our [post on Google ratings](/blog/google-ratings/).

Google's [search engine optimisation starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) recommends a page for each distinct topic. These three service pages follow that advice for a small trade.

## What changed

The owner says calls now open with the right question more often: "Can you look at my boiler?" rather than "Do you do boilers?" The firm keeps the old list of jobs as a short page linking to each service page.
