Plant garlic in autumn, and it is one of the least demanding crops on the plot. Each clove you put in the ground grows into a whole bulb by the next summer. Garlic needs a spell of cold weather to split into cloves, which is why autumn planting works so well in a cold climate like ours.

## Choose good cloves

Buy garlic sold for planting, from a seed company or a garden centre, rather than garlic from the shop. Shop garlic may carry disease, and it may be a variety suited to a warmer climate. Pick the biggest, firmest cloves from each bulb, because big cloves grow big bulbs.

## How to plant garlic

::::howto{name="Plant garlic"}
:::step{name="Prepare the bed"}
Choose a sunny bed that does not get waterlogged. On heavy clay, plant into a raised or no-dig bed.
:::
:::step{name="Split the bulbs"}
Split each bulb into cloves just before planting. Keep the papery skin on each clove.
:::
:::step{name="Plant pointed end up"}
Push each clove into the soil, pointed end up, so the tip is just below the surface. Space them about a hand's width apart.
:::
:::step{name="Keep the bed weeded"}
Garlic hates competition. Weed gently by hand through winter and spring.
:::
::::

Birds sometimes pull new cloves out. Cover the bed with netting for the first few weeks, until roots hold them down.

## When to harvest

Lift the bulbs in summer, when the lower leaves turn yellow. Do not wait until every leaf has died back, or the bulbs start to split apart in the ground. Ease them out with a fork rather than pulling, so the stems do not snap.

Dry them somewhere airy and out of the rain for a few weeks before you store them. I hang mine in bunches from the shed roof. Once the skins are papery and the roots are dry, rub off the soil, trim the roots and keep the bulbs somewhere cool and dry.

Garlic is the crop I recommend most in a [first year on an allotment](/blog/first-allotment/), because it grows while you sleep. When you lift it, sow [a winter cover crop](/blog/green-manure/) on the empty bed. Like tomatoes, you can keep your best cloves to plant again, much as I do when I [save tomato seed](/blog/tomato-seeds/).

The [entry on garlic at Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic) has more on its varieties and history.
