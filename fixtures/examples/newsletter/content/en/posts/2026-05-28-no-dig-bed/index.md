A no-dig bed is a growing bed made on top of the ground, not dug into it. You smother the weeds with cardboard, pile compost on top and plant straight into the compost. The soil life underneath is left alone to do its work. I made my first no-dig bed on a weedy corner of my plot in one afternoon, and I have grown in it every year since.

## What you need

- Plain brown cardboard, with the tape and labels taken off.
- Enough compost to cover the bed a good hand's depth deep. Garden compost, green waste compost from the council or well-rotted manure all work.
- Something to edge the bed if you want one, such as old boards. It is not essential.

## Build the no-dig bed in four steps

::::howto{name="Make a bed without digging"}
:::step{name="Cut the weeds down"}
Cut tall weeds and grass to the ground. Leave the roots where they are.
:::
:::step{name="Lay the cardboard"}
Lay cardboard over the whole bed, with wide overlaps so no light gets through. Wet it well.
:::
:::step{name="Spread the compost"}
Pile compost on top, a good hand's depth deep, and firm it gently with the back of a rake.
:::
:::step{name="Plant straight in"}
Plant young plants into the compost the same day, and water them in.
:::
::::

## What happens next

Most weeds die under the cardboard in a season. A few tough ones, such as bindweed, will find a way through. Pull them as soon as you see them, and they weaken over time.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Each autumn, add a thinner layer of compost or [leaf mould](/blog/leaf-mould/) to the top. That is all the feeding the bed needs.
:::

## Why I stopped digging

Digging heavy clay in spring was my least favourite job, and it brought new weed seeds to the surface every time. Since I stopped, my beds stay softer and I weed less. In winter I cover any bare beds with [a sown cover crop](/blog/green-manure/) instead.

If you are planning your [first season on an allotment](/blog/first-allotment/), a bed built this way is the quickest way to get growing. For the background on the method, see the [entry on no-dig gardening at Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-dig_gardening).
