A flat bike tyre is the repair we see most at the workshop, and it is the one you can most easily do yourself. With a pump, two tyre levers and a spare tube or a patch kit, you can fix a flat at home in well under an hour, even the first time.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Find out why the tyre went flat before you fit a new tube, or the new one goes flat too.
- Always feel around the inside of the tyre for what caused the hole.
- A new tube is quicker at the roadside. A patch is fine at home.
:::

## What you need to fix a flat bike tyre

- A pump that fits your valve. Most bikes have one of two kinds: a wide car-style valve or a thin valve with a small nut at the tip.
- Two plastic tyre levers. Metal ones can cut the tube or scratch the rim.
- A spare inner tube of the right size, or a patch kit. The size is printed on the side of the tyre.

## Fix a flat bike tyre in six steps

::::howto{name="Fix a flat bike tyre" time="30:00"}
:::step{name="Take the wheel off"}
Shift to the smallest rear cog first if it is the back wheel. Open the brake if it has a quick release, then undo the axle and lift the wheel out.
:::
:::step{name="Let out the rest of the air"}
Press the valve to empty the tube. A tube with no air in it comes out much more easily.
:::
:::step{name="Lever one side of the tyre off"}
Hook a lever under the tyre bead opposite the valve, and clip it to a spoke. Slide the second lever around the rim until one side of the tyre is free.
:::
:::step{name="Find the cause"}
Pull out the tube. Run your fingers slowly around the inside of the tyre to find any thorn or glass. Check the rim tape covers every spoke hole.
:::
:::step{name="Fit the new or patched tube"}
Put a little air in the tube so it holds its shape. Push the valve through the rim, tuck the tube in, then roll the tyre back on with your thumbs.
:::
:::step{name="Pump it up and check"}
Pump to the pressure printed on the tyre. Spin the wheel and check the tyre sits evenly all the way round on both sides.
:::
::::

:::callout{kind=warning}
If the tyre has a cut you can see the tube through, replace the tyre. A new tube will push out through the cut and burst.
:::

## Why the new tube goes flat too

Most second flats have the same cause as the first. A thorn left in the tyre will find the new tube within a few miles. So will a sharp edge on the rim, or rim tape that has slipped and left a spoke hole open. Take your time on step four. It is the step that saves you doing the job again.

If you often get a flat bike tyre, look at the tyre itself. A worn tyre has less rubber between the road and the tube. [Getting your bike ready for winter](/blog/winter-riding/) explains when tougher tyres are worth it. Sheldon Brown's [guide to flat tyres](https://www.sheldonbrown.com/flats.html) goes deeper into the causes, if you are curious.

Once the wheel is back on, run through [the safety check before every ride](/blog/pre-ride-safety-check/) to make sure the brake is closed again.
