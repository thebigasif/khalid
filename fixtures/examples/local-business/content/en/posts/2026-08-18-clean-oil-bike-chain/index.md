A clean and oiled bike chain makes the whole bike feel better. Gears change more smoothly, the bike runs quieter, and the chain and cogs wear out more slowly. Cleaning and oiling a chain at home takes about a quarter of an hour, and you need nothing you cannot buy at a hardware shop.

:::callout{kind=summary}
- Wipe the chain after wet rides and oil it when it looks dry or starts to squeak.
- Oil goes on the inside of the chain, one drop on each roller, then wipe off the extra.
- Too much oil is as bad as too little. It picks up grit that grinds the chain down.
:::

## What you need

- A clean, dry rag. An old cotton T-shirt is ideal.
- A stiff brush, such as an old toothbrush.
- A bike chain oil. Use a wet oil for winter and rain, and a dry oil for dry summer roads.
- Warm water with a little washing-up liquid, for a chain that is very dirty.

## Clean and oil the chain

::::howto{name="Clean and oil a bike chain" time="15:00"}
:::step{name="Wipe off the old dirt"}
Hold the rag loosely around the lower part of the chain. Turn the pedals backwards so the whole chain runs through the rag several times.
:::
:::step{name="Scrub what the rag misses"}
Brush the chain, the small wheels on the rear gear and the cogs. Use soapy water if the dirt is thick, then rinse and dry everything well.
:::
:::step{name="Oil each roller"}
Turn the pedals slowly backwards and put one drop of oil on each roller, on the inside of the chain.
:::
:::step{name="Let it soak in"}
Turn the pedals for half a minute and change through the gears, so the oil works into every link.
:::
:::step{name="Wipe off the extra"}
Run the chain through a clean rag again. The outside of the chain should feel almost dry. The oil that matters is inside the rollers.
:::
::::

:::callout{kind=tip}
Keep the oil off the brakes and the wheel rims. Oil on a braking surface makes the brakes slip. If you spill some, clean the rim with soapy water at once.
:::

## When a bike chain needs replacing

A chain stretches as it wears, and a stretched chain wears out your cogs too. A new chain is cheap. A new set of cogs is not. We check every chain with a gauge during [a bike service](/blog/bike-service/), and you can buy the same gauge for home. If the chain jumps when you pedal hard, bring it in.

Before the wet months, read [getting your bike ready for winter](/blog/winter-riding/). Rain and road salt are hard on a chain, and a few minutes of care each week saves a lot of wear. For more on chain wear, the [Cycling UK technical guides](https://www.cyclinguk.org/) are a good, plain place to start.
