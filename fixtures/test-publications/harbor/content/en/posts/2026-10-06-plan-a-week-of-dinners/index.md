To plan a week of dinners in twenty minutes, pick five meals that share two bases, write one shopping list sorted by aisle, and leave one night free for leftovers. That is the whole method. The rest of this guide shows how we do each step in the Harbor Kitchen test kitchen.

## Pick five meals that share two bases

A base is something you cook once and use twice. A pot of rice can carry a stir fry on Monday and fried rice on Thursday. A tray of roast vegetables can fill a salad on Tuesday and a pasta on Wednesday. When two bases carry five dinners, the week asks for less chopping and less washing up.

Start with what your household already likes. Write down three meals everyone eats without complaint, then add two that use the same bases. Do not plan anything new for a Monday, when the week is at its busiest.

## Write one shopping list sorted by aisle

One list means one trip. Sort it by where things sit in your shop: fresh produce first, then the fridge, then the shelves. A sorted list takes two minutes longer to write and saves ten minutes in the shop, every week.

Check the cupboard before you write the list. Most waste comes from buying a second jar of something already open at the back of the fridge.

## Leave one night for leftovers

Plan five dinners for a seven-night week. One night takes what is left, and one night is free for a meal out or a change of plan. A plan with no slack breaks the first time a day runs late, and a broken plan is how food ends up in the bin.

## Keep the plan where you can see it

Write the week on a note on the fridge. When the plan is in front of you, nobody has to ask what is for dinner, and the cook can start the base the night before. For how we keep herbs fresh all week, read [why we pack herbs in paper](/blog/herbs-in-paper/).
