We pack herbs in paper, not plastic, because paper keeps them fresh for longer. Plastic traps the water herbs breathe out, and wet leaves turn dark and soft within two or three days. Paper takes up that water and lets the leaves breathe, so the same bunch stays green for most of a week.

## What we tested

Over four weeks we stored the same herbs three ways in a home fridge: in a plastic bag, in a paper bag, and standing in a glass of water. Each day we counted how many bunches were still good to cook with.

| Herb | Plastic bag | Paper bag | Glass of water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parsley | 3 days | 6 days | 8 days |
| Coriander | 2 days | 5 days | 6 days |
| Basil | 2 days | 4 days | 5 days, out of the fridge |
| Mint | 3 days | 6 days | 7 days |
| Chives | 4 days | 7 days | 5 days |
| Dill | 2 days | 5 days | 4 days |

A glass of water did best for soft herbs, but it takes room in the fridge and tips over in a box on a delivery van. Paper came close, and it travels well.

## How to store herbs at home

Keep herbs in their paper until you cook. If the paper is soaked, change it for a dry kitchen towel. Keep basil out of the fridge, since the cold turns its leaves black. For the rest, the salad drawer is the best place, away from the back wall where the fridge is coldest.
