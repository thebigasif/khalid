## Who this SEO writing course is for

It is for people who write for a small business blog and want the posts to be found. You might be a marketer who writes on the side, a founder, or a new writer on a content team. You do not need to know any code or own any paid tool.

Pick one real topic before you start the SEO writing course. Every exercise builds on the one before it, so by lesson seven you hold a finished draft about that topic.

## Lesson 1: read the search

**What you learn.** Every query hides a goal. Some searchers want a quick fact, some want a method, and some want to compare products. That goal decides the shape of your post before you write a word.

**Exercise.** Search for your topic. Note the type of page in the top five results: guide, list, tool or product page. Write one sentence on what the searcher wants.

**Read next:** [reading a results page, explained for writers](/blog/search-intent/).

## Lesson 2: choose your questions

**What you learn.** A topic holds many questions. Some are asked often, and some are narrow but easy to win. A small blog often does best with the narrow ones first, the [[long-tail-keyword|long-tail keywords]] that bigger sites skip.

**Exercise.** List ten questions people ask about your topic. Use the search suggestions and the questions on the results page. Circle the one your post will answer.

**Read next:** [our keyword research guide for small blogs](/blog/keyword-research/).

## Lesson 3: brief the post

**What you learn.** A brief is a one-page plan: the question, the reader, the headings and the sources. It saves more time than it costs, and it lets someone else review the plan before the writing starts.

**Exercise.** Fill in a brief for your circled question. Keep it to one page.

**Read next:** [the brief template we use with clients](/blog/content-brief-template/).

## Lesson 4: write the draft

**What you learn.** Readers scan before they commit. Put the answer near the top, give each heading one clear claim, and keep paragraphs short. This lesson is the heart of the SEO writing course, so give it the most time.

**Exercise.** Write the short answer first, in two or three sentences. Then write one section per heading in your brief.

**Read next:** [our complete guide to posts that rank](/blog/seo-writing-guide/).

## Lesson 5: write the title

**What you learn.** The [[title-tag|title tag]] is the blue link on the results page. It has to say what the page answers and give a reason to click, without promising more than the page delivers.

**Exercise.** Write five titles for your draft. Read them aloud and keep the one that a stranger would understand at once.

**Read next:** [headlines that earn the click without the bait](/blog/writing-headlines/).

## Lesson 6: write the description

**What you learn.** The meta description is the short text under the title in many results. Search engines may rewrite it, but a good one often stays, and it is your chance to make a promise.

**Exercise.** Write a description that names the question and what the reader will get. Keep it to one or two sentences.

**Read next:** [how to write the meta description under your title](/blog/meta-description/).

## Lesson 7: edit before you publish

**What you learn.** The second draft is where a post gets good. Cut what repeats, check every number has a source, and read it on a phone.

**Exercise.** Run your draft through the checklist. Cut at least one paragraph that does not serve the question.

**Read next:** [the editing checklist we run on every post](/blog/editing-checklist/).

## After the SEO writing course

Publish the post, then put a date in your calendar to check it in three months. Look at the searches it shows up for in Google's free [Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about). They tell you which questions to add next.

:::callout{kind=tip}
Take the SEO writing course again with a second topic. The lessons go faster the second time, and you will spot what you skipped the first time.
:::
