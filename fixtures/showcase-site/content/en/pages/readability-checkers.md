Our editors check every draft for long sentences before it goes live. We tried two readability checkers on the same client drafts for a month, and this page is what we found. Nobody paid to be listed here.

::toc

## Which readability checker should a small team pick?

Pick the checker your whole team will open each day, because a tool nobody runs finds nothing. For most small teams that is Quillmeter: it flags long sentences and rare words in place, leaves every rewrite to the writer, works offline, and costs nothing to try. Pick Plainly when you need shared settings.

:::pullquote{by="Maren Okafor, editor"}
A checker should point at the sentence and then get out of the writer's way.
:::

## Quillmeter

:::review{item="Quillmeter" type="SoftwareApplication" rating="4" best="5" average="4.3" ratings="128" author="Maren Okafor"}
Quillmeter does one job well. It highlights long sentences and rare words as you type and never rewrites anything for you. It loses a point because it treats every long word as a problem, even the technical terms a specialist reader expects.
:::

:::proscons{pros="What works" cons="What does not"}
+ Marks long sentences in place, as you type
+ Works offline and opens in a second
- Flags technical terms a specialist reader expects
- No way to share settings across a team
:::
